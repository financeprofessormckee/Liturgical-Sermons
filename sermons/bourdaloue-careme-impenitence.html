¶p. 463; p. 464; p. 465
French original

CE sont deux grands maux que le péché & la mort: le péché, par où la mort est entrée dans le monde; & la mort, par où Dieu a puni le péché: le péché, qui dégrade l’homme dans l’ordre de la grace; & la mort, qui le détruit dans l’ordre de la nature: le péché, qui nous a fait tomber de ce bienheureux état d’innocence, où Dieu nous avoit créés; & la mort, qui nous dépouille de tous les biens temporels dont Dieu après le péché nous a encore laissé l’usage. Mais après tout, Chrétiens, ni la mort ni le péché pris séparément, ne sont point des maux extrêmes; & j’ose même dire qu’ils peuvent avoir leur avantage & leur utilité. Car la mort sans le péché peut être sainte & prétieuse devant Dieu; & le péché sans la mort peut servir de matiére aux plus excellentes vertus qui rendent l’homme agréable à Dieu. La mort sans le péché fut dans Jesus-Christ une source de graces & de mérites; & le péché sans la mort, comme l’enseigne la théologie, a été dans les prédestinés & un principe & un effet de leur prédestination. La mort sans le péché acheva de sanctifier Marie; & le péché sans la mort devint un motif de conversion pour Magdelaine. Mais le souverain mal & ce qu’il y a de plus affreux, c’est le péché & la mort unis ensemble: la mort qui met le dernier sceau à l’impénitence du pécheur; & le péché qui imprime à la mort le caractére de sa malice: la mort qui rend le péché pour jamais irrémissible; & le péché qui rend la mort pour toujours criminelle & reprouvée. La mort dans le péché, la mort avec le péché, la mort même, comme il arrive souvent, par le péché: voilà, mes chers Auditeurs, ce qui m’effraye, & ce qui doit vous effrayer comme moi: voilà ce que Dieu a de plus terrible dans les trésors de sa colére; voilà de quoi le Fils de Dieu menace aujourd’hui les Juifs, & de quoi nous avons aussi bien que les Juifs à nous préserver. Pour bien entrer dans ces sentimens, implorons le secours du ciel par l’intercession de la Vierge que nous prions tous les jours de nous être favorable à la mort, & disions-lui, Ave , Maria.

English translation

There are two great evils: sin and death: sin, by which death entered into the world; and death, by which God has punished sin: sin, which degrades man in the order of grace; and death, which destroys him in the order of nature: sin, which made us fall from that blessed state of innocence in which God had created us; and death, which strips us of all the temporal goods of which, even after sin, God had still left us the use. But after all, Christians, neither death nor sin taken separately are extreme evils; and I dare even say that they can have their advantage and their usefulness. For death without sin can be holy and precious before God; and sin without death can serve as the matter of the most excellent virtues which make man pleasing to God. Death without sin was in Jesus Christ a source of graces and merits; and sin without death, as theology teaches, has been in the predestined both a principle and an effect of their predestination. Death without sin completed the sanctification of Mary; and sin without death became a motive of conversion for Magdalene. But the sovereign evil, and the most dreadful thing of all, is sin and death united together: death, which sets the final seal on the sinner's impenitence; and sin, which imprints upon death the character of its malice: death, which renders sin forever irremissible; and sin, which renders death forever criminal and reprobate. Death in sin, death with sin, death even, as often happens, by sin: this, my dear hearers, is what terrifies me, and what ought to terrify you as it does me: this is the most terrible thing God has in the treasures of his wrath: this is what the Son of God today threatens the Jews with, and what we, no less than the Jews, must guard against. To enter well into these sentiments, let us implore the help of heaven through the intercession of the Virgin, whom we pray every day to be favorable to us at death, and let us say to her, Ave, Maria.

French original

C’Etoit, Chrétiens, une triste vérité pour les Juifs, mais une vérité fondée sur la parole même de Jesus-Christ, qu’après avoir vécu dans le péché, ils mourroient dans l’impénitence: In peccato vestro moriemini. Or en quel sens cet oracle doit-il être entendu? car il nous importe de le bien sçavoir, puisque le Sauveur du monde nous parloit à nous-mêmes dans la personne des Juifs, & qu’il n’y va pas moins que d’une éternelle réprobation. Est-ce une simple menace que Jesus-Christ faisoit à cette nation incrédule, pour les obliger à se reconnoître? Est-ce un arrêt définitif qu’il portoit contre eux; & prétendoit-il leur signifier que la mesure de leurs crimes étoit remplie, & qu’ils n’avoient plus de grace à espérer de la part de Dieu? saint Chrysostome l’a pris dans le sens le plus favorable; & ce Pere estime que ce fut seulement comme une sentence comminatoire qui déclaroit aux Juifs ce qu’ils avoient à craindre, s’ils demeuroient plus long-tems dans leur infidélité; de même que Jonas, en prêchant aux Ninivites, leur annonça qu’après le terme de quarante jours Ninive seroit détruite: Adhuc quadraginta1 dies, & Ninive subvertetur. Saint Jérôme s’est attaché à la lettre; & sa pensée est que le Fils de Dieu ne parloit pas seulement aux Juifs en Prophéte pour les intimider, mais en juge & en souverain pour les condamner: c’est-à-dire, qu’il ne leur marquoit pas seulement le danger où ils étoient d’une reprobation prochaine; mais qu’il leur intimoit expressément que leur réprobation étoit déja consommée. Car, reprend ce saint Docteur, quand Dieu dans l’Ecriture veut seulement menacer, il ajoûte toujours à ses menaces des conditions qui en suspendent l’effet & qui les modifient. Ainsi, dit-il à Adam: Si tu manges de ce fruit, tu mourras; In quo enim2 die comederis, morte morieris; au lieu que le Sauveur du monde faisoit une proposition absolue, en disant aux Juifs: vous mourrez dans votre péché: In peccato vestro moriemini.

English translation

It was, Christians, a sad truth for the Jews, but a truth founded on the very word of Jesus Christ, that after having lived in sin, they would die in impenitence: In peccato vestro moriemini. Now in what sense must this oracle be understood? For it matters to us to know this well, since the Savior of the world was speaking to us ourselves in the person of the Jews, and since nothing less than an eternal reprobation is at stake. Was it a simple threat that Jesus Christ made to this unbelieving nation, to oblige them to come to their senses? Was it a definitive sentence that he pronounced against them; and did he intend to signify to them that the measure of their crimes was filled up, and that they had no more grace to hope for from God? Saint Chrysostom took it in the most favorable sense; and this Father holds that it was only like a comminatory sentence which declared to the Jews what they had to fear, if they remained any longer in their unbelief; just as Jonah, in preaching to the Ninevites, announced to them that after a term of forty days Nineveh would be destroyed: Adhuc quadraginta dies, & Ninive subvertetur. Saint Jerome held to the letter; and his thought is that the Son of God was not speaking to the Jews merely as a Prophet to intimidate them, but as a judge and sovereign to condemn them: that is to say, that he was not merely marking out for them the danger they were in of an imminent reprobation; but that he was expressly declaring to them that their reprobation was already consummated. For, this holy Doctor continues, when God in Scripture intends only to threaten, he always adds to his threats conditions which suspend their effect and modify them. Thus he says to Adam: If you eat of this fruit, you shall die; In quo enim die comederis, morte morieris; whereas the Savior of the world was making an absolute statement, in saying to the Jews: you will die in your sin: In peccato vestro moriemini.1, 2

French original

Mais du reste, Chrétiens, soit que ce soit un arrêt, ou que ce soit précisément une menace, n’est-ce pas assez pour nous faire trembler que ce soit la menace d’un Dieu? d’un Dieu, qui ne parle point en vain; d’un Dieu, qui ne parle point par passion; d’un Dieu, qui ne parle point sans connoissance: mais qui pénétrant dans le fond des cœurs, & découvrant d’un coup d’œil tout l’avenir, voit par avance à quoi se doit terminer notre vie & quelle en sera la fin: In peccato vestro moriemini. Ne nous en tenons pas là néanmoins; mais consultons l’expérience, & voyons si l’expérience vérifie à l’égard des pécheurs cette prédiction de Jesus-Christ; car après la parole de Dieu , la preuve la plus convaincante & la plus sensible, c’est l’expérience. Comment donc meurent presque tous les pécheurs du siécle; je dis ces pécheurs d’état & de profession, ces pécheurs obstinés dans leurs désordres, qui jamais n’ont fait une vraie pénitence pendant la vie; comment meurent-ils? Ah! mes Freres , c’est ici que nous devons reconnoître une providence bien sévère & bien terrible sur les impies , comme il y en a une toute aimable & toute bienfaisante sur les justes. Ils meurent, ces pécheurs invétérés, comme ils ont vécu. Ils ont vécu dans le péché, & ils meurent dans le péché. Ils ont vécu dans la haine de Dieu, & ils meurent dans la haine de Dieu. Ils ont vécu en Payens, & ils meurent en reprouvés: voilà ce que l’expérience nous apprend.

English translation

But besides, Christians, whether it be a sentence or whether it be precisely a threat, is it not enough to make us tremble that it is the threat of a God? of a God who does not speak in vain; of a God who does not speak out of passion; of a God who does not speak without knowledge: but who, penetrating to the depths of hearts, and discovering at a single glance the whole future, sees in advance how our life must end and what its outcome will be: In peccato vestro moriemini. Let us not stop there, however; but let us consult experience, and see whether experience verifies, with regard to sinners, this prediction of Jesus Christ; for after the word of God, the most convincing and most palpable proof is experience. How then do almost all the sinners of the world die; I mean those sinners of settled state and profession, those sinners obstinate in their disorders, who never made a true penance during their life; how do they die? Ah! my Brothers, it is here that we must recognize a providence most severe and most terrible toward the impious, just as there is one wholly loving and wholly beneficent toward the just. These inveterate sinners die as they have lived. They have lived in sin, and they die in sin. They have lived in hatred of God, and they die in hatred of God. They have lived as pagans, and they die as reprobates: this is what experience teaches us.

French original

Mais pour vous en donner une idée plus juste, & pour partager ce discours, je les divise en trois espéces différentes. Car les uns meurent dans le désordre actuel de l’impénitence; les autres meurent sans nul sentiment & nulle démonstration de pénitence; & les derniers meurent dans l’exercice, ou, pour mieux dire, dans l’illusion d’une fausse pénitence. Les premiers sont les plus criminels, parce qu’ils ajoûtent à tous les péchés de leur vie celui de l’impénitence finale, par où il est vrai de dire qu'ils se reprouvent eux-mêmes, & qu’ils consomment positivement leur damnation. Les seconds sont plus malheureux & par là-même plus dignes de compassion, parce que sans le vouloir & sans y penser, ils se trouvent privés des secours de la pénitence. Les derniers participent à la condamnation des uns & des autres: & sans être, ni si criminels que les premiers, ni si malheureux que les seconds, ils sont toutefois, & malheureux parce qu’ils sont aveugles, & criminels parce qu’ils sont pécheurs & impénitens. Ainsi, j’appelle l’impénitence des premiers, une impénitence criminelle. J’appelle l’impénitence des seconds, une impénitence malheureuse: & j’appelle l’impénitence des derniers, une impénitence secréte & inconnue, ou si vous voulez, une fausse pénitence qui n’est au fond qu’une véritable impénitence. Ce n’est pas tout. Car après vous avoir marqué ces trois caractéres de pécheurs qui meurent dans leur péché, je dois ajoûter trois réflexions; pour vous faire connoître comment l’impénitence de la vie conduit à l’impénitence de la mort: comprenez ceci. Je dis que l’impénitence de la vie conduit à l’impénitence criminelle de la mort, par voie de disposition, ce sera la premiére partie. Je dis que l’impénitence de la vie conduit à l’impénitence malheureuse de la mort, par voie de punition, ce sera la seconde partie. Enfin je dis que l’impénitence de la vie conduit à l’impénitence secréte & inconnue, ou à la fausse pénitence de la mort, par voie d’illusion, ce sera la troisiéme partie. Commençons.

English translation

But to give you a more exact idea of this, and to divide this discourse, I distinguish them into three different kinds. For some die in the actual disorder of impenitence; others die with no feeling and no sign of penitence; and the last die in the exercise, or rather, in the illusion, of a false penitence. The first are the most criminal, because they add to all the sins of their life that of final impenitence, by which it is true to say that they condemn themselves, and that they positively bring about their own damnation. The second are more unfortunate, and for that very reason more deserving of compassion, because without willing it and without thinking of it, they find themselves deprived of the helps of penitence. The last share in the condemnation of both the others: and without being either as criminal as the first, or as unfortunate as the second, they are nevertheless both unfortunate, because they are blind, and criminal, because they are sinners and impenitent. Thus I call the impenitence of the first a criminal impenitence. I call the impenitence of the second an unfortunate impenitence: and I call the impenitence of the last a secret and unknown impenitence, or if you will, a false penitence which is at bottom nothing but a true impenitence. That is not all. For after having marked out for you these three characters of sinners who die in their sin, I must add three reflections, to make known to you how the impenitence of life leads to the impenitence of death: understand this. I say that the impenitence of life leads to the criminal impenitence of death by way of disposition; this will be the first part. I say that the impenitence of life leads to the unfortunate impenitence of death by way of punishment; this will be the second part. Finally, I say that the impenitence of life leads to the secret and unknown impenitence, or to the false penitence, of death, by way of illusion; this will be the third part. Let us begin.

French original

On peut mourir3 dans le désordre actuel & dans le péché de l’impénitence finale en deux maniéres; ou par une volonté délibérée de renoncer absolument à la pénitence, lors même qu’on se trouve aux approches de la mort; ou par une omission criminelle des moyens ordinaires & marqués de Dieu, pour rentrer en grace avec lui & pour faire pénitence. Or ces deux genres de mort sont si communs dans le monde qu’ils pourroient suffire pour justifier la prédiction du Fils de Dieu; In peccato vestro moriemini. Entrons, Chrétiens, dans cet abysme d’iniquités; tâchons d’en pénétrer la profondeur; & pour nous rendre cette considération plus utile, ne craignons point de descendre à un détail, qui seul servira de preuve à la plus terrible de toutes les vérités du Christianisme.

English translation

One can die in the actual disorder, and in the sin, of final impenitence in two ways: either by a deliberate will to renounce penitence absolutely, even when one finds oneself at the approach of death; or by a criminal omission of the ordinary means, appointed by God, for returning to his grace and for doing penance. Now these two kinds of death are so common in the world that they alone could suffice to justify the prediction of the Son of God: In peccato vestro moriemini. Let us enter, Christians, into this abyss of iniquities; let us try to penetrate its depth; and to make this consideration more useful to us, let us not fear to descend into a detail which, by itself, will serve as proof of the most terrible of all the truths of Christianity.3

French original

Quand je dis mourir dans une volonté délibérée de renoncer absolument à la pénitence, prenez garde, s’il vous plaît, à ce que j’entens. Je ne parle pas de ce qui peut arriver, & de ce qui arrive en effet quelquefois par une impénitence affectée, lorsque le pécheur se voyant forcé de quitter la vie, ne veut pas reconnoître celui dont il l’a reçuë, & qui lui en va demander compte; & que prêt à paroître devant le tribunal de Dieu, il ose encore se révolter contre Dieu-même, en disant comme ce peuple infidèle: Non serviam4; non, je ne m’humilierai point. Car quoique nous en ayons des exemples, & que ceux qui passent pour athées, & qui le sont au moins de mœurs & de conduite, soient sujets à mourir de la sorte; ces exemples, dit judicieusement S. Ainsi sont morts tant d’ennemis de Dieu, dont la fin aussi funeste qu’impie a tant de fois malgré eux rendu témoignage au souverain pouvoir & à la divinité de ce premier être, qu’ils avoient méconnu, ou plus vraisemblablement qu’ils avoient tâché, mais en vain, à méconnoître. Ainsi meurent tous les jours au milieu de nous, je ne sçais combien de mondains, qui sont encore, après avoir vécu sans foi, sans loi, sans religion, sans conscience, assez téméraires & assez emportés, pour vouloir couronner l’œuvre par une persévérance diabolique dans leur libertinage. Mais encore une fois ce sont des monstres dans l’ordre de la grace, sur qui nous ne devons jetter les yeux qu’autant qu’il est nécessaire pour les détester & pour les avoir en exécration.

English translation

When I say dying in a deliberate will to renounce penitence absolutely, take care, if you please, as to what I mean. I am not speaking of what can happen, and what indeed sometimes does happen through a deliberate impenitence, when the sinner, seeing himself forced to leave this life, refuses to acknowledge the One from whom he received it, and who is going to demand an accounting of it from him; and when, ready to appear before the tribunal of God, he still dares to revolt against God himself, saying as that unfaithful people said: Non serviam; no, I will not humble myself. For although we have examples of this, and although those who pass for atheists, and who are at least such in morals and conduct, are liable to die in this manner; these examples, as St. Chrysostom judiciously says, are so monstrous that they inspire horror by themselves, and a minister of the Gospel, so as not to wound the piety of his hearers, ought rather to omit them than undertake to combat them. Thus died a Julian the Apostate, vomiting a thousand blasphemies against heaven, while he vomited forth with his blood his impure and sacrilegious soul. Thus have died so many enemies of God, whose end, as disastrous as it was impious, has so often, in spite of themselves, borne witness to the sovereign power and divinity of that first Being whom they had denied, or more likely whom they had tried, but in vain, to deny. Thus there die every day among us, I know not how many worldlings, who, after having lived without faith, without law, without religion, without conscience, are still rash and headstrong enough to wish to crown their work with a diabolical perseverance in their libertinism. But once again, these are monsters in the order of grace, upon whom we should cast our eyes only insofar as is necessary to detest them and to hold them in execration.4

Ce n’est donc point par de semblables exemples, que je veux vérifier l’oracle de Jesus-Christ: mais je parle seulement de tant d’autres pécheurs, en qui cet état d’impénitence, tel que jai marqué, est aussi souvent un effet de la foiblesse que de la malice de leur cœur, ou plutôt, est un effet tout-ensemble de l’un & de l’autre: & pour vous faire comprendre plus distinctement & plus précisément ma pensée, je parle d’un homme, qui rempli de fiel & d’amertume, après avoir passé sa vie dans des haines & des inimitiés scandaleuses, meurt sans jamais vouloir se réconcilier, protestant qu’il ne le peut; ou s’il le fait en apparence, se disant intérieurement à lui-même qu’il ne le veut pas: témoin ce Chrétien, qui sur le point même d’endurer le martyre, refusa d’embrasser son ennemi, quoique son ennemi humilié à ses piés, lui demandât grace. Or sans nous arrêter à ces circonstances particuliéres, combien voyons-nous de pareilles morts dans le Christianisme, de morts sans réconciliation, de morts accompagnées de toute l’aigreur du ressentiment & de la vengeance; de morts, où tous ces prétendus accommodemens qui se négocient, toutes ces entrevûes qui se ménagent quelquefois avec tant de pompe & presque toujours avec si peu de fruit, ne sont que de pures & de trompeuses cérémonies; de morts, où par une maxime de politique, & par une force d’esprit mal entendue & poussée néanmoins jusques au bout, l’on se rend plus intraitable & plus inflexible que jamais: pourquoi? pour autoriser en mourant la conduite qu’on a tenue jusques-là, & l’animosité où l’on a vieilli; disons mieux, pour exécuter l’arrêt prononcé par le Sauveur du monde: In peccato vestro moriemini.

English translation

It is not, then, by examples such as these that I wish to verify the oracle of Jesus Christ; but I am speaking only of so many other sinners, in whom this state of impenitence, such as I have described, is as often an effect of the weakness as of the malice of their heart, or rather is an effect of both together: and to make you understand my thought more distinctly and more precisely, I am speaking of a man who, full of gall and bitterness, after having spent his life in scandalous hatreds and enmities, dies without ever wishing to be reconciled, protesting that he cannot do so; or if he does so in appearance, saying inwardly to himself that he does not wish to. Witness that Christian who, at the very point of enduring martyrdom, refused to embrace his enemy, although his enemy, humbled at his feet, begged his pardon. Now, without dwelling on these particular circumstances, how many such deaths do we see in Christendom—deaths without reconciliation, deaths accompanied by all the bitterness of resentment and vengeance; deaths in which all those supposed accommodations that are negotiated, all those interviews that are sometimes arranged with so much pomp and almost always with so little fruit, are nothing but pure and deceptive ceremonies; deaths in which, by a maxim of worldly policy, and by a strength of mind ill-conceived yet carried through to the end, one makes oneself more intractable and more inflexible than ever: why? In order to justify in dying the conduct one has maintained up to that point, and the animosity in which one has grown old; let us say rather, in order to carry out the sentence pronounced by the Savior of the world: In peccato vestro moriemini.

Je parle d’un homme qui se trouvant chargé à la mort de biens injustement acquis, dont il s’est fait un état & une fortune, ne veut pas même alors les restituer; gémissant d’une part sous la pesanteur du péché qui l’accable, & de l’autre refusant de se dépouiller; partagé entre l’enfer qu’il craint, & la cupidité qui le domine; mais du reste aimant mieux abandonner son ame que de réparer les injustices qu’il a commises, que de pourvoir au dédommagement de ceux qu’il a trompés, que de reconnoître des dettes dont sa mauvaise foi l’a toujours empêché de convenir, que de satisfaire à des obligations qu’il ne peut ignorer, & dont les remors secrets de sa conscience ne l’avertissent que trop; en un mot, que de relâcher la proie dont il est saisi, & que Dieu malgré lui va bien-tôt lui arracher. 2nd. There is another kind of death equally common, and equally lamentable, when by a criminal omission, though not directly voluntary, a person is precluded from the grace of repentance and debarred from the necessary means of obtaining it. For, says St. Augustin, reasoning with a sinner, if, when death draws near, and Almighty God calls you, you put not yourself as soon as possible in the best condition to appear before him; if, when so safe a harbour as that of a speedy and sincere repentance is open to receive you, you enter not into it and anchor not in safety; if you slip the precious moments, the favourable opportunities which Providence afforded you during the course of your illness; if by too great attention to the relief of your body you forget to make due provision for your soul, and reject instead of asking for the wholesome remedies which are charitably offered you; if you banish from your mind, through servile fear, the thoughts of death as much as possible, unwilling to lend an ear to pious admonitions, but willing to be flattered and deceived in the thing in which it was requisite in a special manner you should be dealt with sincerely—if you make no effort, through a natural infirmity, to surmount the frights arising therefrom, and to attend properly, at least at this crisis, in this last extremity, to the greatest and most important of all affairs; if you hearken to relations and false friends, who dissuade you from it; if you still let your thoughts, by a deplorable perversity, be taken up with your family, while you have scarce wherewithal to provide for eternity, ah! brother, concludes St. Augustin, change then your language and reform your notions. In this state of impenitence, to say that death is the greatest of misfortunes is to speak improperly; we must say that it is the greatest and most inexcusable of all crimes. To say that you die in your sinful state is to explain yourself by halves; you must say that you die in your sinful state by a final sin which surpasses all others. For, what are all the sins of life together if put in comparison with this alone? In what can one carry one’s injustice farther against God and one’s self? To see one’s self reduced to that fatal time, that time after which there is no more time, and yet procrastinate; to be at the gates of hell and yet take no pains to withdraw from them; to be on the point of perishing and yet hesitate to do one's self the most pressing duty, by taking wise measures to avoid perishing—can this be conceived? Can this be forgiven? Nevertheless, Christians, it is thus we are misled by the spirit of the world whenever we follow it. Men are surrounded, as the Scripture speaks, with the sorrows of death and the perils of hell, and yet they venture, they temporize, they keep a good heart, they put off till the morrow; they deceive themselves by fetches, evasions, and subterfuges; in a word, they die in the disfavour and aversion of God. A death doubly criminal—by the impenitence of the life which preceded it, and by the final impenitence which now attends it.

PART II. In order to die in the favour of heaven, it is not enough that the sinner be resolved, one day or other, to have recourse to penance, and that he purpose, at least at the hour of death, to relinquish sin. For whereas the grace of final repentance depends not absolutely on the will of man, and by a secret judgment of the supreme Ruler is connected with a thousand various circumstances which are out of his power, it is necessary, to the end he may enjoy the happiness of asking and obtaining the Almighty’s pardon, that, to effect his conversion, all these circumstances concur at his death. Should one only be wanting, his hopes are frustrated; and though ten thousand times he should beseech God that “ His soul might die the death of the righteous” (Numb. xxiii.), his desires are unprofitable and his hopes unavailing. The reason is this: in the ordinary course of divine Providence an obstacle arising, to all appearance, from natural causes, but yet of a divine and superior order, made the repentance impossible on which he relied, and which he regarded as his last resource. It may, therefore, so happen that without being guilty of a fresh sin, one may die in his sins, because one may die in an involuntary, nay unavoidable want of repentance. And this is what I call an unfortunate impenitence, and what I consider as another abyss, not of the corruption of the human heart, but of the adorable and impenetrable justice of God; a justice which appears in all its lustre at the death of these sinners, taken unawares, deluded, cast off, excluded even in this life from the way of salvation—sinners in whom this Gospel truth is most palpably fulfilled—“ You shall die in your sin.” Renew, Christians, renew your attention.
