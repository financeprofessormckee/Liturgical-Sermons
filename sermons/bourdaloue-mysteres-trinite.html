Introduction & Scripture references

Editorial introduction

Bourdaloue's sermon on the Most Holy Trinity applies belief in one God in three persons to Christian conduct. Its three parts present faith as homage to God, the Trinity as the foundation of hope, and the unity of the divine persons as the model and bond of charity among the faithful. It closes with a prayer for unity and peace.

French original

Voilà, Chrétiens, en trois paroles le sommaire de notre foi, le fondement de notre Religion, le caractère de notre profession, le plus auguste de nos mystères. Le Sauveur du monde en a fait une partie essentielle du premier de tous les Sacremens; il a voulu qu'il entrât presque dans la composition de tous les autres; la primitive Eglise s'en servoit comme d'un sceau public & universel, pour distinguer les Fidèles; & c'est pour nous conformer à ses sentimens, que nous le mettons à la tête de toutes nos actions, voulant qu'elles soient autant de témoignages du culte que nous rendons à l'adorable & très-sainte Trinité. Aussi est-ce cette foi, dit saint Augustin, que nous regardons comme le plus précieux trésor de l'Eglise; cette foi qui justifie les pécheurs, qui sanctifie les justes, qui baptise les Catéchumènes, qui couronne les Martyrs, qui consacre les Prêtres, qui sauve tout le monde. Cependant, mes chers Auditeurs, à quoi m'engage la fête & la solemnité de ce jour? Le Prophète Jérémie disoit à Dieu: Seigneur, je suis un enfant qui ne sait encore que bégayer, & qui ne sçait pas expliquer ses pensées; comment voulez-vous que je parle à votre peuple, & que je lui annonce votre loi? Mais, lui répondit le Dieu d'Israël, ne crains point, c'est moi qui t'envoie; & puisque je t'envoie, je te soutiendrai dans l'exercice de ton ministère: je te mettrai dans la bouche ce que tu auras à dire; & je serai en même-tems dans les cœurs de ceux qui t'écouteront, pour les disposer à te donner une attention favorable. Voilà, mes Freres, ce qui fait aujourd'hui toute ma confiance. J'ai à vous entretenir du plus profond & du plus impénétrable mystère: mais deux choses me rassûrent, l'ordre de Dieu, & votre disposition: l'ordre de Dieu, qui me commande de vous parler; & la disposition où vous êtes de recevoir avec une réflexion toute particuliere sa sainte parole. Implorons néanmoins, pour traiter ce grand sujet, le secours du ciel, par l'intercession de Marie. Ave, Maria.

English translation

Christians, here in three words is the summary of our faith, the foundation of our religion, the mark of our profession, the most august of our mysteries. The Saviour of the world made it an essential part of the first of all the sacraments; he willed that it should enter into the composition of almost all the others. The early Church used it as a public and universal seal to distinguish the faithful; and it is to conform ourselves to her sentiments that we place it at the beginning of all our actions, intending them to be so many testimonies of the worship we render to the adorable and most holy Trinity. It is therefore this faith, says Saint Augustine, that we regard as the Church's most precious treasure: this faith that justifies sinners, sanctifies the just, baptizes catechumens, crowns martyrs, consecrates priests, and saves everyone. Yet, my dear hearers, what obligation does this day's feast and solemnity lay upon me? The prophet Jeremiah said to God: Lord, I am a child who can only stammer, who does not know how to express his thoughts; how do you expect me to speak to your people and proclaim your law to them? But the God of Israel answered him: Do not fear; it is I who send you. Since I send you, I shall sustain you in the exercise of your ministry. I shall put into your mouth what you must say; and at the same time I shall be in the hearts of those who listen to you, to dispose them to give you a favourable hearing. This, my brothers, is the whole ground of my confidence today. I must speak to you of the deepest and most impenetrable mystery; but two things reassure me: God's command and your disposition. God's command requires me to speak to you, and your disposition leads you to receive his holy word with particular reflection. Nevertheless, to treat this great subject, let us implore heaven's help through Mary's intercession. Hail, Mary.

French original

Pour parler utilement, Chrétiens, du mystère de la très-sainte Trinité, & pour le rapporter, autant qu'il est possible, à l'édification de nos mœurs, voici trois propositions que j'avance d'abord, & qui seront le sujet & le partage de ce discours. Je dis que la profession que nous faisons dans le Christianisme, de croire en un seul Dieu une Trinité de personnes, est l'acte le plus glorieux à Dieu que notre foi soit capable de produire, première proposition. Je dis que c'est le fondement le plus essentiel & le plus solide de toute notre espérance, seconde proposition. Et enfin je dis que c'est le lien de la charité qui doit régner entre les hommes, mais particulièrement entre les fidèles, troisième proposition. La première vous montrera ce que nous faisons pour Dieu, en confessant le mystère de la Trinité; la seconde, ce que nous faisons pour nous-mêmes; & la troisième ce que nous devons faire les uns pour les autres. Croire un Dieu en trois personnes, c'est le plus grand hommage de foi que la créature puisse rendre à Dieu; ce sera la première partie. Croire un Dieu en trois personnes, c'est le plus grand sujet de confiance que la créature puisse avoir en son Dieu: ce sera la seconde. Croire un Dieu en trois personnes; c'est avoir devant les yeux le plus puissant motif & le plus excellent modèle de la charité, qui doit tous nous unir en Dieu & selon Dieu; ce sera la dernière. Tout ceci est moral, & mérite toute votre attention.

English translation

To speak profitably, Christians, of the mystery of the most holy Trinity, and to apply it as far as possible to the improvement of our conduct, here are three propositions I put forward at the outset, which will form the subject and divisions of this discourse. I say that the profession we make as Christians of believing a Trinity of persons in one God is the act most glorious to God that our faith can produce: the first proposition. I say that it is the most essential and solid foundation of all our hope: the second proposition. Finally, I say that it is the bond of the charity that must reign among people, and particularly among the faithful: the third proposition. The first will show you what we do for God in confessing the mystery of the Trinity; the second, what we do for ourselves; and the third, what we ought to do for one another. To believe in one God in three persons is the greatest homage of faith that a creature can render to God: this will be the first part. To believe in one God in three persons is the greatest ground for confidence that a creature can have in his God: this will be the second. To believe in one God in three persons is to have before our eyes the most powerful motive and most excellent model of the charity that must unite us all in God and according to God: this will be the last. All this concerns our conduct and deserves your full attention.

PART I.

¶p. 479; p. 480; p. 481; p. 482
French original

De tous les mystères de notre Religion, il n'y en a pas un, où Dieu soit plus incompréhensible à l'homme que le mystère de la Trinité; d'où je conclus qu'il n'y en a aucun dont la créance & la profession soit plus honorable & plus glorieuse à Dieu. Car il est certain que nous ne nous formons jamais d'idée plus haute, ni plus digne de la grandeur de Dieu, que quand nous avouons qu'il est incompréhensible; & la plus excellente protestation que je lui puisse faire, & que vous puissiez tous lui faire avec moi, c'est sans doute, celle-ci: Non, mon Dieu, je ne vous comprends pas, & je ne suis pas capable de vous comprendre. Quand j'épuiserois toutes les forces & toutes les puissances de mon ame, quand j'y employerois toutes celles des Anges, quand tous les dons de la grâce & de la gloire me seroient communiqués, quand je vous verrois aussi parfaitement que les bienheureux & que l'humanité de Jesus-Christ même; non, Seigneur, je ne vous comprendrois jamais, & ma connoissance sera toujours autant éloignée de vous, que le fini l'est de l'infini. Si je vous comprenois, mon Dieu, vous ne seriez plus ce que vous êtes, ou bien je ne serois plus ce que je suis; mais en ne vous comprenant pas, je reconnois que vous êtes mon Dieu, & que je suis votre créature: car comment pourrois-je mieux exprimer l'un & l'autre, & d'une manière plus avantageuse à votre divinité, qu'en disant que vous êtes ce que je ne puis comprendre, & ce qui ne peut jamais être compris? Bien plus, dit saint Augustin, (écoutez, Chrétiens, une belle remarque de ce Pere) à proprement parler, l'unique chose que nous pouvons connoître de Dieu & que nous pouvons lui attribuer, c'est cette qualité d'incompréhensible: Tunc verè aliquid de Deo cognoscimus, cùm ipsum comprehendere non possimus.1 Dans tout le reste nos esprits se perdent, dans tout le reste nous nous égarons souvent, sur tout le reste nous sommes en danger de tomber dans l'erreur. Quand nous disons: Dieu est puissant, Dieu est juste, Dieu est saint, Dieu est miséricordieux; dans la rigueur des termes, toutes ces propositions ne seroient pas convenables, si nous n'ajoutions ou si nous ne supposions l'incompréhensibilité de Dieu pour les modifier. Afin qu'elles soient exactement vraies, il faut dire ou du moins sous-entendre: Dieu est puissant, mais d'une puissance que je ne comprends pas; Dieu est juste, mais d'une justice toute autre que je ne la connois; Dieu est saint, mais d'une sainteté qui passe toutes les vûes de mon esprit. Il en faut donc toujours revenir à son incompréhensibilité, & se réduire au sentiment de saint Augustin, que là où Dieu nous paroît plus incompréhensible, c'est-là que nous le connoissons mieux, là que nous sommes plus en état de le glorifier, là que notre foi lui rend un témoignage plus parfait. Or je vous demande, dans quel mystère de la religion chrétienne Dieu est-il plus incompréhensible à l'homme? n'est-ce pas dans la Trinité? Que concevons-nous dans ce mystère, sinon que nous n'y concevons rien? Et c'est pourquoi les Prophètes, qui en ont eû les premières révélations, lui ont toujours donné ce caractère; nous le représentant, tantôt comme une lumière inaccessible, tantôt comme une obscurité impénétrable, tantôt comme un abysme sans fond, pour nous signifier que la Trinité des personnes divines est le grand mystère de l'incompréhensibilité de Dieu. D'où il s'ensuit que je ne puis plus exalter de ma part, ni plus relever le souverain être de Dieu, que par la créance de cette ineffable Trinité.

English translation

Of all the mysteries of our religion, there is none in which God is more incomprehensible to man than the mystery of the Trinity. From this I conclude that none is more honourable and glorious to God in its belief and profession. For it is certain that we never form an idea higher or more worthy of God's greatness than when we acknowledge that he is incomprehensible; and the most excellent declaration I can make to him, and that you can all make with me, is surely this: No, my God, I do not comprehend you, and I am incapable of comprehending you. Even if I exhausted all the strength and powers of my soul, employed all those of the angels, received every gift of grace and glory, and saw you as perfectly as the blessed and even as the humanity of Jesus Christ: no, Lord, I should never comprehend you, and my knowledge will always be as far removed from you as the finite is from the infinite. If I comprehended you, my God, you would no longer be what you are, or I should no longer be what I am. But in not comprehending you, I acknowledge that you are my God and that I am your creature. How could I express both truths better, or in a manner more favourable to your divinity, than by saying that you are what I cannot comprehend and what can never be comprehended? Moreover, says Saint Augustine—listen, Christians, to a fine observation of this Father—strictly speaking, the only thing we can know of God and attribute to him is this quality of being incomprehensible: Then we truly know something of God, when we cannot comprehend him.1 In everything else our minds lose themselves; in everything else we often go astray; in everything else we are in danger of falling into error. When we say, God is powerful, God is just, God is holy, God is merciful, strictly speaking all these propositions would be inappropriate unless we added or presupposed God's incomprehensibility to qualify them. To make them exactly true, we must say, or at least imply: God is powerful, but with a power I do not comprehend; God is just, but with a justice quite other than I know it to be; God is holy, but with a holiness that exceeds every conception of my mind. We must therefore always return to his incomprehensibility and confine ourselves to Saint Augustine's thought: where God appears more incomprehensible to us, there we know him better, there we are better able to glorify him, there our faith bears more perfect witness to him. Now I ask you: in what mystery of the Christian religion is God more incomprehensible to man? Is it not in the Trinity? What do we conceive in this mystery except that we conceive nothing in it? This is why the prophets, who received its first revelations, always gave it this character, representing it to us now as an inaccessible light, now as an impenetrable darkness, now as a bottomless abyss, to signify that the Trinity of the divine persons is the great mystery of God's incomprehensibility. It follows that I can do nothing more to exalt and magnify God's sovereign being than believe in this ineffable Trinity.

French original

N'en demeurons pas là. Que fais-je, Chrétiens, quand je crois un Dieu en trois personnes? je lui fais un sacrifice, & de quoi? de la plus noble partie de moi-même, qui est ma raison; & comment le fais-je? de la manière la plus excellente & la plus héroïque, & en quoi consiste-t-il? le voici. Je crois un mystère dont je n'ai nulle expérience, & dont il m'est impossible d'avoir la moindre idée, avant que Dieu me l'ait révélé; & quand Dieu me l'a révélé, je le crois de telle sorte, que ma raison ne peut s'en faire juge, ni l'examiner; enfin, ce qui fait la perfection de mon sacrifice, je crois ce mystère, quoiqu'il semble répugner positivement à ma raison. N'est-ce pas là tout l'effort que la raison humaine peut faire pour Dieu? ne sont-ce pas tous les droits auxquels elle peut renoncer? & n'est-ce pas sur-tout dans ce mystère qu'elle y renonce pleinement, & qu'elle se sacrifie toute entiere? Car il n'en est pas de même des autres: je connois mille choses de Dieu, indépendamment des révélations de Dieu. Quand Dieu ne m'auroit jamais parlé, je sçai qu'il est sage, je sçai qu'il a une providence, je sçai que le monde est gouverné par lui: toutes les créatures me le disent; je n'ai qu'à ouvrir les yeux, j'en ai des preuves sensibles. Et en cela la foi ne marche point devant la raison, mais elle la suit; elle ne lui apprend rien de nouveau, quoiqu'elle le lui apprenne mieux: elle augmente ses lumières & les perfectionne; mais elle les suppose en les perfectionnant: je crois ce que je sçavois déja en partie. Mais qu'en Dieu il y ait trois différentes personnes; que la première s'appelle Pere, la seconde Verbe, & la troisième Saint-Esprit; que le Fils soit engendré par la connoissance féconde que Dieu a de soi-même, & que le Saint-Esprit procède du Pere & du Fils par voie d'amour; ce sont des secrets dont je ne découvre aucun vestige dans l'univers, & dont tous les hommes n'auroient pû même se former de conjectures, si Dieu ne les en avoit instruits. On dit qu'un Philosophe païen en a eû autrefois quelque connoissance: mais si cela est, saint Augustin répond qu'elle lui étoit venue du commerce avec les Juifs. C'est donc à la foi seule que je suis obligé de m'en rapporter touchant ce mystère. Mais quand ce mystère m'est révélé de Dieu par la foi, puis-je raisonner, puis-je discourir, puis-je occuper mon esprit à le connoître & à en chercher les principes? Non, Chrétiens, cela n'est point du ressort de ma raison. Dans le mystère de l'incarnation je le puis faire: supposé la foi que le Verbe se soit fait chair, mon esprit y trouve je ne sçai combien de convenances admirables. Je dis qu'il n'y avoit qu'un Dieu qui pût satisfaire à Dieu pour le péché; or ce Dieu ne pouvoit satisfaire, sans se faire homme: ainsi je raisonne alors sur la foi. Quoique la foi précède mon raisonnement, mon raisonnement ne laisse pas de venir ensuite au secours de la foi. Mais quand il s'agit de l'auguste mystère de la Trinité, d'une essence indivisible en plusieurs personnes, du Pere qui n'est pas plus que le Fils, du Fils qui n'a nulle dépendance de son Pere, du Saint-Esprit qui est l'amour substantiel de l'un & de l'autre; c'est là que notre raison demeure, qu'elle s'humilie, qu'elle se couvre de ses ailes, comme ces Anges que vit le Prophète; qu'elle s'interdit tout examen, toute réflexion, toute curiosité. Tout ce qu'elle fait, c'est de reconnoître son ignorance; & cet aveu, dans la pensée d'un Pere, est la seule confession véritable de la Trinité.

English translation

Let us not stop there. What do I do, Christians, when I believe in one God in three persons? I offer him a sacrifice. Of what? Of the noblest part of myself, my reason. And how do I make it? In the most excellent and heroic manner. What does it consist in? Here it is. I believe a mystery of which I have no experience, and of which I cannot have the slightest idea before God reveals it to me. Once God has revealed it, I believe it in such a way that my reason cannot make itself its judge or examine it. Finally, what perfects my sacrifice is that I believe this mystery even though it seems positively repugnant to my reason. Is this not the utmost effort human reason can make for God? Are these not all the rights it can renounce? And is it not above all in this mystery that it renounces them fully and sacrifices itself entirely? The other mysteries are different. I know a thousand things about God independently of God's revelations. Even if God had never spoken to me, I know he is wise, I know he exercises providence, I know the world is governed by him. All creatures tell me so; I need only open my eyes to have sensible proofs of it. In this, faith does not walk ahead of reason but follows it. It teaches it nothing new, although it teaches it better; it increases and perfects its light, but presupposes that light in perfecting it. I believe what I already knew in part. But that in God there are three distinct persons; that the first is called Father, the second Word, the third Holy Spirit; that the Son is begotten through God's fruitful knowledge of himself, and that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son by way of love: these are secrets of which I discover no trace in the universe, and about which all mankind could not even have formed conjectures had God not taught them. A pagan philosopher is said to have had some knowledge of them long ago; but if that is so, Saint Augustine replies, it came to him through contact with the Jews. In this mystery, then, I must rely on faith alone. But when God reveals this mystery to me through faith, may I reason about it, discuss it, apply my mind to knowing it and searching for its principles? No, Christians; this is outside my reason's province. I can do so in the mystery of the Incarnation: once faith that the Word became flesh is presupposed, my mind finds countless admirable harmonies in it. I say that only one who is God could make satisfaction to God for sin; yet this God could not make satisfaction without becoming man. Thus I then reason upon faith. Though faith precedes my reasoning, my reasoning nonetheless comes afterwards to faith's aid. But when it concerns the august mystery of the Trinity, an indivisible essence in several persons, the Father who is no greater than the Son, the Son who has no dependence on his Father, the Holy Spirit who is the substantial love of both: there our reason stops, humbles itself, covers itself with its wings like those angels the prophet saw, and forbids itself all examination, all reflection, all curiosity. All it does is acknowledge its ignorance; and this acknowledgement, in the thought of one of the Fathers, is the only true confession of the Trinity.

Ce qui met le comble au sacrifice que je fais à Dieu, en croyant la Trinité, c'est que je me soumets à croire un mystère qui paroît choquer la raison même, & contredire toutes ses lumières. Car il faut que je croie que trois personnes divines, celle du Pere, celle du Fils, & celle du Saint-Esprit, n'étant qu'une même chose avec l'essence de Dieu, je dis une même chose indivisible, sans composition, sans partie, sont néanmoins distinguées entre elles. Je crois, mon Dieu, votre unité & votre trinité tout ensemble, & je crois l'un & l'autre dans la même disposition de cœur que s'il falloit mourir. En effet, Chrétiens, nous nous flattons, en formant ces résolutions imaginaires, de confesser notre foi à quelque prix que ce fût; & en disant comme nous disons quelquefois, je souffrirois plutôt mille morts, que de la trahir, cette foi. Car nous la trahissons à toute heure; & ce qui est plus déplorable, nous la trahissons pour un vil intérêt, pour un moment de plaisir, pour contenter un désir, une passion honteuse; & tout ce grand zèle n'est qu'en spéculation & en idée, n'est que sous des conditions chimériques, n'est que pour des occasions & des conjonctures où nous ne nous trouverons jamais; rien de réel, ni rien de présent.

English translation

What brings to its height the sacrifice I make to God in believing the Trinity is that I submit to believing a mystery that seems to offend reason itself and contradict all its light. For I must believe that three divine persons—the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit—though they are one and the same thing with God's essence, one and the same indivisible thing, I say, without composition or parts, are nevertheless distinct from one another. Here, if I dare speak thus, is man's stumbling block. Here is the most apparent contradiction to be found in all our mysteries. Yet it is from this very thing that our faith draws its perfection, when we say to God: Yes, Lord, I believe everything you have revealed to me of this incomprehensible mystery. My reason seems at first to oppose it; but I disavow it, I renounce it, I sacrifice it to you at the foot of your altars. My God, I believe your unity and your trinity together, and I believe both with the same disposition of heart as if I had to die for them. By virtue of this faith, which I here profess, I would give my life and shed my blood to defend it. And as you are three in heaven whose testimony I receive today—the Father, the Word and the Holy Spirit—so, Lord, I would wish to be able to render you on earth the three testimonies of which the beloved disciple speaks: the testimony of the spirit, the testimony of the water, and the testimony of the blood. This is what we say, Christians; but do you know what God answers us? It is important that I make you hear it. No, no, he tells us, it is no longer a question of dying or losing your life. Formerly I wanted martyrs to establish my religion; but now things have changed. You must prove your faith in peace rather than persecution; you must show what you are through the practices of a common and ordinary life rather than on scaffolds or torture wheels. You must confess me among your relatives and friends rather than before judges and tyrants. I ask you for the testimony of the spirit rather than of blood. Do not think, then, of what you would do if there were still persecutors in the world. There are no longer any; you are free to declare yourselves. Toutes choses, Chrétiens, nous prêchent la charité que nous nous devons les uns aux autres; mais rien ne nous la prêche plus hautement que la Trinité des personnes Divines. Vous me demandez pourquoi? pour deux raisons qui nous sont marquées dans l'Ecriture, & qui toutes deux portent un certain caractère de l'Esprit de Dieu. La première, parce que la foi de la Trinité est le motif & comme le lien substantiel de la charité, qui doit être entre nous; & la seconde, parce que le mystère de la Trinité en est encore le grand modèle que Jesus-Christ nous a donné dans son Evangile. English translation

Everything, Christians, preaches to us the charity we owe one another; but nothing preaches it more loudly than the Trinity of the divine persons. You ask me why? For two reasons indicated to us in Scripture, both bearing a certain mark of God's Spirit. The first is that faith in the Trinity is the motive and, as it were, the substantial bond of the charity that should exist among us; the second, that the mystery of the Trinity is also the great model of it that Jesus Christ gave us in his Gospel. Two reasons, my dear hearers, worthy of all your reflection and supremely capable of stirring you to practise this virtue.

English translation

I say that belief in the Trinity must be the bond of our mutual charity; Saint Paul teaches us this. For, he says, faith in this mystery unites us all in one body of religion. Christians, listen to this Doctor of the Nations speak for himself. Ah, my brothers, he said to the Ephesians, I entreat you, I who am a prisoner for Jesus Christ: I, a prisoner in the Lord, entreat you.10 To do what? To love one another, to bear with one another: Bearing with one another in charity.11 Be zealous to preserve among yourselves that unity of spirit which is the principle of true peace: Taking care to preserve the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.12 And what motive did he give them? On what did he ground this obligation? Here it is. After all, my brothers, the Apostle adds, you all have only one God, you all have only one faith, you all have only one baptism; you all form only one body, the Church. Is it not therefore just that you should all have the same spirit? One body and one Spirit, one Lord, one faith, one baptism.13 That is to say: what an indignity that, uniting as we do to honour the same God, we should not be united in everything else! In this same God, in this same Lord, we acknowledge a Father whose children we all are; a Son whose brothers we all are; a Holy Spirit by whom we are all animated: One Lord. What a monstrous thing, then, that being all children of the same Father we live together as strangers; that being all brothers of the same Son of God there should be no sign of brotherhood among us; that wishing all to have the same Holy Spirit we should display such opposed sentiments! But what amazes me, Saint Paul continued, according to Saint Chrysostom's paraphrase explaining this passage, is that, although we have all been able to agree on a point as difficult as faith in these three adorable persons, Father, Son and Holy Spirit, we quarrel every day over trifles that become the grounds of our enmities. If there were anything on which we should have difficulty agreeing, anything about which one might fear that minds would be divided, it was belief in one God in three persons. Yet we believe it; we all make the same profession of it; we renounce all the doubts and difficulties our minds might raise; and we do so, we say, to avoid disturbing the unity of faith: One faith? Ah, Christians, is it not strange, then, that we break the unity of charity over matters of no consequence, and cherish animosities and hatreds that utterly destroy one of Christianity's fundamental virtues?

English translation

Such was the Apostle Saint Paul's reasoning to convince the Ephesians: One Lord, one faith. He also presses this reasoning so strongly in another of his epistles, where, addressing the Christians of Corinth, he says: What is this I hear, my brothers? I am told that there are intrigues among you, schisms and factions: one takes Paul's side, another Apollo's, another Peter's. But what? Was it in Peter's name, in Apollo's name, in Paul's name that you were baptized? Were you baptized in Paul's name?14 I thank God that I baptized no one among you, lest it be said that you were baptized in my name: I give thanks to God that I baptized none of you, lest anyone say that you were baptized in my name.15 It was in the name of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit that you received baptism, all in the same form, all with the same character, all through the efficacy and power of the same Trinity. Since this is so, you all have an indispensable obligation to live in the same spirit; and you forget what you are when you allow discord to arise among you. Do you notice, Christians, how Saint Paul grounded the duty of charity on faith in the Trinity? One faith, one baptism. Indeed, if any motive ought to bind us to love one another as brothers, it is this unity of belief and faith. Just as difference of religion has always been, so to speak, a sword of division among men, even breaking entirely the most inviolable bonds of nature, so unity of religion has always been regarded as friendship's most sacred bond. Even our heretics think this way. Once they form a sect and constitute a pretended Church, they begin helping one another. You witness it, my dear hearers, and you know how they are united, how they take up one another's interests, help one another in their needs, assist their poor and visit their sick. What brings this about? It is not unity of faith, since outside the Church they cannot have faith. What, then? Unity of error, unity of falsehood, unity of schism. The little flock in which they are all gathered is what binds them, stops all their quarrels and settles all their differences; this is why they call one another brothers and act as brothers. What a shame that the unity of faith in which we live does less for us than the unity of a false reform does for them! Yet so it is: they unite and we divide; they perform brotherly services for one another, and we often treat one another as enemies. They see it, are astonished and scandalized by it; they even reproach us for it. Who but ourselves can put an end to this reproach? It will cease as soon as charity enters our hearts. All these hatreds, envies, desires for revenge, all our contempt for our neighbour, all the bitter and stinging words that escape us: all this would soon vanish if we had true charity. Faith in one God in three persons should be its motive; and I add that it also presents us with its most perfect model.

When I told you, my brothers, in other discourses, that the Son of God had obliged us to love one another as he loved us—A new commandment I give you, that you love one another as I have loved you16—you did not believe charity could be raised any higher. This love of a God sacrificed for men's salvation seemed to you the highest point to which love of our neighbour could rise. Yet here is something still greater: we must love one another as the three persons of the Trinity love one another; as the Father loves the Son, as the Son loves the Father, as the Father and Son love one another in the Holy Spirit. Then, full of consolation, I would have reason to congratulate you and would cry out with the prophet: How good and how pleasant it is for brothers to dwell together!21 What happiness for these Christians, what happiness for these households, to live in a harmony that maintains tranquillity and makes piety flourish among them.

But what do we see? The exact opposite, which we cannot deplore enough. No unity in Christianity, even among those whom nature's most inviolable and sacred laws should bind closely together. I mean, no unity among whom? Often among relatives; often among brothers and sisters; often among fathers and children. It is for you, God of charity and peace, it is for you to maintain both among us, or rather to establish them here, for they are only too impaired. Almighty Father, you formed our hearts, and you always have the power to turn them as you please. Son, equal to your Father and eternal like him, yet made flesh for us, you have gathered us under one law, a law of love. Holy Spirit, you are the substantial love of the Father and the Son, and through you charity is poured into our souls. Trinity, supremely adorable and lovable, it is from your bosom that we have all come forth, and into your bosom that you will to call us all back. Unite us on earth as we are to be united in blessed eternity, to which may he lead us, &c.
