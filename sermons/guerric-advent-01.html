Editorial introduction

This first Advent sermon belongs to Guerric of Igny's medieval collection of sermons for the liturgical year, here read from the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. Its individual date is unknown; the collection is associated approximately with his abbacy in 1138–1157. Guerric moves from the fulfilment of God's promises in Christ's first coming to patient hope for his return. The final prayer develops an image of remaining with Christ on the Cross. The source names Advent without assigning a particular Sunday.

¶cols. 11–12
Latin original

1. Salvatorem exspectamus (Tit. ii, 13). Vere exspectatio justorum laetitia, exspectantium beatam spem, et adventum gloriae magni Dei et salvatoris nostri Jesu Christi. Et nunc quae est exspectatio mea, inquit justus, nonne Dominus? (Psal. xxxviii, 8.) Et conversus ad Dominum: Scio, inquit, quia non confundes me ab exspectatione mea (Psal. cxviii, 116); quia jam substantia mea apud te est (Psal. xxxviii, 8), quia natura nostra de nobis sumpta, et pro nobis oblata, apud te glorificata est: spem nobis tribuens, quia ad te omnis caro veniet (Psal. lxiv, 3); caputque membra sequentur, ut holocaustum compleatur. Pleniore tamen fiducia, quia tutiore conscientia, Dominum exspectare potest, cui dicere datum est: Substantia possessiunculae meae, o Domine, apud te est; quia facultates meas, vel tibi erogando, vel pro te contemnendo, in coelis thesaurizavi, et ad pedes tuos omnia mea deposui, sciens quia potens es depositum meum non modo servare, sed etiam in centuplum restituere, et vitam aeternam adjicere. Quam beati vos, pauperes spiritu, quia juxta consilium admirabilis Consiliarii thesaurizastis vobis thesauros in coelo; ne si1 in terra thesauri vestri remanerent, corda vestra pariter putrescerent! Ubi enim, inquit, thesaurus tuus, ibi et cor tuum (Matth. vi, 21). Eant igitur, eant corda post thesauros suos; sursum affixa sit cogitatio, et ad Dominum suspensa exspectatio, ut et vobis competat dicere cum Apostolo: Nostra autem conversatio in coelis est, unde etiam Salvatorem exspectamus (Philipp. iii, 20). O exspectatio gentium! universi qui te exspectant non confundentur. Te expectaverunt patres nostri, omnes justi ab origine mundi in te speraverunt, et non sunt confusi (Psal. xxi, 5, 6). Jam enim suscepta misericordia tua in medio templi tui, chorus laetantium et laudantium concinit: Benedictus qui venit in nomine Domini (Matth. xxi, 9). Exspectans exspectavi Dominum, et intendit mihi (Psal. xxxix, 2). Et in humilitate carnis agnoscentes majestatem divinitatis: Ecce, inquiunt, Dominus noster iste; exspectavimus eum, et salvabit nos: iste Dominus, sustinuimus eum; exsultabimus et laetabimur in salutari ejus (Isa. xxv, 9).

English translation

1. We await the Saviour (Tit. ii, 13). Truly, the waiting of the righteous is joy, as they await the blessed hope and the coming of the glory of our great God and Saviour Jesus Christ. ‘And now, what am I waiting for,’ says the righteous person, ‘if not the Lord?’ (Psal. xxxviii, 8.) Turning to the Lord, he says: ‘I know that you will not disappoint my hope’ (Psal. cxviii, 116), ‘because my very being is already with you’ (Psal. xxxviii, 8): for our nature, taken from us and offered for us, has been glorified with you. This gives us hope that all flesh will come to you (Psal. lxiv, 3), and that the members will follow the Head, so that the whole burnt offering may be complete. Yet the person who has been granted these words can await the Lord with fuller confidence, because his conscience is more secure: ‘O Lord, the substance of my little property is with you. By spending my resources for you, or giving them up for your sake, I have stored them in heaven; I have laid all I possess at your feet, knowing that you can not only keep what I have entrusted to you, but return it a hundredfold and add eternal life.’ How blessed you are, poor in spirit! Following the advice of the wonderful Counsellor, you have stored up treasures for yourselves in heaven, lest your hearts decay1 along with treasures left on earth. ‘For where your treasure is,’ he says, ‘there is your heart also’ (Matth. vi, 21). Let our hearts go, then, let them follow their treasures. Let our thoughts be fixed above and our expectation hang upon the Lord, so that you too may say with the Apostle: ‘Our citizenship is in heaven, from which we also await the Saviour’ (Philipp. iii, 20). O expectation of the nations! All who await you will not be put to shame. Our fathers awaited you; all the righteous from the beginning of the world hoped in you and were not put to shame (Psal. xxi, 5, 6). For now that your mercy has been received in the midst of your temple, the choir of those rejoicing and praising sings together: ‘Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord’ (Matth. xxi, 9). ‘I waited and waited for the Lord, and he attended to me’ (Psal. xxxix, 2). And, recognizing the majesty of divinity in the lowliness of flesh, they say: ‘Look, this is our Lord; we have waited for him, and he will save us. This is the Lord; we have waited for him. We shall rejoice and be glad in his salvation’ (Isa. xxv, 9).

¶col. 12
Latin original

2. Sicut autem Ecclesia in antiquis justis exspectavit adventum primum; sic in novis reexspectat secundum: sicut in primo certa de pretio redemptionis; sic in secundo, de praemio remunerationis: atque hujus exspectatione spei suspensa a terrenis, tam feliciter quam ardenter inhiat aeternis. Cum igitur alii festinantes praesentibus beari, nec sustinentes consilium Domini, currunt ad diripiendam praedam hujus mundi; beatus vir cujus est nomen Domini spes ejus, et non respexit in vanitates et insanias falsas (Psal. xxxix, 5). Qui abstinet se a vitiis eorum, tanquam ab immunditiis, sciens melius esse humiliari cum mitibus, quam dividere spolia cum superbis (Prov. xvi, 19). Loquensque secum consolatur se, et ait: Pars mea Dominus, dixit anima mea: propterea exspectabo eum. Bonus est Dominus sperantibus in eum, animae quaerenti illum. Bonum est praestolari cum silentio salutare Dei (Thren. iii, 24–26). Defecit quidem anima mea in salutare tuum, o Domine, sed in verbum tuum supersperavi (Psal. cxviii, 81). Spes quidem quae differtur, scriptum est, affligit animam (Prov. xiii, 12): sed, licet lassa sit ex desiderii dilatione, secura est tamen ex promissione. In ipsum solum sperans, sed et supersperans adjiciam spem: sicut assidue adjicitur tribulatio super tribulationem, dilatio super dilationem. Certus sum enim quia apparebit in finem, et non mentietur. Ideoque, et si moram fecerit, exspectabo eum, quia veniens veniet, et non tardabit (Habac. ii, 3), ultra praestitutum tempus vel opportunum. Quod opportunum? Cum impletus fuerit numerus fratrum nostrorum, et consummatum fuerit tempus misericordiae ad poenitentiam indultum. Audi Isaiam ad superna saepius admissum consilia, quo videlicet consilio Dominus judicium differat. Propterea, inquit, Dominus exspectat ut misereatur vestri; et ideo exaltabitur parcens vobis, quia Deus judicii Dominus. Beati omnes qui exspectant eum (Isai. xxx, 18).

English translation

2. Just as the Church awaited the first coming in the righteous of old, so she awaits the second in the righteous of today: as sure of the price of redemption at the first as of the reward to be bestowed at the second. Lifted away from earthly things by this hopeful expectation, she longs for eternal things as happily as she does eagerly. While others hurry to find happiness in present things and, refusing to wait for the Lord's counsel, rush to seize this world's spoils, blessed is the man whose hope is the name of the Lord, who has not turned to vanities and lying follies (Psal. xxxix, 5). He keeps away from their vices as from filth, knowing that it is better to be humbled with the meek than to divide spoils with the proud (Prov. xvi, 19). Speaking within himself, he takes comfort and says: ‘The Lord is my portion, says my soul; therefore I shall wait for him. The Lord is good to those who hope in him, to the soul that seeks him. It is good to wait in silence for God's salvation’ (Thren. iii, 24–26). ‘My soul has indeed grown faint for your salvation, O Lord, but I have hoped all the more in your word’ (Psal. cxviii, 81). It is written that hope deferred afflicts the soul (Prov. xiii, 12); yet, though wearied by the postponement of its desire, it remains secure because of the promise. Hoping in him alone, and hoping beyond hope, I shall add hope, just as tribulation is continually added to tribulation and delay to delay. For I am certain that he will appear at the end and will not lie. Therefore, even if he delays, I shall wait for him, because he who is coming will come and will not be late (Habac. ii, 3), beyond the appointed or fitting time. What is the fitting time? When the number of our brethren has been completed, and the time mercifully granted for repentance has run its course. Listen to Isaiah, so often admitted to heavenly counsels, explaining why the Lord postpones judgment: ‘Therefore the Lord waits to have mercy on you; therefore he will be exalted in sparing you, because the Lord is a God of judgment. Blessed are all who await him’ (Isai. xxx, 18).

¶col. 12; col. 13
Latin original

4.1 Propterea, si sapis, prospicies tibi quomodo dilationis hujus induciis utaris: utique si peccator es, ad poenitentiam, non ad negligentiam; si sanctus, ut proficias in sanctitate, non ut deficias a fide. Si autem dixerit malus servus ille in corde suo: Moram facit Dominus meus venire; et coeperit percutere conservos suos, manducet autem et bibat cum ebriosis: veniet Dominus servi illius in die qua non sperat, et hora qua ignorat, et dividet eum, partemque ejus ponet cum hypocritis: illic erit fletus et stridor dentium (Matth. xxiv, 48–51). Hujus servi nequam et infidelis videtur esse vox illa taedii et desperationis, quae apud Isaiam legitur: quia videlicet moram faciente Domino, frequentibus nuntiis ejus increduli et illusores insultare videntur: Manda, remanda, manda, remanda; exspecta, reexspecta; modicum ibi, modicum ibi. Sed quae poena sequatur miseros, propheta non tacuit, cum post pauca subintulit: Et erit eis, inquit, verbum Domini, quod videlicet subsannant, dicentes prophetis: Manda, remanda; manda, remanda; exspecta, reexspecta; modicum ibi, modicum ibi: ut vadant et cadant retrorsum, et conterantur, et illaqueentur, et capiantur (Isai. xxviii, 13). Vadunt retrorsum per apostasiam, cadunt in criminalia, illaqueantur mortifera delectatione, vel quadam inextricabili peccandi necessitate, ut nec velint, nec possint poenitere: capiuntur improvisa morte, conteruntur aeterna damnatione. Nescit enim finem suum homo: sed sicut pisces capiuntur hamo, et aves laqueo; sic homines, inquit Salomon, capiuntur tempore malo, cum eis extemplo supervenerit (Eccle. ix, 12). Propterea videlicet, ne pro dilatione spei fides nostra tepescat, aut aestuet patientia, et incipiamus esse de illis qui ad tempus credunt, et in tempore tentationis recedunt (Luc. viii, 13): clamat nobis de coelo qui fidem donat, donatam probat, probatam coronat, inquiens: Quis crediderit, non festinet (Isai. xxviii, 16), scilicet videre quod credit. Si enim quod non videmus, speramus: per patientiam exspectamus. Sponsae quoque suae, quam sibi desponsavit in fide, mandat Deus per Osee, dicens: Dies multos exspectabis me: non fornicaberis, et non eris viro (Osee iii, 3). Hoc nempe est Dominum veraciter exspectare, si fidem ei servemus: ut licet careamus consolatione praesentiae ejus, adulterum tamen non sequamur; sed pendeamus ad reditum ejus. Sic namque Dominus ait in eodem propheta: Pendebit populus meus ad reditum meum (Id. xi, 7). Pulchre omnino et proprie, pendebit, tanquam inter coelum et terram: licet adhuc apprehendere coelestia non valeat, non velit tamen tangere terrena. Et si aliquando tangit, nonnisi summis articulis, id est, extremis partibus animae; propter necessitatem scilicet corruptionis nostrae, cui cogimur servire, quamdiu creatura vanitati subjecta est, non volens (Rom. viii, 12)2. Vulgo dicitur: Male exspectat, qui pendet. Ego autem dico: Feliciter exspectat, qui sic pendet. Quamobrem elegit suspendium anima mea3, et in ipso suspendio mortem ossa mea: ut perseveranter in cruce ista pendere merear, donec immoriar.

English translation

4. Therefore1, if you are wise, consider how to use the respite of this delay: if you are a sinner, for repentance rather than negligence; if you are holy, to advance in holiness rather than fall away from faith. But if that wicked servant says in his heart, ‘My Lord is slow in coming,’ and begins to beat his fellow servants and eat and drink with drunkards, that servant's Lord will come on a day he does not expect and at an hour he does not know, and will cut him apart and assign him a place with the hypocrites: there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth (Matth. xxiv, 48–51). The voice of weariness and despair that we read in Isaiah seems to be that of this wicked and faithless servant. Because the Lord delays, unbelievers and scoffers seem to jeer at his frequent messengers: ‘Command, command again, command, command again; wait, wait again; a little there, a little there.’ But the prophet did not keep silent about the punishment that would follow these wretched people, for a little later he added: ‘And the word of the Lord will be to them’—the word they mock when they say to the prophets, ‘Command, command again; command, command again; wait, wait again; a little there, a little there’—‘so that they may go and fall backward, and be broken, and ensnared, and captured’ (Isai. xxviii, 13). They go backward through apostasy, fall into grave sins, and are ensnared by deadly pleasure or by a kind of inescapable compulsion to sin, so that they neither wish nor are able to repent. They are captured by sudden death and broken by eternal condemnation. For man does not know his end: ‘As fish are caught by a hook and birds by a snare, so men,’ says Solomon, ‘are caught in an evil time when it suddenly falls upon them’ (Eccle. ix, 12). Therefore, lest the delay of hope make our faith grow lukewarm or our patience become agitated, and we begin to be among those ‘who believe for a time and fall away in the time of temptation’ (Luc. viii, 13), he who gives faith, tests the faith he has given, and crowns the faith he has tested, cries to us from heaven: ‘Let the one who believes not hurry’ (Isai. xxviii, 16)—that is, to see what he believes. For if we hope for what we do not see, we await it patiently. God also commands his bride, whom he has betrothed to himself in faith, through Hosea: ‘You will wait for me many days; you will not play the harlot or belong to a man’ (Osee iii, 3). This is truly waiting for the Lord: keeping faith with him, so that although we lack the comfort of his presence we do not follow an adulterer, but hang upon his return. For the Lord says in the same prophet: ‘My people will hang upon my return’ (Id. xi, 7). How beautifully and aptly he says ‘hang,’ as though between heaven and earth: though they cannot yet grasp heavenly things, they are unwilling to touch earthly things. And if they sometimes touch them, it is only with their fingertips, that is, the outermost parts of the soul, because of the necessities of our corruptible condition, which we are compelled to serve as long as creation is subjected to vanity against its will (Rom. viii, 12)2. People commonly say, ‘He who hangs waits badly.’ But I say, ‘He who hangs in this way waits happily.’ Therefore my soul has chosen hanging3, and my bones death in that hanging, so that I may be found worthy to hang perseveringly upon this cross until I die upon it.

¶cols. 13–14
Latin original

4. O Domine Jesu, cum animam potestate positurus esses, et quo genere mortis id facere velles, tuo subesset arbitrio; suspendium elegit anima tua, ut exaltatus a terra, nos quoque ad te traheres, et a terrenis suspenderes. Sed nec ante mortem deponi passus es, et nos usque ad mortem in cruce perseveremus, et de ipsa tanquam de sublimi gradu facilior sit in coelum conscensus. Gratias tibi, Domine Jesu! ibi sumus, ibi te exspectamus, non Eliam, qui ad deponendum nos veniat; sed Eli, id est, Dominum nostrum, qui nos suscipiat. Modicum ibi, modicum ibi, si mandas atque remandas. Ego quidem mandatis tuis semel credidi; sed adjuva incredulitatem meam, ut immobilis ibi te exspectem, reexspectem, donec quod credo videam: credo enim videre bona Domini in terra viventium. Credis et tu: igitur exspecta Dominum, viriliter age, confortetur cor tuum, et sustine Dominum! (Psal. xxvi, 13, 14). Vae his qui perdiderunt sustinentiam, et diverterunt in vias pravas. Et quid facient, cum inspicere coeperit Dominus? Qui tamen longanimiter se mandat exspectari, alibi se cito venturum esse promittit: in altero nimirum sustinentiam informans; in altero pusillanimes confortans, imparatos terrens, et pigros excitans. Ecce, inquit, venio cito, et merces mea mecum est, reddere unicuique secundum opera sua (Apoc. xxii, 12): et ad Jerusalem: Cito veniet salus tua: quare moerore consumeris? (Mich. iv, 9)4. Et revera tempus breve est, maxime uniuscujusque nostrum; licet longum videatur aestuandi, seu prae labore, seu prae amore. Ideoque utrumque necesse est: ut timeamus Judicem qui prope est, et forsitan mihi, forsitan tibi in januis est; et longanimiter exspectemus, si moram fecerit. Veniens veniet ille Dominus, timor et desiderium nostrum, requies et merces laborantium, dulcedo et amplexus amantium, beatitudo omnium, salvator noster Jesus Christus, qui vivit et regnat per omnia saecula saeculorum.

English translation

4. O Lord Jesus, when you were about to lay down your life by your own power, and the kind of death you would choose lay within your own decision, your soul chose hanging, so that, lifted up from the earth, you might draw us too to yourself and suspend us above earthly things. You did not allow yourself to be taken down before death: may we too persevere upon the cross until death, and may ascent into heaven be easier from it, as from a lofty step. Thank you, Lord Jesus! There we are; there we await you—not Elijah, coming to take us down, but Eli, that is, our Lord, to receive us. ‘A little there, a little there,’ if you command and command again. I have indeed believed your commands once for all; but help my unbelief, so that I may remain there unmoved, waiting and waiting again for you, until I see what I believe: for I believe that I shall see the Lord's goodness in the land of the living. You believe too: then wait for the Lord; act courageously, let your heart be strengthened, and wait for the Lord! (Psal. xxvi, 13, 14). Woe to those who have lost their endurance and turned aside into crooked ways. What will they do when the Lord begins his inspection? Yet he who commands us to await him with patience promises elsewhere that he will come quickly: in the one case teaching endurance, in the other comforting the fainthearted, terrifying the unprepared, and rousing the sluggish. ‘Look,’ he says, ‘I am coming quickly, and my reward is with me, to repay each according to his works’ (Apoc. xxii, 12); and to Jerusalem: ‘Your salvation will come quickly; why are you consumed by grief?’ (Mich. iv, 9)4. Truly the time is short, especially for each one of us, although it may seem long in our anguish, whether from toil or from love. Therefore both are necessary: to fear the Judge who is near, perhaps at my door, perhaps at yours, and to wait patiently if he delays. He who is coming will come: the Lord, our fear and our desire, the rest and reward of those who labour, the sweetness and embrace of those who love, the blessedness of all, our Saviour Jesus Christ, who lives and reigns for ever and ever.
