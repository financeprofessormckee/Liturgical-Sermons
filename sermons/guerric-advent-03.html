Editorial introduction

This third Advent sermon belongs to Guerric of Igny's medieval collection of sermons for the liturgical year, here read from the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. Its individual date is unknown; the collection is associated approximately with his abbacy in 1138–1157. Guerric begins from the uncertainty of the hour of Christ's coming and of one's own death: the only security is never to be secure. David is the model of a soul kept ready through persecution, and justice and judgment prepare a dwelling for the Lord. The source names Advent without assigning a particular Sunday.

SERMON III.

¶col. 18
Latin original

Quod homo debeat semper esse paratus Domino advenienti.

English translation

That a man must always be ready for the coming Lord.

¶cols. 17-18; cols. 19-20
Latin original

1. Paratus est Israel in occursum Domini, quoniam venit (Amos IV, 12). Et vos, fratres, estote parati; quia, qua hora non putatis, Filius hominis veniet (Luc. XII, 40). Nihil certius quam quod veniat, sed nihil incertius quam quando veniat. Usque adeo siquidem non est nostrum scire tempora, vel momenta, quae Pater posuit in sua potestate (Act. I, 7), ut nec angelis qui assistunt ei, de die illa vel hora scire datum sit. Dies quoque noster ultimus, certissimus nobis quod veniat: incertissimus est quando, vel ubi, vel unde nobis adveniat; nisi quod, ut ante nos dictum est, senibus est in januis, qui juvenibus est in insidiis. Et utinam vel illi se observarent, qui mortem tam paratam intrare, imo jam intrantem vident. An non ex parte jam intravit, quae aliquas partes corporis jam permortuas occupavit? In pluribus tamen semimortuis videre est adhuc viventem concupiscentiam mundi: frigescunt membra, et fervet avaritia: vita finitur, et ambitio protrahitur. Quandoquidem nobis quoque quibus fortassis aetas, aut salus aliquid longius promittere videtur, quanto minus mors in conspectu est, tanto magis utique, si sapimus, suspecta est: ne videlicet dies illa, sicut fur in nocte, incautos et imparatos nos comprehendat; quae in insidiis posita, quominus videri aut caveri potest, eo magis timenda est. Una igitur securitas est, nunquam esse securum: quatenus timor se observans reddat semper paratum; donec securitas succedat timori, non timor securitati. Observabo me, inquit sapiens, ab iniquitate mea (Psal. XVII, 24), quandoquidem non possum a morte mea; sciens quia justus, si morte praeoccupatus fuerit, in refrigerio erit (Sap. V, 7), imo in morte triumphat, cui non dominatur iniquitas in vita. Quam pulchrum, fratres, quam beatum, in morte non modo securum, sed et gloriosum triumphare; ex auctoritate conscientiae, spiritu et voce Martini cruentam bestiam, si astare praesumpserit, increpare; Judici venienti et pulsanti gratanter aperire! Tum vero videas, heu, similes mei trepidare, inducias petere, nec accipere: oleum conscientiae lamentis poenitentiae velle emere, nec tempus sufficere: larvales illas facies declinare velle, nec posse: a facie irae tonantis velle in corpore delitescere, et cogi exire! Exibit spiritus ejus, et revertetur peccator in terram suam, unde sumptus est. In illa die peribunt omnes cogitationes eorum. Scio quidem, quia conditionis humanae est, sub discrimine exitus conturbari, cum etiam perfecti nolunt exspoliari, sed supervestiri: et qui nihil sibi sunt conscii, quia tamen non in hoc sunt justificati, necesse habent judicium, quod ignorant, vereri. Sed, sive conditionis affectu, sive sanctitatis defectu, sive judicii metu conturbata fuerit anima mea, inquit justus: Tu, Domine, misericordiae memor eris, mittesque misericordiam tuam et veritatem tuam, et eripies animam meam de medio catulorum leonum (Psal. LVI, 4, 5): et qui prius conturbatus eram, postmodum in pace in idipsum dormiam et requiescam (Psal. IV, 9). Vocavit Deus terram, et audivit eum in tremore. Cum enim auditum fecit de coelo judicium, auditu solo terra tremuit, et terrore purgata quievit. Beati qui nunc eatenus excoquunt in se omnem scoriam peccati, ut tunc solo sufficiat timore purgari. Mecum siquidem servo inutili et negligenti bene agitur, si vel per ignem salvus fiam; si, crematis lignis, feno, stipula, quae mihi coarcervavi, vel semiustus evadam. Et quidem comparatione mali, bonum vel per ignem salvari; sed indubitanter longe melius, sola purgatione metus consummari: optimum autem et felicissimum, nec metu turbari.

English translation

1. "Israel is prepared to meet the Lord, for he comes" (Amos IV, 12). "And you, brothers, be ready; for the Son of Man will come at an hour you do not expect" (Luc. XII, 40). Nothing is more certain than that he will come, but nothing is more uncertain than when he will come. Indeed, it is so far beyond our power to know the times or seasons, which the Father has placed in his own power (Act. I, 7), that it has not even been granted to the angels who stand by him to know of that day or hour. Our last day too is most certain to us in that it will come, but it is most uncertain when, or where, or from what quarter it will come upon us — except that, as has been said before our time, it stands at the door for the old, while it lies in ambush for the young. And would that even those would watch themselves who see death so ready to enter — indeed already entering! For has it not in part already entered, seeing that it has already taken possession of some parts of the body already wholly dead? Yet in many who are half-dead one may still see the desire of the world alive: the limbs grow cold, yet avarice burns hot; life comes to its end, yet ambition is drawn out still further. Since even for us, to whom perhaps our age or our health seems to promise a somewhat longer life, the less death is in view, the more, certainly, if we are wise, it ought to be suspected: lest that day, like a thief in the night, seize us unwary and unprepared; for being set in ambush, the less it can be seen or guarded against, the more it is to be feared. There is therefore but one security: never to be secure — so that fear, keeping watch over itself, may render us always ready, until security succeed to fear, and not fear to security. "I will keep myself," says the wise man, "from my iniquity" (Psal. XVII, 24), since I cannot keep myself from my death; knowing that the just man, if he be overtaken by death, shall be in refreshment (Sap. V, 7) — indeed he triumphs in death, over whom iniquity has not had dominion in life. How beautiful, brothers, how blessed, to triumph in death not merely secure but even glorious; with the authority of conscience, in the spirit and voice of Martin, to rebuke the bloody beast should it presume to stand near; to open gladly to the Judge as he comes and knocks! Then indeed you would see, alas, those like me trembling, asking for a delay and not obtaining it; wishing to buy the oil of conscience with the laments of penitence, and time not sufficing; wishing to turn away from those ghastly faces, and not being able; wishing to hide in the body from the face of the thundering wrath, and being forced to go forth! "His spirit shall go forth, and the sinner shall return to his own earth, from which he was taken. In that day all their thoughts shall perish." I know indeed that it belongs to the human condition to be troubled at the crisis of one's departure, since even the perfect do not wish to be stripped, but rather to be clothed over; and those who are conscious of nothing against themselves — because they are not thereby justified — must still fear the judgment which they do not know. But whether my soul be troubled by the feeling natural to this condition, or by a lack of holiness, or by fear of judgment, says the just man: "You, Lord, will be mindful of your mercy, and you will send your mercy and your truth, and you will deliver my soul from the midst of the young lions" (Psal. LVI, 4, 5); and I, who was troubled before, will afterward sleep and rest in peace, in the very same (Psal. IV, 9). "God called the earth, and it heard him in trembling." For when he made judgment heard from heaven, the earth trembled at the mere hearing, and, purged by terror, grew quiet. Blessed are those who now so thoroughly burn away in themselves all the dross of sin, that then it may suffice for them to be purged by fear alone. For with me, a useless and negligent servant, it goes well if I am saved even by fire; if, when the wood, hay, and stubble which I have heaped up for myself are burned away, I escape even half-burned. And indeed, by comparison with evil, it is good to be saved even through fire; but without doubt it is far better to be made perfect by the purification of fear alone; but best and most blessed of all is not even to be troubled by fear.

Latin original

2. Idcirco paratus esto, o verus Israel, in occursum Domini: non solum, ut cum venerit et pulsaverit, aperias ei; sed etiam adhuc illo longe agente, alacer et gratulabundus occurras ei; et tanquam fiduciam habens in die judicii, regnum ejus advenire pleno preceris affectu. Si ergo vis tunc paratus inveniri: Ante judicium para justitiam tibi (Eccli. XVIII, 19), juxta consilium Sapientis; paratus sis ad omne opus bonum faciendum; paratus sis nihilominus ad omne malum patiendum: ut sine reprehensione cordis cantet os tuum, Paratum cor meum, Deus, paratum cor meum. Paratum quidem, ut te cooperante faciam digna; paratum, ut patiar indigna. Et ideo paratum ad utrumque, quod cantabo et psallam in gloria mea: id est, laudabo et gloriabor pro utroque. Et statim in hoc ipsum se justus excitat, Exsurge, inquiens, psalterium et cithara (Psal. LVI, 8, 9); id est, cor meum et caro mea, ad exsultandum in Deum vivum: cor scilicet, propter speciales actiones; caro, propter suas passiones. Cor namque sapiens quae sursum sunt, psalterium est sonans de superiori: caro patiens quae deorsum sunt, cithara sonans ab inferiori. Sic et alibi devotionem suam David offerens Deo, Paratus sum, inquit, ut custodiam mandata tua: et tam paratus, quod non sum turbatus (Psal. CXVIII, 6), cum tentatio irrueret, et persecutio grassaretur. Cum persequeretur aemulus, malediceret servus, animam quaereret filius (II Reg. XV-XVIII); non sum tamen turbatus, quominus custodirem mandata perfectionis evangelicae: retribuentibus mala pro bonis, retribuens bona pro malis; sollicitus de persecutorum salute, moestus de morte; sustinens a servo exprobrari, non sustinens ab amico vindicari. Ecce ante Evangelium evangelica perfectio, charitas scilicet patiens et benigna, etiam erga malignos, quos tolerat. Quia igitur tam paratus erat, fiducialiter Domino nimirum occurrebat: Sine iniquitate, inquit, cucurri, et direxi distortos, quantum in me fuit. Tu exsurge igitur in occursum meum, occurrentis tibi: et quia nec sic pertingere possum ad celsitudinem tuam, nisi tu condescendens operi manuum tuarum porrigas dexteram: Exsurge in occursum meum (Psal. LVIII, 5, 6), et vide si via iniquitatis in me est: et si inveneris nescio quam viam iniquitatis, amove a me: et de lege tua misertus mei, deduc me in via aeterna (Psal. CXXXVIII, 24), id est, Christo, qui via est per quam itur, et aeternitas ad quam pervenitur; via immaculata, mansio beata.

English translation

2. Therefore be ready, O true Israel, to meet the Lord: not only that, when he comes and knocks, you may open to him; but also, while he is still far away, that you may run to meet him eagerly and with gladness; and, as one having confidence for the day of judgment, pray with full affection that his kingdom may come. If, then, you wish to be found ready at that time: Before judgment prepare justice for yourself (Eccli. XVIII, 19), according to the counsel of the Wise Man; be ready to do every good work; be ready likewise to suffer every evil: so that your mouth may sing, without reproach from your heart, My heart is ready, O God, my heart is ready. Ready, indeed, that with you cooperating I may do worthy deeds; ready, that I may suffer unworthy things. And therefore ready for both, so that I shall sing and make melody in my glory: that is, I shall praise and glory for both. And immediately for this very purpose the just man rouses himself, saying, Awake, psaltery and harp (Psal. LVI, 8, 9); that is, my heart and my flesh, to exult in the living God: the heart, namely, for its special actions; the flesh, for its sufferings. For the heart, wise toward the things that are above, is a psaltery sounding from above; the flesh, suffering the things that are below, is a harp sounding from below. So also elsewhere David, offering his devotion to God, says: I am ready to keep your commandments; and so ready that I was not troubled (Psal. CXVIII, 6), when temptation rushed in and persecution raged. When a rival persecuted him, a servant cursed him, a son sought his life (II Reg. XV-XVIII); yet I was not troubled, so as to fail to keep the commandments of evangelical perfection: to those repaying evil for good, repaying good for evil; anxious for the salvation of his persecutors, sorrowful over the death; enduring reproach from a servant, not enduring vengeance taken by a friend. Behold, before the Gospel, evangelical perfection—namely, charity that is patient and kind, even toward the malicious, whom it bears with. Because, then, he was so ready, he indeed ran to meet the Lord with confidence: Without iniquity, he says, I have run, and I have set straight the crooked, as far as it was in me. Arise, therefore, to meet me as I run to meet you: and because even so I cannot attain to your height, unless you, condescending to the work of your hands, stretch forth your right hand: Arise to meet me (Psal. LVIII, 5, 6), and see whether there is a way of iniquity in me: and if you find some way of iniquity I know not of, remove it from me: and, having mercy on me according to your law, lead me in the way everlasting (Psal. CXXXVIII, 24), that is, in Christ, who is the way by which one travels, and the eternity to which one arrives; the way without stain, the blessed dwelling.

3. Puto tamen quia et ante mansionem illam beatam, si paraveris Domino viam immaculatam, ipse dignabitur saepius in via ponere gressus suos, dilatabitque subtus te gressus tuos, ut dilatato corde curras viam mandatorum; cujus angustum forte causabaris initium. Sapientia namque, ut ipsa perhibet, in viis justitiae ambulat (Prov. VIII, 20): et: Qui continens est justitiae apprehendet illam, et obviabit illi quasi mater honorificata. Dignos se ipsa circuit quaerens, et in viis suis ostendit se illis (Eccli. XV, 1, 2, et Sap. VI, 17). Si minus aut nunquam tibi eam occurrere conquereris, vide ne forte viam tuam corruperis. Sic enim scriptum habes: Insipientia viri corrumpit viam suam; Deus autem causabitur in corde suo. Ipse siquidem stat ad ostium et pulsat: ut si quis aperuerit ei, coenet cum eo coelestis mensae delicias. Loquitur sponsa: Vox dilecti mei pulsantis; Aperi mihi, soror mea sponsa (Cantic. V, 2). Aperi mihi cor tuum, et cibabo illud. Aperi mihi os tuum, et implebo illud. Os meum, inquit David, aperui, et attraxi spiritum (Psal. CXVIII, 131). Spiritus enim ante faciem nostram Christus Dominus, non modo invitandus, sed et attrahendus quadam violentia precis, vehementiaque fervoris in hospitium cordis: sicut de duobus discipulis in typum hujus rei historia refert Evangelii (Luc. XXIV, 28, 29). Et ipse siquidem non ob aliud, nisi ut probet devotionem charitatis tuae, aliquando se fingit longius ire: sicut et duo angeli, Loth adorante et obsecrante ut intrarent, dissimulabant tamen dicentes, Minime, sed manebimus in platea. Sed quid dicit Scriptura? Compulit illos oppido, ut diverterent ad eum (Gen. XIX, 2, 3). Pia violentia, qua regnum coeleste rapitur: laudabilis importunitas, quae Christum hospitem, aut angelos promeretur. Sed quid est, inquis, quod Jesus fingit se longius ire? Quid, nisi illud quod Ecclesiastes confitetur de se? Dixi, sapiens efficiar; et ipsa longius recessit a me (Eccle. VII, 24). Hoc autem sponsa manifestius exponit, nostram utique quotidianam exprimens querimoniam, ubi ait: Surrexi aperire dilecto meo: at ille declinaverat, atque transierat. Quaesivi illum, et non inveni (Cantic. V, 5, 6): vocavi, et non respondit mihi, sicut nec mulieri Chananeae. Spiritum sapientiae invocabas, spiritum gratiae quaerebas in oratione: si tibi videtur longius recedere, ne desperes, sed importunus insiste; donec respondeatur tibi, Magna est fides tua, fiat tibi sicut petisti (Matth. XV, 28).

English translation

3. I think, however, that even before that blessed dwelling, if you prepare for the Lord an unstained way, he himself will deign often to set his steps upon the way, and will enlarge your steps beneath you, so that with enlarged heart you may run the way of the commandments, whose beginning you perhaps complained was narrow. For Wisdom, as she herself attests, walks in the ways of justice (Prov. VIII, 20): and: He who holds fast to justice shall lay hold of her, and she shall meet him like a mother held in honor. She goes about seeking out those worthy of herself, and shows herself to them in their ways (Eccli. XV, 1, 2, et Sap. VI, 17). If you complain that she meets you little or never, see whether perhaps you have corrupted your way. For thus it is written: The folly of a man corrupts his way, but he will accuse God in his own heart. For he himself stands at the door and knocks, so that if anyone opens to him, he may dine with him on the delights of the heavenly table. The bride speaks: The voice of my beloved knocking; Open to me, my sister, my spouse (Cantic. V, 2). Open to me your heart, and I will feed it. Open to me your mouth, and I will fill it. I opened my mouth, says David, and drew in the breath (Psal. CXVIII, 131). For the breath before our face is Christ the Lord, who is not only to be invited but also to be drawn in, by a certain violence of prayer and by the vehemence of fervor, into the lodging of the heart: just as the Gospel story relates of the two disciples, as a figure of this matter (Luc. XXIV, 28, 29). And he himself, indeed, for no other reason than to test the devotion of your charity, sometimes pretends to go farther: just as the two angels also, when Lot worshipped them and begged that they would come in, nevertheless feigned, saying, No, but we will stay in the street. But what does Scripture say? He pressed them greatly, so that they turned aside to him (Gen. XIX, 2, 3). A pious violence, by which the kingdom of heaven is seized; a praiseworthy importunity, which wins Christ as guest, or the angels. But what is it, you ask, that Jesus pretends to go farther? What, but that which Ecclesiastes confesses of himself? I said, I shall become wise; and she herself withdrew farther from me (Eccle. VII, 24). But the bride explains this more plainly, expressing indeed our daily complaint, where she says: I rose up to open to my beloved; but he had turned aside and passed on. I sought him, and I did not find him (Cantic. V, 5, 6): I called him, and he did not answer me, just as he did not answer the Canaanite woman either. You were calling upon the spirit of wisdom, you were seeking the spirit of grace in prayer: if he seems to you to withdraw farther off, do not despair, but press on importunately, until it is answered to you, Great is your faith; be it done to you as you have asked (Matth. XV, 28).

4. Cum tamen invitas Jesum, vide ne Deum majestatis ad sordidum et indignum invites hospitium; ubi nec te ipsum patiatur habitare quietum uxor litigiosa, seu fumus, aut stillicidium. Non enim nisi in pace fit locus ejus: nec aliud quam justitia et judicium praeparatio sedis ejus. Nunc autem me, inquit, de die in diem quaerunt, et scire vias meas volunt, quia gens1 quae justitiam fecerit, et judicium Dei sui non dereliquerit (Isa. LVIII, 2). Justitia, inquit, et judicium praeparatio sedis ejus (Psal. LXXXVIII, 15). Noli causari quasi sumptuosum [fort. praesumptuosum] sit, viresque tuae excedat paupertatis, tam magnifico, tamque potenti praeparare domum hospiti: habes ad manum unde id possis. Humanum dico propter infirmitatem carnis tuae, vel magis angustiam mentis tuae. Confitere perfecte de praeterito: bonam voluntatem habe de caetero; pax enim hominibus bonae voluntatis (Luc. II, 14), et hoc judicio et justitia sedem Altissimo praeparasti. Vis manifestius audire de confessione, quod eam oporteat fieri in praeparatione adventus Domini? Justus, ait Scriptura, in initio sermonis accusator est sui. Et quid sequitur? Venit amicus ejus (Prov. XVIII, 17), qui modo ante confessionem inimicus assistebat longius. Cum enim dixit, Confitebor adversum me injustitiam meam Domino (Psal. XXXI, 5); et ipse remisit: Venit, inquam, et investigabit eum. Prorsus investigabit, tanquam potio valida, scrutans corda et renes; pertingens usque ad divisionem animae et spiritus, exhauriens noxia de medullis animae, mentisque visceribus, purgans affectus ut fructum plus afferat: de cujus primitivo fructu confessionis jam gaudet pater agricola. Ipse tamen qui post confessionem nonnunquam et antequam invocetur, adest, nonnunquam exspectat ut tu prior eum invites: et ut meritum tibi augeat, saepe diutius dissimulat; ut tu attentius psallendo, instantius orando, dulciter violentus intrare compellas: alioquin planget propheta quod civitates austri clausae sint, nullo introeunte (Jerem. XIII, 19). Cum ergo dicere potueris, Paratum cor meum, Deus, quia vacuatum a malis; paratum cor meum, quia sanctis plenum desideriis: tunc sedulus fac quod sequitur, Cantabo et psalmum dicam (Psal. LXIV, 8). Et quicunque sit sonus cantantis vel psallentis, haec sit intentio mentis: Exsurge, gloria mea, exsurge in occursum mei; quia, quantum potui, occurri tibi. O benigne Jesu, quam alacer et promptus, quam laetus et festivus occurris hujusmodi devotioni! quam hilarem te ostendis in viis istis! Occurristi, inquit Isaias, laetanti et facienti justitiam: in viis tuis recordabuntur tui (Isa. LXVI, 5). Si enim psallas sapienter, in via immaculata veniet; veniet qui et illuminabit abscondita tua, ut quae nescis intelligas mysteria Scripturarum, eritque ut dicas: Psallam et intelligam in via immaculata, quando veniet ad me (Psal. C, 1, 2). Excita, Domine, potentiam tuam, quae excitet pigritiam nostram in occursum tui: et veni, ut salvos nos facias (Psal. LXXIX, 3), Salvator mundi; qui vivis et regnas per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

4. Yet when you invite Jesus, see that you do not invite the God of majesty into a sordid and unworthy lodging—one in which not even you yourself could be permitted to dwell in peace, on account of a quarrelsome wife, or smoke, or a leaking roof. For his place is made only in peace, and the preparation of his throne is nothing other than justice and judgment. 'But now,' he says, 'they seek me day after day, and desire to know my ways, because they are a nation1 that has done righteousness and has not forsaken the judgment of its God' (Isa. LVIII, 2). 'Justice and judgment,' he says, 'are the preparation of his throne' (Psal. LXXXVIII, 15). Do not object, as though it were a costly thing [perhaps read: presumptuous], and beyond the strength of your poverty, to prepare a house for so magnificent and so powerful a guest: you have at hand the means by which you can do this. I speak in human terms, on account of the weakness of your flesh, or rather the narrowness of your mind. Make full confession concerning the past; have good will concerning what remains; for 'peace is to men of good will' (Luc. II, 14), and by this judgment and justice you will have prepared a seat for the Most High. Do you wish to hear more plainly about confession, that it must be made in preparing for the Lord's coming? 'The just man,' Scripture says, 'at the beginning of his speech is his own accuser. And what follows? His friend comes' (Prov. XVIII, 17)—he who, just before, before the confession, stood far off as an enemy. For when he said, 'I will confess my injustice against myself to the Lord' (Psal. XXXI, 5), he himself forgave it; 'he comes,' I say, 'and will search him out.' He will indeed search him out thoroughly, like a strong medicine, examining hearts and loins, reaching even to the division of soul and spirit, draining out the harmful matter from the marrow of the soul and the inward parts of the mind, purging the affections so that it may bear more fruit; and in the first-fruit of this confession the Father, the husbandman, already rejoices. Yet he himself, who after confession is sometimes present even before he is invoked, at other times waits for you to invite him first; and in order to increase your merit, he often pretends for a longer time not to notice, so that you, by singing more attentively, by praying more insistently, may compel him, sweetly violent, to enter—otherwise the prophet will lament that the cities of the south are shut up, with no one entering (Jerem. XIII, 19). Therefore, when you are able to say, 'My heart is ready, O God,' because it is emptied of evils; 'my heart is ready,' because it is filled with holy desires—then diligently do what follows: 'I will sing and recite a psalm' (Psal. LXIV, 8). And whatever be the sound of the one singing or playing, let this be the intention of the mind: 'Arise, my glory, arise to meet me; for, as much as I was able, I have come to meet you.' O kind Jesus, how eager and ready, how joyful and festive you come to meet such devotion! How cheerful you show yourself along these paths! 'You met,' says Isaiah, 'the one who rejoices and does justice: in your ways they will remember you' (Isa. LXVI, 5). For if you sing wisely, he will come in the undefiled way; he will come who will also illumine your hidden things, so that you may understand the mysteries of the Scriptures which you did not know, and it will come to be that you say: 'I will sing and understand in the undefiled way, when he comes to me' (Psal. C, 1, 2). Stir up, O Lord, your power, that it may stir up our sluggishness to meet you; and come, that you may save us (Psal. LXXIX, 3), O Savior of the world, who live and reign through all ages of ages. Amen.
