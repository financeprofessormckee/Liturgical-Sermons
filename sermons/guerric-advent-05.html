Editorial introduction

This fifth Advent sermon belongs to Guerric of Igny's medieval collection of sermons for the liturgical year, here read from the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. Its individual date is unknown; the collection is associated approximately with his abbacy in 1138–1157. The way for the Lord is prepared by walking it and has no end, so progress must always begin again. Fear of the Lord leads to penance, which goes before grace. Through Isaiah's holy way and the serpent shedding its skin in a narrow passage, Guerric keeps his monks on the royal road toward its promised end. The source names Advent without assigning a particular Sunday.

SERMON V.

¶col. 26
Latin original

Quomodo proficiendum in paranda via Domino.

English translation

How to Make Progress in Preparing the Way for the Lord.

¶cols. 25-26
Latin original

1. Parate viam Domino (Isa. XL, 3). Via Domini, fratres, quam parare jubemur, ambulando paratur, parando ambulatur. Et licet multum profeceritis in ea, semper tamen vobis restat paranda: ut de his in quae pervenistis, tendatis et extendatis vos in ulteriora; quatenus per singulos profectus Dominus, cujus adventui via paratur, quasi novus, ac seipso semper major occurrat. Ideo bene justus orat: Legem pone mihi, Domine, viam justificationum tuarum, et exquiram eam semper (Psal. CXVIII, 33). Ideo forsitan via aeterna dicta est; quia licet providentia semitam cujusque investigaverit, terminumque constituerit quousque proficiat; terminum tamen non habet illius, in quam proficitis, bonitatis natura. Itaque sapiens impigerque viator cum consummatus fuerit, tunc incipiet: ita scilicet obliviscens quae retro sunt, ut quotidie dicat sibi, Nunc coepi (Psal. LXXVI, 11). Exsultat ut gigas, quem nil terreat ad currendam viam mandatorum Dei: Idithumque factus, pigros qui in via resident, facile transilit in impetu cursus sui. Et licet novissima diei hora venerit, consummatus in brevi explet tempora multa; ut repente de novissimo primus, inter primos coronam accipiat.

English translation

1. "Prepare the way for the Lord" (Isa. XL, 3). The way of the Lord, brothers, which we are commanded to prepare, is prepared by walking, and is walked by preparing. And however much progress you have made in it, it always remains for you to prepare further: so that from the point you have reached, you may stretch and extend yourselves into what lies beyond; so that through each advance the Lord, for whose coming the way is prepared, may meet you as it were new, and ever greater than himself. For this reason the just man rightly prays: "Set before me a law, O Lord, the way of your justifications, and I will always seek it" (Psal. CXVIII, 33). For this reason, perhaps, it is called the eternal way; because although providence has traced out the path for each person and set a limit up to which he may progress, the nature of that goodness toward which you are progressing has no limit. And so the wise and tireless traveler, when he has been made complete, then begins; forgetting, that is, the things that are behind, so that he may say to himself every day, "Now I have begun" (Psal. LXXVI, 11). He exults like a giant, whom nothing may frighten from running the way of God's commandments: having become like Idithun, he easily leaps over those slothful ones who sit by the roadside, in the very impetus of his course. And even though he comes at the last hour of the day, being made complete, he fulfills much time in a short span; so that from being last he suddenly becomes first, and receives the crown among the first.

¶cols. 25-26; cols. 27-28
Latin original

2. Sed nos qui de profectu viae ejus loquimur, utinam vel initium ejus apprehenderimus! Meo namque sensu atque judicio non parum in ea proficit qui coepit; si tamen vere coepit, si viam civitatis habitaculi invenit. Quam pauci sunt, ait Veritas, qui inveniunt eam! (Matth. VII, 14.) Quam multi sunt qui in solitudine errant! prorsus omnes solitarii, id est omnes superbi, qui se solos reputant. Nullus eorum adhuc dicere potest: Nunc coepi, haec mutatio dexterae Excelsi (Psal. LXXVI, 11). Non enim est illis commutatio, ut desinant esse quod erant, quia non timuerunt Deum (Psal. LIV, 20), cum initium sapientiae sit timor Domini (Psal. CX, 10). Si initium sapientiae, nimirum et viae bonae. Ipse nempe timor Domini, ipse est qui statim illud sapiens consilium generat in corde viri, ut dicere possit: Cogitavi vias meas, et converti pedes meos in testimonia tua (Psal. CXVIII, 59). Ipse confessionem persuadet, quam Psalmista initium boni operis esse docet: Incipite, inquit, domino in confessione (Psal. CXLVI, 7). Ipse ad poenitentiam superbum inclinat, ut vocem clamantis in deserto viamque parari jubentis, et unde incipiendum sit ostendentis, audiat: Poenitentiam agite; appropinquabit enim regnum coelorum (Matth. III, 2). Huic sententia consonat Salomonis, qui manifesta definitione rudes instruit, dicens: Initium viae bonae, facere justa (Prov. XVI, 5). Quid est enim justa facere, nisi poenitentiam agere? nisi de nobisipsis quod Deo debemus exigere, et quod rapuimus exsolvere? Haec est justitia quae ante Dominum ambulat, viamque ei placitam parat, sicut scriptum est: Justitia ante eum ambulabit, et ponet in via gressus suos (Psal. LXXXIV, 14). Sicut enim Joannes Jesum, sic poenitentia gratiam praecedit; illam scilicet gratiam, qua post satisfactionem reconciliati, suscipimur in osculo pacis. In hac siquidem poenitentiae via, grato atque hilari occursu obviant sibi, seseque osculantur justitia et pax; justitia scilicet hominis se punientis, et pax Dei ignoscentis: laetumque ac jucundum celebrant in osculo sancto foedus reconciliationis. Denique ne parvi aestimetur meriti haec justitia hominis de semetipso facientis judicium et justitiam, neve oratiunculae, et vota pigrorum se laboribus poenitentium comparare praesumant; quid de ipsa Salomon sentiat; audiant: Initium, inquit, viae bonae, facere justa: accepta autem Deo magis, quam immolare hostias (Prov. XVI, 5). Si ergo videris hominem huic poenitentiae professum, regularibus ac sanctis laboribus, quorum debitor est, singulares oratiunculas praeferentem; ingere ei hanc Sapientis sententiam: Initium viae bonae, facere justa: accepta autem magis Deo quam immolare hostias. Similiter, si videris hominem, adversus quem frater suus aliquid habet, munus seu laudum, seu sacramentorum, ad altare offerentem; repete ei nihilominus eumdem sermonem: Initium viae bonae, facere justa, ut fratri reconcilieris: accepta autem Deo magis quam immolare hostias. Nam et illo etiam spectat haec sententia, ut daturus eleemosynam, prius, si quem defraudavit, vel simplum restituat. Sic et tu quoque voluntaria oris tui beneplacita nullatenus Deo esse credideris, illa scilicet quae in psalmis, vel in orationibus secretis ei obtuleris; si propterea canonem psalmorum ex Regula constitutum negligentius exsolveris. Nonne, si recte offeras, et non recte dividas peccasti? (Gen. III, sec. LXX.) Rectas, inquit, facite semitas ejus (Matth. III, 3, et Isa. XL, 3), vos qui paratis viam Domini.

English translation

2. But we who speak of progress on this way—would that we had even grasped its beginning! For in my own sense and judgment, he who has begun makes no small progress on it; provided, however, that he has truly begun, if he has found the way to the city of habitation. "How few are they," says the Truth, "who find it!" (Matth. VII, 14.) How many are there who wander in solitude! Indeed, all the solitary—that is, all the proud, who consider themselves alone. None of them can yet say: "Now I have begun; this is the change of the right hand of the Most High" (Psal. LXXVI, 11). For there is no change for them, that they should cease to be what they were, because they have not feared God (Psal. LIV, 20), since the fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom (Psal. CX, 10). If it is the beginning of wisdom, surely it is also the beginning of the good way. For it is indeed the fear of the Lord itself that immediately generates in a man's heart that wise counsel, so that he can say: "I have thought on my ways, and turned my feet unto your testimonies" (Psal. CXVIII, 59). It is this fear that persuades to confession, which the Psalmist teaches is the beginning of a good work: "Begin," he says, "to the Lord with confession" (Psal. CXLVI, 7). It is this fear that inclines the proud man to penance, so that he hears the voice of the one crying in the desert, commanding that the way be prepared, and showing where one must begin: "Do penance, for the kingdom of heaven will draw near" (Matth. III, 2). With this sentence agrees that of Solomon, who instructs the unlearned with a plain definition, saying: "The beginning of a good way is to do what is right" (Prov. XVI, 5). For what is it to do what is right, except to do penance? Except to exact from ourselves what we owe to God, and to repay what we have stolen? This is the righteousness that walks before the Lord, and prepares for him a way pleasing to him, as it is written: "Righteousness shall walk before him, and shall set her steps in the way" (Psal. LXXXIV, 14). For just as John preceded Jesus, so penance precedes grace; that grace, namely, by which, after making satisfaction, reconciled, we are received with the kiss of peace. In this way of penance, indeed, righteousness and peace meet one another with a welcome and joyful encounter, and kiss one another; namely, the righteousness of the man punishing himself, and the peace of God forgiving; and they celebrate, in a holy kiss, the glad and joyous covenant of reconciliation. Finally, lest this righteousness of a man passing judgment and doing justice upon himself be reckoned of little merit, and lest little prayers and the vows of the slothful presume to compare themselves to the labors of the penitent, let them hear what Solomon thinks of it: "The beginning," he says, "of a good way is to do what is right; but more acceptable to God than to offer sacrifices" (Prov. XVI, 5). If, therefore, you see a man professed to this penance, preferring his own private little prayers to the regular and holy labors of which he is a debtor, press upon him this sentence of the Wise Man: "The beginning of a good way is to do what is right; but it is more acceptable to God than to offer sacrifices." Likewise, if you see a man, against whom his brother has some grievance, offering a gift at the altar, whether of praises or of the sacraments, repeat to him nevertheless the same word: "The beginning of a good way is to do what is right, that you be reconciled to your brother; but it is more acceptable to God than to offer sacrifices." For this sentence also looks to this: that one who is about to give alms should first, if he has defrauded anyone, restore at least the simple amount. So also you yourself should by no means believe that the voluntary offerings of your mouth are pleasing to God—those, namely, which you offer him in psalms or in private prayers—if on that account you perform more negligently the canon of psalms established by the Rule. Have you not sinned, if you offer rightly but do not divide rightly? (Gen. III, sec. LXX.) "Make straight," he says, "his paths" (Matth. III, 3, et Isa. XL, 3), you who prepare the way of the Lord.

Latin original

3. O Domine, tu parasti directiones nobis, si modo nos recte ambulemus in illis. Tu legem posuisti nobis, viam justificationum tuarum, per illum scilicet, quem hujus sanctae institutionis Legislatorem dedisti: Haec via, inquiens, ambulate in ea, neque ad dexteram, neque ad sinistram (Isai. XXX, 21). Haec via plane, sicut propheta promiserat: Via directa nobis, ita ut stulti non errent per eam (Isa. XXXV, 8). Junior fui, etenim senui; et non vidi, si bene memini, stultum errantem per viam: cum vix aliquem sapientem viderim, qui rectitudinem ejus tenere potuerit. Vae, qui sapientes estis in oculis vestris, et coram vobismetipsis prudentes (Isa. V, 21)! quos sapientia vestra devios abducit a via salutis, nec sinit stultitiam sequi Salvatoris. Non ignorabat ille stultus propter Christum, quid haec sapientia terrena, animalis, diabolica mali faciat, qui dicebat: Si quis sapiens esse videtur in hoc saeculo, stultus fiat, ut sit sapiens apud Deum (I Cor. III, 18). Optabilis stultitia, quae divino judicio reputatur sapientia, quae hominem non sinit errare de via. Haec stultitia, nisi fallor, est illa desursum sapientia, pudica, pacifica, modesta, suadibilis, bonis consentiens (Jacob. III, 17): a quibus omnibus quam procul abhorreat sapiens in oculis suis, utinam nunquam experientia ipsa probasset in oculis nostris. Si contristat vos arrogantia, seu pervicacia hujusmodi sapientium, consolatur abundantius nostrorum pax atque modestia stultorum, qui quanto minus sibi credunt, tanto suadibiliores bonis consentiunt. Tales itaque stulti non errant facile de via Dei, sed juxta verbum prophetae semper audiunt vocem post tergum monentis: Haec via, ambulate in ea, neque ad dexteram, neque ad sinistram (Isai. XXX, 21): ne scilicet per fervorem excedentes modum, aut per teporem subsistentes citra modum, legem praescriptam, viamque deseratis regiam. Spiritus est ad dexteram, caro est ad sinistram; et haec sibi colluctantia semper sibi adversantur, ut non quaecunque vult, sive ista, sive ille, faciamus: ne, si plus justo sequimur impetum spiritus, declinemus ad dexteram; aut, si plus justo carni indulgemus, deviemus ad sinistram.

English translation

3. O Lord, you have prepared straight paths for us, if only we walk rightly in them. You have set before us a law, the way of your righteous judgments, through him whom you gave as the Lawgiver of this holy institution, saying: 'This is the way, walk in it, neither to the right hand nor to the left' (Isai. XXX, 21). This is indeed the way, as the prophet had promised: 'A way made straight for us, so that fools should not err therein' (Isa. XXXV, 8). I have been young, and now am old; and I have not seen, if I remember rightly, a fool going astray in the way — though I have scarcely seen any wise man who was able to keep to its straight course. Woe to you who are wise in your own eyes, and prudent in your own sight (Isa. V, 21)! whom your wisdom leads astray from the way of salvation, and does not allow to follow the foolishness of the Savior. That man, a fool for Christ's sake, was not ignorant of what evil this earthly, animal, diabolical wisdom works, who said: 'If any man seem to be wise in this world, let him become a fool, that he may be wise with God' (I Cor. III, 18). Desirable is that folly, which by divine judgment is reckoned wisdom, which does not allow a man to stray from the way. This folly, unless I am mistaken, is that wisdom from above, chaste, peaceable, modest, easy to be persuaded, agreeing with the good (Jacob. III, 17): from all of which how far the man who is wise in his own eyes recoils, would that experience itself had never proved it before our eyes! If the arrogance or stubbornness of such wise men grieves you, the peace and modesty of our fools consoles you all the more abundantly, who, the less they trust themselves, the more readily they agree with what is good. Such fools, then, do not easily stray from the way of God, but, according to the word of the prophet, always hear behind them the voice warning: 'This is the way, walk in it, neither to the right hand nor to the left' (Isai. XXX, 21): that is, lest, by exceeding the measure through fervor, or by falling short of the measure through lukewarmness, you abandon the prescribed law and the royal road. The spirit is on the right, the flesh is on the left; and these, warring against each other, are always opposed to one another, so that we do not do whatever either the one or the other wills: lest, if we follow the impulse of the spirit more than is right, we turn aside to the right; or, if we indulge the flesh more than is right, we deviate to the left.

Latin original

4. Hanc denique viam justificationum Domini, viam veritatis quam elegistis, quanta laude praedicet Isaias, delectat me cum charitate vestra recordari. Et erit, inquit, ibi, id est, in antiquis cubilibus draconum, in terra utique deserta et invia, semita et via, sicut hodie cernere est: quia in feris et incultis hominibus, qui sine lege vivebant, in ipsis invenitur ordo vitae, et disciplina. Et haec via, inquit, sancta vocabitur: quia nimirum sanctificatio est peccatorum, perditorumque salus. Quanta vero virtute ac reverentia sanctitatis praeemineat, inde probat propheta, quia non transibit, inquit, pollutus per eam (Isa. XXXV, 8). O mi Isaia, ibunt igitur qui polluti sunt per aliam viam? Imo potius huc omnes, huc veniant, hac incedant. Immundis enim praecipue Christus hanc viam stravit, qui venit quaerere et salvum facere quod perierat in viis saeculi. Quid igitur? transibit pollutus per viam sanctam? Absit! Veniat quantumlibet pollutus ad eam, non tamen transibit pollutus per eam: quia cum transierit, jam pollutus non erit. Admittit quidem via sancta pollutum, sed statim abluit admissum, quia diluit omne commissum, tanquam vere baptismus alter poenitentium. Hic plane, hic baptizat, non Joannes, sed Jesus baptismo poenitentiae: hic patet fons domus David in ablutionem peccatoris et menstruatae. Propterea namque via haec pollutum admittit, sed pollutum non transmittit: quia via arcta est, et quasi foramen illud angustum est, quo serpens innovandus cum exuviis suae vetustatis venire potest, sed cum ipsis transire non potest; sed novum suaque nuditate melius vestitum, transitus angustia trajicit, extricans omnem, quem attulerat, squalorem vetustatis. Bene ergo prudentiam serpentis imitari rogamur, qui nec aliter innovari possumus, nisi per angustum coarctemur.

English translation

4. Finally, it delights me to recall, together with your charity, with what great praise Isaiah proclaims this way of the Lord's righteous judgments, this way of truth which you have chosen. 'And it shall be there,' he says — that is, in the ancient lairs of dragons, in a land indeed desert and trackless — 'a path and a way,' as may be seen even today: because among wild and uncultivated men, who lived without law, there is found in them an order of life and discipline. 'And this way,' he says, 'shall be called holy': because it is, assuredly, the sanctification of sinners and the salvation of the lost. But how greatly it excels in the power and reverence of holiness, the prophet proves from this, that, he says, 'the unclean shall not pass through it' (Isa. XXXV, 8). O my Isaiah, shall those who are unclean, then, go by another way? Nay rather, let all come here, let them come here, let them walk by this road. For Christ laid this way especially for the unclean, he who came to seek and to save what had perished in the ways of the world. What then? Shall the unclean pass through the holy way? God forbid! Let him come to it however unclean he may be, yet he shall not pass through it unclean: because when he has passed through, he will no longer be unclean. The holy way indeed admits the unclean man, but immediately washes the one it has admitted, because it washes away every sin committed, being truly, as it were, a second baptism for penitents. Here plainly, here it is Jesus, not John, who baptizes with the baptism of penance: here the fountain of the house of David lies open for the washing of the sinner and of the menstruous woman. For this reason this way admits the unclean man, but does not let the unclean man pass through: because the way is narrow, and is like that narrow opening through which the serpent, needing to be renewed, can come with the worn skin of its old age, but cannot pass through still wearing it; rather, made new and better clothed in its own nakedness, it squeezes through the narrowness of the passage, freeing itself from all the squalor of the old age it had brought with it. Rightly, then, are we urged to imitate the prudence of the serpent, since we cannot be renewed in any other way unless we are compressed through a narrow strait.

¶cols. 27-28; cols. 29-30
Latin original

5. Sane quod illius antiqui serpentis insidias evadamus, si per hanc arctam viam hujus novi serpentis exemplum sequamur, Isaias promittit, qui de eadem via subdit: Non erit ibi leo, et mala bestia non ascendet per eam, nec invenietur ibi (Isa. XXXV, 9). Securi ergo simus, si de via non exorbitamus. Potest leo ille, qui circuit quaerens quem devoret, juxta iter scandala ponere, laqueos abscondere, viatores rugitu terrere: sed persistentes in via laedere non potest; quippe cui via ipsa terror et supplicium est. Fortitudo nempe simplicis, via Domini, et pavor his qui operantur malum (Prov. X, 29). Si ergo in via es, hoc solum time, ne devies, ne Dominum offendas, qui te per viam ducit, ut dimittat te vagum in via cordis tui: praeter eum ne timeas alienum. Quod si nimis arctam viam causaris, prospice finem, quo te via ducit. Si enim omnis consummationis videris finem, continuo dices: Latum mandatum tuum nimis (Psal. CXVIII, 96). Si minus videre potes, crede videnti Isaiae, qui oculus tui corporis est. Ipse utcunque videbat, cum id quod sequitur dicebat: Et ecce ambulabunt hac via qui liberati fuerint, et redempti a Domino: et venient in Sion cum laude, et laetitia sempiterna super capita eorum Gaudium quoque et laetitiam obtinebunt, fugiet dolor et gemitus (Isai. XXXV, 9, 10). Qui satis hunc finem cogitat, puto quia non solum viam dilatat sibi; sed etiam pennas, ut jam non ambulet, sed volet, assumit sibi. Semper ergo, fratres, cogitate finem praemiorum, et cum omni facultate et alacritate curretis viam mandatorum. Per ipsam vos deducat et perducat ipse, qui est via currentium, et bravium pervenientium, Christus Jesus; cui honor et gloria per omnia saecula saeculorum.

English translation

5. Isaiah promises that we shall indeed escape the snares of that ancient serpent, if along this narrow way we follow the example of this new serpent; for he adds concerning this same way: "There shall be no lion there, and the evil beast shall not go up by it, nor be found there" (Isa. XXXV, 9). Let us therefore be secure, if we do not stray from the way. That lion, who goes about seeking whom he may devour, can set stumbling blocks along the road, hide snares, and terrify travelers with his roaring; but he cannot harm those who remain on the way, since the way itself is his terror and his torment. For "the way of the Lord is strength to the simple, and fear to them that work evil" (Prov. X, 29). If, then, you are on the way, fear only this: that you may turn aside, that you may offend the Lord who leads you along the way, lest he abandon you to wander in the way of your own heart; apart from him, fear no other. But if you complain that the way is too narrow, look to the end to which the way leads you. For if you see the end of all perfection, you will at once say: "Your commandment is exceedingly broad" (Psal. CXVIII, 96). If you cannot see this well enough, believe Isaiah who sees it, for he is the eye of your body. He himself saw it, after a manner, when he said what follows: "And behold, they shall walk in this way who have been set free and redeemed by the Lord; and they shall come to Sion with praise, and everlasting joy shall be upon their heads. They shall obtain joy and gladness; sorrow and mourning shall flee away" (Isai. XXXV, 9, 10). He who meditates enough on this end, I think, not only widens the way for himself, but also takes to himself wings, so that he no longer walks but flies. Therefore, brethren, always think on the end of the rewards, and with all ease and eagerness you will run the way of the commandments. May he himself lead and bring you through it, who is the way of those who run and the prize of those who arrive, Christ Jesus; to whom be honor and glory for ever and ever.
