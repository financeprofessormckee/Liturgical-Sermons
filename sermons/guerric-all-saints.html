Editorial introduction

Guerric reads the first Beatitude, "Blessed are the poor in spirit," as the opening of the Gospel and the first of eight steps that lead from renunciation of the world to the office of peacemaker and the merit of martyrdom. Those who possess God possess all things, while the miser is possessed by his money. Poverty of spirit lies more in humility than in want, and the poor already hold the kingdom, carrying God in their hearts. The sermon appears under the heading for the Solemnity of All Saints in the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157; the individual sermon is undated.

De pauperibus spiritu.

English translation

On the poor in spirit.

¶cols. 205-206; cols. 207-208
Latin original

1. Beati pauperes spiritu (Matth. V, 3). Agnosco notum illud ac nobile prognosticum, quod in testimonium sui, necdum natus in carne Dei Filius protulit; jam vero natus, sed nondum notus, sibi competere docuit: Spiritus, inquit, Domini super me, evangelizare pauperibus misit me (Isai. LXI, 1). Ecce pauperes evangelizantur, ecce pauperibus Evangelium regni nuntiatur: Beati, inquit, pauperes spiritu, quoniam ipsorum est regnum coelorum (Matth. V, 3). Laetum prorsus ac novae plenum gratiae Novi Testamenti principium, etiam quantumlibet infidelem aut pigrum provocans ad audiendum, sed amplius ad faciendum, cum scilicet beatitudo miseris, exsulibus et egenis promittitur regnum coelorum. Gratum, inquam, et auspicatum novae legis initium, quando in ipso statim initio, tot beatitudinum benedictiones dat Legislator, quibus delectati eant de virtute in virtutem: per istos videlicet octo gradus ascensionis, quos in corde nostro disposuit structura Evangelii, secundum exemplar et imaginem coelestium, quod Ezechieli quoque ostensum est in monte visionum Dei. Est enim manifeste quidam ascensus cordium, et profectus meritorum iste per ordinem digestus octonarius virtutum, gradatim de imis ad summa perducens virum evangelicae perfectionis, donec ad videndum Deum deorum in Sion ingrediatur templum, de quo propheta dicit: Et in octo gradibus ascensus ejus (Ezech. XL, 37).

English translation

1. "Blessed are the poor in spirit" (Matth. V, 3). I recognize that well-known and noble sign which the Son of God, not yet born in the flesh, brought forth as testimony of himself; and indeed, now born, but not yet known, he taught that it applied to himself: "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me," he says, "he has sent me to preach the gospel to the poor" (Isai. LXI, 1). Behold, the poor are evangelized; behold, to the poor the Gospel of the kingdom is announced: "Blessed," he says, "are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven" (Matth. V, 3). A truly joyful beginning of the New Testament, full of new grace, which summons even the most unbelieving or slothful to listen, but still more to act — since blessedness is promised to the wretched, the exiles, and the needy, namely, the kingdom of heaven. A pleasing, I say, and auspicious beginning of the new law, since right at its very outset the Lawgiver gives so many blessings of beatitudes, by which men, delighted, may go from virtue to virtue: namely, through those eight steps of ascent which the structure of the Gospel has arranged in our heart, according to the pattern and image of heavenly things, which was also shown to Ezekiel on the mountain of the visions of God. For there is manifestly a certain ascent of hearts, and a progress of merits, this eightfold order of virtues arranged in sequence, leading step by step from the lowest things to the highest the man of evangelical perfection, until he enters to see the God of gods in Sion, into the temple of which the prophet says: "And by eight steps was its ascent" (Ezech. XL, 37).

Latin original

2. Prima siquidem virtus inchoantium est renuntiatio saeculi, qua pauperes efficimur spiritu : secunda, mansuetudo, qua nos obedientiae subdimus, et assuescimus : deinde, luctus, quo peccata plorantur, vel virtutes implorantur. Ibi nimirum gustamus, unde amplius esuriamus, et sitiamus justitiam, tam in nobis quam in aliis, zeloque contra peccantes incipimus commoveri. Sed ne zelus immoderatior in vitium feratur, sequitur misericordia, qua temperetur. His ergo studiis, vel exercitiis, cum quis justus et misericors esse didicerit, idoneus erit fortassis contemplationi vacare, et mundando cordi, quo Deus videatur, operam dare. Exercitatus autem et probatus tam in actione, quam in contemplatione, tum demum dignus erit, qui nomen et officium Filii Dei gerens, pater et minister factus aliorum, tanquam mediator et sequester pacificet inter ipsos et Deum: pacificet ipsos ad invicem, vel etiam ad eos qui foris sunt, sicut scriptum est in laude sanctorum patrum, Pacificantes in domibus suis (Eccli. XLIV, 6). In hoc officio qui fidelis et constans fuerit, saepe virtutem et meritum sibi martyrii comparabit, persecutionem patiens propter justitiam; aliquando et ab his pro quibus pugnabit, ut sit ei dicere, Filii matris meae pugnaverunt contra me (Cantic. I, 5); et, Cum his qui oderunt pacem, eram pacificus; cum loquebar illis, impugnabant me gratis (Psal. CXIX, 7).

English translation

2. For the first virtue of beginners is renunciation of the world, by which we are made poor in spirit; the second, meekness, by which we submit ourselves to obedience and grow accustomed to it; then, mourning, by which sins are wept over, or virtues are implored. There, surely, we taste that from which we may hunger and thirst all the more for justice, both in ourselves and in others, and we begin to be moved with zeal against sinners. But lest this zeal, growing too immoderate, turn into vice, mercy follows, by which it is tempered. By these pursuits, or exercises, when someone has learned to be just and merciful, he will perhaps be fit to devote himself to contemplation, and to give effort to the cleansing of the heart by which God may be seen. But one trained and proved both in action and in contemplation will then at last be worthy — bearing the name and office of the Son of God, having become a father and minister to others — to make peace, as a mediator and go-between, between them and God: to make peace among themselves, or even with those who are outside, as it is written in praise of the holy fathers, "Making peace in their own houses" (Eccli. XLIV, 6). Whoever is faithful and steadfast in this office will often win for himself the virtue and merit of martyrdom, suffering persecution for the sake of justice — sometimes even at the hands of those for whom he fights, so that he may say, "The sons of my mother have fought against me" (Cantic. I, 5); and, "With them that hated peace I was peaceable: when I spoke to them, they fought against me without cause" (Psal. CXIX, 7).

Latin original

3. Quantae autem gloriae, quamque copiose mercedis in coelo sit culmen hujus perfectionis, inde utcumque licet aestimare, quod ipsa etiam abrenuntiantium saeculo primordia tantae Dominus praedicat felicitatis, ut dicat: Beati pauperes spiritu, quoniam ipsorum est regnum coelorum. Plane beati qui, projectis vilibus, sed gravibus sarcinulis hujus mundi, nolunt divites fieri, nisi de solo Creatore mundi, propter ipsum, tanquam nihil habentes, et per ipsum omnia possidentes. An non possident omnia qui possident continentem et disponentem omnia, quorum Deus portio est et haereditas; qui, ut nihil desit, timentibus eum, caetera prout expedire novit, dispensat ad utendum, et se ipsum servat ad fruendum? Cum haeres Dei, cohaeres autem Christi, adultus et emancipatus introducetur in plenariam possessionem exspectatae haereditatis, tunc profecto jus absolutum, ac liberum imperium habebit in creaturis: qui modo quanto tempore parvulus est, nihil differt a servo, cum sit Dominus omnium; sed sub tutoribus et actoribus est usque ad tempus praefinitum a Patre. Tunc mundus agnoscet justum ac legitimum haeredem Dominum suum, propter quem creatus est: agnoscet, inquam, quia jam non conformem sibi, sed reformatum in novitate sensus sui ad imaginem Dei, ad quam factus est. Sed et nunc quoque fideli homini totus mundus divitiarum est (Prov. XVII, sec. LXX) (12)1; non solum quia ad agnoscendum vel diligendum auctorem mundi, mundanis omnibus utitur tanquam ad hoc sibi datis; et in via testimoniorum Dei, quae videt facturam sicut et Scripturam perhibere factori, delectatur sicut in omnibus divitiis: sed etiam, quia ita sufficiens et gratus esse didicit, ut quasi totius mundi divitias computet illud prope nihil quod habet, sive hoc ipsum quod nihil habet. Ideo jure beatificat Ecclesiam sanctorum Salomon, quae pauper pro Christo, tam dives est in Christo. Multae, inquit, filiae congregaverunt divitias, tu supergressa es universas (Prov. XXXI, 29). Alii siquidem rapiunt non sua, et semper in egestate sunt: sancti dividunt propria, et ditiores fiunt. Divites enim eguerunt et esurierunt; inquirentes autem Dominum non minuentur omni bono (Psal. XXXIII, 11).

English translation

3. How great a glory, however, and how abundant a reward in heaven belongs to the summit of this perfection may in some measure be estimated from this: that the Lord proclaims even the very beginnings of those who renounce the world to be of such great blessedness that he says, "Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven." Truly blessed are they who, having cast off the cheap but heavy burdens of this world, refuse to become rich except from the Creator of the world alone, for his sake — as having nothing, and through him possessing all things. Do they not possess all things who possess him who contains and disposes all things, whose portion and inheritance is God himself; who, so that nothing may be lacking to those who fear him, dispenses the rest for their use as he knows to be fitting, and keeps himself for their enjoyment? When the heir of God and co-heir of Christ, having come of age and been emancipated, is brought into the full possession of the inheritance he has awaited, then indeed he will have absolute right and free dominion over creatures — he who, for as long as he is a little child, differs in nothing from a slave, though he is Lord of all, but is under guardians and stewards until the time appointed by the Father. Then the world will acknowledge him as its Lord, the just and lawful heir for whose sake it was created; it will acknowledge him, I say, because he is now no longer conformed to the world, but reformed in the newness of his mind to the image of God, to which he was made. But even now, too, to the faithful man the whole world is a world of riches (Prov. XVII, sec. LXX) (12)1; not only because he uses all worldly things as given to him for this purpose — to know and love the author of the world — and in the way of the testimonies of God, which he sees creation, like Scripture also, bearing witness to its Maker, he delights as in all riches; but also because he has learned to be so content and grateful that he reckons as the riches of the whole world that almost nothing which he has, or even this very fact — that he has nothing. Therefore Solomon rightly calls blessed the Church of the saints, which, poor for Christ's sake, is so rich in Christ. "Many daughters," he says, "have gathered together riches; you have surpassed them all" (Prov. XXXI, 29). For others indeed seize what is not their own, and are always in want; the saints divide what is their own, and become richer. For "the rich have wanted and suffered hunger; but they that seek the Lord shall not be deprived of any good thing" (Psal. XXXIII, 11).

4. Avarus quo plura possidet, pluribus eget. Quanquam nil minus habeat, quam quod habere se putat; cum habeatur, non habeat, servus pecuniae, mancipium avaritiae, cultorque crumenae, idololatra detestandus, cui nummus est deus. Pulchre autem jam nunc quoque vindicat in peccatores justitia divina, res ipsas quas amant convertens eis in tormenta, et vitia faciens esse supplicia. Pecunia enim quae justum amplius justificat, et verius ditat dispensata, vel semel data; avarum cruciat servata, prodigum polluit profligata. Prorsus beati pauperes Christi, quorum fides sic elusit sapientiam mundi, ut sola, quis sit optimus divitiarum usus, invenerit: quod videlicet divitiae, quae pauperem faciunt et miserum, si diliguntur; tunc demum faciunt divitem et beatum, si pro Christo contemnuntur. Confiteor tibi, Pater, Domine coeli et terrae, quia abscondisti haec a sapientibus et prudentibus, et revelasti ea parvulis (Matth. XI, 25), id est, humilibus; qui utique non sunt alii quam pauperes spiritu, quorum hic beatitudo praedicatur.

English translation

4. The miser, the more he possesses, the more he lacks. Yet nothing is less his than what he thinks he has; since he is possessed rather than possessing, he is the slave of money, the chattel of avarice, the worshiper of his purse, a detestable idolater, for whom a coin is god. And now too divine justice fittingly takes vengeance upon sinners, turning the very things they love into torments for them, and making their vices into their punishments. For money, which more fully justifies and more truly enriches the just man when it is distributed or given away, torments the miser when it is kept, and defiles the prodigal when it is squandered. Truly blessed are the poor of Christ, whose faith has so outwitted the wisdom of the world that it alone has discovered what is the best use of riches: namely, that riches, which make a man poor and wretched if they are loved, then at last make him rich and blessed if they are despised for Christ's sake. I confess to you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, that you have hidden these things from the wise and prudent, and have revealed them to little ones (Matth. XI, 25) — that is, to the humble, who are none other than the poor in spirit, whose blessedness is proclaimed here.

¶cols. 207-208; cols. 209-210
Latin original

5. Hoc enim licet scientes tamen commonere vos velim, fratres, quod vera ac beata paupertas spiritus, plus est in humilitate cordis, quam in angustia rei familiaris: plus consistit in abdicatione superbiae, quam in contemptu substantiae. Substantia nonnunquam utiliter habetur; superbia nunquam nisi damnabiliter retinetur. Diabolus nihil in mundo possidet, aut possidere desiderat, quem sola, vel maxime superbia damnat. Parum igitur prodest renuntiare saeculi possessionibus, nisi renuntietur et moribus: imo stultum ac ridiculum est nudari divitiis, et divitum implicari vitiis; pauperem fieri rebus, nec ditari virtutibus; relinquere omnia, nec sequi Christum, sed magis forsitan in castris Christi juvare partes Antichristi. Plane Antichristi partes adjuvat, quisquis superbiae militat, nomenque sanctum, quod verbis vel habitu profitetur, moribus impugnat. Vexillum Christi humilitas; Antichristi, superbia, vel potius capitis ejus diaboli, qui super omnes filios superbiae regnat, et ab initio per superbiam peccat. Gloriemur igitur, fratres, quia pauperes sumus pro Christo; sed operam demus ut humiles simus cum Christo. Paupere superbo sicut nihil detestabilius, ita nihil miserabilius; cum eum et paupertas nunc affligat, et superbia in perpetuum abdicat. Pauper autem humilis, etsi uritur et purgatur in camino paupertatis, exsultat refrigerio conscientiae divitis: consolatur se promissione sanctae spei, sciens et sentiens quoniam ipsius est regnum Dei; quod jam intra se ipsum velut in semine vel radice gerit, primitias scilicet spiritus, et pignus aeternae haereditatis. Annon vestrum est, fratres, unde toties sicut vobis conscii estis, fructus suavissimos et beata gaudia decerpitis, quorum gustus amarescere vobis jam omnem fecit dulcedinem mundi? Gustastis enim, ni fallor, et vidistis quoniam bona est negotiatio vestra, qui pro re gratis contemnenda ac projicienda, summa comparastis bona. Denique non est regnum Dei esca et potus, sed justitia, et pax, et gaudium in Spiritu sancto (Rom. xiv, 17). Si haec ergo sentimus in nobis, cur non confidenter pronuntiemus intra nos esse regnum Dei? Quod autem intra nos est, illud vere nostrum est, quia nobis invitis eripi non potest.

English translation

5. For though you know this, brothers, I would still like to remind you that true and blessed poverty of spirit consists more in humility of heart than in the straitness of one's household means; it consists more in the renunciation of pride than in the contempt of substance. Substance is sometimes held to good use; pride is never retained except to one's damnation. The devil possesses nothing in the world, nor desires to possess anything, and yet pride alone, or pride above all, condemns him. It profits little, then, to renounce the possessions of the world unless one also renounces its ways; indeed, it is foolish and ridiculous to be stripped of riches and yet entangled in the vices of the rich — to become poor in things but not enriched in virtues; to leave all things and yet not follow Christ, but rather, perhaps, while serving in the camp of Christ, to aid the cause of Antichrist. Clearly he aids the cause of Antichrist who serves in the army of pride, and who, while professing the holy name in words or in habit, fights against it by his conduct. The banner of Christ is humility; the banner of Antichrist is pride — or rather of his head, the devil, who reigns over all the sons of pride, and who from the beginning sins through pride. Let us glory, then, brothers, that we are poor for Christ's sake; but let us take care to be humble with Christ. As nothing is more detestable than a proud poor man, so nothing is more pitiable; for poverty afflicts him now, and pride casts him off forever. But the humble poor man, even though he is burned and purified in the furnace of poverty, exults in the refreshment of a rich conscience; he consoles himself with the promise of holy hope, knowing and feeling that the kingdom of God is his — which he already bears within himself as in a seed or a root, namely, the firstfruits of the Spirit, and the pledge of the eternal inheritance. Is it not yours, brothers, from which, as you yourselves are conscious, you so often pluck the sweetest fruits and blessed joys, the taste of which has already made all the sweetness of the world bitter to you? For you have tasted, unless I am mistaken, and have seen that your business dealing is good, you who for a thing to be freely despised and cast away have purchased the highest goods. For indeed the kingdom of God is not food and drink, but righteousness, and peace, and joy in the Holy Spirit (Rom. xiv, 17). If, then, we feel these things within ourselves, why should we not confidently declare that the kingdom of God is within us? For what is within us is truly ours, because it cannot be snatched away from us against our will.

6. Recte itaque Dominus beatitudinem praedicans pauperum, non ait, ipsorum erit, sed, est regnum coelorum: non solum propter jus firmissimum, sed etiam propter pignus certissimum usumque felicissimum; non solum quia paratum est eis ab origine mundi, sed etiam quia jam in quamdam ipsius possessionem coeperunt introduci, jam habentes thesaurum coelestem in vasis fictilibus, jam portantes Deum in corporibus suis et cordibus. Quam beata gens, cujus est Dominus Deus ejus! quam vicini sunt Dei regno qui regem ipsum, cui servire regnare est, jam possident, et gestant in corde suo! Funes, inquit, ceciderunt mihi in praeclaris; etenim haereditas mea praeclara est mihi. Litigent alii de dividenda haereditate hujus mundi: Dominus pars haereditatis meae et calicis mei (Psal. xv, 6, 5). Pugnent inter se quis eorum fiat miserior, nihil omnium quae ambiunt, illis invideo: ego enim et anima mea delectabimur in Domino. O praeclara haereditas pauperum! o beata possessio nihil habentium! quoniam non solum omnem sufficientiam nobis subministras, sed etiam ad omnem gloriam abundas, ad omnem laetitiam redundas, tanquam in sinu reposita supereffluens mensura. Prorsus tecum sunt divitiae et gloria, opes superbae et justitia.

English translation

6. Rightly, then, did the Lord, in proclaiming the blessedness of the poor, not say, 'theirs shall be,' but 'theirs is the kingdom of heaven' — not only because of the most firm right, but also because of the most certain pledge and the most blessed enjoyment; not only because it has been prepared for them from the foundation of the world, but also because they have already begun to be brought into a certain possession of it, already having the heavenly treasure in earthen vessels, already carrying God in their bodies and hearts. How blessed is the nation whose God is the Lord! How near to the kingdom of God are those who already possess the King himself — whom to serve is to reign — and bear him in their own heart! 'The lines,' he says, 'have fallen for me among goodly places; yes, my inheritance is goodly to me.' Let others quarrel over the dividing of the inheritance of this world: 'The Lord is the portion of my inheritance and of my cup' (Psal. xv, 6, 5). Let them fight among themselves over which of them shall become more wretched; I envy them nothing of all they covet, for I and my soul shall take delight in the Lord. O goodly inheritance of the poor! O blessed possession of those who have nothing! For you not only supply us with every sufficiency, but you also overflow for every glory, and run over for every gladness, like an overflowing measure laid up in the lap. Truly with you are riches and glory, glorious riches and righteousness.

7. Superbiat, o pauperes, superbiat, o humiles, anima vestra, glorians in humilitate sua, omnemque altitudinem hujus mundi longe sub pedibus suis jacentem despiciat; indignumque judicet gloriae suae, ad concupiscentiam vilis praedae suam ulterius majestatem inclinare. Quid? jam jam tollenda es in coelum, et nunc te demum mergeres in coenum? parata tibi sunt aeterna, et praeeligeres transitoria, somnoque similia? te praestolatur curia sanctorum, et praeponeres societatem daemoniorum? Quam miser homo, qui cum in honore esset, non intellexit, ac perinde comparatus est jumentis insipientibus, et similis factus est illis! (Psal. xlviii, 21.) Annon manifeste hoc illis contingit, quos cum beata paupertas fecisset honorabiles coelo, mirabiles mundo, et, ut nihil praeteritum sit, etiam formidabiles inferno: postea arbitrantes miseriam, caecato sensu, paupertatem, humilitatem, ignaviam; voluerunt divites fieri, et inciderunt in tentationes et laqueos diaboli; cumque essent domini universorum, vendiderunt se ipsos sine pretio propter ea quae nihil sunt. Vae his qui perdiderunt sustinentiam, et diverterunt in vias pravas! Et quid facient cum inspicere coeperit Dominus? Ipsi viderint quid facturi sint. Vos enim quibus amica est paupertas, et grata spiritus humilitas, securos fecit de possidendo regno coelorum incommutabilis Veritas: vestrum illud esse asserens, vobis illud repositum fideliter custodiens; si tamen et vos ipsi spem istam in sinu vestro repositam firmiter custodiatis usque in finem, cooperante Domino nostro Jesu Christo, cui est honor et gloria per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

7. Let your soul be proud, O poor; let it be proud, O humble, glorying in its own humility, and let it look down from far above upon all the loftiness of this world lying beneath its feet; let it judge it unworthy of its glory to bend its majesty any further toward the desire for vile prey. What? Are you now, even now, about to be lifted up into heaven, and would you at this very moment sink yourself into the mire? Eternal things are prepared for you, and would you choose instead transitory things, things like a passing dream? The court of the saints awaits you, and would you prefer the company of demons? How wretched is man, who, when he was in honor, did not understand, and so was compared to senseless beasts, and became like them! (Psal. xlviii, 21.) Does not this plainly happen to those whom blessed poverty had made honorable before heaven, admirable before the world, and—that nothing be left unsaid—even fearsome to hell: who afterward, their understanding blinded, judged poverty to be misery, and humility to be cowardice; who wished to become rich, and fell into the temptations and snares of the devil; and though they were lords of all things, sold themselves for no price for the sake of things that are nothing. Woe to those who have lost their endurance, and have turned aside into crooked ways! And what will they do when the Lord begins to examine them? Let them see for themselves what they are to do. But as for you, to whom poverty is a friend, and humility of spirit is dear, the unchangeable Truth has made you secure of possessing the kingdom of heaven: asserting that it is yours, faithfully keeping it stored up for you—if only you yourselves also firmly guard this hope stored up in your bosom even to the end, with the cooperation of our Lord Jesus Christ, to whom be honor and glory through all ages of ages. Amen.
