Introduction & Scripture references

Editorial introduction

Guerric of Igny presents the Annunciation as spiritual joy within Lenten observance: the angel's promise to Mary announces pardon, redemption and life to those humbled by repentance. He contrasts the fullness of salvation with human ingratitude, then contemplates Mary's royal ancestry and Christ's body through the images of Wisdom's house and Solomon's ivory throne. Mary's virginal chastity leads him to urge chastity joined to humility in his hearers. The fragrance of myrrh and incense becomes an image of bodily continence and prayerful devotion, returning at the close to the Word's descent into Mary's womb. The individual sermon is undated; its collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157 and is read here from the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The source names the Annunciation of the Lord.

English translation

On the joy of this day and the praises of the Virgin Mother.

¶cols. 115-116
Latin original

1. Opportune nobis in his diebus Quadragesimalis observantiae solemnitas intervenit Dominicae Annuntiationis, ut qui fatigantur afflictione corporali, gaudio recreari valeant spirituali; et quos humiliavit poenitentiae luctus, annuntiatio illius qui tollit peccata mundi, consoletur. Sic quippe scriptum habes: Moeror in corde viri humiliabit eum; et sermone bono laetificabitur (Prov. xii, 25). Prorsus sermo bonus, sermo fidelis et omni acceptione dignus, Evangelium nostrae salutis; quod angelus missus a Deo, hodierna die Mariae evangelizavit, ac laetum de Incarnatione Verbi, dies diei, angelus Virgini verbum eructavit. Sermo ille, dum Filium promittit Virgini, veniam pollicetur reis, redemptionem captivis, adapertionem clausis, vitamque sepultis. Sermo ille, dum Filii regnum praedicat, justorum quoque gloriam annuntiat; terret inferos, laetificat coelos; et, sicut cognitione mysteriorum, sic etiam novitate gaudiorum perfectionem auxisse videtur Angelorum. Quem ergo non laetificet ille sermo bonus in afflictione sua, quem non consoletur verbum illud in humilitate sua? Memor esto, inquit David, verbi tui servo tuo, in quo mihi spem dedisti; haec enim me consolata est in humilitate mea (Psal. cxviii, 49, 50). Verbum tantum promissionis acceperat, sed nullo adhuc indicio sese effectus prodebat. Affligebat eum dilatio voti, sed consolabatur certitudo spei ex fide promittentis.

English translation

1. The solemnity of the Lord's Annunciation comes at a fitting time for us in these days of Lenten observance, so that those wearied by bodily affliction may be refreshed by spiritual joy, and those whom the sorrow of repentance has humbled may be comforted by the announcement of him who takes away the sins of the world. For you find it written: Sorrow in a man's heart will humble him; and by a good word he will be gladdened (Prov. xii, 25). A good word indeed, a faithful word and worthy of all acceptance, is the Gospel of our salvation, which the angel sent by God proclaimed to Mary on this day; and, as day speaks to day, the angel poured forth to the Virgin a joyful word concerning the Incarnation of the Word. That word, in promising a Son to the Virgin, promises pardon to the guilty, redemption to captives, an opening to those shut in, and life to the buried. That word, in proclaiming the Son's kingdom, announces also the glory of the just; it terrifies the underworld and gladdens the heavens; and it seems to have increased the perfection of the Angels by new joys as well as by knowledge of mysteries. Whom, then, should that good word not gladden in his affliction? Whom should that word not comfort in his lowliness? Remember, says David, your word to your servant, in which you gave me hope; for this has comforted me in my lowliness (Psal. cxviii, 49, 50). He had received only a word of promise, and its fulfillment had not yet shown itself by any sign. The delay of his desire afflicted him, but the certainty of hope, arising from the faithfulness of the one who promised, comforted him.

2. Si ergo David sola spe salutis hujus, quae nobis servabatur, animum pascebat, quod gaudium quae deliciae, rei ipsius exhibitio nobis esse debuerat? O felicitas temporum istorum! o infelicitas temporum istorum! An non felicitas temporum in quibus tanta plenitudo gratiae et omnium bonorum? an non infelicitas temporum in quibus tanta ingratitudo redemptorum? Ecce enim venit plenitudo temporis, in quo mittit Deus Filium suum, ut fiat Filius hominis hominumque Salvator; et ecce magnitudo temporis, ut Salvatorem suum fastidiat homo peccator. Annuntiatur salus perditis, et contemnunt; promittitur vita desperatis, et negligunt; venit Deus ad homines, et non assurgunt. Assurgit, qui aliqua devotione se erigit ut det gloriam gratiae Dei; assurgit, qui saltem cum gaudio suscipit verbum propriae salutis. Scio autem, scio quis est qui laetificatur illo sermone bono. Ille procul dubio quem prius humiliavit pius moeror in corde suo: moeror de peregrinatione et exsilio, moeror de vinculis mortis et periculis inferni; qui moerens quotidie plangit, quod praeoccupaverint eum laquei mortis, et pericula inferni invenerint. Huic nimirum jucundus advenit hodiernus ille nuntius de coelo, hic gratulabundus excipit sermonem de Dei Filio; huic, inquam, lugenti et moerenti quod tantis sit malis praeventus et circumventus, non sine gaudio liberator annuntiatur; qui oleum gaudii daturus est pro luctu, pallium laudis pro spiritu moeroris; qui videlicet finem dabit miseriis, et sine fine beatitudinem dabit miseris. Beati ergo qui lugent, quoniam ipsi consolabuntur; beati duos humiliavit pius moeror in corde suo2, quoniam laetificabuntur sermone bono. Bonus plane sermo et consolatorius omnipotens Sermo tuus, Domine, qui hodie a regalibus sedibus venit in uterum Virginis, ubi etiam sedem sibi regalem exstruxit; in qua cum etiam nunc sedeat, quasi Rex circumstante exercitu Angelorum in coelis, est tamen moerentium consolator in terris.

English translation

2. If, then, David nourished his soul on the mere hope of this salvation, which was being kept for us, what joy, what delight should the manifestation of the thing itself have been to us! O happiness of these times! O unhappiness of these times! Are they not happy times in which there is such fullness of grace and of every good thing? Are they not unhappy times in which there is such ingratitude among the redeemed? For see, the fullness of time has come, in which God sends his Son to become the Son of man and the Savior of men; and see the enormity of the time, that sinful man should disdain his Savior. Salvation is announced to the lost, and they despise it; life is promised to the despairing, and they neglect it; God comes to men, and they do not rise to greet him. He rises who lifts himself up with some devotion to give glory to God's grace; he rises who at least receives with joy the word of his own salvation. But I know, I know who is gladdened by that good word. It is undoubtedly the man whom devout sorrow has first humbled in his heart: sorrow over his pilgrimage and exile, sorrow over the bonds of death and the perils of hell; the man who mourns and laments daily that the snares of death have overtaken him and the perils of hell have found him. To him that welcome messenger from heaven comes today; he welcomes with rejoicing the word concerning the Son of God. To him, I say, grieving and sorrowing that he has been overtaken and surrounded by such great evils, a deliverer is announced, bringing no little joy: one who will give the oil of gladness in place of mourning, a mantle of praise in place of a spirit of sorrow; one who will put an end to miseries and give the miserable blessedness without end. Blessed, then, are those who mourn, for they shall be comforted; blessed are those whom devout sorrow has humbled in their hearts2, for they shall be gladdened by a good word. Truly good and comforting is your almighty Word, Lord, who today came from the royal throne into the Virgin's womb, where he also built himself a royal seat. Although even now he sits upon it as King in heaven, surrounded by the host of Angels, he is nevertheless the comforter of the sorrowful on earth.

¶col. 117
Latin original

3. Regali siquidem ex progenie Virgo electa est, generositatis quidem regiae nobilis proles, sed virtutis regiae nobilior indoles: ut aeterno Regi, Filio Regis, materna quoque nobilitas regium honorem deferret, et venientem a regali sede Patris, regalis etiam thronus in aula virginali Reginae susciperet Matris. In ipsa quippe et ex ipsa, Sapientia aedificavit sibi domum ((Prov. ix, 1), in ipsa et ex ipsa paravit sibi thronum, cum in ea et ex ea corpus aptavit sibi ita ad omnia perfectum et congruum, ut et domus ei sit ad quiescendum, et thronus ad judicandum, quod primo tabernaculum ei fuit ad pugnandum, et cathedra ad docendum. Vide autem ne forte ipsa sit sedes David patris ejus (Luc. i, 70)3, quam ei dandam promittit angelus; non quod David in ea sederit, sed quod ex semine David assumpta et fabricata sit. Et certe, si sedes David non est, tamen sedes tua, Deus, in saeculum saeculi ipsa est. Si solium David non est, certe solium excelsum et elevatum super omnem creaturam ipsa est. Si quis autem etiam thronum illum grandem, quem rex Salomon sibi fecit de ebore, corpus ipsum interpretetur, quod Pacificus noster hodie sumpsit ex Virgine, non multum a vero videbitur abhorrere; quandoquidem et illud quod sequitur, et vestivit eum auro fulvo nimis (III Reg. x, 18)2, eidem satis corpori congruit; quod jam Dominus decorem induit fulgoris intolerabilis. Vestivit, inquit, eum auro fulvo. Prorsus, si interroges oculos Apostolorum qui transfiguratum in monte viderunt (Matth. xvii, 1, 2), confitebuntur aurum nimis esse fulvum, id est, fulgorem corporis esse nimium, quem ferre nequiverunt, quamvis nimis pro Valde ponere consuetudinis sit Scripturarum. Caetera namque quae prosequitur Scriptura de illius throni magnificentia (III Reg. x, 18-20), accommodatius nisi fallor, si quis tractare voluerit, accipientur de corpore Christi, quod est Ecclesia.

English translation

3. For the Virgin was chosen from royal stock, a noble offspring of royal birth, but nobler still in the character of her royal virtue, so that to the eternal King, the Son of the King, his mother's nobility too might render royal honor, and a royal throne in the virginal court of his Queen Mother might receive him as he came from the Father's royal seat. For in her and from her Wisdom built herself a house ((Prov. ix, 1); in her and from her he prepared himself a throne, when in her and from her he fitted himself with a body so perfect and suitable in every respect that it might be both a house for his rest and a throne for his judgment, having first been a tent for his warfare and a chair for his teaching. Consider, however, whether this may be the seat of David his father (Luc. i, 70)3, which the angel promises will be given to him: not because David sat upon it, but because it was taken and fashioned from David's seed. And certainly, if it is not David's seat, it is nevertheless your seat, O God, for ever and ever. If it is not David's throne, it is certainly a throne high and lifted up above every creature. But if anyone also interprets that great throne which King Solomon made for himself out of ivory as the very body which our Peacemaker took today from the Virgin, he will seem to be not far from the truth. For the words which follow, and he covered it with exceedingly bright gold (III Reg. x, 18)2, fit that body well, since the Lord has now clothed it in the beauty of unbearable radiance. He covered it, it says, with bright gold. Indeed, if you question the eyes of the Apostles who saw him transfigured on the mountain (Matth. xvii, 1, 2), they will confess that the gold is exceedingly bright, that is, that the body's radiance is excessive, which they could not endure; although Scripture customarily uses 'excessively' to mean 'very.' For the other things Scripture goes on to say about the magnificence of that throne (III Reg. x, 18-20) will, unless I am mistaken, be more fittingly understood of Christ's body which is the Church, should anyone wish to expound them.

Latin original

4. Mihi sane magis mirari modo libet illud ebur tam pretiosum, imo et impretiabile, virginalis castimoniae, de quo scilicet sedem sibi facere delegit, qui sedet super Cherubim: Haec, inquiens, requies mea in saeculum saeculi; hic sedebo, quoniam elegi eam (Psal. cxxxi, 14). Quam nitidum ebur illud quod oculis tanti tamque divitis Regis placuit, in cujus diebus argentum nullius est pretii! quam frigidum, quod nec a conceptu incaluit, quam solidum, quod nec partus violavit! quam candidum simul et rubicundum2, quod candor lucis aeternae ignisque Spiritus sancti, universa sui plenitudine replevit! Maria siquidem et ipsa candidior nive, rubicundior ebore antiquo; cui scilicet incomparabilem et castitas candorem, et charitas seu martyrium rutilantem prae antiquis electis noscitur contulisse ruborem. Nam et suam ipsius animam pertransivit gladius, ut mater summi Virginis et Martyris esset et virgo ipsa et martyr, candida et rubicunda, quemadmodum dilectus ejus candidus et rubicundus (Cantic. v, 10). Denique, sicut Salomon in omnibus thesauris suis et opibus tantis nihil habuit tam pretiosum, quod in opus illud, magnificum thronum scilicet gloriae suae judicaret ebori praeferendum, sic Maria singularem prae omnibus electis Angelorum et hominum invenit gratiam apud Deum, ut videlicet Dei conciperet et pareret Filium atque ex ebore corporis ejus thronum sibi virtus Altissimi sine manibus excideret gloriosum. Gloriosus prorsus ille thronus ac mirabilis, de quo Scriptura perhibet quia non est factum tale opus in universis regnis (III Reg. x, 20), quod facile testimoniis probaretur Angelorum, qui semper et insatiabiliter in gloriam et decorem Dominici corporis prospicere concupiscunt. Quia enim in universis regnis non est tale opus factum, quidquid factum est, flectat ei genu in omnibus regnis, coelestium, terrestrium, et infernorum. Quam beatus igitur ille venter eburneus, unde caro eburnea sumpta est Redemptoris, pretium animarum, miraculum Angelorum, solium summae majestatis, thronusque potestatis, cibus vitae immortalis, medicina peccati, restitutio sanitatis! Quotquot, inquit, tangebant eum, sanabantur a languoribus suis (Marc. vi, 56): nam virtus de illo exibat, et sanabat omnes (Luc. vi, 49)4.

English translation

4. For my part, I would rather marvel now at that precious, indeed priceless, ivory of virginal chastity, from which he who sits above the Cherubim chose to make himself a seat, saying: This is my resting place for ever and ever; here I will sit, for I have chosen it (Psal. cxxxi, 14). How lustrous is that ivory which pleased the eyes of so great and wealthy a King, in whose days silver is of no value! How cool, since even conception did not heat it; how firm, since even childbirth did not violate it! How white and ruddy at once2, filled with all the fullness of the brightness of eternal light and the fire of the Holy Spirit! For Mary herself is whiter than snow, ruddier than ancient ivory: chastity is known to have bestowed upon her an incomparable whiteness, and charity, or martyrdom, a glowing redness surpassing that of the elect of old. For a sword passed through her own soul too, so that the mother of the supreme Virgin and Martyr should herself be both virgin and martyr, white and ruddy, just as her beloved is white and ruddy (Cantic. v, 10). Finally, just as Solomon, among all his treasures and such great riches, had nothing so precious that he judged it preferable to ivory for that work, the magnificent throne of his glory, so Mary found singular grace before God above all the elect among Angels and men: namely, that she should conceive and bear the Son of God, and that the power of the Most High should carve for himself, without hands, a glorious throne from the ivory of her body. Glorious indeed and wonderful is that throne, of which Scripture declares that no such work was made in all the kingdoms (III Reg. x, 20). This could readily be proved by the testimony of the Angels, who always and insatiably long to look upon the glory and beauty of the Lord's body. For since no such work has been made in all the kingdoms, let everything that has been made bend the knee to him in all the kingdoms, in heaven, on earth and under the earth. How blessed, then, is that ivory womb, from which the Redeemer's ivory flesh was taken: the price of souls, the wonder of Angels, the seat of supreme majesty and throne of power, the food of immortal life, the medicine for sin, the restoration of health! As many as touched him, it says, were healed of their infirmities (Marc. vi, 56); for power went out from him and healed them all (Luc. vi, 49)4.

Latin original

5. Beatus igitur venter, qui te, Domine Jesu, portavit! felix castitas uteri virginalis, quae huic operi materiam ministravit! Felix prorsus, fratres mei, nitor illius eboris, id est, candor castitatis: cui nec aurum mundanae sapientiae, nec argentum eloquentiae, nec gemmam alicujus excellentis gratiae, nostri praefert electio Salomonis; si modo tamen castitas humilitate commendetur, quia humilitatem ancillae suae respexit Dominus (Id. i, 48)4. Proinde namque Salomon, cum in carmine amoris castitatem praedicaret virginis, seu sponsae, videlicet seu sponsi (quia quorum spiritus unus et caro una, in plerisque etiam laus una), ut castitatem illius adornatam nostramque adornandam etiam aliis virtutibus doceret, ait Venter ejus eburneus distinctus sapphiris (Cantic. v, 14). Ut autem noveris quia castitas aliarum virtutum seu gratiarum sit receptaculum, apothecae aromaticae seu unguentariae nostri Salomonis non nisi domus eburneae sunt, sicut David in illo epithalamio cecinit, quod inscriptio tituli, Canticum pro dilecto, de Sponso Ecclesiae accipiendum ostendit. Myrrha, inquit, et gutta, et casia2 a vestimentis tuis, a domibus, eburneis ex quibus delectaverunt te filiae regum in honore tuo (Psal. xliv, 9, 10). Corpora siquidem eburnea sanctorum, domus Christi sunt, vestimentum Christi sunt: membra Christi sunt; templum Spiritus sancti sunt. Ab his vestimentis Christi, ab his domibus eburneis spirat ei omnis fragrantia virtutum et charismatum, ex quibus delectant et honorant Regem regum filiae Apostolorum et Prophetarum. Probastis et ipsi, fratres mei, ex quo coepistis possidere unusquisque vas suum in sanctificatione et honore, non passione desiderii, sicut et gentes quae ignorant Deum (I Thess. iv, 4, 5), quid unguentorum, quid odoramentorum divina largitate sit infusum; quo modo corpus, quod erat sentina sordium, nunc Spiritus sancti sit venerabile sacrarium; et quod erat vorago flagitiorum, nunc quaedam sit apotheca gratiarum.

English translation

5. Blessed, then, is the womb that bore you, Lord Jesus! Happy the chastity of the virginal womb which supplied the material for this work! Happy indeed, my brothers, is the luster of that ivory, that is, the whiteness of chastity: our Solomon's choice prefers neither the gold of worldly wisdom nor the silver of eloquence nor the jewel of any excellent grace to it, provided, however, that chastity is commended by humility, for the Lord has looked upon the humility of his handmaid (Id. i, 48)4. Thus Solomon, when in the song of love he praised the chastity of the virgin, whether of the bride or of the bridegroom, since those who are one spirit and one flesh also share one praise in many things, wished to teach that the chastity of the one is adorned, and ours must be adorned, with other virtues too. He says: His belly is of ivory, set with sapphires (Cantic. v, 14). And so that you may know that chastity is a receptacle for other virtues or graces, our Solomon's storehouses of spices or ointments are nothing but houses of ivory. So David sang in that wedding song which the inscription of its title, A Song for the Beloved, showed was to be understood of the Bridegroom of the Church: Myrrh, he says, and stacte and cassia2 from your garments, from the ivory houses, from which the daughters of kings have delighted you in your honor (Psal. xliv, 9, 10). For the ivory bodies of the saints are Christ's houses, Christ's clothing, Christ's members and the temple of the Holy Spirit. From these garments of Christ, from these ivory houses, there breathes for him every fragrance of virtues and spiritual gifts, by which the daughters of Apostles and Prophets delight and honor the King of kings. You yourselves too have experienced, my brothers, since each of you began to possess his vessel in sanctification and honor, not in the passion of desire like the nations who do not know God (I Thess. iv, 4, 5), what ointments and perfumes have been poured in by divine bounty; how the body which was a cesspool of filth is now a venerable sanctuary of the Holy Spirit, and what was an abyss of shameful deeds is now a kind of storehouse of graces.

Latin original

6. Myrrha, inquit, et gutta, et casia2 a vestimentis tuis, a domibus eburneis. Inhabitamus quidem domos luteas, sed quae materiae qualitate sunt luteae, continentiae virtute fiunt eburneae; ex quibus si myrrha prima spirare coeperit per mortificationem voluptatum, consequentur et aliae species aromaticae, quae multiformem virtutum gratiam spirabunt. Spirabunt autem Christo delectabiliter, tanquam de vestimentis suis; spirabunt etiam longe lateque proximis, tanquam bonus odor Christi in omni loco. Horum vestimentorum Christi fragrantiam statim ut sensit Isaac: Ecce, inquit, odor filii mei sicut odor agri cui benedixit Dominus (Gen. xxvii, 27). Sed et Sponsus ad sponsam, quae etiam corpus et vestimentum ipsius est, loquitur dicens: Odor vestimentorum tuorum, sicut odor thuris (Cant. iv, 11). Quem nimirum odorem thuris Sponso gratissimum, pietas, o fratres, vestrorum spirabit cordium, si ita fragrans et devota fuerit ut dirigatur oratio vestra sicut incensum in conspectu Altissimi, et ascendat coram eo sicut virgula fumi ex aromatibus myrrhae et thuris (Id. iii, 6). Et quidem myrrha fragrat ei de corporibus vestris; fragrat utique et mihi: utinam ita et thus fragret de cordibus vestris, quatenus Sponsus probans vos sicut castos, sic etiam devotos, frequentius vos sua visitatione dignetur, et dicat: Ibo mihi ad montem myrrhae, et ad collem thuris (Id. iv, 6). Videtur tamen sermo iste, prophetia hujus esse diei, per Salomonem olim praedicta, per Jesum hodie impleta. Hodie namque venit in illum montem altissimum montium, montem non modo myrrhae et thuris, sed et omnium aromatum; dico autem Virginem virginum, omnium plenam gratiarum, inter quas tamen praecipue, nisi fallor, redolebat sponso myrrha castitatis, et thus pietatis. Odor, iste, fratres, mei, super omnia aromata; odor iste Dominum majestatis de excelsis attrahit et invitat, ut inclinet coelos et descendat, sicut probat dies haec, cum Altissimus, angelo misso de coelis, ipse quoque descendit in uterum Matris, qui semper manet in sinu Patris, cum quo vivit et regnat per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

6. Myrrh, it says, and stacte and cassia2 from your garments, from the ivory houses. We do indeed inhabit houses of clay, but those which are clay by the quality of their material become ivory through the virtue of continence. If myrrh first begins to breathe from them through the mortification of pleasures, other kinds of spices will follow, breathing forth the manifold grace of virtues. They will breathe pleasantly for Christ, as from his own garments; they will also breathe far and wide for their neighbors, as the good fragrance of Christ in every place. As soon as Isaac perceived the fragrance of these garments of Christ, he said: Behold, the fragrance of my son is like the fragrance of a field which the Lord has blessed (Gen. xxvii, 27). The Bridegroom too speaks to the bride, who is also his body and his clothing, saying: The fragrance of your garments is like the fragrance of incense (Cant. iv, 11). That fragrance of incense, so pleasing to the Bridegroom, will surely be breathed forth, brothers, by the devotion of your hearts, if it is so fragrant and devout that your prayer is directed like incense before the Most High, and rises before him like a little column of smoke from spices of myrrh and incense (Id. iii, 6). Myrrh does indeed breathe forth for him from your bodies; its fragrance reaches me too. Would that incense might likewise breathe forth from your hearts, so that the Bridegroom, finding you devout as well as chaste, may more often deign to visit you and say: I will go to the mountain of myrrh and to the hill of incense (Id. iv, 6). Yet this saying seems to be a prophecy of this day, foretold long ago through Solomon and fulfilled today through Jesus. For today he came to that mountain, the highest of mountains, a mountain not only of myrrh and incense but of every spice: I mean the Virgin of virgins, full of every grace, among which, unless I am mistaken, the myrrh of chastity and the incense of devotion were especially fragrant to the Bridegroom. This fragrance, my brothers, surpasses every spice; this fragrance draws and invites the Lord of majesty from the heights to bow the heavens and descend, as this day proves, when the Most High, having sent an angel from heaven, himself also descended into his Mother's womb, he who always remains in the Father's bosom, with whom he lives and reigns for ever and ever. Amen.
