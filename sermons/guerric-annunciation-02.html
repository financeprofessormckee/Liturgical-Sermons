Introduction & Scripture references

Editorial introduction

Guerric considers the Incarnation in Mary and the formation of Christ in the faithful soul. He connects Mary's hearing and faith with love of God and neighbor, humility, chastity and care for the life of grace. The sermon appears under the Annunciation heading in the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157; the individual sermon is undated.

English translation

On the incarnation of the Word in Mary and in the faithful soul.

¶cols. 119-120
Latin original

1. Hodie Verbum caro factum est, et habitare coepit in nobis, secundum regulam scilicet sanae fidei, quam ecclesiasticorum dogmatum definitio tradidit nobis (7)1. Firmissime namque tenet, et nullatenus dubitat Ecclesia, carnem Christi non prius conceptam quam a Verbo susceptam; sed ipsum Verbum Dei carnis acceptione conceptum, carnemque Verbi incarnatione conceptam. Hodie itaque Sapientia aedificare coepit sibi domum corporis nostri in utero Virginis, et ad aedificandam unitatem Ecclesiae, angularem lapidem de monte sine manibus abscidit; dum sine opere humano de corpore virginali carnem sibi nostrae redemptionis separavit. Ab hoc ergo die Dominus virtutum nobiscum, susceptor noster Deus Jacob, quia hodie Domini est assumptio nostra, ut inhabitet gloria in terra nostra (Psal. LXXXIV, 10). Prorsus hodie benedixisti, Domine, terram tuam, illam benedictam in mulieribus. Hodie dedisti benignitatem Spiritus sancti, ut terra nostra daret benedictum fructum ventris sui, et rorantibus coelis desuper uterus virginalis Salvatorem germinaret. Maledicta terra in opere praevaricatoris, quae etiam exercitata spinas et tribulos germinat haeredibus maledictionis (Gen. III, 17, 18). At nunc benedicta terra in opere Redemptoris, quae remissionem peccatorum fructumque vitae parturit universis, et filiis Adae praejudicium originalis dissolvit maledicti. Prorsus benedicta illa terra, quae omnino intacta, nec fossa, nec seminata, de solo rore coeli Salvatorem germinat, et mortalibus panem Angelorum, alimoniam vitae aeternae ministrat. Haec itaque terra, quia inculta erat, videbatur esse deserta, sed erat opimo fructu referta; videbatur esse eremus solitudinis, sed erat paradisus beatitudinis. Plane hortus deliciarum Dei, eremus cujus campi germinaverunt germen odoris, plane desertum refertum, de quo Agnum dominatorem terrae Pater emisit. Emitte, inquit, Agnum, Domine, de petra deserti (Isai. XVI, 1), id est, abscinde Petram de Petra, Sanctum et inviolabilem sancta et inviolata proferat virginitas. Sane in hoc ipso satis decenter Christi occasus ortui, sepultura respondet conceptioni, dum videlicet emittitur Agnus de petra deserti, condendus in petra monumenti; et cujus corpori monumentum excidendum erat in petra, ipse ab initio conceptus sui, et corpus sibi excidit de petra, et corpori locum in petra; sic integritatem petrae, cum de ea emitteretur, non imminuens, sicut nec signatam sepulturae petram, cum de ea egrederetur, aperiens.

English translation

1. Today the Word was made flesh, and began to dwell in us, according, that is, to the rule of sound faith which the definition of the Church's teachings has handed down to us (7)1. For the Church holds most firmly, and in no way doubts, that the flesh of Christ was not conceived before it was taken up by the Word; but that the Word of God itself was conceived by the taking of flesh, and the flesh was conceived by the incarnation of the Word. Today, then, Wisdom began to build for herself the house of our body in the womb of the Virgin, and in order to build the unity of the Church, cut from the mountain the corner-stone without hands; while, without human labor, from the virginal body he separated for himself the flesh of our redemption. From this day, therefore, the Lord of hosts is with us, our protector, the God of Jacob, because today the assumption of our nature is the Lord's, so that glory may dwell in our land (Psal. LXXXIV, 10). Today, indeed, O Lord, you have blessed your land, that land blessed among women. Today you gave the kindness of the Holy Spirit, that our land might give forth the blessed fruit of its womb, and that, with the heavens raining down dew from above, the virginal womb might bring forth the Savior. Cursed was the earth in the deed of the transgressor, which even when tilled brings forth thorns and thistles for the heirs of the curse (Gen. III, 17, 18). But now the earth is blessed in the work of the Redeemer, which brings forth for all the remission of sins and the fruit of life, and dissolves for the sons of Adam the sentence of the original curse. Truly blessed is that land which, wholly untouched, neither dug nor sown, from the dew of heaven alone brings forth the Savior, and ministers to mortals the bread of angels, the nourishment of eternal life. This land, then, because it was untilled, seemed to be a desert, but it was filled with rich fruit; it seemed to be a wilderness of solitude, but it was a paradise of blessedness. Truly a garden of God's delights, a wilderness whose fields brought forth the bud of fragrance, truly a desert filled full, from which the Father sent forth the Lamb, ruler of the earth. "Send forth," he says, "O Lord, the Lamb from the rock of the desert" (Isai. XVI, 1), that is, cut off the Rock from the Rock; let holy and inviolate virginity bring forth the Holy and Inviolable One. Indeed, in this very thing it is quite fitting that Christ's setting should correspond to his rising, his burial to his conception, since the Lamb is sent forth from the rock of the desert, to be laid in the rock of the tomb; and he whose body was to have a tomb hewn for it in rock, himself from the beginning of his conception both hewed for himself a body from a rock, and a place for his body in a rock; thus not diminishing the integrity of the rock when he was sent forth from it, just as he did not open the sealed rock of the tomb when he came forth from it.

2. Si ergo petra Christus (I Cor. X, 4), ut ait Apostolus, non degenerat a Matre Filius, quando et ipsa petrae nomine censetur. An non recte vocatur petra, quae et in amorem integritatis proposito firma, affectu solida, sensu quoque ipso adversus illecebram peccati tota insensibilis erat et lapidea? an non recte petra virginalis integritas, quae et nihil parit per naturam sui; et cum parit, roris virtute divini, nec admittens conceptum, nec emittens partum, novit aperiri? Sed aperietur terra, inquit Isaias, aperiatur, et germinet Salvatorem (Isai. XLV, 8). O sancte Isaia, qui dicis aperiatur, quid ergo est quod Dominus ad Ezechielem testatur: Porta haec clausa erit, et non aperietur (Ezech. XLIV, 2)? An tibi reseratum fuit tantummodo consilium fecundandae Matris, et clausum tibi fuit mysterium clausae perenniter integritatis? Absit! inquit; nemo magis quam ego conscius hujus secreti. Quomodo enim me latuisset perpetuae virginitatis mysterium, qui tanto ante praedixi: Ecce virgo concipiet, et pariet filium (Isai. VII, 14)? Quaeris igitur quomodo dicamus, ego, aperiatur; ille, non aperietur? Non aperietur viro, aperietur Domino; sicut ibidem ad ipsum dicitur Ezechielem: Vir non transibit per eam, quoniam Dominus Deus Israel ingressus est per eam. Aperietur autem Domino non virginalis integritas corporis, quia, sicut Ezechiel post praedicta subdidit: Haec clausa erit ipsi etiam principi (Ezech. XLIV, 2, 3), sed aperietur auris et porta cordis; quia videlicet intravit per aurem Virginis Verbum incarnandum, et exivit per clausam portam corporis incarnatum. Omnipotens namque Verbum Dei, etsi infirmitatem nostram suscepit, nihil tamen de sua sibi omnipotentia minuit, quominus contra naturam carnis, et supra intelligentiam sensus nostri, corpus suum palpabile per clausa posset et integra trajicere, sicut etiam [ad]2 discipulos ingrediens januis clausis, oculis probavit, quod ratio non capit.

English translation

2. If then Christ is the Rock (I Cor. X, 4), as the Apostle says, the Son does not degenerate from his Mother, since she too is reckoned by the name of rock. Is she not rightly called rock, who was in the resolve of love for integrity firm, solid in affection, and even in sense itself wholly insensible and stony against the enticement of sin? Is not virginal integrity rightly called rock, which brings forth nothing by its own nature and, when it brings forth by the power of the divine dew, knows no opening either to receive conception or to bring forth the child? But Isaiah says, "The earth shall be opened; let it be opened and bud forth the Savior" (Isai. XLV, 8). O holy Isaiah, you who say "let it be opened," what then is it that the Lord testifies to Ezekiel: "This gate shall be shut, and shall not be opened" (Ezech. XLIV, 2)? Was the plan for making the Mother fruitful revealed to you alone, while the mystery of her perpetually closed integrity was hidden from you? "Far from it!" he says; "no one is more conscious of this secret than I. For how could the mystery of perpetual virginity have escaped me, who so long before foretold: 'Behold, a virgin shall conceive and bear a son' (Isai. VII, 14)?" You ask, then, how we can say, I, "let it be opened"; he, "it shall not be opened"? It shall not be opened to man, it shall be opened to the Lord; just as it is said there to Ezekiel himself: "No man shall pass through it, because the Lord God of Israel has entered through it." But it is not the virginal integrity of the body that is opened to the Lord, because, as Ezekiel adds after the foregoing: "This shall be shut even to the prince himself" (Ezech. XLIV, 2, 3); rather, it is the ear and the gate of the heart that are opened; because, namely, the Word to be incarnated entered through the ear of the Virgin, and came forth incarnate through the closed gate of the body. For the almighty Word of God, although he took up our weakness, nevertheless diminished nothing of his omnipotence, so that, contrary to the nature of flesh and beyond the understanding of our sense, he could pass his palpable body through closed and intact things, just as also, entering [to]2 the disciples through closed doors, he proved by sight what reason cannot grasp.

3. Secura igitur, o Virgo intemerata, o porta sanctuarii perenniter clausa, secura aperi Domino Deo Israel, qui tibi jam dudum clamat: Aperi mihi, soror mea, amica mea (Cantic. V, 2). Non est quod timeas integritati tuae: Deus non novit integra violare, sed violata consolidare. Si Verbo Dei apta es, tum demum non modo clausa, sed et signata es. Pone me, inquit, ut signaculum super cor tuum, ut signaculum super brachium tuum (Id. VIII, 6). Jesus quippe impressus in corde, expressus in opere, sigillum plane et munimentum est inviolabile castitatis sponsae suae, et eo ipso quo se imprimit imitationis formam, imponit etiam incorruptionis custodiam. Sit ergo, o Virgo fidelis, auris tua aperta ad audiendum et mens tua ad credendum: aure percipe verbum angeli, et corde suscipe Verbum Altissimi, et corpore concipe Filium Dei. Dic et tu, o beata, tam humilis quam fidelis, dic Dominus aperuit mihi aurem; ego autem non contradico, retrorsum non abii (Isai. L, 5). Ecce ancilla Domini: praesto sum ejus voluntati; quinimo juvabo votis si possum, fiat mihi secundum verbum tuum (Luc. I, 38). Hoc dicere, sic suam devotionem offerre, istud plane est Domino pectus aperire, istud est etiam os aperire, et spiritum attrahere. Sic profecto aperiebatur terra, ut, quem rorabant coeli desuper, susciperet rorem, et germinaret Salvatorem. Nobile germen, germen justum, germen odoris, germen Domini, quod jam est in magnificentia et gloria, cum idem fructus terrae sub imis est super omnia, id est, exaltatus est super coelos Deus, et super omnem terram gloria ejus. Nihil enim interest sive germen, sive fructum aut florem dixeris, cum haec omnia, imo infinita alia unus Christus sit, res una, sed multiformis gratia, et virtutis unius operatio multifaria; cui si infinita nomina paupertas humani sensus et sermonis pro similitudine commodaverit, rei tamen implere significationem non poterit. Bene autem simul vocatur germen, et flos, et fructus, qui sine gradu profectus, ab initio conceptus sui omni virtute et gratia in se ipso exstitit perfectus. In nobis enim primum ipse germen est, cum fides in confessionem prorumpit vel opus manifestum: postmodum flos, cum super proficientem efflorescit sanctificatio Dei quodam spectabili decore virtutum; demum autem fructus, cum beatitudo satiat hominem consummatum. Pulcherrime sane ac vigilantissime non solum mysteria, sed et mysteriorum praesagia dispensatio praeordinavit divina, ut locus in quo terra germinavit Salvatorem germen justum, et flos de virga et radice Jesse ascendit, Nazareth, id est sanctitas, germen, flos, virgultum vocaretur; ut videlicet negotium loco, locusque negotio consona voce contestaretur, pariterque et nomen loci rem gerendam pronuntiaret, et res gesta causam nominis indicaret.

English translation

3. Therefore, without fear, O undefiled Virgin, O gate of the sanctuary closed forever, without fear open to the Lord God of Israel, who has long been crying out to you: 'Open to me, my sister, my love' (Cantic. V, 2). You have no reason to fear for your integrity: God does not know how to violate what is whole, but to make whole what is violated. If you are fitted to the Word of God, then you are not only closed, but also sealed. 'Set me,' he says, 'as a seal upon your heart, as a seal upon your arm' (Id. VIII, 6). For Jesus, impressed upon the heart, expressed in deed, is plainly the seal and the inviolable safeguard of the chastity of his bride; and by the very fact that he impresses himself as a pattern for imitation, he also imposes upon her the guardianship of incorruption. Let your ear, then, O faithful Virgin, be open to hear, and your mind to believe: with your ear perceive the word of the angel, with your heart receive the Word of the Most High, and in your body conceive the Son of God. Say, too, O blessed one, as humble as you are faithful, say: 'The Lord has opened my ear; but I do not contradict, I have not drawn back' (Isai. L, 5). 'Behold the handmaid of the Lord: I am ready for his will; indeed I will even assist his purposes if I can — be it done to me according to your word' (Luc. I, 38). To say this, to offer one's devotion in this way, is plainly to open one's breast to the Lord; it is also to open one's mouth and draw in the Spirit. Thus indeed was the earth opened, so that it might receive as dew him whom the heavens rained down from above, and bring forth the Savior as its sprout. A noble sprout, a righteous sprout, a sprout of fragrance, the sprout of the Lord, who is now in magnificence and glory, since that same fruit of the earth, though from the lowest depths, is above all things — that is, God has been exalted above the heavens, and his glory above all the earth. For it makes no difference whether you call him sprout, or fruit, or flower, since all these things — indeed, countless others besides — are one Christ, one reality, but grace of many forms, and the working of one power in many ways; and to him, even if the poverty of human understanding and speech lends countless names by way of likeness, it can never fulfill the full meaning of the reality. Rightly, however, is he called at once sprout, and flower, and fruit, who, without any gradual advance, existed perfect in himself in every virtue and grace from the very beginning of his conception. For in us, he is first a sprout, when faith breaks forth into confession or into a manifest work; afterward a flower, when the sanctification of God blossoms upon the one who is advancing, with a certain visible beauty of virtues; and finally a fruit, when beatitude satisfies the person brought to completion. Most beautifully indeed, and most watchfully, divine providence foreordained not only the mysteries themselves but also the foreshadowings of the mysteries, so that the place in which the earth brought forth the Savior, the righteous sprout, and where the flower arose from the rod and root of Jesse, should be called Nazareth — that is, 'holiness,' 'sprout,' 'flower,' 'shoot' — so that the event might bear witness to the place, and the place to the event, with a single harmonious voice, and that likewise the name of the place might proclaim the deed to be accomplished, and the deed accomplished might reveal the reason for the name.

4. Ignoscite mihi, fratres mei, quia, qui morum vestrorum servire debueram instructioni, plus forsitan quam necesse erat demoratus sum in illius ineffabilis admiratione et praeconio mysterii. Quid enim mirum si miror quod stupent Angeli, si praedicare gestio, quam coeli enarrant, gloriam Dei? quid mirum si me delectat quod beatos spiritus laetificat, quod peccatores justificat, justosque glorificat? Nescio tamen si ulla esse possit efficacior ac suavior morum aedificatio, quam hujus mysterii, id est Verbi incarnati, fidelis et pia consideratio. Quid enim sic hominem excitare potest ad amorem Dei, quam praeveniens hominem amor Dei tam vehemens ad hominem, ut homo propter hominem velit fieri? Quid autem ita nutrit amorem proximi, quam similitudo et natura proximi in humanitate Dei? Nam humilitatis exemplum nec cogitari posse majus aliquod existimo, quam exinanitionem Dei in formam servi, et servitutem plus quam servi. Castitatem porro quid aeque commendat, sicut quod ipsa castitas Salvatorem germinat? Aut fidei virtutem ac meritum quid evidentius ostendit, quam quod Virgo Deum fide concepit, fide omnia sibi promissa divinitus impleri promeruerit? Beata, inquit, quae credidit, quoniam perficientur quae dicta sunt ei a Domino (ibid. 45). Et ut plenius noveris conceptum Virginis non solum esse mysticum, sed et moralem; quod sacramentum est ad redemptionem, exemplum quoque tibi est ad imitationem; ut manifeste evacues in te gratiam sacramenti, si non imiteris virtutem exempli. Quae enim Deum fide concepit, si fidem habeas, idem tibi promittit, quod videlicet si verbum ex ore nuntii coelestis fideliter velis suscipere, Deum quem totus orbis non potest capere, possis et ipse concipere. Concipere autem corde, imo et corpore, licet non corporali opere aut specie, tamen plane corpore tuo; quandoquidem jubemur ab Apostolo glorificare et portare Deum in corpore nostro (I Cor. VI, 20). Attende itaque, ut scriptum est, diligenter auditui tuo, nam fides ex auditu, auditus autem per Verbum Dei (Rom. X, 17); quod tibi absque dubio evangelizat angelus Dei, cum de timore aut amore ejus tecum agit praedicator fidelis, quem angelum Domini exercituum dici et esse non est tibi fas ambigere (Malach. II, 16, 173). Quam beati qui dicere possunt: A timore tuo, Domine, concepimus et parturivimus spiritum salutis (Isa. XXVI, 17, 18)! qui nimirum non est alius quam spiritus Salvatoris, quam veritas Jesu Christi. Vide ineffabilem dignationem Dei, simulque virtutem incomprehensibilis mysterii: qui creavit te, creatur in te; et, quasi parum esset te ipsum habere patrem, vult etiam te sibi fieri matrem. Quicumque, inquit, fecerit voluntatem patris mei, ipse meus et frater, et soror, et mater est (Matth. XII, 50). O fidelis anima, expande sinus, dilata affectus; ne angustieris in visceribus tuis, concipe quem creatura non capit. Aperi Verbo Dei aurem audiendi: haec est ad uterum cordis via spiritus concipiendi, hac ratione compinguntur ossa Christi, id est, virtutes in ventre praegnantis.

English translation

4. Forgive me, my brothers, because I, who ought to have served the instruction of your morals, have perhaps lingered longer than was necessary in admiration and praise of that ineffable mystery. For what wonder is it if I marvel at what makes the Angels stand amazed, if I am eager to proclaim the glory of God which the heavens declare? What wonder is it if I am delighted by what gladdens the blessed spirits, what justifies sinners, and glorifies the just? Yet I do not know whether there can be any more effective or sweeter edification of morals than the faithful and pious consideration of this mystery, that is, of the Word incarnate. For what can so arouse man to the love of God as the love of God going before man, so vehement toward man that he willed to become man for man's sake? And what so nourishes love of neighbor as the likeness and nature of a neighbor found in the humanity of God? For I judge that no greater example of humility can even be conceived than the emptying of God into the form of a servant, and a servitude greater than that of a servant. Furthermore, what commends chastity so much as the fact that chastity itself brings forth the Savior as its offspring? Or what shows the power and merit of faith more clearly than that the Virgin conceived God by faith, and by faith merited that all things divinely promised to her should be fulfilled? Blessed, he says, is she who believed, for the things that were spoken to her by the Lord shall be fulfilled (ibid. 45). And that you may know more fully that the Virgin's conception is not only mystical but also moral — what is a sacrament for redemption is also an example for you to imitate — so that you plainly render void in yourself the grace of the sacrament, if you fail to imitate the virtue of the example. For she who conceived God by faith promises you the same thing, if you have faith: namely, that if you are willing to receive faithfully the word from the mouth of the heavenly messenger, you too can conceive the God whom the whole world cannot contain. And to conceive — in heart, indeed also in body, though not by bodily act or bodily form, yet truly in your body — since we are commanded by the Apostle to glorify and to carry God in our body (I Cor. VI, 20). Attend, therefore, as it is written, diligently to your hearing, for faith comes from hearing, and hearing through the word of God (Rom. X, 17); which without doubt the angel of God announces to you as gospel, when a faithful preacher deals with you concerning fear or love of him — and you may not rightly doubt that he is rightly called, and truly is, the angel of the Lord of hosts (Malach. II, 16, 173). How blessed are they who can say: From fear of you, O Lord, we have conceived and brought forth the spirit of salvation (Isa. XXVI, 17, 18)! — which indeed is none other than the spirit of the Savior, the truth of Jesus Christ. Behold the ineffable condescension of God, and at the same time the power of this incomprehensible mystery: he who created you is created in you; and, as though it were not enough for him to have you yourself as a father, he wills also that you become a mother to him. Whoever, he says, does the will of my Father, he is my brother, and sister, and mother (Matth. XII, 50). O faithful soul, open wide your bosom, enlarge your affections; do not be straitened in your inmost being — conceive him whom no creature can contain. Open to the Word of God the ear of hearing: this is the way to the womb of the heart for conceiving the spirit; by this means the bones of Christ — that is, the virtues — are knit together in the womb of her who is pregnant.

Latin original

5. Gratias tibi, sancte Spiritus, qui ubi vis spiras. Video munere tuo non unam, sed innumeras fidelium animas illo generoso germine gravidas; custodi opera tua, ne aliqua illarum abortiat, conceptumque divinae sobolis informem aut mortuum excutiat. Vos quoque, o matres beatae tam gloriosae prolis, attendite vobis ipsis, donec formetur Christus in vobis (Galat. IV, 19); attendite, ne qua foris offensa gravior fetum tenerum laedat, ne quid ingeratis ventri, id est menti, quod spiritum quem concepistis exstinguat. Parcite, si non vobis, certe Filio Dei in vobis; parcite, inquam, non solum ab operibus et sermonibus malis, sed etiam a cogitationibus noxiis et delectationibus mortiferis, quae plane suffocant semen Dei. Omni itaque custodia servate corda vestra, quia ex ipsis vita procedet, cum scilicet maturus se partus absolvet, et vita Christi, quae nunc abscondita est in cordibus vestris, manifestabitur in carne vestra mortali. Concepistis spiritum salutis, sed adhuc parturitis, nondum peperistis. Si labor in parturiendo, magna de spe partus consolatio. Mulier cum parit, tristitiam habet (Joan. XVI, 21) laboris; cum autem pepererit puerum, jam non memor erit pressurae propter gaudium, quia natus erit homo Christus in mundum exteriorem corporis nostri, quod et minor mundus solet appellari. Qui enim nunc conceptus est Deus in spiritibus nostris, configurans eos spiritui charitatis suae, tunc velut homo nascetur in corporibus, configurans ea corpori claritatis suae, in qua vivit et gloriatur Deus per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

5. Thanks be to you, Holy Spirit, who breathe where you will. I see, by your gift, not one but countless souls of the faithful made heavy with that noble offspring; guard your works, lest any of them miscarry, and cast out the conceived offspring of divine progeny unformed or dead. You too, O blessed mothers of so glorious a child, attend to yourselves, until Christ be formed in you (Galat. IV, 19); take heed lest some graver offense from without injure the tender offspring, lest you introduce into the womb — that is, into the mind — anything that might extinguish the spirit you have conceived. Spare — if not yourselves, then certainly the Son of God within you; spare, I say, not only from evil deeds and words, but also from harmful thoughts and deadly pleasures, which utterly suffocate the seed of God. Keep your hearts, therefore, with all vigilance, for from them life shall proceed, when, that is, the mature birth is brought to completion, and the life of Christ, which is now hidden in your hearts, shall be made manifest in your mortal flesh. You have conceived the spirit of salvation, but you are still in labor; you have not yet given birth. If there is labor in giving birth, there is great consolation in the hope of the birth. A woman, when she gives birth, has sorrow (Joan. XVI, 21) of her labor; but when she has borne the child, she no longer remembers the anguish, for joy, because the man Christ shall have been born into the outer world of our body, which is also commonly called the lesser world. For he who is now conceived as God in our spirits, conforming them to the spirit of his charity, shall then, as it were, be born as man in our bodies, conforming them to the body of his glory, in which God lives and is glorified through all ages of ages. Amen.
