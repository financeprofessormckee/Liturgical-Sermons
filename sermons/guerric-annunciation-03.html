Introduction & Scripture references

Editorial introduction

Guerric reads Isaiah's sign of the Virgin through the Incarnation, Passion and exaltation of Christ. His argument includes polemic against Jewish interpretations of the prophecy. He turns from Christ's hidden life in the womb to monastic silence, nourishment by the divine Word and patience within the Church. The sermon appears under the Annunciation heading in the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157; the individual sermon is undated.

SERMON III.

Latin original

De hujus mysterii signis.

English translation

On the signs of this mystery.

¶cols. 123-124
Latin original

1. Audite, domus David: ecce dabit Dominus ipse vobis signum. Ecce virgo concipiet (Isai. VII, 13, 14). Hodie impleta est haec prophetia in auribus vestris. Hodie namque illud ineffabile conceptus virginalis miraculum, sicut audieratis promissum, sic audistis impletum: Virgo hodie concepit. Et hoc signum, prioribus retro saeculis inauditum, nostris saeculis Dominus dedit. Et sicut Jeremias eodem spiritu et eodem sensu praedixit: Creabit Dominus novum super terram, quia femina circumdabit virum (Jerem. XXXI, 22). Quid est enim, creabit Dominus novum super terram, femina circumdabit virum, quod ait Jeremias, nisi quod ait Isaias: Dabit Dominus signum, Virgo concipiet filium (Isai. VII, 13, 14)? Hoc nempe est, circumdabit. Non de consortio virili suscipiet, sed de se sola intra semetipsam concipiet, et solius materni corporis involucro vestiet. Alioquin dicant, si possunt Judaei, quod signum Dominus in hoc dedit, si non virgo, sed adolescentula, ut ipsi falso interpretantur, concepit; aut quid novum Dominus creavit, si femina masculum de masculo susceptum circumdedit. Potest quidem mentiri iniquitas sibi; sed in multitudine virtutis tuae, o Domine, mentientur tibi inimici tui, ut virtutem tuam dum negat pravitas et paucitas Judaeorum, gloriosius et copiosius confiteatur fides omnium populorum. Confiteantur tibi populi, Deus, confiteantur tibi populi omnes, quia terra dedit fructum suum, Virgo germinavit Jesum (Psal. LXVI, 61). Velint, nolint Judaei, creavit Dominus novitatem hujus miraculi, in signum eorum incredulitatis; utique in signum cui contradicunt usque hodie, plus, nisi fallor, pertinacia odibili, quam ignorantia miserabili.

English translation

1. Hear, O house of David: behold, the Lord himself will give you a sign. Behold, a virgin shall conceive (Isai. VII, 13, 14). Today this prophecy has been fulfilled in your hearing. For today that ineffable miracle of virginal conception, just as you had heard it promised, so you have heard it fulfilled: today a Virgin has conceived. And this sign, unheard of in former ages, the Lord has given to our age. And just as Jeremiah foretold in the same spirit and with the same meaning: The Lord will create something new upon the earth, for a woman shall encompass a man (Jerem. XXXI, 22). For what is it that Jeremiah says, "The Lord will create something new upon the earth, a woman shall encompass a man," except what Isaiah says: "The Lord will give a sign, a Virgin shall conceive a son" (Isai. VII, 13, 14)? This indeed is what "encompass" means: she will not conceive through union with a man, but will conceive within herself alone, from herself alone, and will clothe him solely in the wrapping of a maternal body. Otherwise, let the Jews say, if they can, what sign the Lord gave in this, if it was not a virgin but a young woman who conceived, as they falsely interpret it; or what new thing the Lord created, if a woman merely enclosed within herself a male conceived from a male. Iniquity can indeed lie to itself; but in the multitude of your power, O Lord, your enemies will lie to you, so that while the wickedness and the small number of the Jews deny your power, the faith of all peoples may confess it more gloriously and abundantly. Let the peoples confess to you, O God, let all the peoples confess to you, for the earth has given its fruit, the Virgin has brought forth Jesus (Psal. LXVI, 61). Whether the Jews wish it or not, the Lord has created the novelty of this miracle as a sign of their unbelief; indeed as a sign which they contradict even to this day, more, unless I am mistaken, out of hateful obstinacy than out of pitiable ignorance.

2. Non alienum tamen ab origine et consuetudine viperei germinis, si modo contradicunt huic signo, postquam factum est, qui ab initio in patre et rege suo impiissimo Achaz contradixerunt ne fieret. Locutus est enim Dominus ad Achaz, dicens: Pete tibi signum. Non petam, inquit, et non tentabo Dominum (Isai. VII, 10-12). O religio profana! o pietas exsecranda! o humilitas dolosa! Ne tentes, ut ais, Dominum, contemnis Dominum. Quomodo enim tentares, si fideliter obedires? Nunc autem quomodo non graviter tentas, quem manifesto contemptu irritas? Novimus utique, novimus dolum et invidiam Judaicae radicis, quae et antequam Christus nasceretur, invidere coepit gloriae illius. Nam et iste Achaz, quantum ex vita et moribus ipsius intelligi datur (nam cultor idolorum erat), non alia religione aut metu signum recusavit petere cum juberetur, nisi ideo scilicet ne Dominus glorificaretur. Sane mira et plane digna ira Dei et hominum, tam dira perversitas Judaeorum, qui, cum jubentur signa quaerere, renuunt, non, ut fingunt, ne tentent, sed ne glorificent Dominum (Matth. XVI, 4); cum non jubentur, tentant et quaerunt. Nam velut natura et usu Judaei signa quaerunt, et, si dantur, calumniantur et inficiari contendunt, ut manifeste confiteantur quia tentantes quaerebant, non ut crederent facienti, sed ut insultarent non facienti. O gens peccatrix; semen nequam, filii scelerati (Isai. I, 4)! ait Isaias, Numquid parum est vobis molestos esse hominibus, mihi et aliis prophetis, imo universo generi hominum, quia molesti estis et Deo meo? Propter hoc dabit Dominus ipse vobis signum (Isa. VII, 13, 14). Quia enim vos in perversum adversum eum inceditis, et ipse, ut ait, perversus incedet vobis. Non vultis signum dari, ne glorificetur auctor miraculi; et ipse propter hoc dabit, ut ipse glorificetur, et vos confundamini. Fac, Domine, fac mecum signum in bonum, ut videant qui me oderunt, et confundantur (Psal. LXXXV, 17), ait de Judaeis ad Patrem Filius.

English translation

2. Yet it is not foreign to the origin and custom of the viperous brood, if they now contradict this sign after it has been accomplished, seeing that from the beginning they contradicted in their father and most impious king Ahaz that it should ever come to pass. For the Lord spoke to Ahaz, saying: Ask for yourself a sign. I will not ask, he said, and I will not tempt the Lord (Isai. VII, 10-12). O profane religion! O execrable piety! O deceitful humility! When you say, "Lest I tempt the Lord," you are despising the Lord. For how would you be tempting him, if you obeyed faithfully? But now, how are you not gravely tempting him, whom you provoke by manifest contempt? We know indeed, we know the deceit and envy of the Jewish root, which even before Christ was born began to envy his glory. For this Ahaz too, as far as can be understood from his life and character (for he was a worshiper of idols), refused to ask for a sign when commanded, not out of any religious scruple or fear, but precisely so that the Lord might not be glorified. Truly wondrous, and plainly deserving the wrath of God and of men, is such dire perversity of the Jews, who, when commanded to seek signs, refuse — not, as they pretend, lest they tempt God, but lest they glorify the Lord (Matth. XVI, 4); and when not commanded, they tempt and seek. For as if by nature and custom the Jews seek signs, and, if given, they slander them and strive to deny them, so that they plainly confess that in tempting they sought not in order to believe in the one who performed the sign, but in order to insult the one who did not perform it. O sinful nation, wicked seed, corrupt children (Isai. I, 4)! says Isaiah. Is it too little for you to be troublesome to men — to me and to other prophets, indeed to the whole human race — that you are troublesome also to my God? Therefore the Lord himself will give you a sign (Isa. VII, 13, 14). For because you walk perversely against him, he too, as he says, will walk perversely toward you. You do not wish a sign to be given, lest the author of the miracle be glorified; and for this very reason he will give it, that he himself may be glorified, and you confounded. Work, O Lord, work with me a sign for good, that those who hate me may see it and be confounded (Psal. LXXXV, 17), says the Son to the Father concerning the Jews.

Latin original

3. Primum autem signum quod Pater et Filius operati sunt in confusionem infidelium et gloriam fidelium, in testimonium suae virtutis et opus nostrae salutis, arbitror hodiernum esse Virginis conceptum. Nam de ipso cum praemissum esset, dabit Dominus signum, quasi quaerentibus quod signum, intulit contextus Prophetae, verbis tamen Evangelistae, Ecce virgo concipiet et pariet filium (Isai. VII, 14, et Luc. I, 31); ut plane aut consequentia vel veritate contextus careat, aut (quod est probabilius) mendacium Judaeorum latebram non habeat. Merito proinde cum jam generatio prava et adultera signum quaerit, signum non datur ei, nisi signum Jonae (Matth. XVI, 4); ut videlicet qui ob perversitatem mentis non aedificantur signo virtutis, scandalizentur signo infirmitatis, triduanae scilicet sepulturae et mortis. Nam verbum crucis et mortis Judaeis, qui pereunt, scandalum est; his autem qui salvi fiunt, id est, nobis, virtus Dei est (I Cor. I, 18); nec minor aut infirmior nobis est Filius hominis in corde terrae, quam in consessu paternae dexterae. Signum quippe quod illi recusaverunt, sive in profundum inferni, sive in excelsum supra, nos fide plena ac veneratione devota suscipimus, agnoscentes Filium quem concipit Virgo, in profundo inferni signum nobis esse liberationis et veniae; in excelsum supra, signum et spem exsultationis et gloriae. Qui enim primum descendit in inferiores partes terrae, ut in sanguine testamenti sui extraheret vinctos de lacu in quo non est aqua, ipse est et qui ascendit super omnes coelos, ut adimpleret omnia (Ephes. IV, 9, 10). Jam levavit Dominus signum primum in patibulo crucis, postmodum in throno regni, et exaltavit signum ad populos nationum, quia contradictum est ei a populo Judaeorum, et quotidie a quatuor ventis congregat dispersos veri Israel ad hoc signum. O Radix Jesse, qui stas in signum populorum, super quem jam continent reges os suum, obstruatur etiam os loquentium iniqua, id est, Judaeorum blasphemantium, qui adhuc signo immaculato conceptus tui contradicunt, et nec angelo Gabrieli credunt dicenti quia non est impossibile apud te omne verbum (Luc. I, 30, 312). Beata quae credidit, cui haec ratio satisfecit, et, cum quaesisset quomodo Filium susciperet, quae non cognosceret virum, tam de integritate quam de prole securam deinceps effecit.

English translation

3. The first sign which the Father and the Son worked for the confusion of unbelievers and the glory of the faithful, in testimony of their power and as the work of our salvation, I judge to be the Son's conception in the Virgin today. For concerning it, when it had been foretold, "The Lord shall give a sign," as though to those asking what that sign might be, the continuation of the Prophet added—though in the words of the Evangelist—"Behold, a virgin shall conceive and bear a son" (Isai. VII, 14, et Luc. I, 31); so that plainly either the context lacks consequence or truth, or (which is more probable) the lie of the Jews finds no hiding place. Fittingly, then, since this crooked and adulterous generation now seeks a sign, no sign is given it except the sign of Jonah (Matth. XVI, 4); so that those who, on account of the perversity of their mind, are not built up by the sign of power, may be scandalized by the sign of weakness—namely, of the three-day burial and death. For the word of the cross and of death is to the Jews who perish a stumbling block; but to us who are being saved it is the power of God (I Cor. I, 18); nor is the Son of Man any less or weaker for us in the heart of the earth than seated at the Father's right hand. For the sign which they refused, whether in the depth of hell or on high above, we receive with full faith and devout veneration, acknowledging that the Son whom the Virgin conceives is, in the depth of hell, a sign to us of liberation and pardon; and on high above, a sign and hope of exultation and glory. For he who first descended into the lower parts of the earth, that in the blood of his testament he might draw out the bound from the pit in which there is no water, is also he who ascended above all the heavens, that he might fill all things (Ephes. IV, 9, 10). The Lord has already raised up his first sign on the gibbet of the cross, afterward on the throne of the kingdom, and he has exalted the sign to the peoples of the nations, because it was contradicted by the people of the Jews, and daily he gathers from the four winds the scattered of the true Israel to this sign. O Root of Jesse, who stand as a sign of the peoples, before whom kings now keep their mouths shut, let the mouth of those who speak iniquity also be stopped—that is, of the Jews who blaspheme, who still contradict the immaculate sign of your conception, and do not even believe the angel Gabriel saying that with you no word is impossible (Luc. I, 30, 312). Blessed is she who believed, for whom this reasoning was satisfying, and who, when she had asked how she should receive a Son, she who knew not man, was thereafter made secure both as to her integrity and as to her offspring.

4. Quidquid ergo garriat impietas infidelium, nobis concipiat et pariat Virgo Filium; nos quippe signum habemus in bonum, etiam Matrem et Filium. Nobis plane Mater tota miraculum est, quae singulariter et sine exemplo mater est et virgo; nobis Filius totus miraculum, qui non solum singulariter, sed incomprehensibiliter Deus est et homo. Mater, virgo concipiens et pariens, signum nobis est quia Deus est homo iste qui concipitur et paritur; Filius, divina faciens, et humana patiens, signum nobis est quia in Deum transferet hominem, pro quo concipitur et paritur, etiam et patitur. Omnium tamen humanarum infirmitatum vel injuriarum quas pro nobis pertulit divina dignatio, sicut tempore primam, sic etiam humilitate fere maximam existimo, quod in utero concipi, in utero novem mensium tempore majestas illa incircumscripta passa est contineri. Ubi enim sic se exinanivit? aut quando ita penitus a semetipso defecisse visus fuit? Tanto tempore nihil illa sapientia loquitur, nihil illa virtus manifestum operatur, nullo signo visibili majestas, quae clausa latet, proditur. Non sic in cruce visus est infirmus, ubi quod infirmum ipsius fuit, statim apparuit fortius omnibus hominibus: quando et moriens glorificat latronem, et exspirans inspirat Centurionem; quando horarius dolor passionis ei non solum compati fecit elementa creaturarum, sed etiam contrarias fortitudines subigit aeternorum passioni dolorum. In utero autem sic est quasi non sit, sic omnipotens virtus vacat quasi nihil possit et Verbum aeternum sub silentio se premit.

English translation

4. Whatever, then, the impiety of unbelievers may chatter, let the Virgin conceive and bear a Son for us; for we indeed have a sign for good, even the Mother and the Son. For us, the Mother is entirely a miracle, who singularly and without precedent is both mother and virgin; for us, the Son is entirely a miracle, who not only singularly but incomprehensibly is God and man. The Mother, a virgin conceiving and bearing, is a sign to us that this one who is conceived and born is God and man; the Son, doing divine things and suffering human things, is a sign to us that he will transfer into God the man for whom he is conceived and born, and also suffers. Yet of all the human infirmities or injuries which the divine condescension bore for us, I judge that, just as it was first in time, so also it was almost the greatest in humility, that his boundless majesty suffered to be conceived in the womb, and contained within the womb for the space of nine months. For where did he so empty himself? Or when did he seem so utterly to have ceased to be himself? For such a long time that wisdom speaks nothing, that power works nothing manifest, and majesty, lying hidden and enclosed, is betrayed by no visible sign. Not so did he appear weak on the cross, where what was weak in him immediately appeared stronger than all men: when, even while dying, he glorifies the thief, and, while breathing his last, he inspires the Centurion; when the brief pain of the Passion not only made the elements of creation suffer with him, but also subjects the opposing powers to the suffering of eternal pains. But in the womb he is as though he were not; thus the omnipotent power lies idle as though it could do nothing, and the eternal Word presses itself down into silence.

5. Vobis tamen, fratres, vobis illud silentium verbi loquitur, vobis clamat; vobis utique disciplinam silentii commendat. In silentio enim et spe erit fortitudo vestra (Isai. XXX, 15), sicut Isaias promittit, qui cultum justitiae silentium definivit (Id. XXXII, 17). Sicut namque puer ille conceptus in utero ad maturitatem partus sub alto et diuturno profecit silentio, sic et spiritum hominis disciplina silentii nutrit, format et roborat, et quanto occultius, tanto tutius et salubrius incrementum praestat. Nescit homo animalis, qui non percipit quae sunt spiritus Dei, quae sit via spiritus, et quomodo compingantur ossa in ventre praegnantis (Eccle. XI, 5); sed non occultatum os meum a te, ait sanctus Deo, quod fecisti mihi in occulto (Psal. CXXXVIII, 15) mentis, sub secreto silentii. Sed nec a vobis occultatum est hoc mysterium, fratres mei, quorum experientia et confessio testis mihi est quomodo quietus et modestus spiritus sub silentio roboretur, pinguescat et floreat; quomodo e contrario per verba, velut quadam paralysi, dissolvatur et defluat; quomodo macrescat, et marcescat, et aridus decidat. Denique si in silentio fortitudo non esset, Salomon nequaquam dixisset: Sicut urbs patens et absque murorum ambitu, ita vir qui non potest in loquendo cohibere spiritum suum (Prov. XXV, 28). Caeterum, si quaeris quo negotio sit occupanda mens in silentio, nihil tibi imponimus onerosum, comede panem tuum, sicut et ipse Dominus conceptus suo tibi commonstrat exemplo. Quid enim propheta de eo dixit, cum loqueretur de porta orientali semper clausa in domo Domini, quae tamen Deum Israel admisit et emisit? Princeps ipse, inquit, sedebit in ea, ut comedat panem coram Domino (Ezech. XLIV, 3). Sedebit, inquit, in ea, quoniam requiescet in ea, de qua et ipse loquitur: Haec requies mea (Psal. XXXI, 143); sedebit in ea tanquam in throno grandi, quem, sicut alias dixi, Salomon rex sibi de ebore fecit (III Reg. X, 18). Si attendas angustias uteri, locus prorsus angustus est, si latitudinem cordis, thronus grandis est, propter quam etiam uterus tantae majestatis capax factus est. In ea ergo princeps sedit et panem comedit; quia si quis, inquit, aperuerit mihi, intrabo ad eum, et coenabo cum eo, et ipse mecum (Apoc. III, 20).

English translation

5. Yet to you, brothers, to you that silence of the Word speaks, to you it cries out; to you indeed it commends the discipline of silence. For "in silence and hope shall your strength be" (Isai. XXX, 15), as Isaiah promises, who defined the practice of righteousness as silence (Id. XXXII, 17). For just as that child, conceived in the womb, advanced toward the maturity of birth under deep and prolonged silence, so too the discipline of silence nourishes, forms, and strengthens the spirit of man; and the more hidden it is, the safer and more wholesome growth it affords. The natural man, who does not perceive the things of the Spirit of God, does not know what is the way of the spirit, and how the bones are joined together in the womb of her that is with child (Eccle. XI, 5); but "my bone is not hidden from thee," says the saint to God, "which thou hast made for me in secret" (Psal. CXXXVIII, 15) — in secret of mind, under the secrecy of silence. But neither is this mystery hidden from you, my brothers, whose experience and confession is witness to me how a quiet and modest spirit is strengthened, grows rich, and flourishes under silence; how, on the contrary, through words, as by a kind of paralysis, it is dissolved and wastes away; how it grows thin, and withers, and falls away dried up. Finally, if there were no strength in silence, Solomon would by no means have said: "As a city that lies open and without the enclosure of walls, so is the man who cannot restrain his spirit in speaking" (Prov. XXV, 28). But if you ask with what business the mind ought to be occupied in silence, we impose on you nothing burdensome: eat your bread, as the Lord himself, conceived in the womb, shows you by his own example. For what did the prophet say of him, when he spoke of the eastern gate, always shut, in the house of the Lord, which nevertheless admitted and sent forth the God of Israel? "The prince himself," he says, "shall sit in it, to eat bread before the Lord" (Ezech. XLIV, 3). "He shall sit," he says, "in it," because he will rest in it, of which he himself also says: "This is my rest" (Psal. XXXI, 143); he shall sit in it as on a great throne, which, as I have said elsewhere, King Solomon made for himself of ivory (III Reg. X, 18). If you consider the narrowness of the womb, it is altogether a narrow place; if you consider the breadth of the heart, it is a great throne — on account of which the womb was made capable of so great a majesty. In it, therefore, the prince sat and ate bread; for "if any man," he says, "shall open to me, I will come in to him, and will sup with him, and he with me" (Apoc. III, 20).

Latin original

6. Non est haec coena sine pane, quando et ipse qui coenat panis est vitae, panis qui hodie descendit de coelo et dat vitam mundo. Sed res mira, si idem est qui coenat, et quod coenatur; et qui comedit, ipse est panis qui ab eo comeditur. Revera res mira, sed res vera; quia Christus non alio pane quam se ipso pascitur. Ipse namque totus est panis, Verbum propter se, caro propter unionem Verbi. Alioquin caro non prodest quidquam, cum spiritus sit qui vivificat (Joan. VI, 64), nec in solo pane vivat homo, sed in omni verbo quod procedit de ore Dei (Matth. IV, 4). Omne verbum procedens de ore Dei unum et unigenitum est Verbum Patris, quod, cum simplex sit, in se tamen continet rationem et formam omnis verbi divini. Verbum itaque pascitur de Verbo, Filius vivit de se ipso; quia sicut Pater habet vitam in semetipso, sic dedit et Filio vitam habere in semetipso (Joan. V, 26). Alia tamen ratione, sed tamen ineffabili beatitudine, Verbo pascebatur anima illa, Verbo ipsi in persona conjuncta; et incomparabili felicitate princeps ille sedens in porta virginalis uteri, panem verbi coram Domino comedebat. Id igitur operis tu quoque, si sapis, in silentio tuo actitabis, ut panem verbi divini comedas coram Domino, conservans, sicut Maria, quae de Christo dicuntur, et conferens in corde tuo. Hunc panem tecum Christus manducare delectabitur; et qui te pascit, ipse in te pascetur (Luc. XXII, 15, 19); et panis ipse quo plus editur, plus abundabit edendus: quia gratia non minuitur usu, sed augetur.

English translation

6. This supper is not without bread, since he himself who sups is the bread of life, the bread which today came down from heaven and gives life to the world. But it is a wondrous thing, if he who sups is the same as that which is supped upon; and he who eats is himself the bread which is eaten by him. Indeed it is a wondrous thing, but a true thing; for Christ is fed by no other bread than himself. For he himself is wholly bread: the Word on his own account, the flesh on account of its union with the Word. Otherwise the flesh profits nothing, since it is the spirit that gives life (Joan. VI, 64); nor does man live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God (Matth. IV, 4). Every word proceeding from the mouth of God is the one and only-begotten Word of the Father, which, although it is simple, nevertheless contains within itself the reason and form of every divine word. English translation

7. Let Jesus, therefore, conceived and carried in the womb, be an example to you in this: that just as that burden, light and sweet, though it filled Mary's womb with pregnancy, did not weigh it down, so may the womb of the Church not feel you to be heavy or troublesome. The Church, O brothers, is pregnant — not, like Mary, with Jesus alone, but, like Rebecca, with Jacob and Esau — that is, not only with the good and well-behaved, but also with the unruly and undisciplined; yet even these, for the sake of the name of Jesus, or perhaps some small beginning of his substance in them, the womb of the Church receives and embraces. But when the quarreling little ones struggled against each other in the womb of Rebecca — she who had earlier prayed that she might conceive — grieving in her womb over the trouble and pain caused by these evil ones, she almost regretted that she had conceived, saying: 'If it was going to be like this for me, what need was there to conceive?' (Gen. XXV, 22.). If it should happen, brothers, that the womb of our mother thus complains concerning any one of us, I fear it would have been better if that man had not been conceived — except that he who raises up children of Abraham even from stones (Luc. III, 8) does not allow us to despair even over such as these. May he himself soften the stony heart in them, if any such there be, lest they shake the womb of their mother; may he himself console the womb of the mother, lest she grow weary of carrying them, whatever they may be, until Christ be formed in them — who, perfect God, perfect man, lives and reigns through all ages of ages. Amen.
