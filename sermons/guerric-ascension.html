Introduction & Scripture references

Editorial introduction

Guerric of Igny applies Christ's prayer before the Passion to the Ascension, reading his departure through the love shown to his disciples. The Eucharist offers a new manner of remaining with and in them, while the washing of their feet teaches humility and forgiveness. Christ's prayer promises protection from evil, sanctification in truth and glory with him, without excusing negligence. Guerric urges his brothers to practise the daily struggle against vice: like young eagles learning to fly, they must lift their hearts after the ascended Christ. The sermon ends with contemplation, love and the liturgical exchange Lift up your hearts / We have them lifted to the Lord, directing desire toward Christ as heavenly treasure. The individual sermon is undated; its collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138-1157 and is read here from the 1879 Patrologia Latina edition. The source names the day of the Lord’s Ascension.

Latin original

1. Pater, cum essem cum eis, ego servabam eos in nomine tuo (Joan. xvii, 11, 12). Haec oratio Domini habita est ante diem passionis. Non absurde tamen aptatur diei Ascensionis, quando scilicet supremo discessurus erat a filiolis suis, quos Patri commendabat. Qui enim in coelis creavit, docet et regit multitudinem Angelorum, in terris aggregaverat sibi pusillum gregem discipulorum sub praesentia suae carnis erudiendum, quoadusque grandiusculi sensus effecti, disciplinae Spiritus fierent idonei. Magnus itaque parvulos magna dilectione diligebat, quippe quos ab more mundi2 abstraxerat, et relicta omni spe saeculi, de se solo pendere videbat. Quandiu tamen cum eis corporaliter conversari voluit, non facile aut multum hunc suum eis affectum prodidit; maturiorem se eis, quam teneriorem exhibens, sicut magistrum decebat et patrem. Cum autem tempus quo ab eis recessurus erat instaret, tunc veluti vinci tenero eorum affectu visus est, ut magnam multitudinem dulcedinis suae, quam eis absconderat, dissimulare non posset. Hinc illud est, quod cum dilexisset suos qui erant in mundo, in finem dilexit eos (Id. xiii, 1). Tunc enim propemodum omnem vim amoris effudit amicis, antequam etiam ipse sicut aqua effunderetur pro inimicis. Tunc eis sacramentum Corporis et Sanguinis sui tradidit, et celebrandum instituit, nescio virtute an charitate mirabiliori, hoc novum genus mansionis adinveniens in consolationem recessus sui, ut si discederet ab eis specie corporis, maneret non solum cum eis, sed etiam in eis virtute Sacramenti. Tunc veluti prorsus suae oblitus majestatis, et velut injuriam faciens sibi, nisi quia gloria est charitatis humiliare se pro amicis, dignatione ineffabili Dominus, et talis Dominus, pedes servorum lavit: uno opere condens eis et humilitatis exemplum, et remissionis sacramentum.

English translation

1. Father, when I was with them, I kept them in your name (Joan. xvii, 11, 12). This prayer of the Lord was offered before the day of his Passion. Yet it is fittingly applied to the day of the Ascension, when he was about to leave his little children for the last time and was commending them to the Father. For he who in heaven created, teaches and rules the multitude of Angels had gathered for himself on earth a little flock of disciples, to be instructed in the presence of his flesh until their understanding had grown somewhat and they became fit for the teaching of the Spirit. Thus the great one loved the little ones with great love: he had drawn them away from the ways of the world2 and saw them depending on him alone, having abandoned every worldly hope. Yet as long as he chose to live bodily among them, he did not readily or fully reveal this affection of his to them, showing himself more reserved than tender, as befitted a teacher and father. But when the time was drawing near for him to depart from them, he seemed overcome by tender affection for them, so that he could no longer conceal the great abundance of his sweetness that he had hidden from them. Hence the saying that, having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end (Id. xiii, 1). For then he poured out almost the whole force of his love upon his friends, before he himself was also poured out like water for his enemies. Then he handed on to them the sacrament of his Body and Blood and instituted its celebration, devising this new manner of remaining to console them for his departure, with power or charity more wonderful, I know not which. Thus, though he departed from them in bodily appearance, he would remain not only with them but also in them through the power of the Sacrament. Then, as though utterly forgetful of his own majesty and as though doing himself an indignity, except that it is the glory of charity to humble oneself for friends, the Lord, and such a Lord, with ineffable condescension washed his servants' feet, establishing for them in a single act both an example of humility and a sacrament of forgiveness.

2. Tunc denique post exhortationem prolixioris sermonis Patri eos commendans, sublevatis in coelum oculis, inter caetera sic ait, Pater, cum essem cum eis, ego servabam eos in nomine tuo, et nemo ex his periit, nisi filius perditionis. Nunc autem ad te venio: serva eos in nomine tuo quos dedisti mihi. Non rogo ut tollas eos de mundo, sed ut serves eos a malo; et caetera, quae non est hujus temporis retexere, multo minus exponere. Summa tamen orationis, sicut indicat textus lectionis, est in his tribus, in quibus est et summa salutis, imo et perfectionis, ut nihil possit addi, scilicet, ut a malo serventur, ut in veritate sanctificentur, ut cum ipso glorificentur. Pater, inquit, quos dedisti mihi, volo ut ubi sum ego, et ipsi sint mecum, ut videant claritatem meam (Joan. xvii, 11-13, 15, 24). O felices, quorum advocatus ipse judex est; pro quibus orat qui pari honore cum eo quem orat adorandus est! Voluntate labiorum ejus non fraudabit eum Pater, cum quo est ei una voluntas et una potestas, quia unus Deus est. Omnino quod Christus orat, impleri necesse est: cujus sermo virtus est, et voluntas effectus; qui de omnibus quae sunt, dixit, et facta sunt, mandavit, et creata sunt (Psal. xxxii, 9). Volo, inquit, ut ubi ego sum, et ipsi sint mecum. O quanta securitas fidelium! quanta fiducia credentium! tantum non abjiciant gratiam quam acceperunt. Non enim solis Apostolis proposita est haec securitas, vel condiscipulis eorum, sed omnibus qui per eorum verbum credituri sunt in Dei verbum. Non pro eis, inquit, rogo tantum, sed pro eis qui credituri sunt per verbum eorum in me (Joan. xvii, 24, 20)3.

English translation

2. Then, finally, after the exhortation of a lengthy discourse, commending them to the Father and lifting his eyes to heaven, he said among other things: Father, when I was with them, I kept them in your name, and none of them perished except the son of perdition. But now I come to you: keep in your name those whom you have given me. I do not ask that you take them out of the world, but that you keep them from evil; and the rest, which this is not the time to repeat, much less explain. Yet the substance of the prayer, as the text of the reading shows, lies in these three things, in which also lies the fullness of salvation, indeed of perfection, so that nothing can be added: that they be kept from evil, that they be sanctified in truth, and that they be glorified with him. Father, he says, those whom you have given me, I will that where I am, they too may be with me, so that they may see my glory (Joan. xvii, 11-13, 15, 24). O happy people, whose advocate is himself their judge, for whom he prays who is to be adored with equal honor alongside him to whom he prays! The Father will not deny him the desire of his lips: with the Father he has one will and one power, because they are one God. What Christ prays for must assuredly be fulfilled: his word is power, and his will is accomplishment. Concerning all things that exist, he spoke and they were made; he commanded and they were created (Psal. xxxii, 9). I will, he says, that where I am, they too may be with me. O how great the security of the faithful! How great the confidence of believers! Only let them not cast away the grace they have received. For this security is offered not to the Apostles alone or to their fellow disciples, but to all who through their word will believe in the Word of God. I pray, he says, not for them alone, but for those who through their word will believe in me (Joan. xvii, 24, 20)3.

3. Vobis autem fratres, donatum est, ut ait Apostolus, non solum ut in eum credatis, sed etiam ut patiamini pro eo (Philipp. i, 29): quos scilicet fides promissionis Christi, non securitate negligentiores, sed alacritate faciens ferventiores, et quotidiano vitiorum conflictu, martyrio coronat assiduos: assiduo, sed facili2; facili, sed sublimi. English translation

3. But to you, brothers, it has been granted, as the Apostle says, not only to believe in him but also to suffer for him (Philipp. i, 29). Faith in Christ's promise makes you more fervent through eagerness rather than more negligent through security, and crowns you, steadfast in the daily struggle against vices, with martyrdom: continual, yet easy2; easy, yet sublime. It is easy where nothing beyond one's strength is commanded; sublime where the whole power of that strong armed one is overcome. Is it not easy to bear Christ's sweet yoke? Is it not sublime to excel in his kingdom? What is easier, I ask, than to carry wings that carry their bearer? What is more sublime than to fly above the whole height of the heavens, where Christ has ascended? Surely the saints, whose youth will be renewed like the eagle's, will take wings like eagles and fly. Where will they fly? Wherever the body is, he says, there the eagles too will gather (Matth. xxiv, 28, et Luc. xvii, 37.3

4. Sed quid putamus, fratres, quomodo tunc repente de terris ad coelos evolare poterit, qui nunc exercitio et usu quotidiano volitare non didicerit? Si quaeris quo doctore, quo duce, nunquid non Christus sicut aquila, provocabat hodie ad volandum pullos suos, quando super eos volitabat, cum scilicet videntibus illis elevaretur, diuque sequerentur oculis euntem in coelum? Poterat utique repente in ictu oculi, ex oculis eorum rapi, et ubi vellet, constitui. Sed plane, sicut aquila provocans ad volandum pullos suos, et super eos volitans (Deut. xxxii, 14)3, et corda sursum levare post se nitebatur amore sui, et corpora similiter posse sublevari pollicebatur exemplo corporis sui: sicut Apostolus ait, aeterni conscius mysterii, quia et nos bajulis nubibus rapiemur obviam Christo (I Thess. iv, 16) redeunti. Ipse quidem ascendit super Cherubim, et volavit super pennas ventorum (Psal. xvii, 11), id est supergressus est virtutes Angelorum: tuae tamen condescendens infirmitati, expandet alas suas, et assumet te, atque portabit in humeris suis, si modo pullus degener2 non existas, ut a terra levari, et aura puriore perfrui non extimescas.

English translation

4. But what do we think, brothers? How will someone suddenly be able then to fly from earth to heaven if he has not now learned to fly through daily practice and exercise? If you ask who is the teacher and who the guide, did not Christ, like an eagle, encourage his young to fly today when he flew above them, that is, when he was lifted up before their eyes and they followed him with their gaze for a long time as he went into heaven? He could certainly have been snatched from their sight suddenly, in the blink of an eye, and set wherever he wished. But indeed, like an eagle encouraging its young to fly and flying above them (Deut. xxxii, 14)3, he strove to lift their hearts up after him through love of himself and promised, by the example of his own body, that their bodies too could be lifted up. As the Apostle says, privy to the eternal mystery, we too, with clouds bearing us, shall be caught up to meet Christ (I Thess. iv, 16) as he returns. He himself indeed ascended upon Cherubim and flew upon the wings of the winds (Psal. xvii, 11), that is, he surpassed the powers of the Angels. Yet, stooping to your weakness, he will spread his wings, take you up and carry you on his shoulders, provided that you are not a degenerate fledgling2, afraid to be lifted from the earth and enjoy a purer air.

5. Alii volant contemplando, tu saltem amando. Paulus mente excedit, et ad tertium usque pervolat coelum (II Cor. xii, 2); Joannes ad id quod id principio4 erat Verbum: tu saltem humi degenerem non trahas animum, nec patiaris obrutum inertia in terra putrescere cor tuum; sed clamanti, « Sursum corda, » Pontifici magno (qui hodie introivit in Sancta, aeterna redemptione inventa, ubi et assistit vultui Dei interpellans pro nobis) responde fideliter, « Habemus ad Dominum. » Sed et si aliquando quaesivisti non quae sursum sunt, sed quae super terram; jam te ipsum increpa, et dic Domino cum propheta: Quid enim mihi est in coelo? et a te quid volui super terram? (Psal. lxxii, 25)3 Heu, quam misere errabam! tam magnum est illud in coelo mihi repositum, quod contemnebam; tam nihil hoc super terram, quod tantopere expetebam! Christus itaque thesaurus tuus ascendit in coelum: ibi sit et cor tuum. Inde ducis originem, ibi habes Patrem et haereditatem, inde exspectas Salvatorem. Amen.

English translation

5. Others fly by contemplation; you, at least, by love. Paul passes beyond himself in mind and flies as far as the third heaven (II Cor. xii, 2); John to that Word who was in the beginning4. You, at least, must not drag a debased spirit along the ground or let your heart, buried in sloth, rot in the earth. Rather, to the great High Priest crying, 'Lift up your hearts' (who today entered the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption, where he also stands before the face of God, interceding for us), answer faithfully, 'We have them lifted to the Lord.' And if at times you have sought not the things above but the things on earth, now rebuke yourself and say to the Lord with the prophet: For what have I in heaven? And apart from you, what have I desired upon earth? (Psal. lxxii, 25)3 Alas, how wretchedly I was mistaken! So great is that which was laid up for me in heaven and which I despised; so utterly nothing is this upon earth which I sought so eagerly! Christ, then, your treasure, has ascended into heaven: let your heart be there too. From there you draw your origin; there you have your Father and inheritance; from there you await the Savior. Amen.

2. Editorial note

cols. 153-154 (PDF 81); cols. 155-156 (PDF 82)

“From the ways of the world” translates ab more mundi, not “from love of the world.” Christ’s bodily departure is contrasted with his continuing sacramental presence in the disciples. The daily martyrdom described here is spiritual combat against vice, not bodily execution.
