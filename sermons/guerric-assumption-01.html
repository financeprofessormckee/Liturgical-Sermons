Editorial introduction

This first sermon for the Assumption of Blessed Mary belongs to Guerric of Igny's medieval liturgical collection, associated approximately with his abbacy in 1138-1157 and read here from the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The individual delivery year is unknown. Beginning with an invitation from the Office of Virgins, Guerric presents Mary as mother both of Christ and, through him, of Christians, contrasting her with Eve and relating her to the Church. He develops the image of Mary as God's throne and ends with a prayer for her intercession. The Assumption is the source’s historical occasion.

SERMON I.

¶col. 187
Latin original

Quod Maria duplici titulo sit mater, quodque recte dicatur thronus Dei.

English translation

That Mary is mother by a double title, and is rightly called the throne of God.

¶col. 187
Latin original

1. Veni, electa mea, et ponam in te thronum meum (11)2. Multi vocati, pauci vero electi. Beati quos elegisti, Domine; habitabunt in atriis tuis (Psal. lxiv, 5) : imo tu in eis habitabis, tu in eis regnabis, thronumque regni tui in eis collocabis. Sane omnium beatorum beatissima est Maria, quae de numero omnium electorum singulariter est electa et praeelecta : quoniam elegit eam Dominus, elegit eam in habitationem sibi, dicens, Hic requies mea in saeculum saeculi : hic habitabo quoniam elegi eam (Psal cxxxi, 14). Habitavit in ea novem mensibus, habitavit cum ea et sub ea annis pluribus. Habitans in ea replebat eam singulari gratia charismatum : habitans cum ea pascebat eam incomparabili piorum suavitate morum, et divinorum desiderabili sapientia sermonum. Nunc autem in ea et cum ea, sicut interminabili aevo, sic incomprehensibili modo habitans, satiat eam beatificantium gloria visionum; foris quidem glorificatae carnis formam exhibens, intus autem formam Verbi glorificantis imprimens. O Maria, non vocaberis, ait Dominus, ultra derelicta, et terra tua non vocabitur ultra desolata, ut quia virgo, ideo sis infecunda : sed vocaberis Voluntas mea, id est, dilectus Filius meus in ea, quia complacuit Domino in te, et terra tua inhabitabitur. Habitabit enim juvenis in virgine, et habitabit in te Filius tuus; imo, si magis placet, ut non recedamus a verbis Scripturae, habitabunt in te filii tui (Isa. lxii, 4, 5)3.

English translation

1. Come, my chosen one, and I will set my throne in you (11)2. Many are called, but few are chosen. Blessed are those whom you have chosen, Lord; they will dwell in your courts (Psal. lxiv, 5) : rather, you will dwell in them, you will reign in them, and you will establish the throne of your kingdom in them. Certainly Mary is the most blessed of all the blessed, singled out and chosen before all among the whole number of the elect : for the Lord has chosen her, chosen her as a dwelling for himself, saying, This is my rest for ever and ever : here I will dwell, for I have chosen her (Psal cxxxi, 14). He dwelt in her for nine months; he dwelt with her and under her for many years. Dwelling in her, he filled her with a unique grace of spiritual gifts : dwelling with her, he nourished her with the incomparable sweetness of his holy ways and the desirable wisdom of his divine words. But now, dwelling in her and with her both for an endless age and in an incomprehensible manner, he satisfies her with the glory of visions that make her blessed; outwardly displaying the form of his glorified flesh, and inwardly impressing the form of the Word who glorifies her. O Mary, the Lord says, you will no longer be called forsaken, nor will your land any longer be called desolate, as though being a virgin meant that you must be barren : instead you will be called My Will, that is, my beloved Son is in her, because the Lord has taken pleasure in you, and your land will be inhabited. For the young man will dwell in the virgin, and your Son will dwell in you; or rather, if you prefer, so that we do not depart from the words of Scripture, your sons will dwell in you (Isa. lxii, 4, 5)3.

2. Quid, haeretice, caput erigis? quid mysterium pietatis ad occasionem perfidiae rapis? Unum quidem genuit : qui sicut unicus est Patri in coelis, ita unicus est Matri in terris. Neque enim sicut tu impie blasphemas, alios postea filios genuit; sed intemeratum manet, sicut signaculum perpetuae virginitatis in matre, ita sacramentum catholicae unitatis in prole. Ipsa tamen unica virgo Mater, quae se Patris Unicum genuisse gloriatur, eumdem unicum suum in omnibus membris ejus amplectitur, omniumque in quibus Christum suum formatum agnoscit, vel formari cognoscit, matrem se vocari non confunditur. Eva vetus illa, non tam mater quam noverca, quae filiis ante propinavit praejudicium mortis, quam initium lucis. Dicta est quidem mater cunctorum viventium : sed inventa est verius interfectrix viventium, seu genitrix morientium; cum suum generare nihil aliud sit, quam mortem ingenerare (Gen. iii). Et quia illa non potuit fideliter interpretari nomen suum, ista implevit mysterium : quae et ipsa, sicut Ecclesia, cujus forma est, mater est omnium ad vitam renascentium. Mater siquidem est Vitae qua vivunt universi; quam dum ex se genuit, nimirum omnes ex ea victuri sunt4, quodammodo regeneravit. Unus generabatur, sed nos omnes generabamur : quia videlicet secundum rationem seminis, quo regeneratio fit, jam tunc in illo omnes eramus. Sicut enim in Adam fuimus ab initio propter semen carnalis generationis; sic in Christo ante initium, propter semen spiritualis regenerationis.

English translation

2. Why do you raise your head, heretic? Why do you seize the mystery of godliness as an occasion for unbelief? She bore one Son : who, just as he is the Father's only Son in heaven, is also his Mother's only Son on earth. For she did not afterwards bear other sons, as you impiously blaspheme; rather, just as the seal of perpetual virginity remains inviolate in the mother, so does the mystery of Catholic unity in her offspring. Yet this unique virgin Mother, who glories in having borne the Father's Only-begotten, embraces that same only Son of hers in all his members, and is not ashamed to be called the mother of all in whom she recognizes her Christ already formed or knows him to be taking form. That Eve of old was less a mother than a stepmother, who gave her children the sentence of death to drink before they first saw the light. She was indeed called the mother of all the living : but she proved more truly the slayer of the living, or the mother of the dying; for her giving birth means nothing other than bringing death into being (Gen. iii). And because she could not faithfully express the meaning of her name, this other mother fulfilled the mystery : she too, like the Church of which she is the figure, is mother of all who are born again to life. For she is the Mother of the Life by which all live; and in giving birth to that Life from herself, she in a certain manner gave rebirth to all who would live by it4. One was being born, but we were all being born : for, in respect of the seed through which rebirth takes place, we were all already in him then. Just as we were in Adam from the beginning through the seed of bodily generation, so we were in Christ before the beginning through the seed of spiritual regeneration.

3. Porro beata Mater illa Christi, quia se matrem Christianorum cognoscit ratione mysterii; cura quoque se matrem eis praestat et affectu pietatis. Neque enim duratur ad filios, quasi non sint sui, cujus viscera semel quidem foeta, sed nunquam effoeta, nunquam desinunt fructum parturire pietatis. Benedictus siquidem fructus ventris tui, gravidam te, o pia Mater, inexhausta pietate reliquit; ex te quidem semel nascens, sed in te semper manens et affluens, et in horto concluso castitatis fontem signatum charitatis semper abundare faciens; qui licet signatus sit, foras tamen derivatur, et in plateis aquae ejus nobis dividuntur. Licet namque fons charitatis, proprius Ecclesiae, incommunicabilis sit alienis, beneficium tamen ejus impertire gaudet etiam inimicis. Denique si servus Christi filiolos suos iterum atque iterum parturit cura atque desiderio pietatis, donec formetur in eis Christus (Galat. iv, 19); quanto magis ipsa Mater Christi? Et Paulus quidem genuit eos, verbum veritatis quo regenerati sunt praedicando : Maria autem longe divinius atque sanctius, Verbum ipsum generando. Laudo quidem in Paulo praedicationis mysterium, sed plus miror ac veneror in Maria generationis mysterium.4

English translation

3. Moreover, because that blessed Mother of Christ knows that she is the mother of Christians by reason of the mystery, she also shows herself their mother by her care and loving devotion. She does not harden herself toward her children as though they were not her own : her womb, fruitful once but never exhausted, never ceases to bring forth the fruit of loving devotion. For the blessed fruit of your womb left you, O loving Mother, pregnant with inexhaustible devotion; born from you once, yet always remaining and overflowing in you, he makes the sealed fountain of charity abound continually within the enclosed garden of chastity. Although sealed, it is nevertheless channeled outward, and its waters are shared with us in the streets. For although the fountain of charity belongs to the Church and cannot be shared with those outside her, it gladly shares its benefit even with enemies. Indeed, if the servant of Christ labors in birth for his little children again and again, with devoted care and longing, until Christ is formed in them (Galat. iv, 19); how much more does the Mother of Christ herself? Paul indeed begot them by preaching the word of truth through which they were born again : but Mary did so in a far more divine and holy manner, by giving birth to the Word himself. I praise in Paul the mystery of preaching, but I admire and venerate still more in Mary the mystery of giving birth.4

4. Vide autem si non et filii Matrem videntur agnoscere, dictante utique ipsis veluti quadam naturali pietate fidei, ut ad invocationem nominis ejus, primo vel maxime refugiant in omnibus necessitatibus et periculis, tanquam parvuli ad sinum matris. Unde nimirum de his filiis puto non absurde intelligi, quod per prophetam promissum est ei. Habitabant in te filii tui (Isai. lxii, 5)3; salvo duntaxat intellectu, quo de Ecclesia haec prophetia principaliter accipitur. Et nunc siquidem habitamus in adjutorio Matris Altissimi, in protectione ipsius commoramur, tanquam sub umbra alarum ejus : et postmodum in consortio gloriae ipsius, tanquam in sinu ipsius confovebimur. Tunc erit vox una laetantium et aggratulantium Matri : Sicut laetantium omnium nostrum habitatio est in te (Psal. lxxxvi, 7), sancta Dei Genitrix. Nullatenus autem credideris majoris esse felicitatis et gloriae, habitare in sinu Abrahae, quam in sinu Mariae, cum thronum suum in ea posuerit Rex gloriae.

English translation

4. See, too, whether the children do not seem to recognize their Mother, prompted, as it were, by a certain natural devotion of faith to flee first or above all to the invocation of her name in every need and danger, like little children to their mother's bosom. Hence I think it is reasonable to understand of these children what was promised to her through the prophet. Your sons dwelt in you (Isai. lxii, 5)3; always preserving the interpretation according to which this prophecy is understood principally of the Church. Even now we dwell in the help of the Mother of the Most High; we remain under her protection, as though beneath the shadow of her wings : and afterwards, sharing her glory, we shall be cherished as though in her bosom. Then there will be one voice of those rejoicing with their Mother and congratulating her : The dwelling of all of us who rejoice is in you (Psal. lxxxvi, 7), holy Mother of God. Never believe that dwelling in Abraham's bosom is a greater happiness and glory than dwelling in Mary's bosom, since the King of glory has established his throne in her.

5. Veni, inquit, electa mea, et ponam in te thronum meum. Non poterat signantius aut elegantius describi praerogativa gloriae ejus, quam ut thronus Dei regnantis esse diceretur. Nulli siquidem animae tanta plenitudine aut familiaritate copiam sui divina majestas videtur indulgere, sicut illi in qua specialiter prae caeteris residere delegerit. Discipulis quidem Dominus loquebatur, qui pauperes facti pauperem sequebantur : In regeneratione, cum sederit Filius hominis in sede majestatis suae, sedebitis et vos super sedes (Matth. xix, 28). Alibi quoque promittit idem Agonotheta4 noster, de coelo spectans et incitans decertantes : Qui vicerit, inquit, dabo ei sedere mecum in throno meo, sicut et ego vici et sedi in throno Patris mei (Apoc. iii, 21). Matri vero, cujus longe differens est meritum, nihilominus differens promittit praemium. Veni, inquit, electa mea, et ponam in te thronum meum. Parum est, inquit, ut judicanti consedeas, nisi et ipsa mihi sedes fias : ut majestatem regnantis eo felicius quo familiarius in te contineas, et specialius prae caeteris incomprehensibilem comprehendas. Continuisti parvulum in gremio : continebis immensum in animo. Fuisti diversorium peregrinantis; eris palatium regnantis. Fuisti tabernaculum pugnaturi in mundo; eris solium triumphantis in coelo. Fuisti thalamus Sponsi incarnati; eris thronus Regis coronati.

English translation

5. Come, he says, my chosen one, and I will set my throne in you. The prerogative of her glory could not be described more expressively or beautifully than by calling her the throne of God who reigns. For the divine majesty seems to grant no soul such full or intimate possession of himself as the soul in which he has chosen to dwell especially above all others. The Lord indeed spoke to the disciples who had become poor and followed him in his poverty : In the regeneration, when the Son of man sits on the seat of his majesty, you too will sit upon seats (Matth. xix, 28). Elsewhere our same Master of the contest4, watching from heaven and urging on those who struggle, promises : To him who conquers, he says, I will grant to sit with me on my throne, just as I too conquered and sat on the throne of my Father (Apoc. iii, 21). But to his Mother, whose merit is far different, he promises a reward no less different. Come, he says, my chosen one, and I will set my throne in you. It is little, he says, for you to sit beside me as I judge, unless you yourself become my seat : so that you may contain within yourself the majesty of him who reigns, the more blessedly for being the more intimately, and may embrace the incomprehensible more particularly than all others. You held the little child in your lap : you will hold the boundless one in your soul. You were the lodging of the traveler; you will be the palace of him who reigns. You were the tent of him who was to fight in the world; you will be the throne of him who triumphs in heaven. You were the bridal chamber of the incarnate Bridegroom; you will be the throne of the crowned King.

6. O Fili Dei, nihil tibi, nihil in illo tuo displicuit hospitio quod tam libenter repetit, tamque affluenter remunerat tua dignatio. Nihil utique in illo sordidum offendisti, quia nulla erat libido, sed purissima castitas : nihil ruinosum, quia nulla erat superbia, sed fundatissima humilitas : nihil obscurum, quia exclusa infidelitas : nihil angustum, quia diffusa erat charitas. Adornaverat thalamum Virgo prudentissima, te regem Christum non solum hospitem susceptura; sed et sponsum habitura. Adornaverat, inquam, multiplici decore virtutum et gloria, tanto fortasse locupletius, quanto interius totum erat. Hunc ornatum mirabatur qui dicebat, Omnis gloria ejus filiae regis ab intus, in fimbriis aureis, circumamicta varietate (Psal. xliv, 14, 15) : et alius, O quam pulchra est casta generatio cum charitate ! (Sap. iv, 1.)3 Domum tuam decet Domine, hujusmodi sanctitudo et decor. Is decor te invitavit ut intrares : illexit, ut redires. Intrans benedictionis gratiam multiplicasti : sed rediens cumulasti. Cum intrasti, homo natus es in ea : cum redisti, Deus glorificatus es in ea. Tunc tibi posuisti in ea sacrarium gratiae; nunc autem, thronum gloriae.

English translation

6. O Son of God, nothing displeased you, nothing in that lodging of yours which your graciousness so gladly revisits and so abundantly rewards. Certainly you found nothing defiled there, for there was no lust, but the purest chastity : nothing ruinous, for there was no pride, but humility most firmly founded : nothing dark, for unbelief was excluded : nothing cramped, for charity was poured abroad. The most prudent Virgin had adorned the bridal chamber, ready to receive you, Christ the King, as her guest, and also to have you as her bridegroom. She had adorned it, I say, with the manifold beauty and glory of virtues, perhaps all the more richly because it was wholly inward. He admired this adornment who said, All the glory of her, the king's daughter, is within, in golden fringes, clothed about with variety (Psal. xliv, 14, 15) : and another said, O how beautiful is a chaste generation with charity ! (Sap. iv, 1.)3 Such holiness and beauty befit your house, Lord. That beauty invited you to enter : it drew you to return. By entering you multiplied the grace of blessing : but by returning you heaped it up. When you entered, you were born in her as man : when you returned, you were glorified in her as God. Then you established in her a sanctuary of grace for yourself; but now, a throne of glory.

7. Dicuntur quidem et sunt alii throni, quidam scilicet spiritus divini, prae aliis, ut putamus, subjectis ordinibus, Dei praesidentis majestate pleni : quorum quae sit praerogativa, Scriptura non exprimit, licet innuat ex vocabulo quod nonnulla sit. Sed et anima cujuslibet justi dicitur sedes sapientiae. Quae autem nunc es sedes sapientiae4, profecto tunc erit et gloriae. Sit igitur illud coeli palatium plenum sedibus et thronis, sedeatque Deus in omnibus, accommodans se et aptans cuique pro meritis. Non immerito tamen creditur, sine injuria vel invidia caeterarum sedium quoddam esse speciale Regis solium excelsum et elevatum super gloriam omnium : Mariam dico exaltatam super choros Angelorum, ut nihil contempletur supra se Mater nisi Filium solum; nihil miretur supra se Regina nisi Regem solum; nihil veneretur supra se Mediatrix nostra nisi Mediatorem solum : cui nos ipsa precibus suis conciliet, commendet, et repraesentet unico Filio suo Jesu Christo, cui est honor et gloria in saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

7. There are indeed others who are called thrones and truly are thrones : certain divine spirits, filled with the majesty of God who presides, beyond the other subordinate orders, as we suppose. Scripture does not express what their prerogative is, although their name suggests that they have some prerogative. The soul of every just person is also called a seat of wisdom. But you who are now a seat of wisdom4 will assuredly then be a seat of glory as well. Let that heavenly palace, then, be full of seats and thrones, and let God sit in all, accommodating and adapting himself to each according to their merits. Yet it is reasonably believed, without wrong or envy toward the other seats, that there is one particular royal throne, lofty and raised above the glory of them all : I mean Mary exalted above the choirs of Angels, so that the Mother contemplates above herself only the Son; the Queen admires above herself only the King; our Mediatress venerates above herself only the Mediator. May she by her prayers reconcile us to him, commend us to him, and present us to her only Son Jesus Christ, to whom belong honor and glory for ever and ever. Amen.

Mary’s motherhood and throne, the contrast with Eve, and the distinction between Mediatress and Mediator are Guerric’s own imagery. “Mystery of Catholic unity” translates sacramentum catholicae unitatis without imposing a later sacramental classification. “Master of the contest” renders agonotheta. In section 2 the translation follows the sense of a grammatically irregular Latin clause. In section 7 a damaged final letter may be the t of est (“is”); the Latin retains the visible es, while the English gives the contextual sense. The phrase about those rejoicing means “one voice of those rejoicing.”
