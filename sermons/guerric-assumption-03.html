Introduction & Scripture references

Editorial introduction

Preaching on a feast that gives the brothers rest from the harvest, Guerric takes the words "In all things I sought rest" as the voice of Wisdom, of the Church and of Mary, who finds rest today. Christ still seeks rest among the humble and quiet, while the restless are driven like a wheel. Guerric urges quiet through humility and manual work, and recalls Paul's rebuke of those who walk unquietly. The sermon appears under the heading for the Assumption of Blessed Mary in the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157; the individual sermon is undated.

De quiete spirituali.

English translation

On spiritual rest.

¶cols. 193-194
Latin original

1. In omnibus requiem quaesivi (Eccli. XXIV, 11). Grata quies fessis. Grate igitur et opportune vobis, qui fessi estis, dies iste quietis et feriationis intervenit, ut dum sanctae Dei Genitricis requiem celebramus, non solum corpora recreentur hac diurna quiete a labore messis, sed etiam corda respirent in recordationem et amorem illius aeternae quietis. Et tibi tamen, o fratres, ibi etiam metetis, sed requiem metetis qui nunc laborem hujus messis seminatis. Fructus hujus laboris, illa requies erit: requies a labore, merces pro labore, cujus etiam recordatio fideles vires reparat in labore. Umbra est aestuantibus, cibus esurientibus. Sic enim ait illa, quae memoriam abundantiae suavitatis ejus eructabat: Sub umbra illius quem desideraveram sedi, et fructus ejus dulcis gutturi meo (Cantic. II, 5). O qui laboratis, o qui portatis pondus diei et aestus! sub umbra alarum Jesu invenietis requiem animabus vestris, firmamentum, ut scriptum est, virtutis, tegimen ardoris, umbraculum meridiani (Eccli. XXXIV, 19); ut ex sententia cordis sit illa confessio oris: Domine, Domine, virtus salutis meae, obumbrasti super caput meum in die belli (Psal. CXXXIX, 8), in die aestus et laboris, certaminis et tentationis. Cum enim meditatio aeternae requiei capitibus laborantium obumbrat, non solum ab aestu tentationum refrigerat, sed etiam ad laborem animos innovat, juxta illud quod scriptum est de asino forti Issachar, Vidit requiem quod esset bona, et terram quod optima, et supposuit humerum ad portandum (Gen. XLIX, 15): id est, desiderio illius quietis et haereditatis sponte humiliavit se ad laborandum. Et iste ergo dicere potuit: In omnibus requiem quaesivi, et in haereditate Domini morabor.

English translation

1. "In all things I sought rest" (Eccli. XXIV, 11). Rest is pleasant to the weary. Pleasantly, then, and opportunely for you who are weary, this day of rest and holiday has come, so that while we celebrate the rest of the holy Mother of God, not only may our bodies be refreshed by this day's rest from the labor of harvest, but our hearts too may breathe again in remembrance and love of that eternal rest. And yet for you, O brothers, there too you shall reap — but you who now sow the labor of this harvest shall reap rest. The fruit of this labor will be that rest: rest from labor, wages for labor — the very remembrance of which restores the faithful's strength in labor. It is shade for those who are scorched, food for those who hunger. For thus she speaks, who poured forth the memory of his abundant sweetness: "Under the shadow of him whom I desired I sat, and his fruit was sweet to my throat" (Cantic. II, 5). O you who labor, O you who bear the burden of the day and the heat! Under the shadow of Jesus' wings you will find rest for your souls — a stronghold of strength, as it is written, a covering from the heat, a shade from the noonday sun (Eccli. XXXIV, 19); so that from the conviction of the heart may come that confession of the mouth: "Lord, Lord, the strength of my salvation, you have overshadowed my head in the day of battle" (Psal. CXXXIX, 8), in the day of heat and labor, of struggle and temptation. For when meditation on eternal rest overshadows the heads of those who labor, it not only cools them from the heat of temptations but also renews their minds for labor, according to what is written of the strong ass Issachar: "He saw rest, that it was good, and the land, that it was excellent, and he bowed his shoulder to bear burdens" (Gen. XLIX, 15): that is, out of desire for that rest and inheritance, he willingly humbled himself to labor. And so he too could say: In all things I sought rest, and I will abide in the inheritance of the Lord.

2. Felix qui in omnibus laboribus et viis suis requiem quaerit beatam: semper festinans ingredi, ut Apostolus monet, in illam requiem (Hebr. IV, 3), ejus desiderio corpus affligens, animum autem jam illi requiei parans et componens: cum omnibus hominibus, quod ex ipso est, pacem habens; requiem et otium Mariae voluntate praeeligens, laborem autem et negotium Marthae necessitate quidem suscipiens; sed quanta praevalet pace et quiete spiritus adimplens, et semper ad illud unum necessarium de illa multiplici distractione sese recolligens. Homo hujusmodi etiam cum laborat, quiescit: sicut e diverso impius etiam cum quiescit, laborat. Quomodo enim quiescerent, quibus Deus immittit rotam malorum: quibus juravit in ira sua, Si introibunt in requiem meam? (Psal. XCIV, 12.) Extra illam non est nisi afflictio et miseria; et in circuitu ejus tempestas valida: sicut et in natura rerum fit, ut quidquid extra simplicitatem et unitatem puncti fuerit, in motu et agitatione sit; tantoque concitatiore quilibet circulus impetu rotetur, quanto ab immobilitate principii sui, id est, a medietate centri recesserit longius. Sane in circuitu impii ambulant (Psal. XI, 9), quibus Deus immittit rotam malorum: ideoque in illam internam et aeternam requiem intrare non possunt. Ideo contritio et infelicitas in viis eorum, quia viam pacis non cognoverunt (Psal. XIII, 3): quia nec quaesierunt, ut dicere possint: In omnibus requiem quaesivi; ut videlicet in multiplicitate actuum suorum, quibus turbantur, et turbant, unum quod est necessarium, cogitent aut requirant. Justorum potius est vox illa, qui et dicere possunt, Unam petii a Domino, hanc requiram. Vultum tuum, Domine, quaesivi, vultum tuum requiram (Psal. XXVI, 4, 8): qui nonnisi quietis illius amore laborant, qui omnino praeoptant ingredi putredinem in ossibus suis, ut requiescant in die tribulationis, quam in bonis ducere dies suos, et in puncto ad infernum descendere.

English translation

2. Happy is he who in all his labors and ways seeks blessed rest: always hastening, as the Apostle counsels, to enter into that rest (Hebr. IV, 3), afflicting his body with desire for it, yet already preparing and composing his mind for that rest; having peace with all men, so far as it depends on him; preferring by choice the rest and leisure of Mary, yet undertaking, by necessity indeed, the labor and business of Martha — but carrying it out with as much peace and quiet of spirit as he can, and always gathering himself back, out of that manifold distraction, to that one thing which is necessary. Such a man, even when he labors, rests; just as, on the contrary, the wicked man, even when he rests, labors. For how could they rest, upon whom God sends the wheel of evils, to whom he swore in his wrath, "If they shall enter into my rest" (Psal. XCIV, 12.)? Outside that rest there is nothing but affliction and misery; and round about it a violent storm — just as happens also in the nature of things, that whatever is outside the simplicity and unity of the point is in motion and agitation; and the farther any circle recedes from the immobility of its origin, that is, from the center-point, the more violent is the impetus with which it is driven round. Indeed, the wicked walk round about (Psal. XI, 9), upon whom God sends the wheel of evils: and therefore they cannot enter into that inward and eternal rest. Therefore ruin and unhappiness are in their ways, because they have not known the way of peace (Psal. XIII, 3): because they have not even sought it, so that they might say, In all things I sought rest — that is, so that amid the multiplicity of their actions, by which they are disturbed and by which they disturb others, they might think of or seek that one thing which is necessary. Rather that voice belongs to the just, who can also say, "One thing I have asked of the Lord, this will I seek. Your face, Lord, have I sought; your face will I seek" (Psal. XXVI, 4, 8): who labor only out of love of that rest — who altogether prefer that rottenness enter into their bones, that they may rest in the day of tribulation, rather than spend their days in good things and in a moment go down to hell.

3. Si quis tamen curiosius inquirere velit, cujus potissimum vox illa sit, In omnibus requiem quaesivi: vox est utique Sapientiae, vox est Ecclesiae, vox est Mariae, vox est cujuslibet sapientis animae. Sapientia in omnibus requiem quaesivit, sed in solis humilibus invenit. Ecclesia in omnibus mundi nationibus requiem quaesivit, sed in solis credentibus invenit. Maria, sicut et quaelibet fidelis anima, in omnibus actionibus suis requiem quaesivit; sed hodie demum invenit, cum post persecutionem Herodis, et fugam Aegypti, post tot insidias et atrocitates Judaicae impietatis, post tot passiones suas, de passione et morte Filii; postquam tot et tam acerbi ipsius animam pertransierunt gladii, tandem hodie dicere datum est ei: Convertere, anima, in requiem tuam, quia Dominus benefecit tibi (Psal. CXIV, 7). Qui creavit me, creatus ex me, requievit in tabernaculo corporis mei, negare non poterit mihi requiem coeli sui. Qui enim gratiam ultro cumulat aliis, par pari quomodo non referet matri? Perge, Maria, perge secura in bonis Filii tui; fiducialiter age tanquam regina, mater Regis et sponsa. Requiem quaerebas, sed amplioris gloriae est, quod tibi debetur, regnum et potestas. Indivisum habere tecum cupit imperium, cui tecum in carne una, et uno spiritu indivisum fuit pietatis et unitatis mysterium: dum scilicet salvo honore naturae, geminato munere gratiae, juncta est mater in matrimonium. Requiesce igitur, o felix, inter brachia Sponsi. Replicabit tibi, ni fallor, inter amplexus et oscula, quam suaviter requieverit in tabernaculo corporis tui, quam suavis in cubiculo cordis tui. Non est injustus Deus, o fratres, ut obliviscatur operis boni: vivit semper apud eum memoria beneficii semel accepti. Beatus, apud quem Deus vel semel requiem invenerit, in cujus tabernaculo vel hora una requieverit.

English translation

3. Yet if anyone wishes to inquire more carefully whose voice above all this is, "In all things I sought rest": it is indeed the voice of Wisdom, the voice of the Church, the voice of Mary, the voice of any wise soul. Wisdom sought rest in all things, but found it only in the humble. The Church sought rest among all the nations of the world, but found it only among believers. Mary, like any faithful soul, sought rest in all her actions; but only today has she finally found it, when, after the persecution of Herod, and the flight into Egypt, after so many plots and atrocities of Jewish impiety, after so many sufferings of her own concerning the passion and death of her Son—after so many bitter swords had pierced her soul—it was at last granted to her today to say: "Return, O my soul, into thy rest, for the Lord hath been bountiful to thee" (Psal. CXIV, 7). He who created me, having been created from me, rested in the tabernacle of my body; he cannot deny me the rest of his heaven. For he who freely heaps grace upon others—how will he not repay his mother in like measure? Go forward, Mary, go forward secure in the goods of your Son; act with confidence as a queen, mother of the King and his spouse. You were seeking rest, but what is owed to you is of a greater glory—kingdom and power. He desires to have undivided rule with you, he with whom you had, in one flesh and one spirit, an undivided mystery of devotion and unity: since, with the honor of nature preserved, and the gift of grace doubled, the mother was joined to him in marriage. Rest, then, O blessed one, between the arms of the Bridegroom. He will tell you, unless I am mistaken, amid embraces and kisses, how sweetly he rested in the tabernacle of your body, how sweet he was in the chamber of your heart. God is not unjust, O brothers, that he should forget a good work: the memory of a benefit once received lives always with him. Blessed is he with whom God has found rest even once, in whose tabernacle he has rested even for a single hour.

4. Ecce nunc quoque Sapientia clamitat in plateis: in omnibus requiem quaesivi. Pulsavi, nec fuit qui aperiret: vocavi, nec fuit qui responderet. Filius hominis, factus, secundum prophetam, velut vir vagus, et quasi viator declinans ad manendum (Jerem. XIV, 9, 8), non habet ubi reclinet caput suum: foris stat plenus caput rore, et cincinnos guttis noctium. Quis ille in nobis tam humanus atque hospitalis, ut surgat et aperiat ei, et inducat in cubiculum suum; aut certe ostendat ei coenaculum grande stratum, ubi cum discipulis pascha manducet novum? Hoc enim dico, fratres, nisi requiem quam quaerit, invenerit apud nos, nec nos optatam requiem inveniemus in illo. Ait quidem Dominus per prophetam: Haec est requies mea, reficite lassum, et hoc est meum refrigerium (Isa. XXVIII, 12). Beatus qui intelligit super egenum et pauperem: in die mala (Psal. XL, 2) parabit ei Dominus vicissitudine debita requiem et refrigerium. Si ergo humanitatem membris suis exhibitam Deus imputat sibi: quanto magis quod ipsius fit spiritui, cum gratiarum actione recolet, Hospes, inquiens, fui et suscepistis me? (Matth. XXV, 35.) Nunquid enim multorum paupertas sanctorum, quae vagos colligere, esurientes pascere non sufficit, Domino qui magis apud pauperes hospitari consuevit, inhumana et inhospitalis erit? Super quem autem requiescam, inquit, nisi super humilem et quietum, et trementem verba mea? (Isai. LXVI, 2.) O humilitas angusta tibi, ampla divinitati; pauper et insufficiens tibi, sufficiens ei quem non capit orbis: copiose ac deliciose reficiens illum, qui et Angelos pascit. Super quem, inquit, requiescam nisi super humilem? In omnibus requiem quaesivi: sed apud humilem ancillam inveni. Non est inventa similis illi in gratia humilitatis: ideo in plenitudine humilitatis requievit etiam corporaliter omnis plenitudo divinitatis. Quanquam aliter requieverit in Filio: quia etsi Mater humillima, longe tamen Filius humilior. Idcirco Spiritus septiformis non solum super ipsum requievit, sed etiam omnibus qui mites et humiles ab eo esse didicerunt, diversas in eo felicissimae quietis mansiones praeparavit; imo totum ipsum quoddam aureum fecit reclinatorium ad quiescendum. Hujus nimirum reclinatorii beatissimam requiem visus est quadam specie praelibasse, qui supra pectus ejus meruit in coena recumbere.

English translation

4. Behold, even now Wisdom cries aloud in the streets: "In all things I sought rest." I knocked, and there was none to open; I called, and there was none to answer. The Son of man, made, according to the prophet, like a wandering man, and like a traveler turning aside to lodge (Jerem. XIV, 9, 8), has nowhere to lay his head: he stands outside, his head full of dew, and his locks with the drops of the night. Who among us is so humane and hospitable as to rise and open to him, and bring him into his chamber; or at least show him a large furnished upper room, where he may eat the new Passover with his disciples? For I say this, brothers: unless he finds among us the rest he seeks, neither shall we find in him the rest we desire. The Lord indeed says through the prophet: "This is my rest, refresh the weary, and this is my refreshment" (Isa. XXVIII, 12). Blessed is he who understands concerning the needy and the poor: in the evil day (Psal. XL, 2) the Lord will prepare for him, in due return, rest and refreshment. If then God counts as done to himself the kindness shown to his members: how much more will he, giving thanks, recall what was done to his own spirit, saying, "I was a stranger, and you took me in" (Matth. XXV, 35.)? For shall the poverty of many saints, which does not suffice to gather in the wandering or feed the hungry, be inhospitable and inhumane to the Lord, who is accustomed rather to lodge among the poor? "But upon whom shall I rest," he says, "but upon him that is humble and quiet, and trembleth at my words?" (Isai. LXVI, 2.) O humility, narrow to yourself, but spacious to divinity; poor and insufficient in yourself, yet sufficient for him whom the world cannot contain: abundantly and delightfully refreshing him who feeds even the Angels. "Upon whom," he says, "shall I rest but upon the humble?" "In all things I sought rest": but I found it with a humble handmaid. None was found like her in the grace of humility: therefore in the fullness of her humility the whole fullness of divinity rested even bodily. Though it rested differently in the Son: for although the Mother was most humble, the Son was far more humble still. For this reason the sevenfold Spirit not only rested upon him, but also prepared various dwellings of most blessed rest in him for all who learned from him to be meek and humble; indeed, he made him wholly a kind of golden resting-place for repose. The most blessed rest of this resting-place he who deserved to recline upon his breast at the supper seemed, in some fashion, to have tasted beforehand.

5. Sed adhuc libet intueri, quam plenum rationis et congruentiae sit quod ait: Super quem requiescam nisi super humilem et quietum? Quomodo namque super inquietum aliquid quiesceret? quomodo super basem, quae nutaret aut vacillaret, columna immobilis staret? Quis autem, nisi humilis, quietus esse potest? quis, nisi humilis in pace quieti et modesti spiritus, seipsum possidere potest? Impium projicit ventus a facie terrae (Psal. I, 4), circumferturque omni vento doctrinae. Impius, inquit propheta, quasi mare fervens, quod quiescere non potest (Isai. LVII, 20). Fervet ira, aestuat avaritia, intumescit superbia, se ipsum jugiter pugna quatit intestina, ac seditione collidit domestica. Ut ergo requiescat in vobis, o fratres mei, amator ille largitorque quietis, date operam, juxta consilium Apostoli, ut quieti sitis. Quonam modo istud erit? Dico, inquit, vobis: Ut vestrum negotium agatis, et operemini manibus vestris (I Thess. IV, 11). Opus est onus, quo veluti pondus navibus, ita quies et gravitas inquietis additur cordibus, sed exterioris hominis fundatur atque componitur status. Magnorum initium malorum est, sicut legistis, mulier vaga, quietis impatiens, nec valens consistere in domo pedibus suis: nunc foris, nunc in plateis, nunc juxta angulos insidians (Prov. VII, 10-12). Nec sine causa est quod Magister gentium hoc inquietudinis malum sic habet suspectum, ut non solum correptione, sed etiam separatione censeat illud persequendum. Rogamus, inquit, vos, fratres, corripite inquietos (I Thess. V, 14). Et item in secunda ad eosdem Thessalonicenses, inter alia quae super inquietos velut e coelis tonabat: Audivimus, inquit, inter vos quosdam ambulantes inquiete, nihil operantes, sed curiose agentes. Hic qui ejusmodi sunt denuntiamus, ut cum silentio operantes, suum panem manducent. Quod si quis non obedit verbo nostro per epistolam, hunc notate: et ne commisceamini cum illo, ut confundatur (II Thess. III, 11, 12, 14).

English translation

5. But it is still worth considering how full of reason and fittingness is what he says: Upon whom shall I rest, but upon him that is humble and quiet? For how could he rest upon something unquiet? How could an immovable column stand upon a base that trembled or wobbled? Who, indeed, except the humble, can be quiet? Who, except the humble man at peace, of a quiet and modest spirit, can possess himself? The wind casts away the wicked from the face of the earth (Psal. I, 4), and he is carried about by every wind of doctrine. The wicked man, says the prophet, is like the raging sea, which cannot rest (Isai. LVII, 20). He burns with anger, seethes with avarice, swells with pride, perpetually shakes himself with inward battle, and collides in domestic sedition. Therefore, my brothers, that he who loves and bestows rest may rest in you, strive, according to the counsel of the Apostle, to be quiet. How shall this be? I say to you, he says: that you do your own business, and work with your own hands (I Thess. IV, 11). Work is a burden, by which, just as weight is added to ships, so quiet and gravity are added to unquiet hearts, and the condition of the outer man is founded and composed. The beginning of great evils is, as you have read, a wandering woman, impatient of rest, unable to abide with her feet in her own house: now abroad, now in the streets, now lying in wait near the corners (Prov. VII, 10-12). It is not without cause that the Teacher of the Gentiles holds this evil of restlessness in such suspicion that he thinks it should be pursued not only by rebuke but even by separation. We beseech you, brothers, he says, rebuke the unquiet (I Thess. V, 14). And again in the second epistle to the same Thessalonians, among other things which he thundered down as from heaven against the unquiet: We have heard, he says, that there are some among you who walk unquietly, doing no work, but being busy about other men's business. Now them that are such, we charge, that working with silence, they eat their own bread. And if any man obey not our word by this epistle, note that man: and do not keep company with him, that he may be ashamed (II Thess. III, 11, 12, 14).

6. Si quis forte (quod absit!) ejusmodi invenitur inter vos, pulchrius et honestius, antequam tam graviter et publice notetur, ultro confundetur, inde scilicet, quia notabilis est, et dissimulatur; reprehensibilis, et toleratur. Sic autem confundatur, ut confusione corrigatur, correptione laetetur, laetificetque non modo nos, sed Spiritum Dei: qui cum dicat, In omnibus requiem quaesivi, non invenit eam nisi in quietis, nec praestat nisi quietis. Unde et inquietos per prophetam revocat et compescit: Si revertamini, et quiescatis, salvi eritis (Isai. XXX, 15). Omnes ergo pariter sic operam demus, ut quieti simus, ut in quiete nostra semper in meditatione aeternae quietis occupemur, ac desiderio illius ad omnem parati laborem inveniamur. Hoc nobis impetret beata Dei Genitrix, cujus requiem celebramus, ab eo qui requievit in tabernaculo corporis et cordis ejus. Ipse est requies aeterna Christus Jesus; cui est honor, et gloria per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

6. If perhaps (which God forbid!) someone of this sort is found among you, it would be fairer and more decent that, before he is so gravely and publicly noted, he should be shamed of his own accord beforehand—precisely because he is notable, and yet is overlooked; reprehensible, and yet is tolerated. But let him be so shamed that by shame he may be corrected, that he may rejoice in correction, and may give joy not only to us but to the Spirit of God: who, since he says, In all things I sought rest, finds it only in the quiet, and grants it only to the quiet. Hence through the prophet he recalls and restrains the unquiet: If you return and be quiet, you shall be saved (Isai. XXX, 15). Let us all, then, equally take care to be quiet, so that in our quiet we may always be occupied in meditation on eternal rest, and may be found ready, by desire of that rest, for every labor. May the blessed Mother of God, whose rest we celebrate, obtain this for us from him who rested in the tabernacle of her body and heart. He himself is eternal rest, Christ Jesus; to whom be honor and glory for all ages of ages. Amen.
