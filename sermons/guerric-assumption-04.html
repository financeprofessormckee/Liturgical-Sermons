Editorial introduction

Guerric applies "Mary has chosen the best part" to the Mother of the Lord, who served Christ as Martha did and listened as her sister did. He explains why Christ seemed to set his mother aside, and why he entrusted her to the virgin disciple John. Contemplation is the reward of faithful labor, as Leah came before Rachel, and Mary now reaps what she sowed. The sermon appears under the heading for the Assumption of Blessed Mary in the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157; the individual sermon is undated.

SERMON IV

¶cols. 197-198
Latin original

Quod beata Virgo Mariae et Marthae officia implevit.

English translation

That the Blessed Virgin fulfilled the offices of Mary and of Martha.

¶cols. 197-198
Latin original

1. Maria optimam partem elegit (Luc. X, 42). Hoc de Maria sorore Marthae scriptum est: sed in Maria matre Domini plenius et sanctius impletum est. Hodie namque beata virgo Maria optimam partem elegit, imo quam dudum elegerat, hodie perpetuo possidendam accepit: ut scilicet inseparabiliter adhaereat Domino, perenniter fruatur Dei Verbo. Nec alienum aut incongruum, si quod de illa Maria dictum est, in istam transumatur; cum ad convenientiam transformationis, similitudo non solum nominis, sed etiam operis suffragetur. Illa siquidem Dominum hospitio tecti, haec thalamo suscepit uteri. Et qui creavit me, inquit, requievit in tabernaculo meo (Eccli. XXIV, 12). Illa sedens secus pedes Domini audiebat verbum illius (Luc. X, 39): haec sedula circa curam humanitatis ejus, conservabat omnia verba quae de illo erant, conferens in corde suo (Eccli. II, 19, 511). Sed et cum evangelizans circumiret Jesus civitates et castella, Maria comes individua vestigiis ejus adhaerebat, pendebatque ex ore docentis: adeo, ut nec procella persecutionis, nec horrore supplicii a consectatu Filii et Magistri potuerit absterreri, Stabat, inquit, juxta crucem Jesu Maria mater ejus (Joan. XIX, 25). Plane Mater, quae nec in terrore mortis filium deserebat. Quomodo enim morte terreri poterat, cujus charitas fortis ut mors, imo fortior quam mors erat? Plane juxta crucem Jesu stabat, cujus mentem dolor crucis simul cruci figebat, suamque ipsius animam tam multiplex pertransibat gladius, quantis confossum corpus Filii cernebat vulneribus. Merito igitur ibi mater agnita est, et cura ipsius idoneo tutori delegata est, ubi maxime probata est et Matris ad Filium sincera charitas, et Filii de Matre vera humanitas. Nam alias velut dissimulasse Matrem visus est: sive cum in nuptiis flagitanti miraculum ait, Quid mihi et tibi, mulier? (Joan. II, 4.) sive cum in mediis sermonibus Evangelii dicenti cuidam, Ecce mater tua et fratres tui stant quaerentes te: Quae est, inquit, mater mea? (Matth. XII, 47, 48.) Sed et Matri, cum miraculum posceret, sic respondendum erat, ut aliunde quam a Matre se miracula habere significaret: et illi qui sermonem Evangelii parentes annuntiando interrumpebat, melius respondere non poterat, ut videlicet spiritualia carnalibus praeferenda monstraret. Ac si more suo diceret parentibus, occupatum in negotio Evangelii quaerentibus: Quid est quod me quaeritis? Nescitis quia in his quae Patris mei sunt oportet me esse? (Luc. II, 49.)

English translation

1. Mary has chosen the best part (Luc. X, 42). This was written of Mary the sister of Martha; but it was fulfilled more fully and more holily in Mary the Mother of the Lord. For today the blessed Virgin Mary has chosen the best part—or rather, the part which she had long ago chosen, she today received to possess forever: namely, that she might cling inseparably to the Lord, and enjoy the Word of God perpetually. Nor is it foreign or unfitting that what was said of that other Mary should be transferred to this one; since, for the fittingness of the transfer, the likeness supports it not only in name but also in deed. For the one received the Lord under the hospitality of a roof; this one, in the bridal chamber of her womb. And 'He who created me,' she says, 'rested in my tabernacle' (Eccli. XXIV, 12). The one, sitting beside the Lord's feet, was listening to his word (Luc. X, 39); this one, diligently occupied with the care of his humanity, kept all the words concerning him, pondering them in her heart (Eccli. II, 19, 511). But also, when Jesus went about preaching the gospel through the cities and towns, Mary, an inseparable companion, clung to his footsteps, and hung upon the mouth of the teacher: so much so that neither the storm of persecution nor the horror of the punishment could frighten her away from following her Son and Master. 'There stood,' it says, 'by the cross of Jesus, Mary his mother' (Joan. XIX, 25). Truly a Mother, who did not abandon her son even in the terror of death. For how could she be terrified by death, whose love was strong as death—indeed, stronger than death? Truly she stood beside the cross of Jesus, whose mind the sorrow of the cross fixed to the cross at the same time, and through her own soul there passed a sword as manifold as the wounds with which she saw the body of her Son pierced. Deservedly, then, she was there acknowledged as mother, and the care of her was entrusted to a fitting guardian, at the very place where both the Mother's sincere love for her Son and the Son's true humanity toward his Mother were most fully proven. For elsewhere he seemed, as it were, to disregard his Mother: whether when, at the wedding, as she urged him to a miracle, he said, 'Woman, what is that to me and to you?' (Joan. II, 4.); or when, in the midst of the discourses of the Gospel, to one who said, 'Behold, your mother and your brothers stand seeking you,' he said, 'Who is my mother?' (Matth. XII, 47, 48.). But to his Mother, when she was asking for a miracle, he had to answer in such a way as to show that he had his miracles from some source other than his Mother; and to the one who was interrupting the discourse of the Gospel by announcing his kinsfolk, he could not have answered better, in order to show that spiritual things are to be preferred to carnal ones. As if, according to his custom, he were saying to his parents, who sought him while he was occupied in the business of the Gospel: 'Why is it that you seek me? Did you not know that I must be about those things which are my Father's?' (Luc. II, 49.).

2. Absit enim ut Matrem aspernatus sit, qui tanta cura de parentibus honorandis legem dedit! Absit, inquam, ut in terra fastidierit Matrem, cujus e coelo concupivit decorem! Imo potius ordinabat in nobis charitatem, tam verbis quam exemplis nobiscum agens, ut carnalium affectibus necessitudinum, non solum Dei praeponamus dilectionem, sed etiam eorum qui Dei faciunt voluntatem. Ille siquidem affectus de cordibus exigitur omnium nostrum, quos summi Patris adoptavit dignatio, quo fideliter dicamus cum ipsius Unigenito: Quicunque fecerit voluntatem Patris mei qui in coelis est, ipse meus frater, et soror, et mater est (Matth. XII, 49). Prorsus filiis Dei competit vox ista, nec ullum fidelius testimonium reddit ipse Spiritus spiritui nostro quod simus filii Dei, quam ut sonet de cordibus nostris haec vox Unigeniti Dei. Itaque Mariam, quae secundum carnem mater erat, alia quoque ratione matrem sibi Jesus probat: quandoquidem et ipsa voluntatem Patris in tantum faciebat, ut de ipsa praediceret Pater, Vocaberis voluntas mea in ea (Isai. LXII, 4). Igitur ubi eam visus est ignorasse Filius, ibi amplius honorasse invenitur: cum ei scilicet materni nominis honor duplicatur, pro eo quod eumdem Filium, quem alvo gestaverat incarnatum, etiam nunc animo gestabat inspiratum.

English translation

2. Far be it that he should have scorned his Mother, he who gave the law with such care concerning the honoring of parents! Far be it, I say, that he should have disdained on earth the Mother whose beauty he had desired from heaven! Rather, he was ordering love within us, acting with us both by words and by examples, so that we might set the love of God—and also the love of those who do the will of God—above the affections of carnal ties. For that affection is demanded of the hearts of all of us whom the condescension of the most high Father has adopted, so that we may faithfully say with his Only-Begotten: 'Whoever shall do the will of my Father who is in heaven, he is my brother, and sister, and mother' (Matth. XII, 49). This utterance indeed belongs to the children of God, nor does the Spirit himself give any more faithful testimony to our spirit that we are children of God, than when this utterance of the Only-Begotten of God resounds from our hearts. And so Jesus proves Mary, who was his mother according to the flesh, to be his mother also by another title: since she herself did the will of the Father to such a degree that the Father foretold concerning her, 'You shall be called my will in her' (Isai. LXII, 4). Therefore, where the Son seemed to have disregarded her, there he is found to have honored her the more: since for her the honor of the maternal name is doubled, in that the same Son whom she had carried incarnate in her womb, she even now carried in her soul by inspiration.

3. Caeterum cum dilexisset eam Jesus, in finem dilexit eam, ut non solum propter ipsam finem vivendi, verum et in ipsam finem pene faceret loquendi: dum velut inter ultima verba testamenti, curam Matris, cujus debitorem se cognoscebat, charissimo transcribit haeredi. Sic quippe inter Petrum qui plus diligebat, et Joannem qui plus diligebatur, haereditatem suam Christus divisit, ut Petrus sortiretur Ecclesiam, Joannes Mariam. Huic plane competebat haec portio non solum jure propinquitatis, sed etiam amoris privilegio testimonioque pudoris. Decebat namque ut nonnisi virgo virgini deserviret, quatenus et Virgo beata, divino languens prae amore, floribus fulciretur castitatis, et virginitas adolescentis id interim perciperet remunerationis, ut de contubernio proficeret tantae sanctitatis. Qui etiam, quia fidelis probatus est in obsequio incorruptae matris; mysteria quoque divinitatis, et arcana Verbi incorruptibilis sibi credi promeruit. Decebat, inquam, ut Matri Domini non alius obsequeretur quam dilectus Filii: quatenus et Mater semper suspirans Filium in dilectum Filii suavius respiraret, et discipulus Magistrum sibi conquerens nimis cito subtractum, Magistram totius veritatis se reperisse gauderet. Idque satis commode provisum, ut scripturus Evangelium familiarius de singulis conferret cum ea, quae conscia erat omnium; quippe quae ab initio omnia Filii sui diligentius observaverat, conservans omnia verba quae de illo erant, conferens in corde suo (Luc. II, 19, 51). Sic nempe se Martham exhibuit in cura pueri nutriendi; ut nihilominus Mariae partes exsecuta credatur in studio verbi cognoscendi. Maxime tamen postquam idem Filius ascendit ubi erat prius, Mater absoluta omni sollicitudine temporali, sanctoque Spiritu, quem praeter singulares illas conceptus sui primitias communiter cum Apostolis acceperat, plenius illuminata, gaudebat nimirum vacare, et videre quoniam Jesus est Deus. Visio prorsus ineffabilis gaudii, summaeque delectationis omnibus qui diligunt Jesum; sed prae omnibus illi quae genuit Jesum: cui sicut singulariter excepta est gratia Deum generandi; sic et praerogativa gloriandi in eo quem genuit. Omnino singularis atque incomparabilis gloria Virginis matris, videre Deum regem omnium in diademate carnis quo coronavit eum; ut et Deum agnoscat et adoret in corpore proprio, et corpus proprium glorificatum videat in Deo. His interim, ut opinor, contemplationibus pascebatur Maria, hanc optimam partem elegerat: quae non ablata, sed perfecta est hodie in ea. Quia enim negligens aut segnis non exstitit in opere Marthae, nequaquam vacua derelicta est a fructu Mariae. Labor in actione, fructus seu merces in contemplatione. Propterea, inquit, quod laboravit anima ejus, videbit et saturabitur (Isa. LIII, 11).

English translation

3. Moreover, when Jesus had loved her, he loved her unto the end, so that for her sake he not only made an end of living, but in her regard almost made an end of speaking as well: since, as it were among the last words of his testament, he transferred the care of his Mother—whose debtor he knew himself to be—to his most beloved heir. For thus Christ divided his inheritance between Peter, who loved more, and John, who was loved more: so that Peter obtained the Church as his lot, and John, Mary. This portion plainly belonged to John not only by right of kinship, but also by the privilege of love and the testimony of chastity. For it was fitting that none but a virgin should serve a virgin: so that the Blessed Virgin, languishing for divine love, might be propped up by the flowers of chastity, and that the virginity of the young man might meanwhile receive this as its reward, that he might profit from the companionship of such great holiness. He also, because he was proved faithful in the service of the incorrupt Mother, deserved that the mysteries of the Godhead and the secrets of the incorruptible Word should be entrusted to him. It was fitting, I say, that none other than the beloved of the Son should serve the Mother of the Lord: so that the Mother, ever sighing for her Son, might breathe more sweetly in the beloved of the Son, and the disciple, lamenting that his Master had been taken from him too soon, might rejoice that he had found a Mistress of all truth. And this was quite fittingly provided, that he who was to write the Gospel might confer more intimately about particular matters with her who was privy to all things; since from the beginning she had more diligently observed all things concerning her Son, keeping all the words that were about him, and pondering them in her heart (Luc. II, 19, 51). Thus indeed she showed herself a Martha in the care of nursing the child; yet is nonetheless believed to have carried out the part of Mary in the pursuit of knowing the Word. But most of all, after that same Son ascended to where he was before, the Mother, freed from all temporal care, and more fully illumined by the Holy Spirit—whom, besides those singular first-fruits of her conceiving, she had received in common with the Apostles—rejoiced indeed to be at leisure, and to see that Jesus is God. A vision truly of ineffable joy and of the highest delight to all who love Jesus; but above all to her who bore Jesus: to whom, just as the grace of begetting God was singularly granted, so too was the prerogative of glorying in him whom she bore. Altogether singular and incomparable is the glory of the Virgin Mother, to see God, the King of all, in the diadem of flesh with which she crowned him; so that she both recognizes and adores God in her own body, and sees her own body glorified in God. With such contemplations, I suppose, Mary was being fed meanwhile; this best part she had chosen, which is not taken away but today is perfected in her. For because she was not negligent or slothful in the work of Martha, she was by no means left empty of the fruit of Mary. The labor is in action; the fruit, or reward, is in contemplation. Therefore, he says, because her soul labored, she shall see and be satisfied (Isa. LIII, 11).

4. Vobis haec dicimus, fratres, ut si quis desiderio tangitur illius optimae partis quae in Maria laudatur, sciat quia haec praemium est illius quae in Martha non improbatur: nec aequum est ut praemium ante meritum requiratur. Jacob prius oportet jungi Liae, quam fruatur amplexibus Rachel (Gen. XXIX): sed et ipsum prius vocari et esse Jacob, quam Israel. Rectorem, inquit, te posuerunt? curam illorum habe, et sic considera, et omni cura tua explicita recumbe, ut laeteris propter illos, et ornamentum gratiae accipias coronam (Eccli. XXXII, 1-3). Labor siquidem operis, seu sollicitudo administrationis, semina sunt justitiae: pro quibus gaudia metenda sunt ex ore consolantis misericordiae. Sic enim ait propheta: Seminate vobis in justitia, metite in ore misericordiae (Osee X, 12). At enim qui parce seminat, parce et metet: et qui seminat in benedictione, de benedictionibus et metet (II Cor. IX, 6). Nullus sane tam profusa benedictione seminavit, sicut illa benedicta in mulieribus; quae benedictum semen de ventre profudit. Sed semen dicam, an fructum? Melius utrumque. Qui enim semen est operantibus justitiam, fructus erit metentibus gloriam. Semen, in passione: fructus, in resurrectione. Potens in terra semen istud, quod cadens in terram mox excitavit potentiam suam, ut multum fructum afferret, et in semine illo benedicerentur omnes gentes. Unde et ibi sequitur: Generatio rectorum benedicetur (Psal. CXI, 2).

English translation

4. We say these things to you, brethren, so that if anyone is touched by desire for that best part which is praised in Mary, he may know that this is the reward of that part which is not disapproved in Martha: nor is it right that the reward be sought before the merit. Jacob must first be joined to Leah, before he enjoys the embraces of Rachel (Gen. XXIX): and he himself must first be called and be Jacob, before he is Israel. Have they made you a ruler? he says; take care of them, and so consider, and when all your care has been discharged, take your rest, that you may rejoice because of them, and receive the crown as the ornament of grace (Eccli. XXXII, 1-3). For the labor of work, or the solicitude of administration, are the seeds of righteousness: for which joys are to be reaped from the mouth of consoling mercy. For thus says the prophet: Sow for yourselves in righteousness, reap in the mouth of mercy (Osee X, 12). But indeed he who sows sparingly shall also reap sparingly; and he who sows in blessing shall also reap of blessings (II Cor. IX, 6). No one indeed has sown with so abundant a blessing as she who is blessed among women, who poured forth the blessed seed from her womb. But shall I call it seed, or fruit? Better to call it both. For he who is seed to those laboring in righteousness will be fruit to those reaping glory. Seed, in the Passion; fruit, in the Resurrection. Mighty on earth was that seed, which, falling into the earth, soon roused its power, so that it might bring forth much fruit, and in that seed all nations might be blessed. Whence it follows there also: The generation of the upright shall be blessed (Psal. CXI, 2).

5. De benedictionibus suis itaque Maria metat: et quae benedictionem omnium gentium seminavit, benedictionem omnium gentium singulariter accipiat. Beatam me dicent, inquit, omnes generationes (Luc. I, 48). Parum est hoc. Beatam te dicent omnes beatorum spirituum ordines. Viderunt hodie ascendentem filiae Sion coelestis et beatam dixerunt, et reginae laudaverunt eam (Cantic. VI, 8). Prorsus de benedictionibus suis hodie Maria metit, quia refusa est spiritualiter in eam omnimoda benedictio illa quam de se profudit. Date ei, inquit Spiritus sanctus, de fructu ventris sui; et saturetur illo quem genuit. O Mater misericordiae, saturare gloria Filii tui; et dimitte reliquias tuas parvulis tuis. Tu jam ad mensam, Domina; nos sub mensa catelli. Sicut oculi ancillae in manibus dominae suae, ita familia haec famelica de te praestolatur alimoniam vitae. Per te fructum vitae communicavimus, in mensa praesentium sacramentorum: per te eumdem fructum vitae communicemus in mensa perennium gaudiorum, Jesum benedictum fructum ventris tui; cui est honor et gloria, per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

5. So let Mary reap from her own blessings: and let her who sowed the blessing of all nations receive singularly the blessing of all nations. "All generations shall call me blessed," she says (Luc. I, 48). This is too little. All the ranks of the blessed spirits shall call you blessed. Today the daughters of heavenly Sion saw her ascending, and called her blessed; and queens praised her (Cantic. VI, 8). Indeed, today Mary reaps from her own blessings, because that whole blessing which she poured forth from herself has been poured back upon her spiritually. "Give her," says the Holy Spirit, "of the fruit of her womb; and let her be satisfied with him whom she bore." O Mother of mercy, be satisfied with the glory of your Son; and leave your leftovers to your little ones. You are now at the table, O Lady; we are the little dogs beneath the table. As the eyes of the handmaid are on the hands of her mistress, so this famished household awaits from you the nourishment of life. Through you we have shared the fruit of life at the table of the sacraments now present: through you may we share that same fruit of life at the table of everlasting joys—Jesus, the blessed fruit of your womb; to whom be honor and glory, forever and ever. Amen.
