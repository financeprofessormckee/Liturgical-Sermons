Editorial introduction

This second Epiphany sermon belongs to Guerric of Igny's medieval collection of sermons for the liturgical year, here read from the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. Its individual date is unknown; the collection is associated approximately with his abbacy in 1138–1157. From Isaiah's "Arise, be enlightened, Jerusalem", Guerric gathers the day's three manifestations: the star to the Magi, the Jordan baptism, and the water made wine. The Magi's faith, which saw only weakness and still adored, shows how faith begins and grows. He bids his hearers look up to the lights of heaven, the saints, before they can bear the Sun itself. An editorial note records a printer's error. The source explicitly names Epiphany.

De variis modis quibus nos Deus illuminat.

English translation

On the Various Ways in Which God Enlightens Us

¶cols. 51-52
Latin original

1. Surge, illuminare, Jerusalem, quia venit lumen tuum (Isa. LX, 1). Hodiernum diem luminum illuminavit nobis et consecravit lumen de lumine, quod hodie cum lateret incognitum, ad illuminationem omnium gentium sese dignatum est mundo revelare. Hodie namque se revelavit Chaldaeis novi sideris indicio, cum eorum primitiis omnium fidem gentium dedicavit. Hodie se revelavit Judaeis, non solum Joannis, sed et Patris et Spiritus sancti testimonio, ubi in Jordane baptizatus, baptisma omnium consecravit. Hodie manifestavit gloriam suam coram discipulis suis, quando mutatatione1 aquae in vinum, illud ineffabile praeostendit mysterium, in quo, verbo ipsius, substantiae mutantur rerum. His omnibus Dei apparitionibus praevidens Spiritus illuminandam Ecclesiae fidem, alloquitur eam sub typo et nomine Jerusalem, dicens: Surge, illuminare, Jerusalem, quia venit lumen tuum. Venerat quidem lumen; in mundo erat, et mundus per ipsum factus erat; sed mundus ipsum non cognoscebat. Erat natus, sed non erat notus, donec declarare eum coepit luminosa dies ista. Ait ergo propheta: O Jerusalem nova, civitas magna novi regis, mons Sion, latera aquilonis (Psal. XLVII, 3), id est, de utroque aedificanda pariete circumcisionis et praeputii; Surge, illuminare, quia venit lumen tuum. Surgite, qui sedetis in tenebris; intendite lumini, quod exortum est in tenebris, sed non comprehenditur a tenebris. Accedite ad eum, et illuminamini (Psal. XXXIII, 6), et in lumine ejus videbitis lumen, diceturque vobis, Fuistis aliquando tenebrae, nunc autem lux in Domino (Ephes. V, 8). Intendite lumini aeterno quod se vestris aspectibus temperavit, ut, qui lucem habitat inaccessibilem, infirmis quoque et lippientibus oculis posset accedi. Advertite lumen in lucerna testae, solem in nube, Deum in homine, in luteo vasculo carnis nostrae splendorem gloriae, candoremque lucis aeternae.

English translation

1. "Arise, be enlightened, Jerusalem, for your light has come" (Isa. LX, 1). The Light from Light has illumined and consecrated this day of lights for us: the Light which, though today it lay hidden and unknown, deigned to reveal itself to the world for the enlightenment of all nations. For today he revealed himself to the Chaldeans by the sign of a new star, when in their firstfruits he dedicated the faith of all the nations. Today he revealed himself to the Jews, not only by John's testimony, but by the testimony of the Father and the Holy Spirit, when, baptized in the Jordan, he consecrated the baptism of all. Today he manifested his glory before his disciples, when, by the changing of water into wine1, he showed forth beforehand that ineffable mystery in which, by his own word, the substances of things are changed. Foreseeing that the faith of the Church would be enlightened by all these appearings of God, the Spirit addresses her under the type and name of Jerusalem, saying: Arise, be enlightened, Jerusalem, for your light has come. Indeed the light had come; it was in the world, and the world was made through it; but the world did not know it. He had been born, but was not yet known, until this day of light began to declare him. The prophet therefore says: O new Jerusalem, great city of the new King, Mount Sion, on the sides of the north (Psal. XLVII, 3), that is, to be built up from both walls, of circumcision and of the uncircumcision; Arise, be enlightened, for your light has come. Arise, you who sit in darkness; attend to the light which has risen in the darkness, but is not comprehended by the darkness. Come to him, and be enlightened (Psal. XXXIII, 6), and in his light you will see light, and it will be said to you: You were once darkness, but now you are light in the Lord (Ephes. V, 8). Attend to the eternal light which has tempered itself to your sight, so that he who dwells in unapproachable light might also be approached even by weak and bleary eyes. Observe the light in the lamp of clay, the sun in the cloud, God in man, in the little earthen vessel of our flesh the splendor of glory and the brightness of eternal light.

Latin original

2. Latet quidem majestas in humanitate, virtus in humilitate; sed signa virtutum erumpentia, unde haec prodeant, non sinunt ambigere. Opera, inquit, quae ego facio, testimonium perhibent de me (Joan. X, 25). Magnum quidem testimonium Joannis, qui venit ut testimonium perhiberet, lucerna de lumine; at enim multo majus illud testimonium coeleste, quod Pater et Spiritus sanctus Filio perhibuere (Luc. III, 16, 21, 22), Pater, in voce, Spiritus, in specie, cum in ore duorum testium stet omne verbum. Sed et hoc ipsum si minus accipitur: Opera, inquit, innumerabilia et irrefragabilia, quae ego facio, testimonium perhibent de me (Joan. X, 25). Plane incorruptus et irrevincibilis testis, cum creatura insensibilis sensum quemdam accipit in confessionem Creatoris, voluntatem ejus exaudiens, ac nutui incunctanter obediens. Quid enim divinius poterat exhiberi sensibus mortalium, quam hodiernae novitatis signum, quod etiam ante signorum suorum praemisit Jesus initium? Vagit puer novus in terris, et sidus novum creat in supernis, ut lumen testetur de lumine, stella de sole, et reges in splendore ortus sui, ad exortum quoque aeternum splendorem, ab oriente ad verum Orientem, id est, virum cui nomen Oriens (Zach. VI, 12), stella perducat; non quasi stella, sed quasi rationale animal in via praecedens, in fine viae consistens, ac velut digito ipsum qui quaerebatur ostendens. Sed esto, calumnietur infidelis hanc stellam non esse opus Christi, dummodo confiteatur opus esse Patris in testimonium Filii. Attamen obruet eum multitudo infinita ac magnitudo mirabilium plane divinorum, quae absque contradictione Jesus noster operatus est in carne, sed non ex carne.

English translation

2. Majesty indeed lies hidden in humanity, power in humility; but the signs of power breaking forth do not allow us to doubt whence they proceed. "The works," he says, "that I do bear witness of me" (Joan. X, 25). Great indeed is the testimony of John, who came to bear witness, a lamp testifying of the Light; but far greater is that heavenly testimony which the Father and the Holy Spirit bore to the Son (Luc. III, 16, 21, 22), the Father in the voice, the Spirit in the visible form, since every word stands firm in the mouth of two witnesses. But if even this be deemed insufficient: "the works," he says, "innumerable and irrefutable, which I do, bear witness of me" (Joan. X, 25). Clearly an incorruptible and unconquerable witness, when insensible creation receives, as it were, a certain sense in confessing the Creator, hearkening to his will and obeying his command without delay. For what more divine sign could be shown to mortal senses than the sign of today's newness, which Jesus sent forth even before the beginning of his own [other] signs? A new child cries on earth, and creates a new star in the heavens above, so that light might testify of Light, the star of the Sun, and lead kings, by the splendor of its rising, to the eternal Splendor, who likewise has arisen—from the east to the true East, that is, to the man whose name is the Orient (Zach. VI, 12)—the star leading them not as a mere star, but as a rational creature going before on the way, standing still at the end of the way, and pointing out, as it were with a finger, the very one who was being sought. But suppose the unbeliever should slander this, saying that the star is not the work of Christ—so long as he confesses it to be the work of the Father in testimony of the Son. Yet even so he will be overwhelmed by the infinite multitude and the magnitude of the plainly divine wonders which our Jesus worked in the flesh, but not from the flesh, without any possibility of contradiction.

Latin original

3. Sed operiant tenebrae terram, et caligo populos, qui, cum lux venit in mundum, dilexerunt magis tenebras quam lucem (Joan. III, 19); dummodo tu illumineris, Jerusalem, civitas coelestis, quae in omni terra et populo, Deo paritura es filios lucis, quos de potestate tenebrarum transferet in regnum claritatis suae lumen verum. Gratias tibi, Pater luminum, qui de tenebris nos vocasti in admirabile lumen tuum (I Petr. II, 9). Gratias tibi, qui dixisti de tenebris lumen splendescere, et illuxisti in cordibus nostris ad illuminationem scientiae in facie Christi Jesu (II Cor. IV, 6). Haec siquidem est lux vera, imo vita aeterna, ut cognoscamus te unum Deum, et quem misisti Jesum Christum. Cognoscimus te, quia cognoscimus Jesum, quoniam Pater et Filius unum (Joan. XVII, 23, 11). Cognoscimus quidem per fidem, ipsam tenentes arrham fidelem, quia cognoscemus te per speciem. Interim tamen auge nobis fidem, deducens nos ex fide in fidem, a claritate in claritatem, tanquam a tuo Spiritu, ut de die in diem thesauros luminis profundius ingrediamur, quo fides amplior, scientia plenior, charitas ferventior fiat et diffusior, donec per fidem perducamur ad faciem, tanquam per stellam praeducem ad nostrum Bethlehemiticum ducem, qui egressus de Bethlehem regit Israelem, et regnat in Jerusalem; non ista quae occidit prophetas, ipsumque Dominum prophetarum, sed illa in qua coronat occisos idem Dominus, causa, virtus, et gloria martyrum

English translation

3. But let darkness cover the earth, and gloom the peoples, who, when the light came into the world, loved darkness rather than the light (Joan. III, 19); provided only that you be enlightened, O Jerusalem, heavenly city, who in every land and people are to bear sons of light for God—sons whom the true light will transfer from the power of darkness into the kingdom of its own brightness. Thanks be to you, Father of lights, who have called us out of darkness into your wonderful light (I Petr. II, 9). Thanks be to you, who said that light should shine out of darkness, and who have shone in our hearts for the illumination of the knowledge in the face of Christ Jesus (II Cor. IV, 6). For this indeed is the true light, nay rather eternal life, that we should know you the one God, and him whom you sent, Jesus Christ. We know you because we know Jesus, since the Father and the Son are one (Joan. XVII, 23, 11). We know you indeed through faith, holding fast that same faithful pledge, because we shall know you through vision. Meanwhile, however, increase our faith, leading us from faith to faith, from brightness to brightness, as it were by your own Spirit, so that from day to day we may enter more deeply into the treasures of light, whereby faith may become ampler, knowledge fuller, charity more ardent and more widely diffused, until through faith we be brought to the vision of your face, as it were through a guiding star to our Bethlehem-born Leader, who, having gone forth from Bethlehem, rules Israel, and reigns in Jerusalem—not that Jerusalem which killed the prophets, and the very Lord of the prophets, but that one in which the same Lord crowns those who were slain—he who is the cause, the strength, and the glory of the martyrs.

¶cols. 51-52; cols. 53-54
Latin original

4. O quanto gaudio ibi tripudiat fides Magorum, cernentium in illa Jerusalem regnantem, quem in Bethlehem adoraverunt vagientem! Hic visus est in diversorio pauperum, ibi in palatio videtur Angelorum; hic in pannis parvulorum, ibi in splendoribus sanctorum; hic in gremio Matris, ibi in solio Patris. Plane digna beatorum fides Magorum, ut tam felici visione remuneretur; quae cum in eo nihil nisi infirmum et contemptibile videret, non tamen scandalizari potuit, quominus Deum in homine, et hominem in Deo veneraretur. Procul dubio stella ex Jacob illuxerat in cordibus eorum; stella, inquam, matutina, lucifer qui nescit occasum, qui etiam foris stellam, matutini ortus sui indicem, accenderat cenderat in cordibus eorum. De his omnino convenienter intelligi potest quod apud Salomonem scriptum est: Justorum semita, quasi lux splendescens procedit, et crescit usque ad perfectum diem (Prov. IV, 18). Primo namque semitam justitiae ingressi sunt ad lucem splendentis sideris, cujus ductu profecerunt ad videndum novum ortum matutinae, lucis sicque demum pervenerunt ad contemplandam faciem meridiani Solis, in die virtutis suae rutilantis.

English translation

4. Oh, with what great joy does the faith of the Magi there leap for joy, beholding reigning in that Jerusalem the one whom they adored crying as an infant in Bethlehem! Here he was seen in the lodging-place of the poor, there he is seen in the palace of the Angels; here in the swaddling clothes of infants, there in the splendors of the saints; here in the lap of his Mother, there on the throne of the Father. Truly worthy was the faith of the blessed Magi, to be rewarded with so happy a vision—they who, though they saw in him nothing but what was weak and contemptible, could not be scandalized so as to be kept from venerating God in man, and man in God. Without doubt the star out of Jacob had shone in their hearts; the star, I say, the morning star, the light-bearer who knows no setting, who had kindled also outwardly the star, the sign of his own morning rising, within their hearts. Of these it can altogether fittingly be understood what is written in Solomon: The path of the just, as a shining light, goes forward, and increases even to perfect day (Prov. IV, 18). For first they entered the path of righteousness by the light of the shining star, under whose guidance they advanced to the seeing of the new rising of the morning light, and so at last they arrived at the contemplation of the face of the noonday Sun, in the day of his glowing power.

¶cols. 53-54
Latin original

5. Pulchre igitur signanterque in his Gentium primitiis, in his nascentis Ecclesiae primordiis, praeostensus est fidei profectus in singulis: unde scilicet incipiat, quomodo proficiat, et quo demum perveniat; ut facile ipsorum qui patres sunt, in filiis agnoscantur vestigia. Sicut illi coeperunt a visione sideris, profecerunt ad visionem pueri, pervenerunt ad visionem Dei, sic in nobis fides nascitur supernorum praedicatione luminarium; roboratur visione quarumdam imaginum, per speculum et in aenigmate Deum velut incarnatum nobis exhibentium; consummabitur, cum praesens et nuda videbitur veritas rerum facie ad faciem contemplantibus quod vix tenuiter et raptim in aenigmate nunc attingitur; cum et ipsa fides transibit in cognitionem, spes in possessionem, desiderium in fruitionem. Lucent namque et nobis stellae, non una, sed multae; nisi quia multae una, quarum scilicet cor unum est, et anima una, fides eadem, praedicatio consona, similisque vita. Quae sint hae stellae, si dubitas, Danielem interroga: Qui ad justitiam, inquit, erudiunt multos, quasi stellae in perpetuas aeternitates (Dan. XII, 3). Paulus etiam luminaria vocat eos, qui lucent in medio nationis pravae et perversae (Philipp. II, 15), continentque verbum vitae, tanquam splendorem mutuatum ab aeterna luce, quo et noctem hujus mundi jussi sunt illuminare. Ideo Dominus fons et origo lucis, cum ordinaret lunam et stellas in potestatem noctis, dicebat illis, Vos estis lux mundi: et iterum, Sic luceat lux vestra coram hominibus, ut videant opera vestra bona, et glorificent Patrem vestrum, qui in coelis est (Matth. V, 14, 16). Lucent itaque verbo, lucent exemplo, et utroque lucis radio ortum aeternae lucis annuntiant, dum coelestem, quem voce praedicant, etiam coelestis vitae similitudine commendant.

English translation

5. Beautifully then and significantly, in these firstfruits of the Gentiles, in these beginnings of the nascent Church, the progress of faith in each soul was shown beforehand: namely, whence it begins, how it advances, and to what at last it arrives; so that the footsteps of the fathers may easily be recognized in the children. Just as they began from the sight of the star, advanced to the sight of the child, and arrived at the sight of God, so in us faith is born from the preaching of the heavenly luminaries; it is strengthened by the vision of certain images, which, through a mirror and in a riddle, display God to us as it were incarnate; it will be consummated when the present and naked truth of things shall be seen, face to face, by those who contemplate it—that truth which is now attained but faintly and fleetingly, in a riddle—when faith itself shall pass into knowledge, hope into possession, desire into enjoyment. For stars shine for us too—not one, but many; except that many are one, namely those whose heart is one, and soul one, faith the same, preaching harmonious, and life alike. If you doubt what these stars are, ask Daniel: Those who instruct many unto righteousness, he says, shall shine as stars for all eternity (Dan. XII, 3). Paul too calls them luminaries, those who shine in the midst of a crooked and perverse nation (Philipp. II, 15), and who hold fast the word of life, as a brightness borrowed from the eternal light, by which they are also commanded to illumine the night of this world. Therefore the Lord, the fountain and origin of light, when he ordained the moon and the stars for dominion over the night, said to them, You are the light of the world; and again, Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father who is in heaven (Matth. V, 14, 16). They shine therefore by word, they shine by example, and by both rays of light they announce the rising of the eternal light, while they commend, by the likeness of a heavenly life, that heavenly one whom they preach by voice.

6. Suspicite, fratres, in coelum; in coelo siquidem est conversatio eorum; suspicite, et intendite mentis oculos, si necdum potestis in rotam solis, saltem in fulgorem siderum; admiramini splendorem sanctorum, imitamini fidem, aemulamini sanctitatem. Stellae istae sicut flamma coruscant, et lumen luminum ortum demonstrant; ducunt ad cunabula novi Regis, ad cubiculum Virginis matris, quod est inviolabile fidei mysterium; imo perducent in templum Regis, in sanctuarium Dei Patris; quod est incogitabile fidei praemium. Interim autem, dum idonei non sumus, sapientiam Dei, quae in mysterio abscondita est, scrutari, vel majestatem quae in praemium reposita est, contemplari; contenti simus sanctorum claritatem mirari, sanctitatem imitari. Fili, inquit, concupisti sapientiam? serva mandata; et Dominus praebebit tibi illam (Eccli. I, 33). Altiora autem te ne quaesieris, et fortiora te ne scrutatus fueris: sed quae praecipit Dominus tibi, ipsa conserva semper (Eccli. III, 22). Praeceptum namque Domini lucidum, illuminans oculos (Psal. XVIII, 9), quibus postmodum ipsum corpus luminis valeas intueri, cum in luce radiorum, id est, in opere mandatorum, versari consueveris. Dulce quidem lumen, ut ait Salomon, et delectabile oculis videre solem (Eccle. XI, 7). Dulce prorsus et delectabile, sed illis quibus tolerabile. Sanos oculos fovet, infirmos torquet. Et quis est tam sano et acuto visu, ut in aspectu illius invisibilis Solis non caecutiat? quis ille scrutator majestatis, qui non opprimatur a gloria? Mirabilis facta est, o Domine, claritas tua, tum ex se, tum etiam ex me; quia confortata est adversum me, dum acies mea infirmata est in me, et jam non potero ad eam, sicut poteram in Adam. Potero forsitan ad claritatem siderum, qui non possum ad solem ipsum, et per hanc quandoque confortabor ad illum.

English translation

6. Look up, brothers, to heaven; for their way of life is in heaven. Look up, and fix the eyes of your mind—if you cannot yet gaze upon the wheel of the sun, at least upon the brightness of the stars. Admire the splendor of the saints, imitate their faith, strive to equal their holiness. These stars flash like flame, and show forth the rising of the light of lights; they lead to the cradle of the new King, to the chamber of the Virgin Mother—which is the inviolable mystery of faith; indeed, they will lead us further, into the temple of the King, into the sanctuary of God the Father—which is the inconceivable reward of faith. Meanwhile, however, since we are not fit to search out the wisdom of God which lies hidden in mystery, or to contemplate the majesty laid up as reward, let us be content to admire the brightness of the saints and to imitate their holiness. Son, it says, have you desired wisdom? Keep the commandments, and the Lord will give it to you (Eccli. I, 33). But do not seek things higher than yourself, and do not search into things stronger than yourself; but keep always what the Lord commands you (Eccli. III, 22). For the commandment of the Lord is lightsome, enlightening the eyes (Psal. XVIII, 9), with which you may afterward be able to gaze upon the very body of light itself, once you have grown accustomed to dwell in the light of its rays—that is, in the doing of the commandments. Light is sweet indeed, as Solomon says, and it is delightful to the eyes to see the sun (Eccle. XI, 7). Sweet indeed and delightful, but only to those for whom it is bearable. It soothes sound eyes, it torments weak ones. And who has sight so sound and so keen that he would not be blinded at the sight of that invisible Sun? Who is that searcher of majesty who would not be overwhelmed by its glory? Wonderful, O Lord, has your brightness become, both in itself and also because of me; for it has grown strong against me, since my sight has grown weak within me, and now I can no longer attain to it as I could in Adam. Perhaps I shall be able to attain to the brightness of the stars, I who cannot attain to the sun itself, and through this I shall in time be strengthened toward that.

Latin original

7. Eia, surge, illuminare, anima mea, quae sedebas in tenebris suspice luminaria coeli, leva oculos tuos in montes unde veniet auxilium tibi, si veneris ad illum qui habitat in coelis, id est, in montibus ipsis. A montibus, inquam, veniet auxilium tibi, quia lux tibi inaccessibilis illuminat mirabiliter a montibus aeternis. Susceperunt montes lucem populo, a quibus ad valles camposque descendat subjectos. Hoc itaque, dico, fratres, optimum est, nostraeque infirmitati congruum illuminationis initium, si intendamus in eos qui illuminati sunt. Rectissima via est inveniendi Jesum, si praecedentium patrum sequamur lumen praevium. Semita justi recta est, rectus callis justi ad ambulandum (Isa. XXVI, 7). Qui justum sequitur, non ambulat in tenebris; non modo videbit, sed habebit lumen vitae. Videbit ad consolationem praesentis vitae: habebit in possessionem haereditatis aeternae. Pietas nempe promissionem habet vitae quae nunc est, et futurae. Exerceamur ad pietatem, et neutro nos fraudabit qui se ultro nobis fecit debitorem (I Tim. IV, 8, 7, 9). Exerceamur in operibus lucis, et qui abscondit lucem in manibus suis, annuntians de ea amico suo, quod possessio ejus sit, et ad eam possit ascendere (Job XXXVI, 32, 33), nunc aliquando in solatium operis eam nobis demonstrabit, et postmodum in praemium dabit, ipse lux nostra Jesus Christus qui vivit et regnat per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

7. Come now, arise, be enlightened, my soul, you who were sitting in darkness; look up at the lights of heaven; lift up your eyes to the mountains, whence help will come to you, if you come to him who dwells in the heavens—that is, in those very mountains. From the mountains, I say, will help come to you, because the light inaccessible to you shines wonderfully from the eternal mountains. The mountains have received light for the people, that from them it may descend to the valleys and plains lying below. This, then, I say, brothers, is the best and most fitting beginning of enlightenment for our weakness: that we should fix our gaze upon those who have been enlightened. It is the most direct way of finding Jesus, if we follow the guiding light of the fathers who went before us. The path of the just is straight; the way of the just is straight for walking (Isa. XXVI, 7). He who follows the just man does not walk in darkness; he will not only see but will possess the light of life. He will see it for the consolation of the present life; he will possess it as an eternal inheritance. For godliness holds the promise of the life that now is, and of that which is to come. Let us train ourselves unto godliness, and he who has of his own accord made himself our debtor will cheat us of neither (I Tim. IV, 8, 7, 9). Let us exercise ourselves in the works of light; and he who hides light in his hands, declaring to his friend concerning it that it is his possession and that he can ascend to it (Job XXXVI, 32, 33), will now at times show it to us for the comfort of our labor, and afterward will give it as our reward—he himself, our light, Jesus Christ, who lives and reigns through all ages of ages. Amen.
