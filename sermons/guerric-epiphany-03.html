Editorial introduction

This third Epiphany sermon belongs to Guerric of Igny's medieval collection of sermons for the liturgical year, here read from the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. Its individual date is unknown; the collection is associated approximately with his abbacy in 1138–1157. Guerric sets out the steps by which the soul comes to the light of wisdom: faith first, then righteousness, then knowledge, and at last wisdom, the taste of eternal things. Along the way he asks for the light to see one's own sins, and he draws on the healing of the blind man in two stages and on David's understanding gained from the commandments. The source explicitly names Epiphany.

Quibus gradibus ad sapientiae lumen perveniatur.

English translation

By What Steps One Comes to the Light of Wisdom

¶cols. 53-54; cols. 55-56
Latin original

1. Surge, illuminare, Jerusalem, quia venit lumen tuum (Isa. LX, 1). Benedictum lumen quod venit in nomine Domini, Deus Dominus, et illuxit nobis; de cujus munere etiam dies iste, dies sanctificatus de illuminatione Ecclesiae illuxit nobis. Gratias tibi, lux vera, quae illuminas omnem hominem venientem in hunc mundum, quae ad hoc ipsum venisti homo in hunc mundum. Illuminata est Jerusalem mater nostra, mater omnium qui illuminari meruerunt: adeo ut jam luceat omnibus qui in mundo sunt. Gratias tibi, lux vera, quae lucerna es facta, ut Jerusalem illuminares, et lucerna pedibus meis Verbum Dei fieret. Gratias, inquam, tibi, quoniam ipsa Jerusalem illuminata facta est lucerna, ut luceat omnibus qui sunt in domo magni patris familias. Non solum enim illuminata est, sed exaltata, et super candelabrum illud totum aureum sita est; civitas super montem montium, illa quondam derelicta et odio habita, posita est in superbiam saeculorum, ut tam longe lateque fulgeat ejus Evangelium, quam longe lateque mundi vigebat imperium. Et si opertum est ejus Evangelium, in his qui pereunt opertum est (II Cor. IV, 3), in quibus Deus hujus saeculi excaecavit mentes infidelium, ut non fulgeat eis gloria Ecclesiae Christi. Quibus tamen non fulget ut videant, fulget ut invideant, et Ecclesiae torquentur gloria, qui ipsius illuminari nolunt gratia. Verum Ecclesia nec propter gloriam qua floret, superbiens, nec gratiam qua pollet, invidens, etiam illis quos molestos tolerat, lumen quod accepit implorat, dicens: Quoniam tu illuminas lucernam meam, Domine; Deus meus, illumina tenebras meas (Psal. XVII, 29). Mei forsitan sunt, quia praedestinati; sed adhuc tenebrae sunt, quia nondum vocati aut justificati. Voca et ipsos in admirabile lumen tuum, et praedicabunt mecum admirabile nomen tuum. Quanquam justus quisque ac sanctus in Ecclesia ita se illuminatum gaudeat, ut adhuc se non minima ex parte tenebrosum videat et doleat; et ideo necessario, cum illuminatus sit, adhuc se illuminari petat. Quanto namque amplius lucerna ejus illuminata fuerit, tanto verius ex lucerna ipsa tenebras suas deprehendit. Nec statim contrarium putaveris, quod in Evangelio Veritas ait: Lucerna corporis tui est oculus tuus. Si oculus tuus simplex fuerit, totum corpus tuum lucidum erit (Matth. VI, 22 et Luc. XI, 34). Non enim, quia ex oculo sincerae intentionis totum opus nostrum lucidum fit, ideo consequens est ut statim omnes tenebrae ignorantiarum nostrarum illuminatae sint. Is nempe modus est adhuc illuminationis nostrae, ut in lumen veritatis multum existimetur profecisse, qui defectum suum potuerit nosse, et scire quid desit sibi. Inde est quod et apud sapientes hujus mundi, qui magis sobrie disputaverunt de scientia, primus, numeratur gradus scientiae, scire quod nescias.

English translation

1. Arise, be enlightened, O Jerusalem, for thy light has come (Isa. LX, 1). Blessed is the light that comes in the name of the Lord — God is the Lord, and he has shone upon us; by whose gift this day too, a day made holy by the enlightenment of the Church, has shone upon us. Thanks be to thee, true light, who dost enlighten every man coming into this world, who for this very purpose didst come as man into this world. Jerusalem our mother has been enlightened, the mother of all who have deserved to be enlightened, so that now she shines for all who are in the world. Thanks be to thee, true light, who hast become a lamp, that thou mightest enlighten Jerusalem, and that the Word of God might become a lamp to my feet. Thanks, I say, to thee, because Jerusalem herself, once enlightened, has become a lamp, so that she might shine for all who are in the house of the great father of the family. For she was not only enlightened but exalted, and set upon that lampstand all of gold; a city upon the mountain of mountains — she who was once forsaken and held in hatred — has been set up as the pride of the ages, that her Gospel might shine as far and wide as the empire of the world once flourished far and wide. And if her Gospel is veiled, it is veiled in those who are perishing (II Cor. IV, 3), in whom the god of this world has blinded the minds of unbelievers, so that the glory of the Church of Christ may not shine upon them. Yet for them it does not shine so that they may see, but shines so that they may envy, and they are tormented by the glory of the Church, they who refuse to be enlightened by her grace. But the Church, neither growing proud on account of the glory in which she flourishes, nor begrudging the grace by which she is strong, even for those whom she patiently endures as troublesome, begs for the light she has received, saying: For thou wilt light my candle, O Lord; O my God, enlighten my darkness (Psal. XVII, 29). Perhaps they are mine, because predestined; but they are still darkness, because not yet called or justified. Call them too into thy wonderful light, and they will proclaim thy wonderful name together with me. Although every just and holy person in the Church rejoices that he has been enlightened, yet he sees and grieves that he is still, in no small part, dark; and so, necessarily, even though he has been enlightened, he still asks to be enlightened further. For the more his lamp has been enlightened, the more truly does he discover his own darkness by that very lamp. And you should not at once think this contrary to what the Truth says in the Gospel: The lamp of thy body is thine eye. If thine eye be single, thy whole body shall be full of light (Matth. VI, 22 et Luc. XI, 34). For it is not the case that, because our whole work is made full of light from the eye of sincere intention, all the darkness of our ignorance is therefore at once illumined. For this indeed is still the measure of our enlightenment, that he is reckoned to have made great progress in the light of truth who has been able to know his own defect, and to know what he lacks. Hence it is that even among the wise men of this world, who have discussed knowledge more soberly, the first degree of knowledge is reckoned to be this: to know what you do not know.

Latin original

2. Fratres, omnes vos filii lucis estis, et filii Dei. Non sumus noctis, neque tenebrarum. Nox enim praecessit, dies autem appropinquavit (Rom. XIII, 12). Et si fuimus aliquando tenebrae, nunc autem lux in Domino (Ephes. V, 8). Verumtamen si, quoniam non sumus tenebrae nec filii tenebrarum, dixerimus nos nihil pati tenebrarum, nos ipsos seducimus, et tenebras mortis, quae illuminari non mereantur, nobis inducimus. Quid enim, ait Lux mundi, quae in judicium venit in hunc mundum, ut qui non vident videant, et qui vident caeci fiant? Quia, inquit, dicitis, Videmus, peccatum vestrum manet (Joan. IX, 41). Video quantulumcunque, quoniam tu illuminas lucernam meam, o Domine, sed, quia parum est quod video, Deus meus, illumina tenebras meas (Psal. XVII, 29). Videmus ut multum per speculum et in aenigmate; sed multum longe est hoc ipsum a claritate vera claraque veritate, quae videbitur in facie. Ideo forsitan caecus, quem apud Marcum Dominus illuminare coeperat, homines quasi arbores ambulantes videbat (Marc. VIII, 22-24), quia videlicet non nisi per speculum et in aenigmate videbat; sed secundo imponens manum restituit eum, ita ut videret clare omnia; quod in nobis fiet, cum nos reficiet, et sanitatem perficiet impositione manuum secunda, quam coepit prima, restituens nos in claritate perfecta.

English translation

2. Brothers, you are all children of light, and children of God. We are not of the night, nor of darkness. For the night has passed, and the day is at hand (Rom. XIII, 12). And if we were once darkness, now we are light in the Lord (Ephes. V, 8). Yet if, because we are not darkness nor children of darkness, we should say that we suffer nothing of darkness, we deceive ourselves, and bring upon ourselves the darkness of death, which deserves not to be enlightened. For what does the Light of the world say, who came into this world for judgment, that those who do not see might see, and those who see might become blind? Because, he says, you say, We see: your sin remains (Joan. IX, 41). I see some little, however small, since thou, O Lord, dost light my candle; but because what I see is little, O my God, enlighten my darkness (Psal. XVII, 29). At most we see through a glass and in a dark manner; but this is very far indeed from the true clarity and clear truth which shall be seen face to face. Perhaps that is why the blind man, whom the Lord in Mark had begun to enlighten, saw men as though they were trees walking (Marc. VIII, 22-24), evidently because he saw only through a glass and in a dark manner; but by laying his hand on him a second time, he restored him so that he saw all things clearly — which shall happen in us, when he renews us and perfects our healing by a second laying on of hands, continuing what he began with the first, and restoring us to perfect clarity.

¶cols. 55-56
Latin original

3. Sed hoc ipsum per speculum et in aenigmate, cui, aut quando, vel quandiu potest contingere? Quam misericorditer mecum ageretur, quam feliciter mihi viderer illuminatus, si peccata mea possem videre, cui quotidie necesse est plangere ac dicere: Circumdederunt me mala quorum non est numerus, et non potui ut viderem! (Psal. XXXIX, 13.) Nam delicta quis intelligit (Psal. XVIII, 13), quando etiam specie virtutis vitium decipiens interficit, quasi vere angelus tenebrarum transfigurans se in angelum lucis? Magnus utique, multumque illuminatus, qui delicta ad liquidum intelligere potuit et voluit; nam neque et dolosus potuit, et noluit intelligere, ut bene ageret (Psal. XXXV, 4). Deus illuminator omnium gentium, de te nobis cantabatur: Ecce veniet Dominus, et illuminabit oculos servorum suorum. Ecce venisti, o lumen meum, illumina oculos meos, ne unquam obdormiam in morte, ne peccatum ad mortem oculos nostros aliqua delectatione claudat, et ad consensum illiciat, aut, velut Isboseth, dormientem inveniens transfodiat (II Reg. IV, 7). Venisti, o lumen fidelium omnium, et ecce hodie de illuminatione verae fidei, id est, lucernae nostrae, dedisti nobis gaudere; da etiam de illuminatione tenebrarum nostrarum, quae residuae sunt, semper in Domino gaudere. Dedisti lumen verae fidei, da et lumen verae justitiae, da lumen divinae scientiae, da etiam et divinae sapientiae.

English translation

3. But this very seeing "through a glass and in a dark manner" — to whom, or when, or for how long can it happen? How mercifully would it be dealt with me, how happily would I seem to myself illumined, if I could see my sins — I for whom it is necessary daily to lament and say: "Evils without number have surrounded me, and I was not able to see" (Psal. XXXIX, 13.)! For "who understands his faults" (Psal. XVIII, 13), when even vice, deceiving under the appearance of virtue, kills — as if truly an angel of darkness transforming himself into an angel of light? Great indeed, and much illumined, is he who was able and willing to understand his sins clearly; for the deceitful man could not and would not understand, that he might do well (Psal. XXXV, 4). O God, enlightener of all nations, of you it was sung to us: "Behold, the Lord will come, and he will enlighten the eyes of his servants." Behold, you have come, O my light; enlighten my eyes, lest I ever fall asleep in death, lest sin unto death close our eyes with some delight and entice us into consent, or, like Ishbosheth, find us sleeping and pierce us through (II Reg. IV, 7). You have come, O light of all the faithful, and behold, today you have given us to rejoice over the illumination of the true faith, that is, of our lamp; grant also that we may always rejoice in the Lord over the illumination of the darkness that still remains in us. You have given the light of true faith; give also the light of true righteousness, give the light of divine knowledge, give also the light of divine wisdom.

Latin original

4. His profecto gradibus proficiendum, hac via proficiscendum tibi esse puto, o anima fidelis, quo, fallacis mundi hujus tenebris exuta, pervenias ad patriam claritatis aeternae, ubi tenebrae tuae erunt sicut clara meridies, et nox sicut dies illuminabitur. Tunc prorsus, tunc videbis et afflues, et mirabitur, et dilatabitur cor tuum (Isa. LX, 5), quando replebitur divina majestate incircumscripti luminis omnis terra, et gloria ejus in te videbitur. Domus Jacob, venite, ambulemus in lumine Domini (Id. II, 3): ut veri filii lucis ambulemus de claritate in claritatem, tanquam praelucente Domini Spiritu, ut per singulos gradus virtutum regnum claritatis ulterius invadamus. Qui igitur jam per fidem sumus in lumine, ex ea et per eam in amplius et serenius lumen progrediamur, primo justitiae, dein scientiae, demumque sapientiae. Quod enim credimus per fidem, consequenter operandum vel promerendum est per justitiam, post intelligendum per scientiam, ac postremo contemplandum per sapientiam. Prima namque omnium accensa est lucerna fidei, ad quam operemur in nocte hujus saeculi. Inde scriptum est in laudem mulieris fortis, quae de nocte surrexit, et, de nocte ac die operans manibus suis, panem otiosa non comedit, Non exstinguetur, inquit, in nocte lucerna ejus (Prov. XXXI, 18), id est, non deficiet in tentatione fides ejus. Ad hanc lucernam non fiunt opera tenebrarum, sed exstinguitur cum fiunt. Qui enim male agit, odit lucem (Joan. III, 20). Ideoque exstinguit lucernam fidei, postponit memoriam Dei; non est timor Dei ante oculos ejus, et de tenebris quas sibi fecit, arbitrorumque absentia blanditur sibi, dicens: Quis me videt? Tenebrae circumdant me, et parietes cooperiunt me, et nemo circumspicit me. Quem vereor? Delictorum meorum non memorabitur Altissimus. Et non intelligit quoniam omnia videt oculus illius (Eccli. XXIII, 26, 27). Bona vero opera, etsi aliquando propter vanitatem declinandam in abscondito fiunt, ad lucem tamen internam et aeternam, id est sub judicio fidei et testimonio Dei fiunt, quia opera lucis sunt; lucernae utique ardentes in manibus operantium, et exspectantium adventum sponsi, qui educet quasi lumen justitiam tuam ad conspectum hominum et angelorum, et, sicut lux meridiana clara est, clarescere faciet opera tua, quia in Deo sunt facta, ut Deum in te, et te in Deo clarificent omnium judicia.

English translation

4. By these steps indeed, I think, O faithful soul, you must advance; by this road you must set out, so that, stripped of the darkness of this deceitful world, you may arrive at the homeland of eternal brightness, where your darkness shall be as the clear noonday, and night shall be lit up as day. Then truly, then you shall see and overflow, and your heart shall wonder and be enlarged (Isa. LX, 5), when the whole earth shall be filled with the divine majesty of uncircumscribed light, and his glory shall be seen in you. "House of Jacob, come, let us walk in the light of the Lord" (Id. II, 3): that as true children of light we may walk from brightness to brightness, as the Spirit of the Lord shines before us, so that through each degree of the virtues we may press on further into the kingdom of brightness. Therefore we who are already in the light through faith, from faith and through it let us advance into a fuller and more serene light — first of righteousness, then of knowledge, and finally of wisdom. For what we believe by faith must consequently be worked out, or earned, through righteousness; afterward it must be understood through knowledge; and finally it must be contemplated through wisdom. For of all lamps the first to be lit is the lamp of faith, by which we must work in the night of this present age. Hence it is written in praise of the valiant woman, who rose while it was yet night, and, working with her hands by night and by day, did not eat her bread in idleness: "Her lamp shall not be put out in the night" (Prov. XXXI, 18), that is, her faith shall not fail under temptation. By this lamp works of darkness are not done; rather, it is extinguished when such works are done. For "he who does evil hates the light" (Joan. III, 20). And so he extinguishes the lamp of faith, he puts away the memory of God; the fear of God is not before his eyes, and from the darkness he has made for himself, and the absence of witnesses, he flatters himself, saying: Who sees me? Darkness surrounds me, and walls cover me, and no one looks around at me. Whom do I fear? The Most High will not remember my sins. And he does not understand that the eye of the Most High sees all things (Eccli. XXIII, 26, 27). But good works, even when sometimes done in secret in order to avoid vanity, are nevertheless done before the inward and eternal light, that is, under the judgment of faith and the witness of God, because they are works of light — lamps indeed burning in the hands of those who work and who await the coming of the Bridegroom, who will bring forth your righteousness as light before the sight of men and angels, and, as the light of noonday is clear, he will make your works to shine clearly, because they were done in God, so that the judgment of all may glorify God in you, and you in God.

Latin original

5. Quanta vero claritate justitia resplendeat etiam in praesenti, quanta luce laetificet conscientiam justi, familiarius et suavius conscientiae vestrae testimonium vos docebit. Saepe autem et fides radiat, et justitia coruscat, et adhuc tamen intellectus caligat, ut mysterium fidei, quod velut involutum tenens veneratur, explicare nesciat; signatus sit ei liber Scripturae, quasi litteras nesciat; sed nec ad discretionem boni et mali, veri et falsi sensus exercitatos habeat. Et tales quidem multi in Ecclesia, quorum fides multa, abundans justitia, scientia fere nulla. Quod tamen ad lumen justitiae accedere oporteat lumen scientiae, docet sermo prophetae, qui, cum praemisisset: Seminate vobis ad justitiam, ut ostenderet quas interim primitias colligamus de hoc semine, subjunxit: Illuminate vobis lumen scientiae (Osee. X, 12). Sic et Apostolus ait: Fructificantes in opere bono, et crescentes in scientia Dei (Coloss. I, 10). Tu quoque, David, sedens in cathedra sapientissimus princeps inter tres, quomodo super senes intellexisti? Quia mandata, inquit, quaesivi. A mandatis prorsus intellexi, quia intellectus bonus omnibus facientibus timorem Domini. Nam et unctio docens de omnibus, sciens nimirum quo ordine quidque apud eam discendum sit, prius docet bonitatem et disciplinam, et postmodum scientiam: si tamen qui docendus est, primo omnium crediderit, id est, elementa exordii sermonum justitiae didicerit. Ideo ille discipulus unctionis tam prudenter precem ordinabat, atque allegabat: Bonitatem, inquit, et disciplinam et scientiam doce me, quia mandatis tuis credidi (Psal. CXVIII, 100, 66). Ac si diceret: Didici prima elementa, id est, fidem non fictam; jam, quod sequitur, doce justitiam, id est bonitatem et disciplinam, ut sic demum provehat ad sanctorum scientiam. Vocasti ad gratiam fidei, jam justifica per bonitatem amoris, et disciplinam casti timoris, ut post magnifices munere scientiae spiritualis. Quos autem vocavit, hos et justificavit; et quos justificavit illos et magnificavit (Rom. VIII, 30).

English translation

5. With how great a brightness righteousness shines even in the present life, with how great a light it gladdens the conscience of the just, the testimony of your own conscience will teach you more intimately and more sweetly. But often it happens that faith shines, and righteousness glitters, and yet understanding remains dark, so that one who holds and venerates the mystery of faith as though wrapped up cannot unfold it; the book of Scripture is sealed to him as if he did not know letters; nor does he have senses trained to discern good from evil, true from false. And indeed there are many such in the Church, in whom faith is great, righteousness abundant, but knowledge almost none. Yet that the light of knowledge ought to be added to the light of righteousness is taught by the word of the prophet, who, having first said: 'Sow for yourselves unto righteousness,' in order to show what first-fruits we are meanwhile to gather from this seed, added: 'Light for yourselves the light of knowledge' (Osee. X, 12). So too the Apostle says: 'Being fruitful in good work, and increasing in the knowledge of God' (Coloss. I, 10). You too, David, sitting in the chair, the wisest prince among the three—how did you understand above the elders? 'Because,' he says, 'I sought the commandments.' Entirely from the commandments I understood, for good understanding belongs to all who do the fear of the Lord. For the anointing which teaches concerning all things, knowing full well in what order each thing is to be learned from it, first teaches goodness and discipline, and afterward knowledge—provided, however, that the one to be taught has first of all believed, that is, has learned the elementary first principles of the words of righteousness. Therefore that disciple of the anointing so prudently ordered his prayer, and pleaded: 'Teach me goodness,' he says, 'and discipline and knowledge, for I have believed your commandments' (Psal. CXVIII, 100, 66). As if he were saying: I have learned the first elements, that is, unfeigned faith; now, what follows, teach me righteousness, that is, goodness and discipline, so that it may at last advance me to the knowledge of the saints. You have called to the grace of faith; now justify through the goodness of love and the discipline of chaste fear, so that afterward you may magnify with the gift of spiritual knowledge. 'Whom he called, these he also justified; and whom he justified, these he also magnified' (Rom. VIII, 30).

¶cols. 57-58
Latin original

6. Sciendum sane quod, cum sit haec scientia sanctorum, non tamen omnes sancti docentur eam, sed illi duntaxat in quibus nec industria gratiam, nec gratia destituit industriam; sed utrumque mutuo sibi suffragio cooperatur, ut et docibilis sit homo discipulus, et spiritus adsit doctor sedulus. Cum autem varia charismata contineat haec scientia, Spiritus tamen moderator non in unum facile confert omnia, sed dividit singulis prout vult, alii notitiam mysteriorum, alii intellectum Scripturarum, alii interpretationem sermonum, alii discretionem spirituum, alii illam, quae pernecessaria est, velut in gustu dignoscentiam, atque dijudicationem virtutum ac vitiorum, ne fallant vitia specie virtutum. Non nocebunt, inquit, et non occident in universo sancto monte meo; quia repleta est terra scientia Domini (Isai. XI, 9).

English translation

6. It should indeed be known that, although this is the knowledge of the saints, not all saints are taught it, but only those in whom neither does industry fail grace, nor grace fail industry; rather, each cooperates with the other by mutual support, so that the man is a teachable disciple, and the Spirit is present as a diligent teacher. But since this knowledge contains various charisms, the Spirit who governs does not readily confer all of them on one person, but divides them to each as he wills: to one, knowledge of the mysteries; to another, understanding of the Scriptures; to another, interpretation of discourses; to another, discernment of spirits; to another, that most necessary gift, as it were a tasting discernment, and a judging apart of virtues and vices, lest vices deceive under the appearance of virtues. 'They shall not hurt,' it says, 'nor shall they kill in all my holy mountain; for the earth is filled with the knowledge of the Lord' (Isai. XI, 9).

¶cols. 57-58; cols. 59-60
Latin original

7. Jam vero si quis post haec et per haec tria, fidem, justitiam atque scientiam, proficiat ad sapientiam, id est, saporem et gustum aeternorum, ut possit vacare et videre, videndoque gustare quam suavis Dominus; et quod oculus non vidit, et auris non audivit, et in cor hominis non ascendit, ei per Spiritum reveletur: hunc omnino dixerim illuminatum magnifice et gloriose, tanquam qui gloriam Domini revelata facie speculetur, et super quem gloria Domini saepius oriatur. Huic utique dicere habet non propheta, sed prophetarum Spiritus: Surge, illuminare, Jerusalem, quia venit lumen tuum, et gloria Domini super te orta est (Id. LX, 1). Fratres, non omnes capiunt hoc verbum, sed qui potest capere capiat. Non condemnatur qui non capit: sed qui non cupit, utique teporis arguitur. Qui autem cupit, noverit quia hoc lumen sapientiae fervens accendit oratio, sicut et lumen scientiae frequens lectio; si tamen cum legis, lucernam ardentem adhibueris, id est justitiam operum, piorumque experientiam sensuum. Tu autem, Domine, Pater luminum, qui lumen de te lumine unicum tuum, misisti sisti illuminare tenebras mortalium, da nobis per viam luminum pervenire ad lumen aeternum, ut placeamus coram te in lumine viventium, qui vivis et regnas per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

7. Now if anyone, after these and through these three—faith, righteousness, and knowledge—advances to wisdom, that is, to the savor and taste of eternal things, so that he may be free to see, and in seeing to taste how sweet the Lord is; and so that what eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has entered into the heart of man, may be revealed to him through the Spirit: this man I would call altogether magnificently and gloriously illumined, as one who beholds the glory of the Lord with unveiled face, and upon whom the glory of the Lord more often arises. To such a one, surely, it is not the prophet but the Spirit of the prophets who must say: 'Arise, be illumined, Jerusalem, for your light has come, and the glory of the Lord has arisen upon you' (Id. LX, 1). Brothers, not all grasp this word, but let him who can grasp it, grasp it. He who does not grasp it is not condemned; but he who does not desire it is surely convicted of lukewarmness. But let him who desires it know that this light of wisdom is kindled by fervent prayer, just as the light of knowledge is kindled by frequent reading—provided, however, that when you read, you bring to it a burning lamp, that is, the righteousness of works and the experience of devout senses. But you, O Lord, Father of lights, who have sent your Only One, Light from your Light, to illumine the darkness of mortals, grant us to arrive by the way of lights at the eternal light, that we may be pleasing before you in the light of the living, you who live and reign through all ages of ages. Amen.
