Introduction & Scripture references

Editorial introduction

This fourth Epiphany sermon belongs to Guerric of Igny's medieval collection of sermons for the liturgical year, here read from the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. Its individual date is unknown; the collection is associated approximately with his abbacy in 1138–1157. Christ's baptism today brings a second birth for believers, but Guerric fears that monks keep the appearance of piety while denying its power. Naaman, washed seven times in the Jordan, becomes the figure of a sevenfold humility of Christ, in which those who lost the grace of their first Baptism may be cleansed again. The source explicitly names Epiphany.

English translation

On Our New Birth and Life

¶cols. 59-60
Latin original

1. Secunda nobis hodie, fratres, nativitas celebratur, quae de illa prima nativitate, tanquam de causa effectus, consequenter nata videtur. Illa siquidem, quam usque hodie celebravimus, nativitas Christi est; ista, quam hodie celebramus, nativitas nostra est. In illa namque Christus natus est, in ista Christianitas nata est. Cum enim tria sint quae nos faciunt christianos, fides, Baptismus, et altaris participatio, hodierna dies fidem nobis initiavit, Baptisma consecravit, ac mensae coelestis miracula praeformavit. Quomodo sane prima gentium illuminatio fidem nobis initiaverit, quomodo Christus baptizatus Baptisma nostrum consecraverit, qualiter illa conversio aquae in vinum, mutationem rerum in convivio mensae Dominicae praeostenderit, nequaquam jam opus est ut doceamus: sed illud arbitror operae pretium, ut nosmetipsos commoneamus, ne fides in nobis a suis degeneret initiis, ne vacuetur in nobis gratia Baptismatis, ne cedat nobis in judicium participatio calicis Christi.

English translation

1. Today, brothers, we celebrate a second birth for ourselves, which seems to have been born as a consequence of that first birth, as an effect follows from its cause. For that birth which we have celebrated up until today is the birth of Christ; but this one, which we celebrate today, is our own birth. For in that birth Christ was born, but in this one Christianity was born. Since there are three things that make us Christians—faith, Baptism, and participation at the altar—today's feast has initiated faith in us, consecrated Baptism, and prefigured the miracles of the heavenly table. Indeed, there is no longer any need for us to teach how that first illumination of the Gentiles initiated faith for us, how Christ's being baptized consecrated our Baptism, how that turning of water into wine foreshadowed the change of things in the banquet of the Lord's table: but I think it worthwhile that we remind ourselves, lest faith in us should degenerate from its beginnings, lest the grace of Baptism be made void in us, lest our participation in the cup of Christ turn into judgment against us.

¶cols. 59-60
Latin original

2. Et ipsa quidem fides, qualis ab illis Magis, primis videlicet auctoribus suis, nobis hodie tradita sit; qualiter maximum fidei sacramentum mysticis insinuaverint munerum figuris, satis abundeque disseruere patres nostri, ut ex ea parte nullus jam alius labor quam in labores eorum introire nobis resederit. Sed neque magnopere postulat status, imo ruina temporis hujus, ut de mysterio fidei disputetur sed ut magis id modis omnibus elaboretur ut, sicut Apostolus ait, in conscientia pura idem fidei mysterium habeatur (I Tim. III, 9). Ecce enim hodie si de mysterio fidei interrogas, omnes fere invenies christianissimos; si conscientias discutias, paucos admodum invenies vere christianos. Totus fere mundus confitetur verbis se nosse Deum, factis autem negat (Tit. I, 16); adeo ut et qui speciem pietatis videntur habere, virtutem ejus saepius inveniantur propemodum abnegare. Quid putatis autem, fratres mei, quos in hoc significare voluerim, quos scilicet dicam pietatis habere speciem, sed abnegare virtutem? Si illos qui spatiosam viam saeculi ambulant, intelligitis, quid illi vel de sola praeferunt pietatis specie, qui nomine solo christiani, atque in vacuum assumentes nomen sanctum Christi, tota ac libera professione sectantur quae huic inimica sunt nomini; qui omni specie conversationis et modo vitae, se protestantur inimicos crucis Christi? Haeccine est species pietatis quam in eis videmus, habitus et incessus meretricius, sermo scurrilis, oculus impudicus, venter qui pro Deo colitur, et omnis illa vitae contumelia, quae publice et gloriabunde, velut in faciem pietati irrogatur? Quae enim in occulto ab ipsis fiunt, turpe est et dicere (Ephes. V, 12). Fodiat parietem eorum qui ad ipsos missus est prophetare, ut adhuc majores abominationes videat, quas non erubescunt aut verentur coram illa tremenda majestate perpetrare. Quid enim mihi, qui monachus videor, de his qui extra hoc monasterium sunt judicare? Utinam nec de his ipsis qui hic intus sunt, mihi esset judicare. Ad me ipsum namque conturbata est anima mea, dum scilicet vereor ne haec ipsa species pietatis, quam utcunque vobiscum gero, gravius me incipiat accusare, si pietatem ipsa me convicerit abnegasse.

English translation

2. As for faith itself—how it was handed down to us today by those Magi, who were indeed its first authors; how they expressed the greatest sacrament of faith through the mystical figures of their gifts—our fathers have discussed this fully and more than abundantly, so that in that regard no other labor remains for us than to enter into their labors. But neither does the condition of the times, or rather the ruin of this present age, greatly demand that we dispute about the mystery of faith, but rather that we strive by every means to hold, as the Apostle says, the same mystery of faith in a pure conscience (I Tim. III, 9). For behold, today, if you ask about the mystery of faith, you will find almost everyone most Christian; but if you examine consciences, you will find very few who are truly Christian. Almost the whole world confesses in words that it knows God, but denies it by its deeds (Tit. I, 16); so much so that even those who seem to have the appearance of piety are often found to deny its power almost entirely. But what do you suppose, my brothers, I meant to signify by this—whom indeed do I mean by those who have the appearance of piety, but deny its power? If you understand those who walk the broad way of the world, what mere appearance of piety do even they put forward, who are Christians in name only, and who, taking up the holy name of Christ in vain, pursue, by their whole and free profession, things that are hostile to this name; who, by every outward show and manner of life, declare themselves enemies of the cross of Christ? Is this the appearance of piety that we see in them—harlot-like bearing and gait, scurrilous speech, shameless eye, the belly that is worshiped in place of God, and all that reproach of life which is publicly and boastfully inflicted, as it were, upon the very face of piety? For the things that are done by them in secret are shameful even to mention (Ephes. V, 12). Let him who was sent to them to prophesy dig through their wall, that he may see even greater abominations, which they do not blush or fear to commit in the presence of that tremendous majesty. But what right have I, who only appear to be a monk, to judge those who are outside this monastery? Would that I had no need to judge even those who are within it. For within myself my soul is troubled, since indeed I fear lest this very appearance of piety, which I somehow bear among you, may begin to accuse me the more gravely, if that appearance itself should convict me of having denied piety.

Latin original

3. Omnino, fratres, verendum est mihi, an vero et vobis ipsis, vos videritis, ne ad nos praecipue spectet ille sermo qui per Apostolum prophetatus est de temporibus novissimis; ubi, cum malitiam hominum describeret tunc futurorum, qualis nunc manifeste legi potest in vita et moribus eorum, longum tandem sic conclusit malorum catalogum: Habentes, inquit, speciem pietatis, virtutem autem ejus abnegantes (II Tim. III, 5). Quid enim ita speciem pietatis habet, sicut haec quam praetendimus, humilitas tonsurae et habitus, parcimonia cibi et potus, labor fere continuus, et ordinatissimus per omnia vivendi modus? Caeterum de virtute ipsa pietatis, quam haec species exterior promittit, quam prope nihil in me meique similibus valeat inveniri, pudor mihi esset confiteri, si non omnibus essemus manifesti. Quae est enim virtus pietatis, nisi charitas non ficta, humilitas vera, patientia longanimis, obedientia non segnis? In his virtutibus earumque similibus, quantum vobis liceat gloriari, vestro, fratres, relinquimus judicio; de me siquidem ingenue confiteor, quia nomina quidem earum scio, saporem adhuc ignoro. Merito itaque pudorem mihi pariter et timorem incutit habitus quem gero religionis, cui fere nullum suffragatur testimonium virtutis. Numquid enim tuto mihi possum usurpare monachi nomen et honorem, cujus non habeo meritum et virtutem; cum, sicut ante nos dictum est, simulata sanctitas, duplicata sit iniquitas (AUG. in psal. LXIII); et lupus sub ovina pelle deprehensus graviore sit sententia percellendus? Propterea vos quoque, fratres, ut tuto gloriari possitis, in hac, qua corpora vestra decorata sunt, specie pietatis, ponite corda vestra in virtute ejus; ut species in corporibus, virtus in cordibus, probatos vos exhibeant tam angelis quam hominibus, sitque, sicut scriptum est: Omnes qui viderint eos, cognoscent illos quia isti sunt semen cui benedixit Dominus (Isa. LXI, 9).

English translation

3. Brothers, I must altogether fear—whether you too should, you yourselves must judge—that this saying which was prophesied by the Apostle concerning the last times applies especially to us; where, describing the wickedness of the men who were then to come, such as can now clearly be read in the life and conduct of men, he concluded his long catalogue of evils thus: "Having," he says, "the appearance of godliness, but denying the power thereof" (II Tim. III, 5). For what has so much the appearance of godliness as this which we put forward—the humility of the tonsure and habit, the sparingness of food and drink, the almost continuous labor, and the altogether well-ordered manner of life? But as for the very power of godliness which this outward appearance promises, how little it is to be found in me and those like me—it would shame me to confess, were we not already manifest to all. For what is the power of godliness, but unfeigned charity, true humility, long-suffering patience, ready obedience? How far you may boast in these and similar virtues, brothers, I leave to your own judgment; as for myself, I confess frankly that I know the names of these virtues, but I do not yet know their taste. Rightly, then, the habit of religion which I wear strikes me with shame and fear alike, since it is supported by almost no testimony of virtue. For can I safely assume the name and honor of monk, when I do not have the merit and virtue of one? As it has been said before us, "feigned holiness is doubled iniquity" (AUG. in psal. LXIII); and a wolf caught under sheep's clothing is to be struck with a graver sentence. Therefore you too, brothers, so that you may boast safely in this outward appearance of godliness with which your bodies are adorned, place your hearts in its power; so that the appearance in your bodies and the power in your hearts may show you approved both to angels and to men, and so that it may be as it is written: "All that see them shall know them, that these are the seed which the Lord hath blessed" (Isa. LXI, 9).

Latin original

4. Haec est, fratres, christianae fidei pietas vera, hoc est habere mysterium fidei in conscientia pura. Ut enim mysterium fidei, quod hodie nobis mystica commendaverunt Magorum munera, fideliter et digne conservaremus immaculatum in conscientia pura, hodie itidem Christus baptizatus, non sibi sed nobis, Aspersit, sicut Apostolus ait, corda a conscientia mala, et abluit corpora aqua munda (Hebr. X. 22). Quam felix, o fratres, anima illa, quae sic lota in salutem, mox e vestigio secuta est Salvatorem, ut deinceps adhaerens ei cum Apostolis, illam meruit audire vocem: Qui lotus est, non indiget nisi ut pedes lavet, sed est mundus totus! (Joan. XIII, 10.) Caeterum qui baptizatur a mortuo, et iterum tangit eum, quid proficit lavatio illius? (Eccli. XXXIV, 30.) Ego postquam Baptismo lotus fui, non unum mortuum, sed tot mortuos quot opera mortis, tetigi; et, juxta Petrum apostolum, contigit mihi illud veri proverbii, Canis reversus ad vomitum suum, et sus lota in volutabro luti (II Petr. II, 4). Quid igitur faciam? quibus ulterius aquis sordes tantas diluam? Audio quia sicut una fides, sic unum Baptisma est, nec secundo Baptismati locus est, quoniam quicunque baptizamur, in morte Christi baptizamur; qui sicut semel mortuus est, sic et semel baptizatus est, ut et nobis unici modum et formam Baptismatis praescriberet. Diffiderem forsitan ulterius posse mundari, nisi quia desperantem reducit in spem, inter alias Scripturae consolationes, etiam fidele consilium Elisei, etiamsi totus instar Naaman Syri lepra sim perfusus, ac veterno incurabili possessus. Vade, inquit, et lavare septies in Jordane, et mundaberis. Si dixisset, Lavare ter, mundaberis, continuo intellexissem trinam mersionem qua inundantur baptizati, diceremque totum illud miraculum gratiam prophetare Baptismatis, ut jam tunc Jordanis de virtute Dominici Baptismi coepisset ad salutem gentium operari. Nunc autem cum signanter dicat, lavare septies in Jordane, quid sibi vult septenarius iste, aut quis est Jordanis ille? Jordanis, ut aiunt, interpretatur Descensus eorum. Quorum? Illorum utique qui humiliantur ad poenitentiam, qui nimirum quanto profundius descendunt per gradus humilitatis, tanto se penitius mergunt et plenius abluunt spiritualis alveo Jordanis.

English translation

4. This, brothers, is the true godliness of the Christian faith; this is to hold the mystery of faith in a pure conscience. For in order that we might faithfully and worthily keep undefiled in a pure conscience the mystery of faith which the mystical gifts of the Magi commended to us today, Christ, baptized today, not for himself but for us, as the Apostle says, "sprinkled our hearts from an evil conscience, and washed our bodies with clean water" (Hebr. X. 22). How happy, O brothers, is that soul which, thus washed unto salvation, straightway followed the Savior in his very footsteps, so that thereafter, clinging to him with the apostles, it deserved to hear that voice: "He that is washed needeth not but to wash his feet, but is clean wholly!" (Joan. XIII, 10.). But he who is washed after touching a dead body, and touches it again, what does his washing profit him? (Eccli. XXXIV, 30.) I, after I was washed in Baptism, have touched not one dead body, but as many dead bodies as there are works of death; and, according to the apostle Peter, that true proverb has befallen me: "The dog returned to his vomit," and "the sow washed, to her wallowing in the mire" (II Petr. II, 4). What, then, shall I do? With what further waters shall I wash away such great filth? I hear that, just as there is one faith, so there is one Baptism, and there is no place for a second Baptism, since whoever of us are baptized, are baptized into the death of Christ; who, just as he died once, so was baptized once, that he might prescribe for us too the manner and form of a single Baptism. I might perhaps despair of being able to be cleansed any further, were it not that, among the other consolations of Scripture, the faithful counsel of Elisha brings back the despairing soul to hope—even if I were, like Naaman the Syrian, entirely covered with leprosy, and possessed by an incurable lethargy. "Go," he said, "and wash seven times in the Jordan, and you shall be made clean." If he had said, "Wash three times, and you shall be made clean," I would at once have understood the threefold immersion by which the baptized are overwhelmed, and I would say that that whole miracle prophesied the grace of Baptism, so that the Jordan even then began, by the power of the Lord's future Baptism, to work for the salvation of the nations. But now, since he expressly says, "wash seven times in the Jordan," what does this number seven mean, or what is that Jordan? Jordan, as they say, is interpreted "Their Descent." Whose descent? Surely of those who are humbled unto repentance, who indeed, the more deeply they descend by the steps of humility, the more inwardly do they immerse themselves and the more fully do they wash in the channel of the spiritual Jordan.

5. Ad meipsum, o Deus, conturbata est anima mea, memor peccatorum suorum; propterea memor ero tui de terra Jordanis, quomodo scilicet in descensu humiliationis mundaveris Naaman leprosum. Descendit, inquit, et lavit septies in Jordane, juxta sermonem viri Dei, et mundatus est. Descende et tu, o anima mea, de curru superbiae in salubres aquas Jordanis, qui de fonte domus David, toto jam profluit orbe in ablutionem peccatoris et menstruatae. Haec est nimirum humilitas poenitendi, quae, de domo pariter et exemplo profluens Christi, toto jam praedicatur orbe, totiusque purgat crimina mundi. O vos, Naaman Syri: non enim unus, sed innumeri: o, inquam, divites, sed leprosi; superbi, sed aerumnosi; quare tam vehementer abhorretis lavari his medicinalibus aquis? cur vobis ita viluit noster iste Jordanis prae fluviis Damasci? Dicitis enim, si forte quaerentibus aliquando consilium salutis imitanda praedicetur humilitas et paupertas Christi: Nunquid non meliores sunt fluvii Damasci omnibus aquis Israel, ut laver in eis, et munder? (IV Reg. V, 10, 14, 12.) Nunquid non est melius, et ad purgationem peccatorum efficacius, quotidie benefacere de affluentia mundi, quam semel omnia relinquendo pauperem fieri? Sed enim audi, Naaman. Fluvium Damascenum absorbebit Leviathan, et non mirabitur; nam de Jordane si habeat fiduciam quod influat in os ejus (Job XL, 18), spes eum frustrabitur. Neque enim grande reputat quantiscunque, praeter humilitatem christianam, virtutibus affluas: cum etiam pauperes Christi devorare se posse confidat, sed nihil in eis inimicus proficiat. Damascus secundum nomen suum, Civitas sanguinis est, et aquae ejus sanguine mistae sunt; quia opera etiam bona carnalium et saecularium, vix a quocunque peccato pura sunt. Et quomodo id quod infectum est sanguine, sanguinem mundabit? qua ratione lepram, id quod leprosum est curabit? At Jordanis noster ipse quidem purum profluit, nec te calumniari poterunt superbi, si totus in eo mersus fueris, ac velut consepultus humilitati Christi.

English translation

5. Within myself, O God, my soul is troubled, mindful of its sins; therefore I will remember you from the land of the Jordan—namely, how, in the descent of humiliation, you cleansed Naaman the leper. He went down, it says, and washed seven times in the Jordan, according to the word of the man of God, and was cleansed. Go down, you too, O my soul, from the chariot of pride into the healthful waters of the Jordan, which, flowing from the fountain of the house of David, now pours forth through the whole world for the washing of the sinner and of her who is unclean. This indeed is the humility of repentance, which, flowing alike from the household and the example of Christ, is now preached throughout the whole world, and purges the crimes of the whole world. O you Naamans of Syria—for there is not one, but countless many: O, I say, rich, yet leprous; proud, yet wretched—why do you so vehemently shrink from being washed in these healing waters? Why has our Jordan grown so cheap in your eyes compared to the rivers of Damascus? For you say, if perchance to those seeking the counsel of salvation the humility and poverty of Christ is preached as something to be imitated: "Are not the rivers of Damascus better than all the waters of Israel, that I may wash in them, and be made clean?" (IV Reg. V, 10, 14, 12.). Is it not better, and more effective for the cleansing of sins, to do good daily out of the abundance of the world, than, by forsaking all at once, to become poor? But hear this, Naaman. Leviathan will swallow up the river of Damascus, and not marvel at it; for if he should have confidence that the Jordan will flow into his mouth (Job XL, 18), his hope will be disappointed. For he does not reckon it a great thing, however much you may abound in virtues apart from Christian humility, since he is confident that he can devour even the poor of Christ—but in them the enemy makes no progress. Damascus, according to its name, is the "City of Blood," and its waters are mingled with blood; for even the good works of carnal and worldly men are scarcely pure from any sin whatsoever. And how shall that which is stained with blood cleanse blood? By what reasoning shall that which is itself leprous cure leprosy? But our Jordan flows pure indeed, nor will the proud be able to slander you, if you have been wholly immersed in it, and as it were buried together with the humility of Christ.

6. Audi saltem, o Naaman, domesticos tuos, humiles amicos, fideles consiliarios. Naaman recedebat indignans adversum virum Dei, quem consuluerat; at illi, Pater, inquiunt, etsi rem grandem dixisset tibi propheta, certe facere debueras. Fidele prorsus verbum, rationis et sapientiae plenum; quippe suggestio est rationalium hominis sensuum, quos sibi Deus testes retinuit adversus hominem ipsum. Pater, inquiunt, etsi rem grandem dixisset tibi Propheta, certe facere debueras. Non credere namque Prophetae, Dei est injuria: non tentare omnia pro salute, odium est sui ipsius et invidia. Quid enim grande esse potest in praecepto, ubi salus est in promisso? Quanto magis non debet videri grande vel difficile, discere ab Jesu quia mitis est et humilis corde, cum in eo pariter inveniatur et requies animae, et praesens medela spiritualis leprae, pignusque salutis aeternae? Tandem vix aegreque obtinetur a superbo peccatore ut descendat in Jordanem et lavet; si tamen septies lavat, totumque se plenius et saepius mergat, non moratur effectus salutis fidem prophetico facere sermoni. Sed, proh dolor! quanta est hujus nostri miseria temporis! quia non solum divites, sed etiam pauperes leprosi, ita inveniuntur delicati, ut vix usque ad talos salubribus intingi patiantur aquis: aut si semel laverint, mox sibi plenissime videantur mundati.

English translation

6. Listen at least, O Naaman, to your own household, your humble friends, your faithful counselors. Naaman was going away indignant against the man of God whom he had consulted; but they said, "Father, even if the prophet had told you a great thing, surely you ought to have done it." A thoroughly faithful word, full of reason and wisdom; indeed it is the suggestion of a man's rational senses, which God has kept for himself as witnesses against the man himself. "Father," they say, "even if the Prophet had told you a great thing, surely you ought to have done it." For not to believe the Prophet is an injury to God; not to attempt everything for one's salvation is hatred and envy toward oneself. For what great thing can there be in a command, where salvation lies in the promise? How much less ought it to seem a great or difficult thing to learn from Jesus, because he is meek and humble of heart, since in him is found alike both rest for the soul, and a present remedy for spiritual leprosy, and the pledge of eternal salvation? At last, it is only with difficulty and reluctance that a proud sinner is brought to go down into the Jordan and wash; yet if he washes seven times, and plunges himself more fully and more often, the effect of salvation does not delay in proving the prophetic word true. But, alas, how great is the misery of this our present time! For not only the rich, but even the poor who are leprous, are found so delicate that they scarcely allow themselves to be dipped up to the ankles in these healthful waters; or if they wash once, they straightway seem to themselves most fully cleansed.

Latin original

7. Et Elisaeus non ita sentit, sed signanter exprimit, Septies lavare, et mundaberis. Sciebat nimirum humilitatem Christi, quae nobis imitanda est, si perfecte volumus mundari, septemplicis esse virtutis. Prima virtus, quod, cum dives esset, pauper factus est. Secunda, quod paupertati extremitatem adjiciens, in praesepi positus est. Tertia, quod Matri subditus. Quarta, quod servi manibus hodie inclinavit caput. Quinta, quod discipulum furem tulit et proditorem. Sexta, quod sic fuit mansuetus ante iniquum judicem. Septima, quod crucifixoribus tam clemens intercessor fuit apud Patrem. His vestigiis, licet satis a longe, gigantem sequeris, si paupertatem diligis, si inter pauperes extremitatem eligis, si monasterii subditus es disciplinae, si minorem tibi pateris praeesse, si falsos fratres aequanimiter tuleris, si mansuetudine vincas cum judicaris, si charitatem rependas illis a quibus injuste pateris. Haec humilitas plane sine injuria unici Baptismi rebaptizat: quia mortem Christi non iterat, sed mortificationem et sepulturam criminum instaurat; et quod in illo Baptismo gestum est specie, hic rursus impletur veritate. Haec siquidem humilitas coelos aperit, spiritum adoptionis restituit: Pater agnoscit filium, quippe in innocentia et puritate pueri regenerati reformatum. Unde bene de Naaman Scriptura meminit quia restituta est caro ejus, sicut caro pueri parvuli (IV Reg. V, 13, 10 et 14): significans ut arbitror, quia eorum, qui sic se abluunt, non solum corda recuperant innocentiam parvulorum; sed etiam corpora secundum quemdam modum puerilem sibi vindicant puritatem mortificatione membrorum. Sic enim de quodam sanctorum legitur, quia in membris defuncti, septennis quodammodo pueri gratia videbatur (De S. Martino dictum apud Sulpicium). Ecce, fratres, qui primi gratiam Baptismatis tantis voluptatum sordibus amisimus, ecce verum Jordanem, descensum scilicet humilium, ubi die rebaptizari licet, invenimus. Hoc tantum est, ut non parcamus nobis de die in diem descendere profundius, et plenius mergi, Christoque penitus consepeliri. Ipsi gratias agamus, cujus humilitas et formam Baptismi hodie dedicavit credentibus, et gratiam non disparem reservavit poenitentibus. Ipsi gloria per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

7. And Elisha thinks no differently, but expressly states: 'Wash seven times, and you shall be clean.' For he knew that the humility of Christ, which we must imitate if we wish to be perfectly cleansed, is of a sevenfold virtue. The first virtue: that, though he was rich, he became poor. The second: that, adding the extremity of poverty, he was laid in a manger. The third: that he was subject to his Mother. The fourth: that today he bowed his head to the hands of a servant. The fifth: that he bore a disciple who was a thief and a betrayer. The sixth: that he was so meek before an unjust judge. The seventh: that he was so merciful an intercessor with the Father for those who crucified him. In these footprints, though at quite a distance, you follow the giant, if you love poverty, if among the poor you choose the lowest place, if you are subject to the discipline of the monastery, if you allow one lesser than yourself to be set over you, if you bear false brothers with equanimity, if you conquer by meekness when you are judged, if you repay with charity those at whose hands you suffer unjustly. This humility plainly rebaptizes without injury to the one Baptism, because it does not repeat the death of Christ, but renews the mortification and burial of sins; and what was accomplished in that Baptism as a figure is here fulfilled again in reality. Indeed, this humility opens the heavens, restores the spirit of adoption: the Father recognizes the son, inasmuch as he is reshaped into the innocence and purity of a regenerated child. Hence Scripture rightly records of Naaman that his flesh was restored like the flesh of a little child (IV Reg. V, 13, 10 et 14): signifying, as I judge, that the hearts of those who wash themselves in this way not only recover the innocence of little children, but their bodies too, in a certain childlike manner, claim for themselves a purity through the mortification of the members. For thus it is read of a certain one of the saints, that in the limbs of the deceased there seemed to appear, as it were, the grace of a seven-year-old child. (Said of St. Martin, in Sulpicius.) Behold, brothers, we who lost the grace of the first Baptism through such great filth of pleasures—behold, we have found the true Jordan, namely the descent of the humble, where it is permitted to be rebaptized daily. This alone is needed: that we not spare ourselves from descending more deeply day by day, and being more fully submerged, and being wholly buried together with Christ. Let us give thanks to him, whose humility today both consecrated the form of Baptism for believers, and reserved a grace not unlike it for penitents. To him be glory forever and ever. Amen.
