Introduction & Scripture references

Editorial introduction

Guerric, pleading with Jeremiah that he is a child, praises John the Baptist, who prophesied before he could speak and made his parents prophets. Grace flowed principally into Mary, from her into John and from John into his parents. John stood nearer to Christ than any other mortal and prepared a perfect people for the Lord. Guerric urges the brothers to bring forth fruits worthy of repentance and seeks John's intercession. The sermon appears under the heading for the Nativity of Saint John the Baptist in the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157; the individual sermon is undated.

¶cols. 163-164
Latin original

1. Ah! Domine Deus, ecce nescio loqui, quia puer ego sum (Jerem. I, 6). Fratres, si Jeremias, sicut ex lectione audistis hesterna, ne praedicationis susciperet officium, verecundius quam justius minorem causabatur aetatem, quanto rectius ego sensum causarer minorem? Si sanctum ex utero, et quem manifeste divina mittebat electio, aetas deterrebat infirmior, mihi quae frons esse potest ad loquendum, cui nec ad sanctitatem suffragatur conscientia. nec ad sermonis facultatem suppetit scientia? Illi erat timori pueritia sua, mihi non erit puerilitas mea? Verumtamen tu, puer propheta Altissimi, de quo sermonem hodie nostri debet locus officii, puerilitatem nostram non pateris excusatam haberi, quin saltem gestiamus more puerorum de te quoquomodo balbutire; qui etiam in materia leviori, plena necdum didicimus verba formare. Tuum erit, o vox Verbi, vox sapientiae, tibi devotae solvere vinculum linguae, qui necdum loquens, usum loquendi muto potuisti patri reformare. Nec sine fenore magno rediit intercepta facultas loquendi, cui simul accessit gratia prophetandi, ut videlicet uterque parens aliquid sibi gauderet praerogatum de praerogativa filii.

English translation

1. "Ah, Lord God, behold, I know not how to speak, for I am a child" (Jerem. I, 6). Brethren, if Jeremiah, as you heard from yesterday's reading, pleaded his younger age—more out of modesty than out of right—so as not to take up the office of preaching, how much more rightly might I plead my lesser understanding? If a man holy from the womb, and one whom the divine election was manifestly sending, was deterred by his tender age, what boldness can there be for me to speak, for whom conscience lends no support toward holiness, nor knowledge supplies ability of speech? If his boyhood was a source of fear to him, shall not my childishness be one for me? Yet you, O boy-prophet of the Most High, of whom our office today must speak, do not allow our childishness to excuse us from being at least eager, in the manner of children, to stammer something about you however we can—we who, even in lighter matters, have not yet learned to form complete words. It will be yours, O voice of the Word, voice of Wisdom, to loose the bond of the tongue devoted to you—you who, before you could yet speak, were able to restore to your mute father the use of speech. Nor did the recovered faculty of speech return without great profit, since along with it came also the grace of prophesying, so that each parent might rejoice in having received something as a prerogative from the prerogative of the son.

2. Qui enim propheta, et plus quam propheta futurus erat, imo ante prophetare quam profari, prius Deum quam se ipsum sentire coeperat, parentes quoque suos prophetas faciebat; et in eos qui in se transfuderant carnis substantiam, spiritus sui et gratiae superabundantiam refundebat. Nam et Elisabeth, postquam exsultavit in gaudio infans in utero ejus, tunc et ipsa prophetavit repleta Spiritu sancto; quia necdum loquens filius, matri tamen occultam Domini praesentiam exsultatione manifesta revelavit. Salutabat Salvatorem motu, quo poterat, et in occursum Domini praecursor impiger erumpere gestiebat. Plane incomparabilis gratia, inaestimabilis divinae virtutis potentia. Vox Mariae dum auribus Elisabeth insonat, ad cor Joannis penetrat, qui abstrusus intra materna latebat viscera, spiritum ejus animat, gaudioque salutari vegetat: et cui virtus naturae vix adhuc totam infuderat animam, virtus vocis Mariae pleniorem infudit prophetiam, adeo ut etiam de plenitudine filii copiose videatur in matrem refundi. Vere gratia plena Maria: manifeste Deus totius gratiae in ea erat, de cujus munificentia tam copiose, tam magnifice, principaliter in Matrem, de Matre in Joannem, de Joanne in parentes gratiae largitas profluebat. Flumina prorsus de ventre Mariae fluebant aquae vivae, et fons vitae et gratiae oriebatur de medio paradisi ad irriganda ligna paradisi.

English translation

2. For he who was to be a prophet, and more than a prophet—indeed, who began to perceive God before he perceived himself, and to prophesy before he could speak—made his own parents prophets as well; and upon those who had transfused into him the substance of their flesh, he poured back the overflow of his spirit and grace. For Elizabeth too, after the infant leapt for joy in her womb, then herself prophesied, filled with the Holy Spirit; because the son, not yet able to speak, nevertheless revealed to his mother by manifest exultation the hidden presence of the Lord. He greeted the Savior by the movement he was able to make, and, as forerunner, longed eagerly to burst forth to meet the Lord. Truly incomparable grace, inestimable power of divine virtue! While the voice of Mary sounded in the ears of Elizabeth, it penetrated to the heart of John, who lay hidden within his mother's womb; it animated his spirit and quickened him with saving joy. And to him, into whom the power of nature had scarcely yet infused a complete soul, the power of Mary's voice infused a fuller prophecy, so much so that from the fullness of the son there seems to have overflowed abundantly even into the mother. Truly "full of grace" is Mary: manifestly the God of all grace was in her, from whose munificence the bounty of grace flowed so abundantly, so magnificently—principally into the Mother, from the Mother into John, from John into the parents. Truly rivers of living water flowed from the womb of Mary, and the fountain of life and grace arose from the midst of paradise to water the trees of paradise.

3. Proxima erat fonti cedrus ista nobilis, Joannem loquor, consobrinum et amicum sponsi, praecursorem, Baptistam, et martyrem Domini: ideoque nimirum uberius irrigata in tantum excrevit, ut inter natos mulierum nihil illa sublimius posset inveniri. Omnino Salvatori proximus erat, qui non solum copula sanguinis conjunctus, aut amicitia intimus erat, sed etiam gloria annuntiationis, novitate nativitatis, sanctitate pene originali, praedicatione consimili, auctoritate baptizandi, virtute patiendi, ante mortales omnes ei propius accedebat. Denique si alia cessarent omnia, si cuncta de eo silerent prophetarum praeconia; solius gratia nominis ejus (9)1, quod vocatum est ab angelo, priusquam in utero conciperetur, satis abundeque singularem in eo futuram Dei gratiam testabatur. Dignum quippe erat ut gratiam Dei, quam gratia plena profudit, plenus gratia praedicaret, et gratia non mediocri emineret, qui gratiae tempus a tempore legis velut medius limes disterminaret. Usque ad Joannem enim Lex et Prophetae prophetaverunt (Luc. XVI, 16); qui videlicet primus ostendit praesentem, quem Lex et Prophetia promiserant venturum.

English translation

3. Nearest to the fountain was this noble cedar—I speak of John, cousin and friend of the Bridegroom, forerunner, Baptist, and martyr of the Lord: and therefore, watered more abundantly, he grew so great that among those born of women none more exalted could be found. He was altogether nearest to the Savior—he who was joined not only by the bond of blood or intimate in friendship, but who also approached nearer to him than all other mortals, in the glory of the annunciation, the novelty of his birth, a holiness almost original, a preaching very similar, authority to baptize, and courage in suffering. In short, if all else were silent, if the proclamations of the prophets said nothing of him; the grace of his name alone (9)1—which was given by an angel before he was conceived in the womb—would sufficiently and abundantly testify to the singular grace of God that was to be in him. For it was fitting that he who was full of grace should preach the grace of God which she who was full of grace poured forth, and that he who was to mark the dividing line, as it were, between the time of grace and the time of the law, should himself stand out with no small grace. For "the Law and the Prophets prophesied until John" (Luc. XVI, 16); he indeed was the first to show as present the one whom the Law and Prophecy had promised as coming.

4. Merito igitur nativitas hujus pueri multos gaudere tunc fecit, hodieque facit, qui in senectute parentum natus, mundo senescenti gratiam praedicaturus erat renascendi. Merito nativitatem istam, quam mirabiliter operatur gratia, miraturque natura, solemniter veneratur Ecclesia: praesertim cum singularis illius nativitatis, cujus gratia naturam reparavit, per hanc pignora fidelia sibi videat esse praemissa. Non ingratam se probat Ecclesia, non immemorem: fideliter agnoscit qua devotione, qua gratiarum actione suscipere debeat Praecursorem, per quem ipsum agnovit Salvatorem. Mihi plane novo nascitur gaudio lucerna mundi, cujus indicio lucem veram agnovi lucentem in tenebris, sed non comprehensam a tenebris. Mihi prorsus ineffabili nascitur gaudio, cum quo tot et tanta mundo nascuntur bona; qui videlicet Ecclesiam primus catechizat, per poenitentiam initiat, per baptismum praeparat, praeparatam Christo consignat et copulat; et quam ad vivendum temperanter informat, etiam ad moriendum fortiter propria morte confirmat, atque in his omnibus plebem perfectam Domino parat.

English translation

4. Rightly, then, did the birth of this boy cause many to rejoice at that time, and it causes rejoicing today as well—he who, born in the old age of his parents, was to preach to an aging world the grace of being born again. Rightly does the Church solemnly venerate this birth, which grace wrought so wondrously and nature marveled at: especially since she sees in it faithful pledges sent before her of that singular nativity whose grace repaired nature. The Church proves herself neither ungrateful nor forgetful: she faithfully recognizes with what devotion, with what thanksgiving, she ought to receive the Forerunner, through whom she came to know the Savior himself. For me indeed the lamp of the world is born with new joy, by whose sign I have recognized the true light shining in the darkness, yet not overcome by the darkness. For me indeed he is born with ineffable joy, with whom so many and such great goods are born to the world—he who first catechizes the Church, initiates her through penance, prepares her through baptism, and, once prepared, consigns and joins her to Christ; and who forms her to live temperately, and also, by his own death, confirms her to die bravely, and in all these things prepares a perfect people for the Lord.

5. O fratres, quorum propositum est (utinam et ita desiderium) ad perfectionem festinare; quam cito christianam assequeretur perfectionem, qui huic magistro animum accommodaret docibilem: cujus etiam prima elementa justitiae modum supergressa sunt perfectionis humanae; cujus rudimenta primaevae aetatis gravitatem sapientiae vicere senilis! Qui enim, antequam natus, sanctus fuit, quid mirum si in processu conversationis plus quam sanctus fuit? Mirari possumus, imitari non possumus sanctitatem tuam, sanctorum sanctissime: omnino necesse est ut, qui de publicanis et peccatoribus plebem perfectam Domino parare festinas, humanius eis loquaris quam vivas; et modum perfectionis non secundum vitae formulam tuae, sed secundum virtutem mediocritatis temperes humanae. Facite, inquit, dignos fructus poenitentiae (Matth. III, 8). Nos quidem, o fratres, gloriamur perfectius loqui, quam vivere: Joannes autem sublimius vivens quam possint homines vel intelligere, loquitur tamen eis prout possunt audire. Facite, inquit, fructus dignos poenitentiae Humanum, inquit, dico, propter infirmitatem carnis vestrae (Rom. VI, 19) Si nondum esse potest in vobis omnium plenitudo bonorum, sit saltem vera omnium poenitudo malorum. Si nondum facere praevaletis perfectae fructus justitiae, sit interim haec vestra perfectio, ut faciatis dignos fructus poenitentiae.

English translation

5. O brothers, whose purpose it is (would that the desire were likewise!) to hasten toward perfection: how quickly would that man attain Christian perfection who made his mind teachable to this master—whose very first elements of righteousness surpassed the measure of human perfection; whose rudiments, in his earliest age, outdid the gravity of old men's wisdom! For he who was holy before he was born—what wonder if, in the course of his life, he was more than holy? We can marvel at your holiness, O holiest of the holy ones, but we cannot imitate it. It is altogether necessary that you, who hasten to prepare for the Lord a perfect people out of publicans and sinners, speak to them more mildly than you live, and temper the measure of perfection not according to the pattern of your own life, but according to the capacity of human mediocrity. 'Bring forth,' he says, 'fruits worthy of repentance' (Matth. III, 8). We indeed, O brothers, boast of speaking more perfectly than we live; but John, living more sublimely than men can even understand, nevertheless speaks to them as they are able to hear. 'Bring forth,' he says, 'fruits worthy of repentance. I speak in human terms,' he says, 'because of the weakness of your flesh' (Rom. VI, 19). If the fullness of all good things cannot yet be in you, let there at least be true repentance for all evil things. If you cannot yet manage to produce the fruits of perfect righteousness, let this be your perfection for the present: that you bring forth fruits worthy of repentance.

6. Si recordari volumus, fratres, lectionis hesternae, ad dignos fructus poenitentiae pertinet quod Jeremiae, aut certe Joanni sub typo Jeremiae dictum est, Ut evellas, et destruas, et disperdas, et dissipes: ad fructus justitiae, ut aedifices et plantes (Jerem. I, 10, et XVIII, 7). Beatus qui manus hujus exstirpatoris adjuverit (nam ipse Dei adjutor est), qui videlicet cooperans verbo et gratiae Dei, in hoc studium et operam dederit ut evellat et disperdat de affectionibus et moribus suis omnem plantationem quam non plantavit Pater coelestis, destruat et dissipet omnem aedificationem Babylonicae superbiae et confusionis; ut postmodum melius aedificet et plantet, fiatque sicut scriptum est, Lateres ceciderunt, sed quadris lapidibus aedificabimus. Sycomoros succiderunt, sed cedros immutabimus (Isa. IX, 10). Ascendat abies pro saliuncula, et myrtus pro urtica (Id. LV, 13): omnis scilicet pulchritudo et gratia virtutum, pro situ et horrore vitiorum. Quis, putas, est ille tam perfectus in nobis, ut saltem hoc initium perfectionis habeat, qui scilicet ita digne perfecteque poenitens sit, ac tota severitate damnet mala sua quae fecit, prioribus usquequaque renuntians vitiis, ut omne germen maledictionis radicitus evellat et disperdat de agro cordis, ita ut de radice pessima, nullus amplius germinet fructus amaritudinis; quique semel ita destruat ac dissipet omnem altitudinem extollentem se adversus humilitatem Christi, ut iterum non reaedificet quae destruxit? Quam felix hodie esset Ecclesia sanctorum, quanta pace et gratia florerent congregationes beatorum pauperum, si fructus istos quos facere debuerat, rudium poenitentia, vel eorum qui perfecti et sancti videri volunt, afferret justitia! Denique, qui nec justitiam sanctorum, nec exactam habemus poenitentiam peccatorum, redimamus vel ex parte aliqua teporem nostrum devota veneratione sanctorum: praecipue beati Joannis, cujus tam magnifice sanctitas effloruit supra modum aliorum, ut crederetur Sanctus sanctorum. Magnificentiam gloriae sanctitatis ejus loquamur ac meditemur, fratres mei, ut propitium faciat peccatis nostris eum, cujus singulariter amicus esse meruit, Filium Dei, qui cum Patre et Spiritu sancto vivit et regnat per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

6. If we wish to recall, brothers, yesterday's reading, it belongs to worthy fruits of repentance what was said to Jeremiah—or indeed to John under the type of Jeremiah—'that you may root out, and pull down, and destroy, and throw down'; and to the fruits of righteousness belongs, 'that you may build and plant' (Jerem. I, 10, et XVIII, 7). Blessed is he who helps the hands of this uprooter (for he himself is God's helper)—namely, one who, cooperating with the word and grace of God, devotes study and effort to this: that he may root out and cast away from his affections and habits every planting which the heavenly Father did not plant, and may destroy and throw down every edifice of Babylonian pride and confusion; so that afterward he may build and plant the better, and it may come to pass as it is written: 'The bricks have fallen, but we will build with squared stones; the sycamores have been cut down, but we will change them for cedars' (Isa. IX, 10). 'Let the fir tree rise up in place of the brier, and the myrtle in place of the nettle' (Id. LV, 13)—that is, let all the beauty and grace of the virtues rise up in place of the squalor and horror of the vices. Who, do you suppose, is so perfect among us that he has even this beginning of perfection—namely, one who repents so worthily and so perfectly, and with total severity condemns the evils he has done, utterly renouncing his former vices, that he roots out and casts away, down to the root, every seed of cursing from the field of his heart, so that from that most evil root no fruit of bitterness may ever sprout again; and who, having once destroyed and thrown down every height that exalts itself against the humility of Christ, does not build up again what he has destroyed? How happy would the Church of the saints be today, with what peace and grace would the congregations of the blessed poor flourish, if the repentance of the untaught, or the righteousness of those who wish to appear perfect and holy, bore those fruits which it ought to have produced In short, since we have neither the righteousness of the saints nor the exact repentance of sinners, let us redeem our lukewarmness, at least in some part, by devout veneration of the saints—especially of blessed John, whose holiness blossomed so magnificently beyond the measure of others that he was believed to be the Holy One of the holy ones. Let us speak of and meditate upon the magnificence of the glory of his holiness, my brothers, so that he may render favorable toward our sins the one whose singular friend he merited to be—the Son of God, who with the Father and the Holy Spirit lives and reigns through all the ages of ages. Amen.
