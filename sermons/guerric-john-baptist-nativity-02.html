Editorial introduction

Guerric reads the saying that from the days of John the Baptist the kingdom of heaven suffers violence. Repentance is the force by which sinners seize what was not granted to nature, and the brothers must wrestle without relenting, as Jacob wrestled with the angel. Christ, weakened even to death by love, cannot resist such force. Guerric calls the brothers to magnanimity and constancy under the example of John. The sermon appears under the heading for the Nativity of Saint John the Baptist in the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157; the individual sermon is undated.

English translation

On the force necessary to seize the kingdom of heaven.

¶cols. 167-168
Latin original

1. A diebus Joannis Baptistae regnum coelorum vim patitur, et violenti diripiunt illud (Matth. XI, 12). Merito igitur laeta nobis est ejus nativitas, cujus tam fausta sunt tempora, ut regnum Dei, jam exinde nobis expositum sit ad diripiendum, quibus utique justitia non sufficiebat ad promerendum. Merito multi in nativitate ejus gaudent, sicut promittebat angelus; sub quo tanta felicitate mutatus est temporum status, ut regnum Dei quod ante nulla innocentium obtinebat justitia, nunc poenitentium invadat et possideat violentia. Quid enim aliud debuit dici poenitentia peccatorum, nisi violentia in regnum coelorum? An non violentia est, rapere virtute quod non erat concessum naturae; ut qui natura erant filii irae et gehennae, labore improbo qui omnia vincit, intrudant se in haereditatem sanctorum et consortium gloriae? An non plane violentus Deo strenuus ille luctator patriarcha Jacob, qui, ut scriptum est, contra Deum fortis fuit et invaluit, qui cum eo luctatus usque mane, constanter et obnixe tenebat rogantem dimitti, Non dimittam te, inquiens, nisi benedixeris mihi? Dico quia cum Deo luctatus est: Deus siquidem erat in angelo, cum quo luctatus est. Alioquin nec angelus diceret, Cur quaeris nomen meum, quod est mirabile? nec Jacob diceret, Vidi Dominum facie ad faciem (Gen. XXXII, 26, 29, 30): sed neque Dominus de Jacob diceret per Osee prophetam, in Bethel locutus est nobiscum (Osee XII, 4). Bona igitur violentia, quae benedictionem extorsit: felix lucta, qua Deus homini succubuit, victusque victorem gratia benedictionis et honore sanctioris nominis muneravit! Quid enim, si tetigit nervum femoris ejus, et emarcuit, ac de caetero claudicavit? Facilis jactura corporis damnumque consolabile, quod tanto compensatum est munere: praesertim illi qui potuit dicere, Super salutem et omnem pulchritudinem dilexi sapientiam (Sap. VII, 10). Utinam marcescat in me non solum nervus femoris, sed virtus totius corporis; dummodo vel unam merear benedictionem angeli. Utinam non solo1 claudicem cum Jacob, sed et moriar cum Paulo, ut gratiam et nomen Israelis obtineam munere perpetuo. Jacob quidem femur gerit marcidum, Paulus vero, corpus emortuum: quia mortificationem membrorum, quam coepit propheticae religionis initium, ad integrum consummavit Evangelium. Jacob claudicans, quia ex parte quae mundi sunt cogitans, alterum pedem a terra suspensum portat. Paulus cogitans solum quae Dei sunt, sive in corpore, sive extra corpus, nescit, Deus scit (II Cor. XII, 3), totus tamen in spiritu liber ad coelum volat.

English translation

1. "From the days of John the Baptist the kingdom of heaven suffers violence, and the violent bear it away" (Matth. XI, 12). Rightly, then, is his birth joyful for us, since its times are so blessed that the kingdom of God is thenceforth set before us to be seized by force—by us, indeed, for whom righteousness alone would not have sufficed to merit it. Rightly do many rejoice at his nativity, as the angel promised; for under him the state of the times was changed to such happiness that the kingdom of God, which before no righteousness of the innocent could obtain, is now invaded and possessed by the violence of the penitent. For what else ought the repentance of sinners to be called, but violence against the kingdom of heaven? Is it not violence, to seize by force what was not granted to nature; so that those who were by nature children of wrath and of hell, by relentless labor that conquers all things, thrust themselves into the inheritance of the saints and the fellowship of glory? Was not that strenuous wrestler, the patriarch Jacob, plainly violent toward God, who, as it is written, was strong against God and prevailed, who, wrestling with him until morning, held fast, steadfastly and insistently, to him who asked to be let go, saying, "I will not let thee go, except thou bless me"? I say that he wrestled with God: for God indeed was in the angel with whom he wrestled. Otherwise the angel would not have said, "Why dost thou ask my name, which is wonderful?" nor would Jacob have said, "I have seen the Lord face to face" (Gen. XXXII, 26, 29, 30): nor would the Lord have said of Jacob through the prophet Hosea, "In Bethel he spoke with us" (Osee XII, 4). Good, then, was the violence that extorted a blessing: happy the struggle, in which God yielded to man, and, being conquered, rewarded the victor with the grace of a blessing and the honor of a holier name! For what matter is it, if he touched the sinew of his thigh, and it withered, and he limped thereafter? Easy is the loss of the body, and consolable the damage, which was compensated by so great a gift: especially for him who could say, "Above health and all beauty have I loved wisdom" (Sap. VII, 10). Would that not only1 the sinew of my thigh might wither in me, but the strength of my whole body, provided only I might merit even one blessing of the angel. Would that I might not only limp with Jacob, but also die with Paul, that I might obtain the grace and name of Israel by an everlasting gift. Jacob indeed bears a withered thigh, but Paul a body as it were dead: because the mortification of the members, which the beginning of prophetic religion first undertook, the Gospel brought to full consummation. Jacob limps, because, thinking in part on the things that are of the world, he carries one foot suspended from the earth. Paul, thinking only on the things that are of God, whether in the body or out of the body, he knows not, God knows (II Cor. XII, 3), yet wholly free in spirit flies to heaven.

2. Vobis itaque dicimus, fratres, qui aggressi estis coelum rapere, qui congressi estis luctari cum angelo qui viam custodit ligni vitae: vobis, inquam, dicimus omnino necesse esse ut constanter et irremisse luctemini; non dico usque ad enervationem femoris, unde generatio propagatur carnis, sed etiam usque ad mortificationem corporis. Hoc tamen ipsum nec sic labor noster poterit assequi, nisi tactu et beneficio divinae virtutis, cum vestram videlicet constantiam invincibilem sibi probaverit. Sic enim scriptum est: Cumque videret quod eum superare non posset, tetigit nervum femoris ejus, et statim emarcuit! (Gen. XXXII, 25). An non tibi cum angelo, imo cum Deo ipso luctari videris, quando quotidie praeproperis tuis resistit votis? Lavaris velut aquis nivis, ut mundus sis corde et corpore, et ipse sordibus intingit te. Dicis, Sapiens efficiar (Eccle. VII, 24), et ipse longius recedit a te. Clamas ad eum, nec exaudit te: vis accedere ad eum, et repellit te. Decernis rem, et in contrarium tibi cedit, et per omnia fere in duritia manus suae adversatur tibi. O dissimulatrix clementia, quae duritiam te simulas; quanta pietate pugnas adversus eos pro quibus pugnas! Licet enim haec celes in corde tuo, scio tamen quia diligis diligentes te, et immensa multitudo dulcedinis tuae, quam abscondis timentibus te. Noli igitur desperare, constanter age, felix anima, quae cum Deo luctari coepisti: amat utique vim abs te pati, desiderat a te superari. Nam et cum iratus est, manumque ad feriendum extendit, quaerit, ut ipse confitetur, virum similem Moysi, qui resistat sibi; et si non invenerit, conqueritur et dicit: Non est qui consurgat, et teneat me (Isai. LXIV, 7). Nam si implacabilis sit ejus ira, inflexibilisque sententia, flebit et dicet Jeremias qui resistere tentaverat: Fortior fuisti, et invaluisti (Jerem. XX, 7).

English translation

2. To you, then, we speak, brothers, who have set out to seize heaven, who have come to grapple with the angel who guards the way of the tree of life: to you, I say, we declare that it is altogether necessary that you wrestle steadfastly and without relenting; I do not say to the withering of the thigh, whence the generation of flesh is propagated, but even to the mortification of the body. Yet even this our labor will not be able to attain, unless by the touch and favor of divine power, when he has proved your constancy invincible to himself. For thus it is written: "And when he saw that he could not overcome him, he touched the sinew of his thigh, and immediately it withered" (Gen. XXXII, 25). Do you not seem to yourself to wrestle with the angel, nay with God himself, when daily he resists your hasty desires? You wash yourself as it were with the waters of snow, that you may be clean in heart and body, and he dips you in filth. You say, "I shall become wise" (Eccle. VII, 24), and he withdraws further from you. You cry out to him, and he does not hear you: you wish to approach him, and he repels you. You determine upon a thing, and it turns out contrary to you, and in almost everything, by the hardness of his hand, he opposes you. O dissembling mercy, that feigns hardness; with how great pity do you fight against those for whom you fight! For although you conceal these things in your heart, I know nevertheless that you love those who love you, and the immense abundance of your sweetness, which you hide for those who fear you. Do not despair, then; act steadfastly, happy soul, who have begun to wrestle with God: he indeed loves to suffer violence from you, he desires to be overcome by you. For even when he is angry, and stretches out his hand to strike, he seeks, as he himself confesses, a man like Moses, who may resist him; and if he does not find one, he complains and says: "There is none that ariseth, and taketh hold of me" (Isai. LXIV, 7). For if his anger were implacable, and his sentence inflexible, Jeremiah, who had attempted to resist, will weep and say: "Thou wast stronger than I, and didst prevail" (Jerem. XX, 7).

3. Sed absit, fratres, qui placita ei postulatis, absit ut fortis adversum vos sit, qui pro vobis infirmari usque ad mortem voluit! Tot vulneribus confossus est, toto corpore crucifixus est; unde, quaeso, virtus ei esse potest ad resistendum illi charitati, quae veluti victum et captum per omnia genera infirmitatum usque ad mortem, mortem autem crucis perduxit? Jam non fortis ut mors, sed fortior quam mors dilectio: cum virtute dilectionis infirmata sit usque ad mortem Dei fortitudo; cujus tamen infirmitas fortior inventa est omni fortissimo; cujus mors probata est esse mors tua, o mors. Virtute igitur dilectionis armatus sis, quicunque es ille pius invasor, qui rapere contendis regnum coelorum: et securus esto, quia facile vinces regem ipsum coelorum. Si qua enim videtur tibi adversari difficultate, aut duritia; ne sis pusillanimis, sed intellige qua mente id faciat. Nempe ut contrarietate ipse tibi acuat animum, sicut solet esse natura magnanimorum ac fortium; ut vires exercitet, constantiam probet, multiplicet victorias, augeatque coronas.

English translation

3. But far be it, brothers—you who ask of him what is pleasing—far be it that he should be strong against you, he who was willing to be weakened even unto death for you! He was pierced with so many wounds, he was crucified in his whole body; whence, I ask, can he have strength to resist that love which led him, as though conquered and captured, through every kind of weakness even unto death—death, moreover, of the cross? Now love is not strong as death, but stronger than death: since by the strength of love the strength of God was weakened even unto death; yet his weakness was found stronger than every strongest thing; whose death has proved to be your death, O death. Be armed, therefore, with the strength of love, whoever you are, you devout invader who strive to seize the kingdom of heaven: and be assured that you will easily conquer the King of heaven himself. For if some difficulty or hardness seems to oppose you, do not be faint of heart, but understand with what mind he does this. Surely it is so that by this opposition he may himself sharpen your spirit, as is the nature of the magnanimous and strong; so that he may exercise your strength, test your constancy, multiply your victories, and increase your crowns.

4. Quamobrem accingimini, filii Israel, et estote filii fortitudinis: non enim opus est nisi magnanimitate et constantia, quae nullis possit terreri adversis. Infirmus dicat, Fortis sum: et prae gaudio spei se nesciat infirmum, cui tam facile jam jamque capiendum est coelum. Plane coelum violenter rapit, qui imbecillitati suae vim facit, aut aetati. Imo, ut magis proprie loquar, plane vim facit propriae perditioni, qui intonante tuba mandati, nescit parcere sibi. Homo enim in laboribus, ut Scriptura ait, laborat sibi, et vim facit perditioni suae (Prov. XVI, sec. LXX). Accingimini, inquam, viri virtutis, et sequimini ducem ac magistrum felicis hujus militiae; Joannem Baptistam loquor, a diebus cujus coelum esse coepit expugnabile. Iste siquidem est, qui velut alter David princeps factus latronum, piorumque ductor praedonum, et victoriosum illum exercitum publicanorum et peccatorum per illam laudabilem ac religiosam violentiam post se induxit in regnum coelorum. Quis enim scelestus aut profanus tubam ejus audivit, Poenitentiam agite, appropinquavit enim regnum coelorum (Matth. III, 2); et non se continuo praeparavit ad bellum? Sequimini, inquam, ducem istum, cujus vexilla proprio rutilant sanguine, cujus hodie virtutes ac triumphos debita decantastis veneratione. Ipse, nisi fallor, quos post se traxerit exemplo, juvabit merito, commendabitque suffragio, cum inter natos mulierum nullus sit acceptior regi summo Jesu Christo Domino nostro, qui vivit et regnat per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

4. Wherefore gird yourselves, sons of Israel, and be sons of fortitude: for nothing is needed but magnanimity and constancy, which can be terrified by no adversities. Let the weak man say, I am strong; and for the joy of hope let him not know himself weak, for whom heaven is now so easily, at any moment, to be seized. He plainly seizes heaven by violence who does violence to his own weakness, or to his age. Indeed, to speak more properly, he plainly does violence to his own ruin who, when the trumpet of the command is sounding, does not know how to spare himself. For man, as Scripture says, labors for himself in his labors, and does violence to his own ruin (Prov. XVI, sec. LXX). Gird yourselves, I say, men of valor, and follow the leader and master of this happy warfare—I speak of John the Baptist, from whose days heaven began to be stormable. For he it is who, like another David made prince of robbers, and leader of devout plunderers, led after him into the kingdom of heaven that victorious army of publicans and sinners through that praiseworthy and religious violence. For what wicked or profane man heard his trumpet, 'Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand' (Matth. III, 2), and did not immediately prepare himself for war? Follow, I say, this leader, whose banners gleam red with his own blood, whose virtues and triumphs you have today sung with due veneration. He himself, unless I am mistaken, will aid by his merit and commend by his intercession those whom he has drawn after him by example, since among those born of women none is more acceptable to the highest King, Jesus Christ our Lord, who lives and reigns through all ages of ages. Amen.
