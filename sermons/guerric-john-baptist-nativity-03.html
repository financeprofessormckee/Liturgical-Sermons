Introduction & Scripture references

Editorial introduction

Guerric takes up Christ's praise that no one born of women is greater than John the Baptist, and sets it beside the praise God gave Noah, Moses and David. John's greatness lay in humility: thought to be the greatest of all, he declared himself unworthy to take off Christ's sandals, and so Christ glorified him. Guerric warns that the proud imitate the devil by envy and detraction, while John lessened himself so that another might appear. The sermon appears under the heading for the Nativity of Saint John the Baptist in the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157; the individual sermon is undated.

English translation

Again, on the praises of the Saint.

¶cols. 169-170
Latin original

1. Inter natos mulierum non surrexit major Joanne Baptista (Matth. XI, 11). Salomon ait: Laudent te labia proximi tui (Prov. XXVII, 2). Sed quam felicius et gloriosius, cum laudant quempiam labia Dei sui! Deus quippe nec falli potest, nec adulari. Deus neminem facile laudat, quem laude posse inflari videat, vel quem ex fine debere reprobari praevideat. Homini quidem recte dicitur, ne laudet hominem in vita sua; cujus sicut scire non potest intima, si nec praescire novissima; cum de seipso quoque confiteri habeat: Nihil mihi conscius sum, sed non in hoc justificatus sum (I Cor. IV, 4). Sunt enim justi et sapientes, et opera eorum in manu Dei sunt et tamen nemo scit utrum sit dignus amore an odio, sed omnia in futurum servantur incerta (Eccle. IX, 1, 2). Felix igitur, qui testimonio ipsius Judicis dignum se amore scire meruerit; nisi quod testimonium justitiae praesentis, mutabilitati humanae suspicionem ac metum non adimit de futuris. Indubitabile tamen eximiae virtutis et magnae perfectionis est indicium, quoties hominem adhuc corruptioni subditum, suae suffragio laudis illud irrefragabile Dei dignatur judicium. Magnum profecto justitiae praeconium quod summa justitia dicit ad Noe: Te vidi justum coram me (Gen. VII, 1). Magni quidem insigne meriti, quod Abrahae testificatur Deus, propter ipsum implendas esse promissiones, quae ad ipsum factae sunt (Gen. XVII, et XXII, 17, 18). Quantae autem illud est gloriae, quod de beato Job Dominus gloriatur adversus invidum: Numquid considerasti servum meum Job, quod non sit ei similis in terra: homo simplex et rectus, et timens Deum, ac recedens a malo? (Job. I, 8.) Quantae etiam illud erat gratiae, quod pro Moyse aemulabatur et confundebat aemulos ejus? Si quis, inquit, fuerit inter vos propheta Domini, in visione apparebo ei, vel per somnium loquar ad illum. At non talis servus meus Moyses, qui in omni domo mea fidelissimus est. Ore enim ad os loquor ei, et palam, non per aenigmata videt Deum. Quare ergo non timuistis detrahere servo meo Moysi? (Num. XII, 6-8.) Quis vero in omnibus sicut David, de quo gratulatur Dominus, quia invenit virum secundum cor suum? (III Reg. XI, 4.)

English translation

1. Among those born of women there has not arisen a greater than John the Baptist (Matth. XI, 11). Solomon says: Let the lips of your neighbor praise you (Prov. XXVII, 2). But how much more blessed and glorious it is when the lips of his God praise someone! For God can neither be deceived nor flattered. God does not easily praise anyone whom he sees could be puffed up by praise, or whom he foresees must in the end be rejected. To a man indeed it is rightly said that he should not praise a man in his lifetime; for just as he cannot know his inmost parts, so he cannot foreknow his final end; since he must also confess of himself: I know nothing against myself, yet I am not thereby justified (I Cor. IV, 4). For there are just and wise men, and their works are in the hand of God, and yet no one knows whether he is worthy of love or of hatred, but all things are kept uncertain for the future (Eccle. IX, 1, 2). Happy, therefore, is he who by the testimony of the Judge himself has deserved to know himself worthy of love; except that the testimony of present righteousness does not remove from human mutability the suspicion and fear concerning things to come. Nevertheless it is an undoubted sign of surpassing virtue and great perfection whenever that unshakable judgment of God deigns to support with its praise a man still subject to corruption. Great indeed is the proclamation of righteousness when supreme Righteousness says to Noah: I have seen you just before me (Gen. VII, 1). Great indeed is the mark of merit when God testifies concerning Abraham, that for his sake the promises made to him would be fulfilled (Gen. XVII, et XXII, 17, 18). But how great is that glory, when the Lord boasts of blessed Job against the envious one: Have you considered my servant Job, that there is none like him on the earth: a simple and upright man, and fearing God, and avoiding evil? (Job. I, 8.) How great also was that grace, that he was jealous on Moses' behalf and confounded his rivals? If there be, he says, a prophet of the Lord among you, I will appear to him in a vision, or speak to him in a dream. But not so my servant Moses, who is most faithful in all my house. For I speak to him mouth to mouth, and openly, not in riddles does he see God. Why then did you not fear to speak against my servant Moses? (Num. XII, 6-8.) But who among all men was like David, of whom the Lord rejoices, because he found a man after his own heart? (III Reg. XI, 4.)

2. Verumtamen quantumlibet magni fuerint, sive isti, sive alii; nec inter istos, nec inter alios natos mulierum, teste Nato Virginis, surrexit major Joanne Baptista. Nam etsi stella differat a stella in claritate, et in choro sanctorum siderum, quae ante exortum veri Solis illustrarunt noctem hujus saeculi, quadam effulserunt claritate mirabili; nullum tamen in omnibus majus aut splendidius lucifero isto, lucerna scilicet ardenti et lucenti, quam Pater Christo suo paravit: lucifero, inquam, praelucanae lucis, qui praecursor Solis diem instantem mortalibus nuntiavit; dormientibus in tenebris et umbra mortis clamitans et dicens: Poenitentiam agite; appropinquavit enim regnum coelorum (Matth. III, 2). Ac si diceret: Nox praecessit, dies autem appropinquavit: abjicite opera tenebrarum (Rom. XIII, 12). Surge, qui dormis, et exsurge a mortuis, et illuminabit te Christus (Ephes. V, 14).

English translation

2. Nevertheless, however great these men were, whether these or others; neither among these nor among others born of women, by the testimony of the Son born of the Virgin, has there arisen a greater than John the Baptist. For although star differs from star in brightness, and in the choir of holy stars which before the rising of the true Sun illumined the night of this age, shining with a certain wondrous brightness; yet none among them all was greater or more splendid than this day-star, that is, the lamp burning and shining, which the Father prepared for his Christ: the day-star, I say, of the light before dawn, who as forerunner of the Sun announced to mortals the day now at hand; crying out and saying to those sleeping in darkness and the shadow of death: Repent; for the kingdom of heaven is at hand (Matth. III, 2). As if he said: The night has passed, but the day has drawn near: cast off the works of darkness (Rom. XIII, 12). Awake, you who sleep, and rise from the dead, and Christ shall enlighten you (Ephes. V, 14).

3. Non est autem negligenter praetereundum, sed etiam atque etiam considerandum, quam sublimis meriti, quam excellentis gratiae et virtutis, oculus ille quem nil fallit, eum perviderit, qui post tanta praecedentium praeconia, hanc de illo sententiam tulit: quod inter natos mulierum, nullus illo major surrexit; nec nisi de ordine Angelorum, vel qui pervenisset ad aequalitatem Angelorum, aliquem ei praeferendum judicavit. Nec semel quidem, aut perfunctorie vel breviter laudem ejus attigit, sed quotiescunque se offerebat occasio sermonis, laudibus ejus delectabatur immorari: quod satis probat historia Evangelii. Propterea Marcus quoque, Lucas et Joannes initia librorum praeconiis ejus dedicaverunt: ut videlicet auctoritas tanti nominis prima fronte praelati, totum deinceps commendabilius redderet Evangelium; et lucerna in ipso ingressu ac vestibulo ardens et lucens perduceret ad lucem, quae lucebat in tenebris, sed a tenebris nequibat comprehendi. Matthaeus enim, quia eum nativitas Domini in initiis occupatum tenuit, mox ut puerum nutriendum Nazareth commendavit, ad Praecursorem et Baptistam ejus stylum convertit, arbitrans imperfectum fore quidquid de Sponso diceret, si de individuo amico Sponsi reticeret. Haec namque cura non minima fuit, non solum Evangelistis narrantibus, sed etiam Prophetis et Angelis praenuntiantibus, ut lucerna Christi, testis Domini talis induceretur, cujus splendore et auctoritate inimici facile confunderentur, et ex cujus magnitudine incomparabilis Altissimi magnitudo manifeste monstraretur. Cum enim maximus inter natos mulierum Natum Virginis testabatur tam longe magnitudinem suam vincere, ut indignum se confiteretur ejus calceamenta portare: quid aliud dabatur intelligi, nisi quia ille erat magnus Dominus, cujus magnitudinis non est finis (Psal. CXLIV, 3); et de quo dicitur: Quoniam quis in nubibus aequabitur Domino, similis erit Deo in filiis Dei? (Psal. LXXXVIII, 7.) Et haec utique illa magnitudo Joannis fuit, qua inter magnos tam magnus excrevit, quia videlicet virtutes suas magnas et innumeras, quibus nulli mortalium secundus fuit, maxima omnium virtutum humilitate cumulavit sibi; cum putaretur summus omnium, ultro ac devotissime praeferens humillimum omnium et in tantum praeferens, ut calceamentis ejus detrahendis se testaretur indignum (Luc. III, 16).

English translation

3. But this must not be passed over carelessly; rather it must be weighed again and again, how sublime the merit, how excellent the grace and virtue, which that eye that nothing deceives beheld in him of whom, after such great praises of those who went before, he pronounced this sentence: that among those born of women none greater than he has arisen; nor did he judge anyone fit to be set above him, except one from the order of Angels, or one who had attained equality with the Angels. Nor did he touch on his praise only once, or in passing, or briefly; but as often as an occasion for speaking presented itself, he delighted to linger over his praises — as the history of the Gospel sufficiently proves. For this reason Mark also, and Luke, and John dedicated the openings of their books to his praises: so that the authority of so great a name, set forth at the very front, might thereafter render the whole Gospel more commendable; and the lamp, burning and shining right at the entrance and in the vestibule, might lead on to the light which was shining in the darkness, but which the darkness could not grasp. For Matthew, since the nativity of the Lord kept him occupied at the outset, as soon as he had committed the child to Nazareth to be raised, turned his pen to the Forerunner and Baptist of the Lord, judging that whatever he said about the Bridegroom would be incomplete if he kept silent about the Bridegroom's inseparable friend. For this concern was no small one, not only for the Evangelists in their narrating, but also for the Prophets and the Angels in their foretelling: that the lamp of Christ, such a witness of the Lord, should be set forth, by whose splendor and authority enemies might easily be confounded, and from whose greatness the incomparable greatness of the Most High might be plainly displayed. For when the greatest among those born of women testified that the Born of the Virgin so far surpassed his own greatness that he confessed himself unworthy to carry his sandals, what else was one given to understand, except that he was that great Lord, of whose greatness there is no end (Psal. CXLIV, 3); and of whom it is said: For who among the clouds shall be equal to the Lord? who shall be like to God among the sons of God? (Psal. LXXXVIII, 7.). And this indeed was that greatness of John, by which he grew so great among the great: namely, that he crowned his great and countless virtues — in which he was second to no mortal — with the greatest of all virtues, humility; so that, though he was thought the highest of all, he freely and most devoutly set before himself the lowliest of all, and preferred him so greatly that he testified himself take off his sandals (Luc. III, 16).

4. Mirentur igitur alii, quod a Prophetis praenuntiatus; quod ab archangelo promissus, et eodem quo Christus; cum tamen Christus in cubiculo, Joannes in oraculo; quod de tam sanctis ac nobilibus parentibus; quod de senibus et sterilibus, contra debitum naturae, dono gratiae; quod ante sanctus quam natus; ante propheta, quam prophetaret; quod plus quam propheta, quia angelus angelicum officium vitamque gerens in terris, in carne praeter carnem vivens; et licet innocentissimus, poenitentiae tamen formam plus exemplo quam verbo tradens; quod in spiritu et virtute Eliae praecurrens adventum Redemptoris, et viam ei parans in eremo; quod corda patrum ad filios, et filiorum ad patres convertit; quod Filium baptizare, Patrem audire, Spiritum sanctum videre meruit; quod denique usque ad mortem pro veritate certavit: ut etiam ad inferos Christum praecurreret; ante passionem Christi, martyr Christi fuit. Mirentur, inquam, haec alii, si tamen est qui digne possit mirari: nobis, fratres, non solum miranda, sed et imitanda proponitur virtus humilitatis ejus, qua major se credi, cum potuit, noluit; imo credentes ne errarent, quantum valuit compescuit. Fidelis enim amicus Sponsi, magis amator Domini sui, quam sui; se minui, ut ille cresceret optabat, et de minoratione sui gloriam illius augere satagebat (Joan. III, 29, 30), ante Apostolum opere et veritate dicens illud apostolicum: Non nosmetipsos praedicamus, sed Dominum Jesum Christum (II Cor. IV, 5). Ideo nimirum magna est gloria ejus in salutari tuo, in Jesu tuo, Domine, cujus justitiae et bonitatis est diligentes se diligere, est glorificantes se glorificare. Magna prorsus gloria ejus in Jesu, magna a Jesu: qui et apud se glorificavit eum consortio gloriae suae, et apud homines testimonio vocis suae. Persuasum sibi habebat Joannes illud fidele Sapientis consilium: Qui gloriatur, in Domino glorietur: non enim qui se ipsum commendat, ille probatus est, sed quem Deus commendat (II Cor. X, 17, 18). Maluit itaque in Domino sane, quam in seipso vane gloriari; quia et maluit veraciter a Domino, quam fallaciter a seipso commendari. Ideo probatus est Deo et hominibus; et gloria ejus veritas apud homines, felicitas apud Deum: qui si seipsum glorificasset, gloria sua nihil esset.

English translation

4. Let others, then, marvel that he was foretold by the Prophets; that he was promised by an archangel, and by the same archangel as Christ, though Christ was announced in a chamber, John in the sanctuary; that he was born of such holy and noble parents; that he was born of parents old and barren, against the due course of nature, by the gift of grace; that he was holy before he was born; a prophet before he prophesied; that he was more than a prophet, because he was an angel, carrying on an angelic office and life on earth, living in the flesh beyond the flesh; and although most innocent, yet delivering the pattern of penance more by example than by word; that, going before the coming of the Redeemer in the spirit and power of Elijah, he prepared his way in the wilderness; that he turned the hearts of the fathers to the children, and of the children to the fathers; that he merited to baptize the Son, to hear the Father, to see the Holy Spirit; and finally, that he strove even unto death for the truth, so that he went before Christ even into the underworld — a martyr of Christ before the passion of Christ. Let others, I say, marvel at these things, if indeed there is anyone who can worthily marvel at them: to us, brothers, there is set forth not only to be marveled at, but also to be imitated, the virtue of his humility, by which — though he could have let himself be believed greater — he was unwilling; indeed, he restrained, as far as he was able, those who so believed, lest they err. For as the faithful friend of the Bridegroom, loving his Lord more than himself, he desired to be made small that the Bridegroom might grow great, and he strove, by his own diminishment, to increase the Bridegroom's glory (Joan. III, 29, 30), saying by deed and in truth, before the Apostle, that apostolic word: We preach not ourselves, but the Lord Jesus Christ (II Cor. IV, 5). Therefore, no wonder, great is his glory in your salvation, in your Jesus, O Lord, whose justice and goodness it is to love those who love him, to glorify those who glorify him. Great indeed is his glory in Jesus, great from Jesus: who glorified him both with himself, by the fellowship of his own glory, and before men, by the testimony of his own voice. John had taken to heart that faithful counsel of the Wise One: He who glories, let him glory in the Lord; for it is not he who commends himself who is approved, but he whom God commends (II Cor. X, 17, 18). He therefore preferred to glory soundly in the Lord, rather than vainly in himself; for he preferred also to be commended truly by the Lord, rather than falsely by himself. Therefore he was approved by God and by men; and his glory is truth before men, happiness before God — for if he had glorified himself, his glory would have been nothing.

5. O filii hominum, usquequo gravi corde? utquid diligitis vanitatem, et quaeritis mendacium? (Psal. IV, 3.) Utquid vanam et mendacem gloriam diligitis, et gloriam quae a Deo solo est, non vultis? Sed quomodo quaeritis? Utinam agendo fortia, non loquendo sublimia! utinam saltem vera loquendo, licet vane, et non mentiendo plane! utinam denique mentiendo tantum de vobis, non etiam detrahendo de aliis! Hoc jam non est gloriam accipere vel quaerere, sed magis furari et rapere. Quaerere siquidem gloriam, non sua, sed contraria via; non illa scilicet cui debetur gloria, sed contumelia, id est, non virtutis titulo, sed mendacii et detractionis latrocinio: quid aliud est hoc, nisi rapere, vel furari nequiter, quod concupieris turpiter? Certe si gloriam ab homine non quaereres, sed oblatam acciperes, jam ex hoc secundum veritatem non ambulares. Nunc autem quid, cum non solum accipis si offertur, sed et quaeris cum non offertur? quid denique, cum alium velut trucidas venenosa et insidiosa lingua, ut gloriam ejus tibi rapias; ut de malo ejus tu melior, de contemptu ejus tu gloriosior appareas? Sit ergo illud, gloriam scilicet accipere vel quaerere ab homine, insipientium vanitas: quid istud, nisi saevientium crudelitas? Sit certe illud, ut mitius loquar, tentatio humana: quid istud, nisi imitatio diabolica? Plane imitantur diabolum qui sunt ex parte illius, id est, omnes superbi. Ipse est enim rex super omnes filios superbiae.

English translation

5. O sons of men, how long will you be dull of heart? Why do you love vanity and seek after a lie? (Psal. IV, 3.) Why do you love vain and lying glory, and refuse the glory that comes from God alone? But how do you seek it? Would that it were by doing brave deeds, not by speaking lofty words! Would that it were at least by speaking the truth, even if vainly, and not by plainly lying! Would that, finally, it were only by lying about yourselves, and not also by detracting from others! This is no longer to receive or seek glory, but rather to steal and to plunder. For to seek glory by a way not its own but contrary to it — that is, not by the way to which glory is properly due, but by disgrace; not, that is, by the title of virtue, but by the robbery of lying and detraction — what else is this but wickedly to seize or steal what you have shamefully coveted? Certainly, if you did not seek glory from man but accepted it when offered, even in this you would not be walking according to truth. But what of it, when you not only accept it if offered, but seek it when it is not offered? What, finally, when you all but murder another with a venomous and treacherous tongue, so as to snatch his glory for yourself — so that from his disgrace you may appear better, from his contempt more glorious? Let it be, then, that to receive or seek glory from man is the vanity of fools: but what is this other thing, if not the cruelty of the savage? Let the former, to speak more gently, be a human temptation: but what is this latter, if not an imitation of the devil? Plainly those who belong to his party — that is, all the proud — imitate the devil. For he is king over all the children of pride.

6. Quomodo, inquis, imitantur? Diabolus quia superbivit, meliori se, id est, Deo invidit: quia invidit, detraxit. Sic filii superbiae ejus, mox ut infecti sunt vitio ejus, id est amore propriae excellentiae, invident alienae. Ut coeperint invidere, incipient, si possunt, detrahere, ut scilicet accrescat eis quod derogaverint aliis. Quanto salubrius imitarentur humilitatem Joannis, qui detrahebat sibi, ut adderet alii: qui contendebat minor credi quam credebatur, ut alius inciperet apparere quod non putabatur? Denique si non placet humilitas ex sui honestate et aequitate, placeat saltem ex utilitate: quia nulla via rectior, aut facilior ad gloriam divinam; sed nec ad gratiam humanam ulla pulchrior aut justior, et saepe nulla compendiosior: Quanto, inquit, magnus es, humilia te in omnibus, et invenies gratiam coram Deo et hominibus (Eccle. III, 20, et Philipp. II, 3). Haec beatum Joannem glorificavit apud Deum et homines, sicut est dies haec, quae de nativitate ejus gloriosa consolationem praestat mundo, gaudium coelo, gloriam Deo: cui honor et gloria in saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

6. How, you ask, do they imitate him? The devil, because he was proud, envied one better than himself, that is, God; and because he envied, he detracted. So the children of his pride, as soon as they are infected with his vice — that is, the love of their own excellence — envy another's excellence. Once they have begun to envy, they will begin, if they can, to detract, so that what they have taken away from others may grow to their own credit. How much more wholesomely would they imitate the humility of John, who detracted from himself in order to add to another; who strove to be thought less than he was thought to be, so that another might begin to appear what he was not thought to be! Finally, if humility does not please by reason of its own honesty and fairness, let it at least please by reason of its usefulness: for there is no path straighter or easier to divine glory; nor is there any way more beautiful or more just — and often none shorter — to human favor either. As it is said: However great you are, humble yourself in all things, and you will find favor before God and men (Eccle. III, 20, et Philipp. II, 3). This is what glorified blessed John before God and men, as this day does, which by his glorious nativity grants consolation to the world, joy to heaven, and glory to God — to whom be honor and glory for ages of ages. Amen.
