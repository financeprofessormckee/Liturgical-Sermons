Introduction & Scripture references

Editorial introduction

For Mary's birthday Guerric takes the words "Like the vine I brought forth the sweetness of fragrance." As the vine's flower gives its scent without losing its bloom, the Virgin brought forth the Savior without losing her virginity. Christ is the sweetness that draws, the honorableness that sanctifies and the honor that glorifies, and Guerric asks the brothers for a chastity that flowers in gaze, speech and dress. The sermon appears under the heading for the Nativity of Blessed Mary in the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157; the individual sermon is undated.

English translation

A comparison of the Virgin's childbearing with a vine that, uncorrupted, gives its fragrance.

¶cols. 199-200; cols. 201-202
Latin original

1. Ego quasi vitis fructificavi suavitatem odoris (Eccli. XXIV, 23). Natalem beatissimae Virginis matris celebramus, de qua Vita omnium accepit natalem. Nata est hodie Virgo, de qua Salus omnium voluit nasci, ut natis ad mortem, daret ad vitam posse renasci. Nata est hodie Mater nova, quae primae matris maledictionem dissolvit; ut per istam benedictionem haereditate possideant, qui per illam sub praejudicio maledicti aeterni fuerant nati. Prorsus nova mater, quae novitatem attulit filiis inveteratis, vitiumque sanavit tam ingenitae, quam superadditae vetustatis. Prorsus nova mater, quae tam novo miraculo parit, ut pariat, et virgo sit; ipsumque pariat, qui omnia matremque ipsam inter omnia creavit. Mirabilis quidem novitas fecundae virginitatis, sed longe mirabilior novitas editae prolis. Nulli enim incredibile jam erit Virginem permansisse quae peperit, qui Deum agnoverit esse qui natus fuit. Nullatenus siquidem cum injuria maternae nasceretur integritatis, qui etiam corrupta redintegrare consuevit: nec veritas assumpti corporis potentiae praejudicavit Creatoris, quominus servaret sibi, quod pluribus dedit creaturis. Multas nempe creaturas invenies nasci sine corruptione gignentium, et quadam voce sua Creatori super inviolabili partu suo perhibere testimonium.

English translation

1. "I, like the vine, have brought forth the sweetness of fragrance" (Eccli. XXIV, 23). We celebrate the birthday of the most blessed Virgin Mother, from whom the Life of all received his birth. Today was born the Virgin from whom the Salvation of all willed to be born, so that to those born unto death he might give the power to be reborn unto life. Today was born the new Mother who dissolved the curse of the first mother, so that through this blessing those might possess as an inheritance who, through that first mother, had been born under the judgment of the eternal curse. Truly a new mother, who brought newness to aged children, and healed the fault both of inherited and of added old age. Truly a new mother, who gives birth by so new a miracle that she both gives birth and remains a virgin; and brings forth him who created all things, and the mother herself among all things. Marvelous indeed is the novelty of fruitful virginity, but far more marvelous is the novelty of the offspring brought forth. For to no one will it now be incredible that she who bore remained a virgin, once he has acknowledged that he who was born is God. For in no way would he be born to the injury of his mother's integrity, he who was accustomed even to restore wholeness to corrupted things; nor did the truth of the body he assumed prejudice the power of the Creator, so that he could not keep for himself what he gave to many creatures. For indeed you will find many creatures born without the corruption of those who bear them, and bearing witness, as it were, by their own voice to the Creator concerning their inviolate birth.

2. Sed loquatur Mater ipsa, quae sui conscia est mysterii, et nos edoceat quomodo vel quid ipsa genuerit. Loquatur autem non novae assertionis argumento, sed antiquo prophetiae oraculo; quia, ut ait Petrus apostolus, etiam miraculis firmior testis est propheticus sermo (II Petr. I, 19). Quid enim minus patet calumniae, aut suspicionem admittit falsitatis, quam testimonium divinitus de nondum natis prolatum? Longe igitur antequam nasceretur Maria, vocem ipsius assumebat Spiritus, qui in ea futurus erat, et tam divinitatem Filii, quam integritatem Matris, opus videlicet suum, contra blasphemias impiorum defendebat, et si persona ipsius, in modo communem intellectum sequimur, quod nunc audistis dicebat: Ego quasi vitis fructificavi suavitatem odoris (Eccli. XXIV, 23). Quod enim personae Sapientiae, id est ipsius Filii, tribuit haec verba contextus lectionis, sicut ipsi scitis, qui regulas nostis Scripturarum, nihil huic praejudicat intellectui, quominus, sicut alia multa, possint aptari personae quoque Virginis matris. Sed neque illud ignoratis quod satis superque suppetant alia testimonia, familiarius ac manifestius huic negotio servientia: sed non erit fraudanda exspectatio vestra de his quae lectio proposuit hodierna.

English translation

2. But let the Mother herself speak, who is conscious of her own mystery, and let her teach us how, or what, she herself brought forth. Let her speak, however, not by the argument of some new assertion, but by the ancient oracle of prophecy; for, as the apostle Peter says, even more firm than miracles is the prophetic word as witness (II Petr. I, 19). For what is less open to calumny, or admits less suspicion of falsehood, than a testimony uttered by God concerning those not yet born? Long, then, before Mary was born, the Spirit who was to be in her took up her voice, and defended both the divinity of the Son and the integrity of the Mother—his own work—against the blasphemies of the impious; and, speaking in her person, if we follow the common understanding of the manner, he was saying what you have just heard: "I, like the vine, have brought forth the sweetness of fragrance" (Eccli. XXIV, 23). For the fact that the context of the reading attributes these words to the person of Wisdom, that is, to the Son himself, as you yourselves know, who know the rules of Scripture, in no way prejudices this understanding, so that, like many other things, they may also be fitted to the person of the Virgin Mother. But you are also not unaware that there are more than enough other testimonies, more closely and manifestly serving this matter; yet your expectation shall not be cheated as regards what today's reading has set before us.

3. Respondeat ergo Maria blasphemis tam pro se, quam pro Filio; cunctasque haereses solo interimat verbo, et dicat: Ego quasi vitis fructificavi suavitatem odoris. Ac si aperte dicat: Partus quidem meus non habet exemplum in sexu mulierum, sed habet similitudinem in naturis rerum. Quaeris quomodo virginitas genuit Salvatorem? Sicut flos vitis odorem. Si corruptum inveneris florem, pro eo quod dedit odorem, violatum crede pudorem, quia edidit Salvatorem. Quid potes calumniari in proprietate similitudinis? Quid enim aliud est virginitas, quam flos inviolati corporis? Quid aliud est filius virginitatis, quam suavitas odoris? Tu tamen cave ne bono moriaris odore. Bonus enim aliis odor vitae in vitam, his qui scilicet salvi fiunt: aliis odor mortis in mortem, his scilicet qui pereunt, nimirum tanquam odor vineae florentis animalibus venenatis. Sane suavitate hujus odoris refocillabatur spiritus patriarchae senis, qui filium tangens, et fragrantiam Christi sentiens, de memoria abundantiae suavitatis illius eructabat, dicens: Ecce odor filii mei, sicut odor agri pleni, cui benedixit Dominus (Gen. XXVII, 27). Suavitatem hujus odoris odoratus est Deus Pater, et delectatus repropitiatus est humano generi, cum Filius offerret semetipsum oblationem et hostiam Deo in odorem suavitatis. Hoc suavi odore trahimur, cum per conversionem currimus ad ipsum: hoc trahuntur adolescentulae, cum per devotionem currunt post ipsum. Quanquam alius sit odor ille qui de fama praedicationis percipitur, alius odor iste qui de vestimentis vel unguentis, vel de ipso fortassis corpore ejus quodammodo crassior aspergitur. Qui utique non est aliud quam virtus quae de illo exit, quae pigros excitat, et fervorem amoris innovat, ut ad currendam viam mandatorum exsultare faciat.

English translation

3. Let Mary, then, answer the blasphemers both on her own behalf and on behalf of her Son, and let her destroy every heresy with a single word, saying: "I, like the vine, have brought forth the sweetness of fragrance." As if she were saying openly: My childbearing indeed has no example among the sex of women, but it has a likeness among the natures of things. You ask how virginity brought forth the Savior? As the flower of the vine brings forth fragrance. If you find the flower corrupted because it gave forth fragrance, then believe that modesty was violated because it brought forth the Savior. What fault can you find with the fitness of the comparison? For what else is virginity but the flower of an inviolate body? What else is the son of virginity but the sweetness of fragrance? Yet take care that you do not die by a good fragrance. For to some it is a good fragrance of life unto life—namely, to those who are saved; to others it is an odor of death unto death—namely, to those who perish—just as the fragrance of a flowering vineyard is to venomous animals. Indeed, by the sweetness of this fragrance was the spirit of the aged patriarch refreshed, who, touching his son and perceiving the fragrance of Christ, breathed out from the memory of that abundant sweetness, saying: "Behold, the smell of my son is as the smell of a plentiful field, which the Lord hath blessed" (Gen. XXVII, 27). The sweetness of this fragrance God the Father smelled, and, being delighted, became favorable again to the human race, when the Son offered himself as an offering and a sacrifice to God in an odor of sweetness. By this sweet fragrance we are drawn, when through conversion we run toward him; by this the young maidens are drawn, when through devotion they run after him. Although the fragrance that is perceived from the report of his preaching is one thing, and that fragrance, in a way more tangible, which is shed from his garments or ointments, or perhaps from his very body, is another. Yet this is nothing other than the power that goes forth from him, which rouses the slothful and renews the fervor of love, so that it makes them exult in running the way of the commandments.

Latin original

4. Quia ergo tam odoriferum fructum edidit, glorietur Maria et dicat: Ego quasi vitis fructificavi suavitatem odoris. Pulchre quasi vitis: nam botrus Cypri dilectus suus illi (Cantic. I, 13), de quo non solum torcular passionis rubens mustum expressit pretiosi sanguinis, quo calix praeclarus inebriat; sed et quotidie devotio sancta sibi vinum exprimit, quod laetificat cor hominis, et inebriat voluptate gaudii et amoris. Verum non prius inebriat gustus saporis, quam trahat suavitas odoris; nec laetificat gaudium visionis, nisi prius alliciat pietas opinionis: quia nisi crediderimus non intelligemus, nec gustabimus quoniam suavis est Dominus (Psal. XXXIII, 9). Fides siquidem est quae odoratur, experientia quae gustat et fruitur. Ideo fortassis Maria Jesum suum describens per virtutes ipsius et efficientias, primo suavitatem odoris eum nominat; quia videlicet hoc initio Jesus in nobis habet subsistere, si redolentia sanctae opinionis suae ad se nos trahat. Quis sit autem fructus unde iste odor prodit, et quo nos trahat, aperit cum subdit: Et flores mei fructus honoris et honestatis (Eccli. XXIV, 23). Prorsus iste est Jesus suavitas odoris invitans, honestas sanctificans, honor glorificans. Suavitas odoris, qua velut ad viam adducimur: honestas, per quam deducimur: honor, ad quem perducimur. Pulchre autem honestas dicitur, quasi honoris status. Neque enim honor ille summae dignitatis et gloriae statum tunc in nobis habere posset, nisi nunc honestas vitae et morum sedem ei praepararet. Nullus tunc, sicut nunc, passim cernitur honoratus sine honestate, sicut nec ullus honestus sine honore. Est ergo Jesus primo suavitas odoris his quos vocat: deinde, honestas his quos justificat: demum, honor illis quos magnificat. Nam quos praedestinavit, hos et vocavit: et quos vocavit, hos et justificavit: quos autem justificavit, illos et magnificavit (Rom. VIII, 30).

English translation

4. Since, then, she brought forth so fragrant a fruit, let Mary glory and say: I, like a vine, have borne the sweetness of fragrance. Beautifully does she say "like a vine": for her Beloved is to her a cluster of henna (Cantic. I, 13), from which not only did the winepress of the passion squeeze forth the reddening must of precious blood, by which the splendid cup inebriates; but also daily holy devotion presses out for itself a wine which gladdens the heart of man, and inebriates with the delight of joy and love. Yet the taste of flavor does not inebriate before the sweetness of fragrance draws us; nor does the joy of vision gladden unless the devotion of belief first entices us: for unless we have believed we shall not understand, nor shall we taste that the Lord is sweet (Psal. XXXIII, 9). Faith indeed is what smells the fragrance; experience is what tastes and enjoys it. Perhaps for this reason Mary, in describing her Jesus by his virtues and effects, first names him the sweetness of fragrance; because indeed this is the beginning by which Jesus must take root in us, if the fragrance of his holy reputation draws us to himself. But what the fruit is from which this fragrance proceeds, and whither it draws us, she reveals when she adds: And my flowers are the fruit of honor and honorableness (Eccli. XXIV, 23). This indeed is Jesus: the sweetness of fragrance inviting, the honorableness sanctifying, the honor glorifying. The sweetness of fragrance, by which we are drawn, as it were, onto the way; the honorableness, through which we are led along; the honor, to which we are brought. And "honorableness" is beautifully so named, as though it were the settled state (status) of honor. For that honor of highest dignity and glory could not then have its state in us, unless now the honorableness of life and conduct prepares a seat for it. No one then is seen honored without honorableness, as many are everywhere now, just as no one will be honorable without honor. Jesus, therefore, is first the sweetness of fragrance to those whom he calls; then, honorableness to those whom he justifies; finally, honor to those whom he magnifies. For whom he predestined, these he also called; and whom he called, these he also justified; and whom he justified, these he also magnified (Rom. VIII, 30).

5. Talis est igitur Dilectus meus, ait Maria: et iste est filius meus, o filiae Jerusalem. Iste est benedictus fructus ventris mei, hunc fructificaverunt flores mei. Non ait. Flos; sed, flores: quia cum virgo sancta est, flos virginitatis in ea multiformis est. In Maria tamen gratia singulari prae omnibus multipliciter floruit, quae tota pulchra intus et foris, tota vernabat quadam florulentia et venustate pudoris. Nam et in te quoque si castimonia perfecta sit, non solum reflorebit caro tua, sed et super te totum efflorebit sanctificatio quaedam divina. Non erit aspectus petulans, aut vagus, sed pudicitia floridus: non sermo lascivus aut ineptus, sed verecundia gratus, aut sapientia conditus. Non prurient aures libidine audiendi nova vel turpia, nec palatum desiderio sumendi dulcia. Non erit incessus inordinatus, sed modestus: non ipse habitus non dicam meretricius, sed neque superstitiosus, imo religiosus; totusque hominis tui status sanctimoniae gratia florulentus, ut recte sponso dicere possis, cum eum ad secretum tuum invitaveris, Lectulus noster floridus (Cantic. I, 15). Quinimo ipse totus eris quidam flos pulcherrimus, qualibus sponsa languorem amoris sui fulciri ac recreari desiderat, cum ait: Fulcite me floribus, stipate me malis, quia amore langueo (Cantic. II, 5). Hujusmodi justus, etiam cum senuerit in terra radix ejus, et in pulvere emortuus fuerit truncus illius, ad odorem aquae vivae in resurrectione, id est in refloritione justorum, germinabit sicut lilium: et florebit in aeternum ante Dominum, florem de flore, natum virginem, virginis filium, Sponsum et coronam virginum; florem, inquam, quo coronanda est non solum integritas virginum, sed et castitas continentium: ipsi gloria per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

5. Such, then, is my Beloved, says Mary: and this is my son, O daughters of Jerusalem. This is the blessed fruit of my womb, which my flowers have borne as fruit. She does not say "flower," but "flowers": because, since she is a holy virgin, the flower of virginity in her is manifold. Yet in Mary, by singular grace above all others, it flowered in manifold ways, for she, wholly beautiful within and without, blossomed entirely with a certain flowering and loveliness of modesty. For in you too, if chastity be perfect, not only will your flesh reflower, but also upon you a certain divine sanctification will flower forth entirely. Your gaze will not be wanton or wandering, but blooming with modesty; your speech not lascivious or foolish, but gracious with reserve, or seasoned with wisdom. Your ears will not itch with lust to hear new or shameful things, nor your palate with desire to taste sweet things. Your walk will not be disorderly, but modest; your very dress will be not, I will not say meretricious, but not even superstitious, but rather religious; and the whole condition of your person will be flowering with the grace of holiness, so that you may rightly say to the Bridegroom, when you have invited him into your secret chamber: Our bed is flowery (Cantic. I, 15). Indeed, you yourself will be wholly a most beautiful flower, of the kind with which the Bride desires the languor of her love to be propped up and refreshed, when she says: Prop me up with flowers, surround me with apples, for I languish with love (Cantic. II, 5). Such a just one, even when his root has grown old in the earth, and his trunk has died away into dust, will sprout like a lily at the scent of living water in the resurrection, that is, in the reflowering of the just: and he will flourish forever before the Lord — a flower born of a flower, a virgin born, the son of a virgin, the Bridegroom and crown of virgins; a flower, I say, by which is to be crowned not only the integrity of virgins, but also the chastity of the continent: to him be glory through all ages of ages. Amen.
