Editorial introduction

Returning to the feast's reading from a year before, Guerric takes Mary's words "I am the mother of fair love." Christ has a bodily, a moral and a divine form, and Christians love him now in the beauty of his life and teaching. Mary labors to form her Son in all the adopted children, and fair love must rule over base, natural and worldly loves. The sermon appears under the heading for the Nativity of Blessed Mary in the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157; the individual sermon is undated.

De pulchritudine sanctae dilectionis.

English translation

On the beauty of holy love.

¶cols. 203-204
Latin original

1. Ego mater pulchrae dilectionis, et timoris, et agnitionis, et sanctae spei (Eccli. XXIV, 24). Meminisse potestis cum praeterito anno loqueremur de principio lectionis hodiernae, non inconvenienter, ut arbitror, beatae Dei Genitrici nos illud assignasse, salvo tamen intellectu illo, quo tota lectio Filio ejusdem proprie competit, id est, Dei sapientiae. Multo autem manifestius in eo quod modo de eadem lectione proposuimus, ejusdem Virginis se demonstrat vox et persona, si tamen advertamus, in eo quod ait, Ego mater pulchrae dilectionis, etc., quam pulchre et commode Filium his virtutum nominibus describat. Notus erat Matri Filius, nec minus utique quam illi qui dicebat: Etsi cognovimus Christum secundum carnem, sed nunc jam non novimus (II Cor. V, 16). Primo novit eum secundum formam carnis, in qua ipsa eum genuit: sed longe hoc a cognitione illius formae, in qua Pater eum genuit. In illa visus est aliquando, et non erat ei species neque decor (Isa. LIII, 2): in ista splendor est gloriae et candor lucis aeternae (Sap. VII, 26), apud quem non est transmutatio, nec vicissitudinis obumbratio (Jac. I, 17). Visio illius formae incredulis auxit peccatum; visio istius justis servatur in praemium. Igitur inter formam carnis et formam Verbi, quasi medius de ista ad illam gradus est, quaedam alia forma Christi, spiritualis quidem, sed quam in carne palam exhibuit; forma scilicet vitae quam in corpore gessit ad informationem eorum qui erant credituri. Si enim secundum exemplar vitae et morum, quod in eo monstratum est, formatus fuerit in nobis Christus; tunc demum idonei erimus videre non formam solum, quae formata est propter nos, sed etiam illam quae formavit nos.

English translation

1. "I am the mother of fair love, and of fear, and of knowledge, and of holy hope" (Eccli. XXIV, 24). You may remember that last year, when we were speaking about the beginning of today's reading, we assigned it, not unfittingly I think, to the Blessed Mother of God—while still preserving that understanding by which the whole reading properly belongs to her Son, that is, to the Wisdom of God. But much more clearly, in what we have now proposed from this same reading, the voice and person of this same Virgin shows itself—if indeed we notice how beautifully and fittingly, in saying, "I am the mother of fair love," etc., she describes her Son by these names of virtues. The Son was known to the Mother, and indeed no less than to him who said: "Though we have known Christ according to the flesh, yet now we know him so no longer" (II Cor. V, 16). First she knew him according to the form of flesh, in which she herself bore him; but this is far removed from the knowledge of that form in which the Father begot him. In the former he was once seen, and "he had no beauty nor comeliness" (Isa. LIII, 2); in the latter he is "the brightness of glory and the whiteness of eternal light" (Sap. VII, 26), with whom there is "no change, nor shadow of alteration" (Jac. I, 17). The sight of that first form increased the sin of unbelievers; the sight of this second form is kept as a reward for the just. Therefore, between the form of flesh and the form of the Word, as a kind of middle step from the one to the other, there is a certain other form of Christ—spiritual indeed, but one which he openly displayed in the flesh: namely, the form of life which he led in the body for the instruction of those who were to believe. For if Christ shall have been formed in us according to the pattern of life and conduct which was shown in him, then at last we shall be fit to see not only the form which was formed for our sake, but also that form which formed us.

2. Est utique in Christo alia forma corporalis, alia moralis, alia intellectualis. In corporali frater est; in morali magister est; in intellectuali Deus noster est. Corporalem suscepit, ut impleret sacramentum: moralem exhibuit, ut praeberet exemplum: intellectualem seu divinam revelabit ad praemium. Quanquam etiam corporalem tunc cernere, in quam angeli concupiscunt prospicere, non minima sit portio gloriae. Beatus autem qui nunc amator factus est formae hujus, quae in exemplum est proposita: nam qui scrutator est illius quae in praemium est reposita, opprimetur a gloria (Prov. XXV, 27). Hujus formae mirator et amator factus erat, qui dicebat: Speciosus forma prae filiis hominum (Psal. XLIV, 3). Vis scire quia non formam corporis, sed cordis, non pulchritudinem membrorum, sed morum praedicabat? audi sequentia: Specie tua, inquit, et pulchritudine tua, intende, prospere procede, et regna. Adhuc fortassis dubium est, nisi addat: Propter veritatem, inquit, et mansuetudinem et justitiam (Psal. XLIV, 5). Prorsus haec est species tua et pulchritudo tua, qua regnum acquisivisti, pulcherrime regum; veritas utique sermonum, mansuetudo morum, justitia judiciorum. Hac pulchritudine facile illexisti et subjecisti tibi etiam corda inimicorum, quippe totus concupiscentia et desiderium. Mirabilis triumphus gratiae, omnino novum ac pulcherrimum genus victoriae, hostem non perdere ad mortem fortitudine, sed convertere ad amorem pulchritudine. Ecce mundus totus post eum abiit concupiscens speciem decoris ejus, non quia faciem ejus viderit, sed quia tam multa amabilia de mansuetudine, veritate et justitia ejus audivit. Ex Sion species decoris ejus, quia de Sion exivit lex, et verbum Domini de Jerusalem (Mich. IV, 2): unde scilicet Evangelium ad nos missum est, in quo quaedam pulchrior Christi facies ostensa est; forma scilicet vitae et doctrinae, quam et tradidit verbo, et in se expressit exemplo.

English translation

2. There is indeed in Christ one form that is bodily, another moral, another intellectual. In the bodily form he is our brother; in the moral form he is our teacher; in the intellectual form he is our God. He took on the bodily form in order to fulfill the sacrament; he displayed the moral form in order to provide an example; he will reveal the intellectual or divine form as a reward. Although even to behold the bodily form then, into which the angels desire to look, is no small portion of glory. But blessed is he who has now become a lover of this form which has been set forth as an example; for he who is a searcher into that form which is reserved as a reward "shall be overwhelmed by its glory" (Prov. XXV, 27). A marveler and lover of this form was he who said: "Thou art beautiful in form above the sons of men" (Psal. XLIV, 3). Do you wish to know that he was praising not the form of the body but of the heart, not the beauty of the limbs but of conduct? Listen to what follows: "With thy comeliness," he says, "and thy beauty, set out, proceed prosperously, and reign." Perhaps this is still doubtful, unless he adds: "Because of truth," he says, "and meekness, and justice" (Psal. XLIV, 5). This indeed is your comeliness and your beauty, by which you acquired your kingdom, O most beautiful of kings: truth of speech, meekness of conduct, justice of judgments. By this beauty you easily enticed and subjected to yourself even the hearts of your enemies, being wholly desire and longing. A wondrous triumph of grace, altogether a new and most beautiful kind of victory—not to destroy the enemy unto death by strength, but to convert him to love by beauty. Behold, the whole world has gone after him, desiring the beauty of his comeliness, not because it saw his face, but because it heard so many lovable things about his meekness, truth, and justice. "Out of Sion is the loveliness of his beauty", because "out of Sion went forth the law, and the word of the Lord out of Jerusalem" (Mich. IV, 2)—whence, namely, the Gospel was sent to us, in which a certain more beautiful face of Christ was shown: namely, the form of life and doctrine, which he both delivered in word and expressed in himself by example.

3. In hac forma nosse Christum, interim pietas est Christianorum; cum nosse in forma carnis, scandalum fuerit Judaeorum: nam nosse in forma divinitatis felicitas est, et gaudium angelorum. Ideo et Paulus sciens quia caro non prodest quidquam, sine spiritu videlicet qui vivificat, nosse jam Christum secundum carnem repudiat (II Cor. V, 16): nimirum ut spiritui vivificanti studiosius totus intendat. Hoc ipsum et Maria sapere videtur, quae dilectum uteri sui, dilectum votorum suorum insinuare cupiens affectibus omnium, describit eum non secundum carnem, sed secundum spiritum: quasi diceret et ipsa: Etsi cognovi Christum secundum carnem, sed nunc jam non novi. Cupit namque et ipsa formare Unigenitum suum in omnibus filiis adoptionis, qui etsi geniti sunt verbo veritatis, nihilominus tamen parturit eos quotidie desiderio et cura pietatis, donec occurrant in virum perfectum, in mensuram plenitudinis aetatis Filii sui, quem semel parturivit et peperit; imo, ut Isaias ait, Antequam parturiret peperit (Isai. LXVI, 7): quia peperit sine dolore, nec experta est difficultatem et molestiam parturitionis, cum pareret gaudii fructum aeterni.

English translation

3. To know Christ in this form is, for the time being, the piety of Christians; whereas to know him in the form of flesh was the stumbling-block of the Jews; for to know him in the form of divinity is the happiness and joy of angels. Therefore Paul also, knowing that the flesh profits nothing without the spirit which gives life, refuses now to know Christ according to the flesh (II Cor. V, 16)—surely so that he might more earnestly direct his whole attention to the life-giving spirit. This same wisdom Mary too seems to show, who, desiring to instill into the affections of all the beloved of her womb, the beloved of her prayers, describes him not according to the flesh but according to the spirit—as if she too were saying: "Though I have known Christ according to the flesh, yet now I know him so no longer." For she too desires to form her Only-begotten in all the sons of adoption, who, although they have been begotten by the word of truth, nevertheless she labors to bring them forth daily by the desire and care of piety, until they attain to a perfect man, to the measure of the fulness of the age of her Son, whom once she labored with and brought forth—or rather, as Isaiah says, "Before she was in labor, she brought forth" (Isai. LXVI, 7); because she brought forth without pain, nor did she experience the difficulty and distress of labor, since she was bringing forth the fruit of eternal joy.

4. Hunc igitur commendans nobis sic ait: Ego mater pulchrae dilectionis, et timoris, et agnitionis, et sanctae spei (Eccli. XXIV, 24). Ergone est iste Filius tuus, o Virgo virginum? ergone talis est dilectus tuus, o pulcherrima mulierum? Plane talis est dilectus meus, et ipse est Filius meus, o filiae Jerusalem. Dilectus meus est pulchra dilectio in se ipso: dilectus meus est pulchra dilectio, timor, et spes, et agnitio in illo qui natus est ex ipso. Ipse enim est non solum quem diligimus, timemus, agnoscimus, et in quem speramus; sed haec etiam omnia in nobis operatur, atque his virtutibus, veluti quibusdam membris et partibus, in nobis perficitur atque formatur. Tunc enim perfecte, pro modulo hujus vitae, Christus in te formatus est, tunc veritas ipsius in te expressa est, si veritatem quae ipse est agnovisti, et agnitam glorificasti, timore videlicet et spe; et ne spes confundat, charitas diffusa sit in corde. Si huic sensui judicas incongruum, quod alius sit istarum ordo virtutum vel perfectuum, et alius ordo positionis nominum: non enim ait: Ego mater agnitionis, et timoris, et spei, et dilectionis; sed ait: Ego mater pulchrae dilectionis, et timoris, et agnitionis, et sanctae spei: fortassis et in hoc ordine ipso quem ponit, inveniri poterit aliquid congruentiae et rationis. Sicut enim de agnitione timor nascitur, quem, ne in desperationem concidat, spes adjuncta consolatur, ipsique spei, ne confundat, dilectio suffragatur: ita versa vice dilectio castum parit timorem, timorque cum dilectione illuminat agnitionem, secundum illud Sapientis, Qui timetis Dominum, diligite illum, et illuminabuntur corda vestra (Eccli. II, 10). Quanto autem verius illuminato corde Deus agnoscitur, tanto fiducialius in eum speratur. Unde est illud: Sperent in te omnes qui noverunt nomen tuum, Domine (Psal. IX, 11). Spes vero sancta est, sicut Joannes de spe videndi Deum loquens ait: Omnis qui habet hanc spem in illum sanctificat se ipsum, sicut et ille sanctus est (I Joan. III, 3).

English translation

4. Commending him to us, therefore, she speaks thus: I am the mother of fair love, and of fear, and of knowledge, and of holy hope (Eccli. XXIV, 24). Is this, then, your Son, O Virgin of virgins? Is this, then, your beloved, O most beautiful of women? Truly such is my beloved, and he is my Son, O daughters of Jerusalem. My beloved is fair love in himself: my beloved is fair love, fear, and hope, and knowledge in him who was born of him. For he is not only the one whom we love, fear, acknowledge, and in whom we hope; but he also works all these things in us, and by these virtues, as by certain members and parts, is perfected and formed in us. For then, perfectly—so far as this life allows—is Christ formed in you, then is his truth expressed in you, if you have acknowledged the truth which he himself is, and having acknowledged it, have glorified it, namely with fear and hope; and lest hope confound, let charity be poured out in the heart. If you judge it inconsistent with this meaning that the order of these virtues or perfections should be one thing, and the order of the naming of them another—for she does not say: I am the mother of knowledge, and of fear, and of hope, and of love; but she says: I am the mother of fair love, and of fear, and of knowledge, and of holy hope—perhaps even in this very order which she sets forth, something of fittingness and reason may be found. For just as fear is born of knowledge, which, lest it fall into despair, is consoled by hope joined to it, and hope itself, lest it confound, is supported by love: so, conversely, love brings forth chaste fear, and fear together with love illumines knowledge, according to that word of the Wise Man: You who fear the Lord, love him, and your hearts shall be enlightened (Eccli. II, 10). But the more truly God is known by an enlightened heart, the more confidently is he hoped for. Hence that word: Let all who know your name, O Lord, hope in you (Psal. IX, 11). And holy is that hope, as John says, speaking of the hope of seeing God: Every one that has this hope in him sanctifies himself, even as he is holy (I Joan. III, 3).

5. Pulchre autem et proprie dicta est pulchra dilectio, cum charitas Deus sit, ac per hoc summa pulchritudo; sitque virtus ista fere tota pulchritudo Ecclesiae, quam ipse Sponsus ejus in Cantico amoris invenitur tantum ac toties in ea mirari et extollere. Prorsus dilectio pulchra, quae de corde puro est, et conscientia bona, et fide non ficta. Ubi enim cor purum est, nulla est ruga; ubi conscientia bona, nulla est macula: ubi fides non ficta, non est aliquid ejusmodi quod oculis Sponsi displiceat, quominus sibi Ecclesiam nunc gratiosam, tunc gloriosam exhibeat. Distinguenda etiam erat hoc nomine dilectio sanctorum ab amore hominum, sive turpi, qui nec nominandus est inter fideles; sive naturali, quo amantur parentes; sive saeculari, quo se invicem amant propter saeculi necessitates vel cupiditates. Procul ergo, procul; imo nusquam prorsus sub oculis Dei amor turpitudinis: seorsum autem interim cedat amor necessitudinis, solusque regnet amor internae et aeternae pulchritudinis, quo solum Deum, vel propter Deum amant vere pulchri. Tu vero, bone Jesu, pulchritudo Sanctorum, speciosus forma, non modo prae filiis hominum, sed et prae virtutibus coelorum; specie tua et pulchritudine tua, intende, prospere procede, et regna: tamque late regnet pulchra dilectio tua, ut turpem de finibus rerum perturbet et eliminet, saecularem ad se convertat et inclinet, naturalem sub se regat et ordinet; ut illa pulchra et vera dilectione te diligat mundus, qua dilexisti mundum, Salvator mundi, qui vivis et regnas Deus, per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

5. Beautifully and fittingly is it called fair love, since charity is God, and by this is the highest beauty; and this virtue is almost the whole beauty of the Church, which her very Bridegroom in the Song of love is found so greatly and so often to admire and extol in her. Truly it is fair love, which comes from a pure heart, and a good conscience, and unfeigned faith. For where the heart is pure, there is no wrinkle; where the conscience is good, there is no stain; where faith is unfeigned, there is nothing of the sort that could displease the eyes of the Bridegroom, so as to prevent him from presenting the Church to himself now as gracious, and then as glorious. It was also necessary that the love of the saints be distinguished by this name from the love of men—whether the base love, which must not even be named among the faithful; or the natural love, by which parents are loved; or the worldly love, by which people love one another on account of the necessities or desires of this world. Far, then, far away—nay, altogether absent from the eyes of God—be the love of baseness: let the love of natural ties withdraw aside for the time being, and let the love of inward and eternal beauty alone reign, by which the truly beautiful love God alone, or love others for God's sake. But you, good Jesus, beauty of the Saints, fair in form not only above the sons of men, but even above the virtues of the heavens; with your beauty and your comeliness, set out, proceed prosperously, and reign; and may your fair love reign so widely that it may disturb and banish base love from the bounds of all things, turn worldly love toward itself and incline it, and rule and order natural love beneath itself; so that the world may love you with that fair and true love with which you loved the world, O Savior of the world, who live and reign, God, through all ages of ages. Amen.
