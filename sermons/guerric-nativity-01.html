Editorial introduction

Guerric's first Nativity sermon contemplates the eternal Word as a vulnerable child. Four numbered paragraphs move from the renewal brought by Christ's birth to humility, confidence in mercy, and the gentleness that should shape Christian conduct. This reading uses the 1879 Patrologia Latina edition; the approximate 1138–1157 composition range is distinct from that publication date. The Nativity heading does not specify a Christmas Mass. Two editorial notes explain inaccurate Scripture locators printed in the edition without changing the sermon.

What God as a child teaches us.

¶cols. 29–30
Latin original

1. Puer natus est nobis (Isai. ix, 6). Puer antiquus dierum. Puer forma corporis et aetate; antiquus dierum, incomprehensibili Verbi aeternitate. Quanquam et in ipsa sua dierum antiquitate et si puer non sit, novus tamen semper sit; imo non tam novus sit, quam ipsa novitas in se semper manens et innovans omnia, a quo quaeque res prout recedit, inveteratur; prout reaccedit, renovatur. Et novo quodam modo unde antiquus, inde novus: quoniam quidem aeternitas ipsius sine initio nascendi, sine defectu veterascendi, ipsa est et antiqua novitas, et nova antiquitas ipsius. Alia tamen est novitas hujus temporalis nativitatis, quia puer natus est nobis innovandis, qui ab aeterno Deus natus est angelis beatificandis. Illa quidem nativitas amplioris gloriae, sed ista profusioris misericordiae. Ista siquidem propter me, qui misericordia indigebam, quoniam miseria circumdatus eram, et miseria quam expiare non poteram. Ostende nobis misericordiam tuam (Psal. lxxxiv, 8), qui nondum idonei sumus videre gloriam tuam: appareat benignitas et humanitas Salvatoris nostri Dei, ut per hanc digni efficiamur et idonei, quibus appareat majestas et divinitas Creatoris nostri Dei. Ostende nobis, Domine, misericordiam tuam, nostra indutam miseria, et novo miserendi genere de ipsa miseria miserorum operantem remedia. Ad hoc siquidem ars misericordiae beatitudinem Dei, et miseriam hominis in unitate Mediatoris temperavit; ut operante sacramento unitatis, per virtutem resurrectionis beatitudo miseriam absorbeat, mortemque vita deglutiat; et sic totus homo glorificatus in divinae consortium naturae transeat. Usque adeo autem universas infirmitates carnis, quibus ob culpam humana subjacet natura, absque culpa videlicet, dignatio suscepit divina; ut nec infantiae recusaret injurias, nec alia, quam communis habet conditio, sortiri vellet exordia: excepto sane, quod per Spiritum sanctum immaculatus nascens de immaculata, vitia nostrae originis emundabat, atque secundae nativitatis nobis sacramenta dedicabat.

English translation

1. A child is born to us (Isaiah ix, 6). A child, the Ancient of Days. A child in bodily form and age; the Ancient of Days in the incomprehensible eternity of the Word. Yet even in that ancientness of his days, although he is not a child, he is always new; indeed, he is not so much new as newness itself, always abiding in itself and making all things new. Every thing grows old as it withdraws from him, and is renewed as it draws near again. And in a certain new way, the very reason he is ancient is the reason he is new: for his eternity, without a beginning through birth or a decline through growing old, is itself both his ancient newness and his new ancientness. Yet the newness of this birth in time is different: he who from eternity was born as God to give the angels blessedness has been born as a child to make us new. That birth indeed has greater glory, but this one has more abundant mercy. This birth is for me, who needed mercy because I was surrounded by misery, and by misery which I could not expiate. Show us your mercy (Psalm lxxxiv, 8), for we are not yet fit to see your glory. Let the kindness and humanity of God our Saviour appear, so that through this we may become worthy and fit for the majesty and divinity of God our Creator to appear to us. Show us, Lord, your mercy, clothed in our misery and, by a new manner of showing mercy, working remedies from the very misery of the miserable. For this purpose the art of mercy has blended God's blessedness and man's misery in the unity of the Mediator, so that, as the sacrament of unity works through the power of the resurrection, blessedness may swallow up misery and life devour death, and thus the whole man, glorified, may pass into fellowship with the divine nature. Indeed, the divine condescension took upon itself, without sin, all the infirmities of the flesh to which human nature is subject because of sin, to such an extent that it did not refuse the hardships of infancy, nor did it wish to have a beginning different from that of our common condition; except, of course, that, born immaculate of the immaculate Virgin through the Holy Spirit, he cleansed the faults of our origin and consecrated for us the sacraments of a second birth.

Latin original

2. Itaque parvulus natus est nobis (Isa. ix, 6), et Deus majestatis exinaniens semetipsum, non solum terreno mortalium corpori, sed etiam infirmae ac pusillae infantium se configuravit aetati. O beata infantia, cujus infirmum et stultum fortius et sapientius est omnibus hominibus; quia nimirum Dei virtus, et Dei sapientia sua in nostris, divina operatur in humanis! Hujus siquidem infantis infirmitas principem mundi triumphat, fortem armatum ligat, crudelem tyrannum captivat, et captivitatem nostram absolvit et liberat. Hujus infantis muta, ut videtur, et elinguis simplicitas linguas infantium facit disertas; linguis hominum loqui facit et angelorum, linguas dividens igneas: inscius iste, ut videtur, ipse est qui docet hominem scientiam et angelum, tanquam vere Deus scientiarum, Sapientia Dei, et Verbum. O sacra et dulcis infantia, quae veram humanis innocentiam refudisti, per quam omnis aetas in beatam redeat infantiam; tibique conformis fiat, non pusillitate membrorum, sed humilitate sensus, et pietate morum. Prorsus vos filii Adae, qui nimis grandes estis in oculis vestris, et in giganteam enormitatem per superbiam excrevistis; nisi conversi fueritis, et effecti sicut parvulus iste, non intrabitis in regnum coelorum (Matth. xviii, 4)1. Ego sum ostium (Joan. x, 9) regni, ait parvulus iste: nisi incurvetur altitudo virorum, non eos admittet humilitatis janua. Ideo nimirum conquassabit capita in terra multorum (Psal. cxi, 6)2; et qui elati accedunt, repulsi capite illiso cadent retrorsum. Quid enim? adhuc tu superbis, terra et cinis, postquam Deus factus est humilis? adhuc magnus es in oculis tuis, postquam Deus parvulus factus est sub oculis tuis? Ille semetipsum exinanivit ut pene videretur esse nihil, sine quo factum est nihil: et tu in immensum inflaris et extolleris, existimans te aliquid esse, cum vere sis nihil! ipse te seducis (Galat. vi, 3), clamat tibi Apostolus; cum, etsi aliquid esses, si magnum aliquid esses, tanto magis humiliari te oporteret. Quanto, inquit Sapiens, magnus es, humilia te in omnibus: et invenies gratiam coram Deo (Eccli. iii, 20), qui superbis resistit, humilibus autem dat gratiam (Jacobi iv, 6 et I Petr. v, 5); qui et ipse, ut tibi fieret exemplum, cum esset maximus omnium, factus est humillimus omnium et minimus. Parum erat ei, ut fieret minor angelis conditione mortalis naturae, nisi et hominibus minor fieret aetate imbecillis infantiae. Videat igitur pius et humilis, et glorietur, videat impius et superbus, et confundatur. Videat, inquam, Deum immensum, parvulum factum, et infantulum adorandum, stupendum mysterium, redemptionem piorum, gloriam humilium, judicium impiorum, ruinam superborum. O venerandum ac tremendum mysterium! Quam sanctum et terribile est nomen tuum, fons misericordiarum, et abyssus judiciorum! Quis de hoc fonte bibit, et non amavit? quis hanc abyssum consideravit, et non expavit? Qui non amavit, nequam et impius est: sicut qui non expavit, vecors et insensatus est. Nihil tamen est cur judicium expavescas, si non adversus misericordiam insolescas. Et ipse, siquidem mallet amari, quam serviliter timeri: magisque probat quod liber ac voluntarius offert affectus filiorum, quam quod coactitius extorquet metus servulorum. Ideo et nunc cum primo se manifestavit mortalibus, puerum se maluit exhibere, magisque amabilis quam terribilis apparere: ut, quia salvare veniebat, et non judicare; praeferret interim unde provocaret amorem, differret unde incutere posset timorem.

English translation

2. Thus a little child is born to us (Isaiah ix, 6), and the God of majesty, emptying himself, conformed himself not only to the earthly body of mortals, but also to the weak and tiny age of infants. O blessed infancy, whose weakness and foolishness are stronger and wiser than all men, because God's power and God's wisdom work what is their own in what is ours, divine things in human things! For this infant's weakness triumphs over the prince of the world, binds the strong armed man, takes the cruel tyrant captive, and releases and frees our captivity. This infant's simplicity, seemingly mute and without speech, makes the tongues of infants eloquent; dividing fiery tongues, it makes them speak with the tongues of men and angels. This seemingly unknowing child is himself the one who teaches knowledge to man and angel, as truly the God of knowledge, the Wisdom of God, and the Word. O holy and sweet infancy, which has poured true innocence back into mankind, through which every age may return to blessed infancy and be conformed to you, not in smallness of limbs, but in humility of mind and holiness of conduct. Truly, you children of Adam, who are too great in your own eyes and have grown through pride to a monstrous gigantic size: unless you are converted and become like this little child, you will not enter the kingdom of heaven (Matthew xviii, 4)1. I am the door (John x, 9) of the kingdom, says this little child: unless the tall stature of men bends low, the gate of humility will not admit them. For this reason he will crush the heads of many upon the earth (Psalm cxi, 6)2; and those who approach with heads raised, striking them when repulsed, will fall backwards. What then? Do you still boast, dust and ashes, after God has become humble? Are you still great in your own eyes, after God has become little before your eyes? He emptied himself so as almost to seem nothing, he without whom nothing was made; and you swell and exalt yourself beyond measure, thinking yourself something when you truly are nothing! You deceive yourself (Galatians vi, 3), the Apostle cries to you; whereas, even if you were something, even something great, you ought all the more to humble yourself. The greater you are, says the Wise Man, humble yourself in all things, and you will find grace before God (Ecclesiasticus iii, 20), who resists the proud but gives grace to the humble (James iv, 6 and I Peter v, 5). He himself, to become your example, though greatest of all, became humblest and least of all. It was not enough for him to become less than the angels through the condition of mortal nature, unless he also became less than men through the age of helpless infancy. Let the devout and humble see and rejoice; let the impious and proud see and be confounded. Let them see, I say, the immeasurable God made little, an infant to be adored: an astounding mystery, the redemption of the devout, the glory of the humble, the judgment of the impious, the downfall of the proud. O mystery worthy of reverence and trembling! How holy and terrible is your name, fountain of mercies and abyss of judgments! Who has drunk from this fountain and not loved? Who has contemplated this abyss and not trembled? Whoever has not loved is wicked and impious; just as whoever has not trembled is senseless and foolish. Yet there is no reason to dread judgment if you do not rise insolently against mercy. He himself would rather be loved than feared as a slave fears; and he approves more highly what the free and willing affection of children offers than what the forced terror of servants wrings out. Therefore, when he first manifested himself to mortals, he chose to show himself as a child and to appear lovable rather than terrible: since he was coming to save and not to judge, he would for the time display what would awaken love, and defer what could strike fear.

Latin original

3. Cum fiducia igitur accedamus ad thronum gratiae ejus, qui nec cogitare sine tremore poteramus de throno gloriae ejus. Nihil hic terroris, nihil severitatis quam metuas; sed omnimoda benignitas atque mansuetudo, de qua praesumas. Et si potestas et terror apud ipsum est, totum tamen interim dissimulat, donec poenitenti parcat, confitentem recipiat. Ne sit tibi scrupulo, quod graviter deliquisti: nescit puer laesus irasci; aut, si irascitur, facile potest placari. Vere nihil placabilius animo hujus pueri, qui in hoc ipsum pacem tuam satisfactionemque praevenit, priorque legatos de pace mittit: ut qui reus es, velis reconciliari. Tantummodo velis, et vere perfecteque velis: non modo veniam indulgebit, sed et gratiam cumulabit: quinimo lucrum non mediocre deputans, ovem recuperasse perditam, diem festum aget cum angelis. Congruebat ergo cum divina mansuetudine aetas innocens ac facilis infantiae, satisque convenienter et commodo salutem peccatorum ab hac exorditus est aetate; ut quos reatus propriae terrebat conscientiae, spes consolaretur non difficilis indulgentiae.

English translation

3. Let us therefore approach with confidence the throne of his grace, we who could not even think of the throne of his glory without trembling. Here there is no terror, no severity for you to fear, but every kind of goodness and gentleness in which to place your trust. Even if power and terror are with him, for the time he conceals it all, until he spares the penitent and receives the one who confesses. Do not let the fact that you have sinned grievously trouble you: a child who has been hurt does not know how to be angry; or, if he is angry, he is easily appeased. Truly nothing is more ready to be appeased than the heart of this child, who anticipates your peace-making and satisfaction for this very purpose, and sends envoys of peace first, so that you, the guilty one, may be willing to be reconciled. Only be willing, truly and fully willing: he will not merely grant pardon, but heap grace upon you; indeed, reckoning the recovery of the lost sheep no small gain, he will celebrate a feast with the angels. The innocent and yielding age of infancy therefore accorded with divine gentleness, and it was fitting and advantageous that he began the salvation of sinners at this age, so that those whom the guilt of their own conscience terrified might be comforted by the hope of pardon readily obtained.

Latin original

4. O puer dulcissime, Jesu bone, quam magna est multitudo dulcedinis tuae, quam abscondisti timentibus te, et perficies sperantibus in te, qui tantam exhibuisti necdum scientibus te! Prorsus dulcedo incomparabilis, et pietas ineffabilis; ut Deum qui creavit me, videam puerum creatum propter me: Deusque majestatis et gloriae non solum similis mihi fiat corporis veritate, sed etiam miserabilis, et velut humanae indigens opis appareat aetatis infirmitate. Vere tu puer Deus, salutare vultus mei, et Deus meus, cum totus sis dulcedo, et desiderium, dulciorem tamen te mihi facit teneritudo membrorum. Haec nimirum te capabilem facit sensibus et affectibus parvulorum, qui nondum sunt te solidum idonei capere cibum. Dulce interim, dulce prorsus et sapidum, cogitare puerum Deum: quin etiam efficax et operatorium ad medendum, ac dulcorandum, si quis in nobis est, rancorem animorum, sermonum amaritudinem, morum austeritatem. Neque enim crediderim, ubi sensus et memoria fuerit hujus divinae dulcedinis, locum irae vel tristitiae inveniri: sed omnis indignatio, et amaritudo cum omni malitia tollatur a nobis. Ita fiet ut quasi modo geniti infantes, modo genitum infantem Dominum digne laudemus, et in consonantia morum et vocum, ex ore infantium et lactentium perficiatur laus infantis, et lactentis Domini Jesu Christi, cui cum Patre et Spiritu sancto laus et jubilatio, per aeterna saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

4. O sweetest child, good Jesus, how great is the abundance of your sweetness, which you have hidden for those who fear you and will bring to fulfilment for those who hope in you, you who have shown so much to those who do not yet know you! Truly, incomparable sweetness and ineffable loving-kindness: that I should see God who created me as a child created for me; and that the God of majesty and glory should not only become like me in the reality of his body, but even appear pitiable and as if needing human assistance through the weakness of his age. Truly, you, the child who is God, the salvation of my countenance and my God, although you are wholly sweetness and desire, the tenderness of your limbs makes you sweeter still to me. For this makes you comprehensible to the senses and affections of little children, who are not yet fit to receive you as solid food. Meanwhile it is sweet, altogether sweet and savoury, to contemplate God as a child; indeed it is effective and powerful to heal and sweeten whatever rancour of spirit, bitterness of speech, or harshness of conduct is in us. For I could not believe that where there is a sense and remembrance of this divine sweetness, room could be found for anger or sadness; rather, let all indignation and bitterness, with all malice, be taken away from us. Thus, like newborn infants, we shall worthily praise the infant Lord newly born, and in the harmony of our conduct and our voices, from the mouths of infants and nursing children shall be perfected the praise of the infant and nursing Lord Jesus Christ, to whom with the Father and the Holy Spirit be praise and jubilation, through the eternal ages of ages. Amen.
