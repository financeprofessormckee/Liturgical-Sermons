Editorial introduction

This second Nativity sermon belongs to Guerric of Igny's medieval collection of sermons for the liturgical year, here read from the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. Its individual date is unknown; the collection is associated approximately with his abbacy in 1138–1157. Taking Isaiah's "a child is born to us, a son is given to us", Guerric reads the gift as the barren Church's joy and the rejected Synagogue's loss: the dowry of the old covenant passes to the new Bride. He then counts what is given with the Son, and asks for right faith, a pure conscience and ready devotion to receive it. The heading names the Nativity without a particular Christmas Mass.

English translation

What Gift the Church Received When the Synagogue Spurned It

¶cols. 31-32; cols. 33-34
Latin original

1. Puer natus est nobis, et filius datus est nobis (Isai. IX, 6). Inde probatur quod natus est nobis, quia scilicet datus est nobis. Illis enim recte dicitur natus, quibus videtur datus. Et omnibus quidem gratia nativitatis hujus oblata est, sed non ab omnibus suscepta est. Non enim omnium est fides. In propria venit, et sui eum non receperunt (Joan. I, 11). Inutiliter autem natus esset nisi et datus esset: frustraque factus esset filius hominis, si non recepissent eum filii hominum, quibus daret potestatem filios Dei fieri. Vae igitur, Judaea incredula, ingrata et impia: ablatum est a te regnum Dei, et datum est genti facienti fructus ejus. Ejectus Jesus est de Synagoga, sed susceptus est ab Ecclesia. Ejectus est de contradictionibus Judaeorum, constitutus est in caput Gentium. Ecce populus, quem non cognovit, servivit illi, auditu auris obedivit ei; cum de vobis filiis alienis, filiis sceleratis et parricidis, sit pii patris illa querela terribilis: Filios enutrivi et exaltavi, ipsi autem spreverunt me (Isai. I, 2). Sed, qui spernit, nunquid non et ipse spernetur? Quia repulistis verbum Dei, inquiunt apostoli, et indignos vos judicastis vitae aeternae, ecce convertimur ad gentes (Act. XIII, 46). O felix gentilitas! ecce affertur tibi Jesus: curre obviis manibus, extende manus, expande sinus, devotionem tuam probet tam actus quam effectus; filium qui tibi offertur, suscipe fide, amplexare affectu; semperque inter ubera tua commoretur. Et ecce videor mihi audire vocem exsultationis et confessionis de cunctis partibus orbis; a finibus terrae laudes audio, gloriam justi. Est autem sonus ut multitudinis, ut sonus castrorum Dei, vox una laudantium et dicentium: Puer natus est nobis, et filius datus est nobis (Isa. IX, 6). Ipsa est, ni fallor, Ecclesia laetabunda, et laudans, toto orbe terrarum; pro eo quod sibi natum, sibi datum amplexatur filium, quem oblatum, sed ablatum merito videt perfidiae Judaeorum. Lauda, sterilis, quae non pariebas, decanta laudem et jubila, quae non parturiebas: quia per hunc filium qui datus est tibi, multi erunt filii desertae, magis quam ejus, quae vinculo legis Deo maritata (Isa. LIV, 1), ei videbatur adhaerere. Tunc mirabitur et dilatabitur cor tuum (Isa. LX, 5). Tunc dices in corde tuo: Quis genuit mihi istos? Ego sterilis et non pariens, transmigrata et captiva: et istos quis enutrivit? Ego destituta et sola, et isti ubi hic erant? (Isa. XLIX, 21.) O mater incorrupta, virgo fecunda, filius, qui datus est tibi, hos tibi dedit. Est enim Filius Altissimi, cui Pater istos adoptavit, ut sint omnes conformes imaginis ejus, et sit ipse primogenitus in multis fratribus. Dilata, inquit, itaque locum tentorii tui ab ortu ad occasum, et a mari ad mare, quia ad dexteram et ad laevam penetrabis, et semen tuum gentes haereditabit (Isa. LIV, 2, 3). Iste namque unigenitus Mariae, primogenitus est omni creaturae, cui dicitur a Patre: Postula a me, et dabo tibi gentes haereditatem tuam, et possessionem tuam terminos terrae (Psal. II, 8).

English translation

1. A child is born to us, and a son is given to us (Isai. IX, 6). From this it is proved that he was born to us, namely because he was given to us. For he is rightly said to be born to those to whom he appears to be given. And indeed the grace of this birth was offered to all, but it was not received by all. For not all have faith. He came into his own, and his own did not receive him (Joan. I, 11). But he would have been born in vain unless he had also been given; and the Son of man would have been made in vain, if the sons of men had not received him, to whom he might give the power to become sons of God. Woe, then, unbelieving Judea, ungrateful and impious: the kingdom of God has been taken away from you, and given to a nation that produces its fruits. Jesus was cast out of the Synagogue, but received by the Church. He was cast out amid the contradictions of the Jews; he was set up as the head of the Gentiles. Behold, a people whom he did not know served him; at the hearing of the ear they obeyed him; while concerning you, strange children, wicked and parricidal children, there stands that terrible complaint of the loving Father: 'I have nourished and raised up children, but they have despised me' (Isai. I, 2). But he who despises—shall he not also himself be despised? Because you have rejected the word of God, say the apostles, and have judged yourselves unworthy of eternal life, behold, we turn to the Gentiles (Act. XIII, 46). O happy Gentile people! Behold, Jesus is brought to you: run to meet him with open hands, stretch forth your hands, open wide your bosom; let both deed and effect prove your devotion; the son who is offered to you, receive with faith, embrace with affection; and let him abide always between your breasts. And behold, I seem to hear a voice of exultation and confession from all parts of the world; from the ends of the earth I hear praises, the glory of the just one. And the sound is as of a multitude, as the sound of the camps of God, one voice of those praising and saying: 'A child is born to us, and a son is given to us' (Isa. IX, 6). This is, unless I am mistaken, the Church, rejoicing and praising throughout the whole world; because she embraces as born to her, given to her, the son whom she sees offered to the faithlessness of the Jews, but deservedly taken away from it. Praise, O barren one who did not bear; sing out praise and shout for joy, you who did not travail: because through this son who is given to you, there will be more children of the deserted one than of her who, bound by the law in marriage to God (Isa. LIV, 1), seemed to cling to him. Then your heart shall marvel and be enlarged (Isa. LX, 5). Then you will say in your heart: Who has borne these for me? I was barren and not bearing, exiled and captive: and who has reared these? I was destitute and alone, and where were these? (Isa. XLIX, 21.) O undefiled mother, fruitful virgin, the son who was given to you has given these to you. For he is the Son of the Most High, for whom the Father has adopted these, that all might be conformed to his image, and that he himself might be the firstborn among many brethren. Enlarge, he says, therefore, the place of your tent from the rising to the setting, and from sea to sea, for you shall spread abroad to the right hand and to the left, and your seed shall possess the nations as an inheritance (Isa. LIV, 2, 3). For this only-begotten of Mary is the firstborn of all creation, to whom it is said by the Father: Ask of me, and I will give you the nations for your inheritance, and the ends of the earth for your possession (Psal. II, 8).

Latin original

2. Sicut autem, suscepto Dei Filio, Ecclesia sterilis peperit plurimos, et roborata est: ita ipso ejecto, aemula ejus Synagoga, quae multos filios habebat, infirmata est. Ideo enim hodie Ecclesia gratulabunda de dato sibi filio gratiarum actione et voce laudis replet coelos: illa muta sedet in tenebris, aut certe ejulatu fatigat inferos. Misera et caeca, cur non advertit quam manifeste Deus ejus ad nos transierit; quam evidenter ipsa repudiata, solumque retinente libellum repudii, litteram dico legis, quae adulteria ipsius, coarguit, universam dotem ipsius ad novam Sponsam nuptam transtulerit? Ecce in manibus nostris tabulae matrimonii, in duobus Testamentis; ecce sacramenta unctionum, dignitas regum, ordo sacerdotum, ministeria levitarum, cultus templi et vasorum, sacrificiorum veritas, omnis denique ipsorum gloria, translata ab ipsis, collata est nobis, quia causa et veritas omnium, Filius datus est nobis. Ipse calicem legis, quae in manu est Mediatoris, ex hoc Judaeo in hunc Christianum inclinavit. Et omne quod in ea merum ac verum fuit, eliquatum nobis transfudit: faex sola, quae non est exinanita, apud Judaeos remansit; ipsam bibunt, ipsa pars calicis eorum.

English translation

2. But just as, having received the Son of God, the barren Church has borne very many children and has been strengthened, so, with him cast out, her rival the Synagogue, who had many children, has been weakened. For this reason, today the rejoicing Church fills the heavens with thanksgiving and the voice of praise for the son given to her; that other one sits mute in darkness, or indeed wearies the depths below with wailing. Wretched and blind one, why does she not notice how plainly her God has passed over to us; how evidently, having repudiated her and left her only the bill of divorce—I mean the letter of the law, which convicts her of her adulteries—he has transferred her whole dowry to the new Bride, now wedded? Behold, in our hands are the marriage-tablets, in the two Testaments; behold the sacraments of anointings, the dignity of kings, the order of priests, the ministries of Levites, the service of the temple and its vessels, the truth of the sacrifices, and in short, all their glory, taken away from them, has been bestowed upon us, because the cause and the truth of all these things, the Son, has been given to us. He himself has tilted the cup of the law—the law which is in the hand of the Mediator—away from this Jew and toward this Christian. And all that was unmixed and true in the law, he has strained out and poured over to us: only the dregs, which were not emptied, remained among the Jews; that is what they drink, that is their portion of the cup.

Latin original

3. Sed quid verbis moror? Numquid enim potest enarrari, quae et quanta sunt data nobis, cum iste Filius datus est nobis? Nonne omne datum optimum, et omne donum perfectum ab ipso, et cum ipso, pariter ad nos descendit? nonne universas opes coeli, omnes thesauros Dei, secum nobis advexit? qui licet adhuc in ipso contineantur absconditi, multa tamen jam et magna, et quasi donativum pugnaturis erogavit; sed nihil omnia ad ea quae victoribus reservavit. Quis enumeret tot munera charismatum, tot genera virtutum, nostrorum scilicet varietatem armorum, subsidia Sacramentorum, fercula Scripturarum, gradus et ordines ministrorum; in omni aetate, sexu, et conditione, natione et lingua, tot martyrum trophaea, confessorum insignia, virginum coronas? si miraris, inquit Ecclesia, unde repente mihi pauperculae haec multitudo divitiarum, et magnitudo gloriae: inde nimirum, quia Filius Dei datus est nobis, Deus gratiae, Dominus virtutum, et Rex gloriae. Imo magis mirum esset, si non sequerentur Dominum bona sua, cum etiam ipse sit omnia bona. Inquirentes, inquit, Dominum, non minuentur omni bono (Psal. XXXIII, 11); quanto magis suscipientes? Si enim miraris ista nobis per Filium data, ego (ut plus mireris) adjiciam omnia omnino nobis cum illo esse donata. Cum enim Filium, per quem et in quo omnia subsistunt, Pater nobis donavit, quomodo non omnia nobis cum illo donavit? (Rom. VIII, 32.) Omnia nostra sunt, ait Apostolus, quia noster est auctor omnium Dominus; ut, etsi videamur tanquam nihil habentes, inveniamur tamen omnia possidentes. Vides igitur quantum, et quale datum sit Filius; quamque probabile sit quod de Sapientia, quae ipse est, legitur: Venerunt mihi omnia bona cum illa, et innumerabilis honestas per manus illius (Sap. VII, 11). Quare autem hoc ita inculco, fratres mei? Quare, nisi ut discatis gloriari, quia filius datus est nobis; et quidquid mundus potest dare, aut promittere vobis, non modo vile et indignum ad illum, sed etiam detrimentum propter illum deputetis? Non quomodo mundus dat, dat ille vobis: tantum grati estote, quia datus est vobis, et satagite ut amplius et perfectius detur vobis. Qui enim semel datus est mundo in forma carnis, et certis diebus aut horis datur fidelibus in specie panis, scilicet in esu Sacramenti sui, saepius et incertis horis datur devotis, in gustu spiritus sui. Primum, ad redemptionem; secundum, ad sanctificationem; tertium etiam ad consolationem. Primum exigit ut fides sit recta; secundum, ut conscientia pura; tertium, ut devotio prompta. Haec mentem elevat, ut gratiae occurrat; cor aperit, ut excipiat; affectum dilatat, ut plurimum ipsius capiat. Non solum autem istam, sed et omnem gratiam abundare faciat in vobis, Deus omnis gratiae, Filius, qui datus est vobis, qui est Deus benedictus in saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

3. But why do I linger over words? For can it be told, how many and how great are the things given to us, when this Son was given to us? Does not every best gift and every perfect gift come down to us from him, and with him equally? Did he not bring with him to us all the riches of heaven, all the treasures of God? For although these still remain hidden, contained within him, yet already he has paid out many and great things, as it were a donative to those about to fight; but all these are nothing compared to what he has reserved for the victors. Who could count so many gifts of graces, so many kinds of virtues—that is, the variety of our weapons, the aids of the Sacraments, the courses of the Scriptures, the ranks and orders of ministers; in every age, sex, and condition, nation and tongue, so many trophies of martyrs, insignia of confessors, crowns of virgins? If you wonder, says the Church, whence suddenly came to me, poor little one, this multitude of riches and this greatness of glory: it is, of course, because the Son of God was given to us, the God of grace, the Lord of powers, and the King of glory. Indeed it would be far more wonderful if his own goods did not follow the Lord, since he himself is all good things. Those who seek the Lord, it says, shall not be diminished in any good thing (Psal. XXXIII, 11); how much more those who receive him? For if you wonder at these things given to us through the Son, I will add (that you may wonder the more) that absolutely all things have been given to us together with him. For when the Father gave us the Son, through whom and in whom all things subsist, how did he not give us all things together with him? (Rom. VIII, 32.) All things are ours, says the Apostle, because the Lord, the author of all things, is ours; so that, although we seem as having nothing, yet we are found possessing all things. You see, then, how great and of what kind a gift the Son is; and how fitting it is that what is read of Wisdom, which he himself is, applies here: All good things came to me together with her, and innumerable riches through her hands (Sap. VII, 11). Why, then, do I press this point so insistently, my brothers? Why, except that you may learn to glory in this, that the Son has been given to us; and that whatever the world can give or promise you, you should reckon not only as vile and unworthy compared to him, but even as a loss on account of him. He does not give to you as the world gives: only be grateful, because he has been given to you, and strive that he may be given to you more fully and more perfectly. For he who was given once to the world in the form of flesh, and is given to the faithful on fixed days or hours under the appearance of bread—that is, in the eating of his Sacrament—is given more often, and at uncertain hours, to the devout, in the tasting of his Spirit. The first was for redemption; the second, for sanctification; the third, also for consolation. The first requires right faith; the second, a pure conscience; the third, ready devotion. This last lifts up the mind, that it may go out to meet grace; opens the heart, that it may receive it; enlarges the affection, that it may grasp as much of it as possible. May God of all grace, the Son who has been given to you, who is God blessed forever and ever, cause not only this grace but every grace to abound in you. Amen.
