Editorial introduction

This third Nativity sermon belongs to Guerric of Igny's medieval collection of sermons for the liturgical year, here read from the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. Its individual date is unknown; the collection is associated approximately with his abbacy in 1138–1157. The child is born for us, not for himself or the angels, and his birth annuls the ancient condemnation of ours. Guerric dwells on God poured out in the manger, the scandal of hidden strength, and Solomon's judgment between the two mothers. Every soul that does the Lord's will is called to be a mother of Christ in others. The heading names the Nativity without a particular Christmas Mass.

English translation

On the Wonderful Exchange Between God and Man

¶cols. 35-36
Latin original

1. Puer natus nobis (Isa. IX, 6). Nobis prorsus; non enim sibi, non angelis. Sibi, inquam, natus non est, ut scilicet hac nativitate esse, aut melius esse inciperet, qui antequam temporaliter nasceretur, aeternaliter erat, et perfecta beatitudo sibi erat, quia de Deo perfecto perfectus Deus natus erat. Angelis natus non est; quia nec angelus ille qui in veritate stabat, reparatione indigebat; nec illius qui ceciderat, casus reparabilis erat. Ideo nusquam angelos apprehendit, sed semen apprehendit Abrahae (Hebr. II, 16); et qui Deus natus erat sibi, puer natus est nobis: se quodammodo relinquens, et angelos transiliens, ut ad nos usque deveniens unus fieret e nobis; et exinanitus a semetipso, minoratur ab angelis, ut aequalis fieret nobis. Qui igitur natus aeternaliter sibi et angelis erat beatitudo, natus temporaliter nobis, factus est nobis redemptio; quia nos solos laborare videbat antiquo nostrae nativitatis praejudicio. Quam felix, quam amabilis nativitas tua, puer Jesu, quae nativitatem omnium emendat, conditionem reformat, praejudicium solvit, chirographum damnatae naturae rescindit: ut si quem piget damnabiliter esse natum, possit felicissime renasci! Quotquot autem receperunt eum, dedisti eis potestatem filios Dei fieri (Joan. I, 12). Gratias agimus gratuitae atque gratiosae nativitati tuae, Deus filii hominis, per quem accessum habemus in gratiam istam in qua stamus, et gloriamur in spe gloriae filiorum Dei (Rom. V, 2). Prorsus admirabile commercium, ut sumens carnem, largiaris deitatem! commercium, inquam, charitate, non cupiditate contractum; indulgentiae quidem tuae gloriosum, sed indigentiae meae omnino lucrosum. Vere tu puer misericors, quem sola misericordia puerum fecit: licet pariter misericordia et veritas in te obviaverunt sibi (Psal. LXXXIV, 11). Vere, inquam, tu misericors puer, natus es nobis, non tibi: lucra nostra, non augmenta tua nascens de nobis quaesisti, quia ad hoc solum nasci dignatus es, ut nos minoratione tui proveheres, humiliatione glorificares. Tu exinanitus nos replesti: tu enim omnem tuae plenitudinem divinitatis in hominem transfudisti. Transfudisti, sed non confudisti. Transfusum tamen Deum in hominem non dicerem, si ad mensuram datum huic homini spiritum audirem: si de omni sua plenitudine penes Deum aliquid recedisset, quod non in hominem, cui unitus est, effudisset.

English translation

1. "A child is born to us" (Isa. IX, 6). To us indeed; for he was not born for himself, nor for the angels. He was not born, I say, for himself, as though by this birth he should begin to exist, or begin to exist better—he who, before he was born in time, existed eternally, and was perfect blessedness to himself, because he who was born was perfect God from perfect God. Nor was he born for the angels; for neither did that angel who stood firm in truth need restoration, nor was the fall of the one who had fallen reparable. Therefore he nowhere took hold of angels, but took hold of the seed of Abraham (Hebr. II, 16); and he who was born God for himself was born a child for us: in a certain manner leaving himself behind, and passing over the angels, so that, coming all the way to us, he might become one of us; and having emptied himself of himself, he was made lower than the angels, that he might become equal to us. He, therefore, who born eternally was blessedness to himself and to the angels, born in time for us, was made redemption for us; because he saw that we alone were laboring under the ancient condemnation attached to our birth. How happy, how lovable is your birth, child Jesus, which amends the birth of all, reforms their condition, annuls the sentence, cancels the bond of condemned nature: so that if anyone is grieved to have been born under condemnation, he may be reborn most happily! "But as many as received him, to them you gave power to become sons of God" (Joan. I, 12). We give thanks to your free and gracious birth, God, Son of man, through whom we have access into this grace in which we stand, and we glory in the hope of the glory of the sons of God (Rom. V, 2). Truly a wonderful exchange, that taking on flesh you should bestow deity! An exchange, I say, contracted by charity, not by greed; glorious indeed for your generosity, but wholly profitable for my need. Truly you are a merciful child, whom mercy alone made a child: although in you mercy and truth have met together equally (Psal. LXXXIV, 11). Truly, I say, you, merciful child, were born for us, not for yourself: being born of us, you sought our gain, not any increase for yourself, because you deigned to be born for this alone, that by your diminishment you might advance us, by your humiliation you might glorify us. You, emptied, filled us: for you poured out the whole fullness of your divinity into man. You poured it out, but you did not confound it. Yet I would not say that God was poured into man, if I heard that the Spirit was given to this man by measure: as if something had remained with God from his whole fullness which he had not poured out into the man to whom he was united.

2. Merito igitur oleum effusum nomen (Cantic. I, 2) Dei hominis, vel certe unguentum exinanitum; cum ita funditus Dei in hominem effusus sit, ut eum semetipsum exinanisse (Phil. II, 7) confiteatur fides Apostoli. Quamquam sic utique se exinanivit, ut in se ipso nihil prorsus minuerit, aut mutaverit; nec alia esse potuit naturae immutabilis exinanitio, quam naturae nostrae, quae terra inanis et vacua est, assumptio. Magna prorsus exinanitio ista in oculis videntium, ut splendor gloriae et figura paternae substantiae, nonnisi formam praeferat servilem, et in ipsa non habens speciem neque decorem. Quasi enim parum exinanitus esset, si homo solum fieret, etiam humanae carnis gloriam ita funditus in se vacuavit, sapientiam infatuavit, infirmavit virtutem, minuit magnitudinem; ut et in nativitate minimum, et in passione se exhiberet novissimum virorum: unde nec reputaverunt eum. Vis autem videre Deum exinanitum a semetipso? vide jacentem in praesepio. Ecce Deus noster (Isa. XXV, 9), Isaias ait, qui de tam longinquo vidit, et cognovit praesepe Domini sui, imo Deum in praesepi. Ecce, inquit, Deus noster. Ubi, quaeso? In praesepio, inquit. Infantulum quidem ibi invenio. Hunccine tu dicis illum esse, qui dicit, Coelum et terram ego impleo (Jerem. XXIII, 24): imo cujus majestati angusta est omnis coeli latitudo? Pannis involutum video. Hunccine tu dicis illum esse, qui gloriam et decorem induit lucis inaccessibilis, amictus incircumscripto lumine sicut vestimento? Vagientem audio. Hiccine est qui in coelis tonat, sub cujus vocis tonitruo potestates angelicae submittunt alas suas? Sic est, inquit alius propheta satisfaciens pro Isaia, omnino sic est: Hic est Deus noster, sed exinanitus est, ut te repleat, et a se quodammodo deficere voluit, ut te reficiat. Conclamat omnis chorus prophetarum uno spiritu, eademque sententia, etsi aliqui voce diversa, sic est: Hic est Deus noster, et non aestimabitur alius praeter eum (Baruch. III, 36); neque enim est alius praeter eum. Sed scio, inquit David, scio, quia non credet signo huic, sed contradicet generatio prava et adultera Judaeorum; enarrate vos, apostoli, in progenie altera Gentium. Quid? Quoniam hic quem Judaeus jacentem in praesepi nec respicit, pendentem in cruce despicit; cum miro modo nascitur, non videt; cum mira operatur, invidet; cum dira patitur, irridet: Hic, inquam, hic est Deus noster in aeternum et in saeculum saeculi, ipse reget nos in saecula (Psal. XLVII, 15).

English translation

2. Rightly, then, is the name of God-made-man called "oil poured out" (Cantic. I, 2), or indeed "ointment emptied out"; since God is so utterly poured out into man that the faith of the Apostle confesses that he "emptied himself" (Phil. II, 7). Although he indeed so emptied himself that he diminished or changed nothing at all in himself; nor could the emptying of an unchangeable nature be anything other than the assumption of our nature, which is earth empty and void. Truly this is a great emptying in the eyes of those who behold it, that the splendor of glory and the figure of the Father's substance should show forth nothing but the form of a servant, and in it have neither comeliness nor beauty. For as though it were too little an emptying to become man only, he so utterly emptied in himself even the glory of human flesh, made foolish wisdom, weakened power, diminished greatness; that both in his birth he showed himself the least, and in his passion the last of men: wherefore they did not even esteem him. Do you wish to see God emptied of himself? See him lying in the manger. "Behold our God" (Isa. XXV, 9), says Isaiah, who from so far off saw and recognized the manger of his Lord, indeed God in the manger. "Behold," he says, "our God." Where, I ask? "In the manger," he says. I find there indeed a little infant. Do you say that this is he who says, "I fill heaven and earth" (Jerem. XXIII, 24); indeed, for whose majesty the whole breadth of heaven is too narrow? I see him wrapped in swaddling cloths. Do you say that this is he who is clothed with glory and beauty of inaccessible light, wrapped in unbounded light as with a garment? I hear him crying. Is this he who thunders in the heavens, under the thunder of whose voice the angelic powers lower their wings? "So it is," says another prophet, answering for Isaiah, "entirely so it is: This is our God, but he has emptied himself, that he might fill you, and he willed in a certain manner to fail from himself, that he might restore you." The whole choir of the prophets cries out together with one spirit and the same judgment, though with different voices: "So it is: This is our God, and none other shall be accounted beside him" (Baruch. III, 36); for indeed there is no other besides him. "But I know," says David, "I know that this perverse and adulterous generation of the Jews will not believe this sign, but will contradict it; you, apostles, declare it among another people, the Gentiles." What? That he whom the Jew does not even look upon lying in the manger, and despises hanging on the cross; when he is born in a wondrous manner, the Jew does not see it; when he performs wondrous works, he envies; when he suffers dreadful things, he mocks: This, I say, this is our God forever and world without end; he himself shall rule us forever (Psal. XLVII, 15).

3. Et ecce jam praedicantibus apostolis, testificantibus prophetis, etsi vellus siccatum est, sed areae latitudo infusa est (Judic. VI, 39, 40): etsi cucurbita aruit, Ninive salvata est (Jonae III et IV); et amissio Judaeorum, divitiae facta est mundi, et salus gentium. Adorant omnes reges terrae parvulum qui natus est nobis, omnes gentes serviunt ei: quia si qui sunt qui non serviant, vel non sint servituri, quasi non sint, sic sunt coram eo (Isa. XL, 17); gens etiam et regnum, quod non servierit ei, peribit. Jam tamen videmus et gaudemus impletum, quod per Isaiam fuerat a Patre promissum: Viri sublimes ad te transibunt, tui erunt, post te ambulabunt, te adorabunt, teque deprecabuntur. Et merito, inquit, quia in te tantum est Deus, et non est absque te Deus. Vere tu es Deus absconditus, Deus Israel Salvator (Isa. XLV, 14, 15). Si unquam manifestius, si expressius potuit dici Jesus Deus, nunquam credat Judaeus: aut certe si derogat haec esse dicta de Jesu, ostendat alium in quo sit Deus, et qui sit Deus, nec praeter eum Deus; quando quidem nec personarum Trinitatem fatetur, ut assignare possit Deum, in quo sit Deus, ita ut alius Deus praeter eum non sit. Sed te, perfide, scandalizat, quod ad pietatem aedificare debuerat; quod scilicet Deus absconditus, et homo oculis adhibitus est, tanquam lutum ex sputo, quod ad videndum Deum caecum illuminaret. Hoc, inquam, scandalo tibi est, quod in infirmitate carnis abscondita est fortitudo Dei; et in infirmitate crucis abscondita est fortitudo hominis Dei; quod inglorius inter viros aspectus ejus, et forma ejus inter filios hominum: unde nec reputasti eum, sed putasti quasi leprosum, et percussum a Deo, et humiliatum; cum Deus posuerit in eo iniquitates omnium nostrum (Isa. LIII, 4, 5). Nam et tuam quoque suscepisset, si tu eam vel ad extremum deponere voluisses. At tu, miser, non deponis, sed apponis iniquitatem super iniquitatem; super sanguinem prophetarum, sanguinem Filii Dei et Apostolorum. Impone igitur, quandoquidem ita vis, impone jugum iniquitatum tuarum, tibi et filiis tuis: mihi enim regnum Christi portare magis decet, ut meas ipse portet iniquitates. Tu porta colligationes impietatis, fasciculos deprimentes: ego portem fasciculum myrrhae, quem Maria mihi colligavit, pannisque involutum in praesepi reposuit.

English translation

3. And behold, now that the apostles are preaching and the prophets bearing witness, even though the fleece was dried up, yet the breadth of the threshing floor was soaked (Judic. VI, 39, 40); even though the gourd withered, Nineveh was saved (Jonae III et IV); and the loss of the Jews has become the riches of the world, and the salvation of the Gentiles. All the kings of the earth adore the little child who is born for us, all nations serve him; for if there are any who do not serve him, or who will not serve him, they are before him as though they were not (Isa. XL, 17); indeed, the nation and the kingdom that does not serve him shall perish. Now, however, we see and rejoice that what was promised by the Father through Isaiah has been fulfilled: 'Lofty men shall pass over to you, they shall be yours, they shall walk after you, they shall worship you, and shall make supplication to you.' And rightly so, he says, because God is in you alone, and there is no God apart from you. Truly you are the hidden God, the God of Israel, the Savior (Isa. XLV, 14, 15). If Jesus could ever have been called God more plainly, more expressly — let the Jew never believe it. Or indeed, if he denies that these things were said of Jesus, let him show another in whom God dwells, and who is God, and besides whom there is no other God — since he does not even confess the Trinity of Persons, so that he might be able to point out some God in whom God is, such that there is no other God besides him. But you, faithless one, are scandalized by what ought to have built up your piety: namely, that God was hidden, and the man was applied to the eyes like the clay made from spittle, to enlighten the blind so that he might see God. This, I say, is a scandal to you: that in the weakness of the flesh the strength of God was hidden; and in the weakness of the cross was hidden the strength of the God-man; that his appearance was without glory among men, and his form among the sons of men — hence you did not esteem him, but thought him as it were a leper, struck by God, and humbled; whereas God had placed upon him the iniquities of us all (Isa. LIII, 4, 5). For he would have taken up your iniquity too, if you had only been willing at last to lay it down. But you, wretched man, do not lay it down, but heap iniquity upon iniquity — upon the blood of the prophets, the blood of the Son of God, and of the Apostles. Impose, then, since you so wish, impose the yoke of your iniquities upon yourself and upon your children: for it is more fitting for me to bear the kingdom of Christ, so that he himself may bear my iniquities. You carry the bundles of impiety bound together, weights that press down; I will carry the bundle of myrrh, which Mary bound up for me, and wrapped in swaddling clothes and laid in the manger.

4. Vos igitur, fratres mei, qui cognovistis praesepe Domini vestri, Dominumque in praesepi, quem Israel non cognovit: vos, inquam, quibus Salvator ideo non viluit, quia misericors esse voluit; sed eo vobis tenerior in affectu, quo minor est in aspectu: cantate, et exsultate et psallite, Puer natus est nobis, filius datus est nobis. De Judaeis quidem est natus, sed nobis est natus; quia illis ablatus, nobis est datus. Cantate Domino canticum novum, quia mirabilia fecit. Notum fecit Dominus salutare suum (Psal. XCVII, 1-3): adeo ut et asinus de Gentibus cognoscat in praesepi Dominum suum, fenum sibi factum, quia omnis caro fenum (Isa. XL, 6). In conspectu namque Gentium revelavit justitiam suam (Psal. XCVII, 2): quam Judaeus ignorat, velum adhuc habens super faciem suam. Velum habet, quia zelum tenet: unde nec velum videt, sed invidet quod puer natus est nobis, filius datus est nobis. Nec invidet, quod cupiat habere sibi, sed quod cupiat perire et mihi et sibi. Mallet aemula et mala meretrix infantem necari, quam vivum mihi dari. Sed judicium nostri Salomonis, cujus sermo penetrabilior omni gladio ancipiti, scrutans corda et renes in invenienda matre non erravit: Date, inquit, Ecclesiae infantem vivum: haec est enim mater ejus (III Reg. III, 26, 27). Quicunque enim fecerit voluntatem ejus, ipse mater, et frater, et soror ejus est (Matth. XI, 50). O Domine Salomon, tu matrem dicis, ego ancillam profiteor. Ancilla Christi sum: fiat mihi secundum verbum tuum (Luc. I, 38). Et quidem matrem amore et sollicitudine me praestabo, quantum potero; sed conditionis meae semper memor ero.

English translation

4. You therefore, my brothers, who have known the manger of your Lord, and the Lord in the manger, whom Israel did not know: you, I say, to whom the Savior has not thereby become worthless, because he wished to be merciful; but is all the dearer to your affection, the less he is in outward appearance — sing, and exult, and make music: 'A child is born to us, a son is given to us.' He was indeed born of the Jews, but he was born for us; because, taken away from them, he was given to us. Sing to the Lord a new song, for he has done wonderful things. The Lord has made known his salvation (Psal. XCVII, 1-3): so much so that even the ass from among the Gentiles recognizes in the manger his Lord, who has been made hay for him, since all flesh is hay (Isa. XL, 6). For in the sight of the Gentiles he has revealed his righteousness (Psal. XCVII, 2), which the Jew does not know, still having a veil over his face. He has a veil, because he clings to jealousy; hence he does not even see the veil, but envies that a child is born to us, a son is given to us. Nor does he envy in the sense of wishing to have him for himself, but in that he wishes him to perish both for me and for himself. The rival and wicked harlot would rather the infant be killed than be given alive to me. But the judgment of our Solomon, whose word is sharper than any two-edged sword, searching hearts and inmost parts, did not err in finding the mother: 'Give,' he said, 'the living child to the Church, for she is his mother' (III Reg. III, 26, 27). For whoever does his will, he is his brother, and sister, and mother (Matth. XI, 50). O Lord Solomon, you call me mother; I profess myself a handmaid. I am the handmaid of Christ: be it done to me according to your word (Luc. I, 38). And indeed I will show myself a mother in love and care, as far as I am able; but I will always remain mindful of my condition.

5. O fratres, hoc nomen matris non est singulare praelatis, quamvis specialis eis incumbat cura maternae sollicitudinis et pietatis: commune est et vobis, qui facitis voluntatem Domini. Utique et vos matres estis pueri qui datus est in vobis, et in vobis, ex quo videlicet a timore Domini concepistis, et parturistis spiritum salutis. Vigila igitur, o mater sancta, vigila super curam recens nati, donec formetur in te Christus, qui natus est tibi: quia quo tenerior est, eo facilius potest perire tibi, qui nunquam perit sibi. Spiritus siquidem qui in te est, si tibi exstinctus fuerit, redit ad Deum qui dedit illum. Vigila, inquam, super curam recens nati, memor quoniam aemula tua dormiens oppressit filium, qui datus erat ei. Quae est enim illa, nisi carnalis anima, quae spiritum exstinguit negligentia et inertia. Hujusmodi homines, cum religionis amiserint fervorem, ipsius tamen sibi arrogant gloriam et nomen. Inde contentiones carnalium adversus spirituales, etiam in capitulis, ubi verus Salomon invisibiliter judex praesidet. Filius meus, inquiunt carnales, vivit, et tuus mortuus est: ego spiritum Dei habeo, tu non habes; in me vivit amor Dei, in te mortuus est: dum scilicet aemulantur sibi religionis auctoritatem, cujus spirituales habent veritatem; ut subducta eis auctoritate, consuetudines inducant pro sui sensus libidine. Et revera quidem mater vult aemulae dari vivum et integrum infantem, non invidens ei gloriam, dummodo habeat et virtutem, sed illa, Nec mihi, inquit, nec tibi sit, sed dividatur (III Reg. III, 26); quia nimirum sanctitatis sibi cupit retinere honorem, et aliis relinquere laborem. Sed non errat qui judicat, etsi aliquandiu dissimulat. Gladius Salomonis matrem invenit, cui adjudicat indivisum, sicut affectum charitatis, sic effectum potestatis; sicut fervorem operandi, sic favorem imperandi. Vos itaque, fratres, in quibus fides per dilectionem operans nata est de Spiritu sancto, custodite eam, pascite, nutrite, tanquam parvulum Jesum, donec formetur in vobis puer qui natus est nobis; qui non solum nascendo, sed vivendo et moriendo, formam cui conformemur tradidit nobis: memor quippe semper, quod non nisi nobis natus fuit, nec nisi nobis vivere voluit, nec mori nisi pro nobis, qui pro se non habuit; ut et nos per ipsum renasceremur, secundum ipsum viveremus; in ipso moreremur, qui vivit et regnat per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

5. O brothers, this name of mother is not reserved to prelates alone, although a special care of maternal solicitude and devotion falls upon them: it is common also to you, who do the will of the Lord. Indeed you too are mothers of the child who is given in you, and in you, from the moment, that is, when you conceived from the fear of the Lord and brought forth the spirit of salvation. Watch, therefore, O holy mother, watch over the care of the newly born, until Christ, who was born for you, be formed in you: for the more tender he is, the more easily he can perish for you, though he never perishes for himself. For the Spirit who is in you, if he be extinguished in you, returns to God who gave him. Watch, I say, over the care of the newly born, remembering that your rival, while sleeping, overlay the son who had been given to her. For who is that rival but the carnal soul, which extinguishes the spirit through negligence and sloth? Such men, when they have lost the fervor of religion, nonetheless claim for themselves its glory and name. Hence arise contentions of carnal men against spiritual men, even in chapters, where the true Solomon invisibly presides as judge. 'My son,' say the carnal, 'lives, and yours is dead: I have the Spirit of God, you do not; in me the love of God lives, in you it is dead'—while, that is, they jealously claim for themselves the authority of religion, of which the spiritual possess the truth; so that, authority having been wrested from them, they may introduce customs according to the lust of their own judgment. And indeed the true mother wishes the living and whole infant to be given to her rival, not envying her the glory, provided she also have the virtue; but that other says, 'Let it be neither mine nor yours, but let it be divided' (III Reg. III, 26); because, of course, she desires to keep for herself the honor of holiness, and leave to others the labor. But he who judges does not err, even though for a while he dissembles. The sword of Solomon finds out the mother, to whom he awards the child undivided, both the affection of charity and the effect of power; both the fervor of working and the favor of commanding. You therefore, brothers, in whom faith working through love has been born of the Holy Spirit, guard it, feed it, nourish it, as the little Jesus, until the child who is born to us be formed in you; who delivered to us not only by being born, but by living and dying, a form to which we should be conformed: ever mindful that he was born for us alone, and willed to live for us alone, and to die for none but us—he who had no need to die for himself—so that through him we might be reborn, according to him we might live, and in him we might die, who lives and reigns through all ages of ages. Amen.
