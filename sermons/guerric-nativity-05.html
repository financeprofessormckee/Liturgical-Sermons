Editorial introduction

This fifth Nativity sermon belongs to Guerric of Igny's medieval collection of sermons for the liturgical year, here read from the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. Its individual date is unknown; the collection is associated approximately with his abbacy in 1138–1157. The brothers came to hear the Word of God, but at Bethlehem they may also see it. Guerric draws lessons from the infant Word's silence, poverty and humility, and from the shepherds' simple faith. He ends at the altar, where the same body lies hidden under humble forms as the infant was wrapped in cloths. The heading names the Nativity without a particular Christmas Mass.

English translation

On God's Wonderful Providence in the Birth of Christ

¶cols. 43-44
Latin original

1. Convenistis, fratres, ad audiendum verbum Dei. Deus autem aliquid melius providit nobis, ut non solum audire, sed etiam videre datum nobis hodie sit Verbum Dei, si modo transeamus usque Bethlehem, et videamus hoc verbum quod fecit Dominus, et ostendit nobis (Luc. II, 15). Noverat hominum sensus invisibilium incapaces, coelestium indocibiles, ad fidem quoque difficiles, nisi res ipsa de qua fides suadetur, visibiliter ipsis sensibus convincendis ingeratur. Nam etsi fides est ex auditu, multo tamen proclivius ac promptius ex visu: sicut illius exemplo docemur, cui dicitur, Quia vidisti me, credidisti (Joan. XX, 29); qui scilicet dum audiebas incredulus fuisti. Quia enim difficilius audita, quam visa creduntur, ideo merito fides illorum qui non viderunt, a Domino beata praedicatur; quippe qui plus detulerint auctoritati sermonis, quam experientiae proprii sensus, aut rationis. Deus tamen per omnia volens satisfacere tarditati nostrae, Verbum suum quod prius fecerat audibile, hodie fecit nobis etiam visibile, imo et tractabile; ut et quidam ex nobis potuerint dicere: Quod fuit ab initio, quod audivimus, quod vidimus oculis nostris, quod perspeximus, et manus nostrae tractaverunt de Verbo vitae (Joan. I, 1). Fuit ab initio illius, quae sine initio est, aeternitatis; audivimus promissum ab initio temporis; vidimus et contrectavimus exhibitum in fine temporis. Alias autem invenies quod non solum visibile et tractabile, sed etiam gustabile et odorabile nobis Verbum Dei factum sit, quippe quod per omnes vias sensuum, aditum sibi ad animam quaesierit, quatenus sicut per sensus mors intraverat, sic per eosdem rediret et vita. Quod ergo Verbum caro factum est (Joan. I, 14), nobis qui toti sumus caro, factum est; ut qui antea Verbum Dei tantum poteramus audire, jam carnem factum et videre possimus et gustare, omnesque sensus in testimonium auditus convocare; quatenus omnes nostri sensus consensu uno, et voce una confiteantur, Sicut audivimus, sic vidimus (Psal. XLVII, 9). Incomparabiliter tamen amplius tunc concessum est visui, quam unquam fuerat auditui, cum modo videatur Verbum, quod Deus est, et antea magnum haberetur audire verbum quodcunque ex Deo. Et verbum quidem quod ex Deo est, vidi aliquando, fratres, cum taedio audiri; sed Verbum quod Deus est, nunquid poterit nisi cum gaudio videri? In me primum sententiam feram: quia Verbum, quod Deus est, cum se hodie mihi videndum praebeat in eo quod sum, si me non laetificat, impius sum; si non aedificat, reprobus sum.

English translation

1. You have come together, brothers, to hear the word of God. But God has provided something better for us, so that today it is given to us not only to hear but also to see the Word of God — if only we go over as far as Bethlehem, and see this word which the Lord has made, and shown to us (Luc. II, 15). He knew that the senses of men are incapable of invisible things, unteachable in heavenly things, and also slow to believe, unless the very thing of which faith is being persuaded is impressed visibly upon the senses themselves so as to convince them. For although faith is from hearing, yet it comes much more readily and easily from sight: as we are taught by the example of him to whom it was said, "Because you have seen me, you have believed" (Joan. XX, 29) — that is, you who, while you were only hearing, were unbelieving. For since things heard are believed with more difficulty than things seen, therefore the faith of those who have not seen is rightly proclaimed blessed by the Lord; since they gave more credit to the authority of the word than to the experience of their own sense or reason. Yet God, wishing in every way to satisfy our slowness, has today made for us even visible — indeed even touchable — his Word, which before he had made only audible; so that some of us have even been able to say: "That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled, concerning the Word of life" (Joan. I, 1). He was from the beginning of that eternity which has no beginning; we heard him promised from the beginning of time; we saw and touched him shown forth at the end of time. Elsewhere, too, you will find that the Word of God was made for us not only visible and touchable, but even tastable and smellable, since through every avenue of the senses it sought an entrance to the soul, so that just as death had entered through the senses, so also life might return through the same senses. Therefore that "the Word was made flesh" (Joan. I, 14) was done for us, who are wholly flesh; so that we, who before could only hear the Word of God, might now both see and taste it made flesh, and might summon all our senses as witness to our hearing; so that all our senses, with one consent and one voice, might confess, "As we have heard, so have we seen" (Psal. XLVII, 9). Yet incomparably more was then granted to sight than had ever been granted to hearing, since now the Word which is God is seen, whereas before it was reckoned a great thing merely to hear any word whatsoever from God. And indeed, brothers, I have sometimes seen a word which is from God heard with weariness; but can the Word which is God possibly be seen except with joy? Let me pass judgment first upon myself: since the Word, which is God, offers itself today to be seen by me in that which I am — if it does not make me glad, I am impious; if it does not build me up, I am reprobate.

2. Si quis ergo, fratres, acediosus invenitur inter nos, nolo diutius ut fatigentur aures ejus sermone nostro contemptibili; transeat usque Bethlehem, et illud in quod desiderant Angeli prospicere, prospiciat ibi, Verbum scilicet Dei, quod Dominus ostendit nobis; imaginetur animo, Sermo Dei vivus et efficax qualis ibi jaceat [ al. qualiter ibi taceat] in praesepi. Si tantum pietas oculum illuminet intuentis, quid tam delectabiliter potest videri, tam salubriter cogitari? Quid aeque mores aedificat, spem roborat, charitatem inflammat? Omnino fidelis sermo, et omni acceptione dignus, omnipotens Sermo tuus, Domine; qui tam alto silentio a regalibus Patris sedibus animalium illapsus praesepibus (Sap. XVIII, 15), melius interim suo nobis silentio sermocinatur. Qui habet aures audiendi, audiat quid nobis hoc pium et mysticum aeterni Verbi loquatur silentium; quoniam, nisi me fallit auditus, inter alia quae loquitur, loquitur pacem in plebem sanctorum, quibus reverentia ipsius et exemplum religiosum imposuit silentium, et rectissime quidem imposuit. Quid enim tanto pondere, tantaque auctoritate disciplinam commendat silentii, quid tanto terrore compescit inquietum malum linguarum procellasque verborum, sicut Sermo Dei silens in medio hominum? Non est sermo in lingua mea, confiteri videtur omnipotens Sermo, dum Matri subditus est; et nos qua dementia dicemus, Linguam nostram magnificabimus, labia nostra a nobis sunt; quis noster Dominus est? (Psal. XI, 5.) Liberet mihi, si liceret, obmutescere et humiliari, et silere etiam a bonis, ut attentius et diligentius aurem possem adhibere vocibus arcanis, sacrisque sensibus hujus divini silentii, et vel tanto tempore sub silentio discere in schola Verbi, quanto Verbum ipsum sub disciplina siluit Matris.

English translation

2. If then, brothers, anyone among us is found weighed down with listlessness, I do not wish his ears to be wearied any longer by our contemptible discourse; let him go over to Bethlehem, and there look upon that into which the angels desire to look, namely the Word of God, which the Lord has shown us; let him picture in his mind how the living and effective Word of God lies there [alternate reading: how it keeps silence there] in the manger. If only piety would illumine the eye of the one beholding, what could be seen so delightfully, thought upon so wholesomely? What equally builds up conduct, strengthens hope, inflames love? Altogether faithful is the saying, and worthy of all acceptance — your almighty Word, O Lord; who, gliding down in so deep a silence from the Father's royal throne into the mangers of animals (Sap. XVIII, 15), meanwhile speaks to us the better by his very silence. Let him who has ears to hear, hear what this pious and mystical silence of the eternal Word speaks to us; for, unless my hearing deceives me, among the other things it speaks, it speaks peace to the people of the saints, upon whom reverence for it and its devout example have imposed silence — and most rightly has it imposed it. For what commends the discipline of silence with such weight and such authority, what restrains with such terror the restless evil of tongues and the storms of words, as the Word of God, silent in the midst of men? "There is no word in my tongue" — the almighty Word seems to confess this, while he is subject to his Mother; and with what madness shall we say, "We will magnify our tongue, our lips are our own; who is our Lord?" (Psal. XI, 5.). It would please me, if it were permitted, to become mute and be humbled, and to keep silence even from good things, so that I might the more attentively and diligently apply my ear to the secret voices and the sacred meanings of this divine silence, and might learn in silence in the school of the Word for at least as long a time as the Word himself kept silence under the discipline of his Mother.

3. O fratres, si pie ac diligenter intendamus huic Verbo quod Dominus fecit hodie, et ostendit nobis, quanta, quamque facile possemus ab ipso perdoceri? Verbum siquidem breviatum est, ita tamen ut in eo consummatum sit omne verbum quod ad salutem est, quia nimirum verbum consummans et abbrevians in aequitate ipsum est. Et haec consummatio abbreviata inundavit justitiam (Isa. X, 23), sicut promissum meministis per Isaiam; quia de sua plenitudine in participes suos redundavit justitiam, ut abundet justitia ipsorum, plus quam et Pharisaeorum et Scribarum; licet illi per tam multiplices se exercitent justificationes legis, isti autem brevi ac simplici contenti sint verbo fidei. Sed quid mirum si omnia verba sua nobis Verbum Dei breviavit, quando et se ipsum breviari, et quodammodo minui voluit, adeo ut de incomprehensibili immensitate sui ad augustias uteri quodammodo se contraxerit, et, continens mundum, in praesepi se passum sit contineri? In coelo Verbum istud horribili altitudine sua virtutes angelicas stupefacit: in praesepi simplices et hebetes pascit: ibi subtilissimis Angelorum intellectibus inscrutabile, hic etiam obtusis hominum sensibus palpabile. Quia enim Deus non poterat loqui nobis quasi spiritualibus, sed quasi carnalibus, Verbum ejus factum est caro, ut non solum audire, sed et videre posset omnis caro quod os Domini locutum est. Et quia in sapientia sua non cognovit mundus sapientiam Dei, dignatione ineffabili eadem Sapientia Dei se stultitiam fecit, ut quamlibet idiotis vel hebetibus se discibilem praeberet, et per stultitiam praedicationis salvos faceret credentes. Confiteor tibi, Pater, Domine coeli et terrae, quia sapientiam tuam abscondisti a sapientibus et prudentibus, et revelasti eam parvulis. Ita, Pater, sic fuit placitum ante te (Matth. XI, 25, 26), ut parvulis daretur parvulus qui natus est nobis, quandoquidem altitudo superborum nimis abhorret ab hujus humilitate parvuli, et quod altum est apud homines, abominabile est apud Deum, qui, cum veraciter altus sit, parvulus factus est pro nobis. Cum solis nimirum parvulis concordat iste parvulus, in solis requiescit humilibus et quietis. Sicut igitur parvuli gloriantes de eo, cantant, Parvulus natus est nobis (Isa. IX, 6), sic et ipse gloriatur de eis: Ecce ego, inquiens, et pueri mei, quos mihi donavit Dominus (Id. V, 18). Ut enim parvulo Filio suo Pater sodales daret de coaetaneis suis, statim primo omnium ab innocentia parvulorum coepit gloria martyrii; hoc significante Spiritu sancto, quia nonnisi talium esset regnum coelorum (Matth. XIX, 14).

English translation

3. O brothers, if we attend to this Word which the Lord has made today, and shown to us, piously and diligently, how much—and how easily—might we be thoroughly taught by it? For indeed the Word has been shortened, yet in such a way that in it every word that pertains to salvation is fulfilled, because it is indeed the word that completes and cuts short in equity. And this shortened completion has overflowed with righteousness (Isa. X, 23), as you recall was promised through Isaiah; for from his own fullness he has poured forth righteousness upon those who share in him, so that their righteousness abounds more than that of the Pharisees and Scribes—though those exercise themselves through so many manifold justifications of the law, while these are content with the brief and simple word of faith. But what wonder is it if the Word of God has shortened all his words for us, when he himself willed to be shortened, and in a certain manner diminished, so much so that from the incomprehensible immensity of himself he contracted himself, as it were, to the narrow confines of a womb, and he who contains the world suffered himself to be contained in a manger? In heaven this Word, by its awesome height, strikes the angelic powers with astonishment; in the manger it feeds the simple and the dull: there inscrutable even to the most subtle intellects of the Angels, here even palpable to the dull senses of men. For since God could not speak to us as to spiritual beings, but as to carnal ones, his Word was made flesh, so that all flesh might not only hear, but also see, what the mouth of the Lord has spoken. And because the world, in its own wisdom, did not know the wisdom of God, by ineffable condescension this same Wisdom of God made itself foolishness, so that it might offer itself as teachable even to the unlearned and the dull, and through the foolishness of preaching might save those who believe. I confess to you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, that you have hidden your wisdom from the wise and prudent, and have revealed it to little ones. Thus, Father, so it was pleasing before you (Matth. XI, 25, 26), that the little one who was born for us should be given to little ones, since the haughtiness of the proud shrinks utterly from the humility of this little one, and what is high among men is abominable before God, who, though he is truly high, was made a little one for us. For this little one agrees indeed only with little ones; he rests only in the humble and the quiet. Therefore, just as little ones, glorying in him, sing, 'A little one is born to us' (Isa. IX, 6), so also he himself glories in them, saying, 'Behold, I and my children, whom the Lord has given me' (Id. V, 18). For in order that the Father might give companions to his little Son from among his own contemporaries, the glory of martyrdom began first of all from the innocence of little children; the Holy Spirit signifying by this that the kingdom of heaven belongs only to such as these (Matth. XIX, 14).

4. Si tales effici volumus, fratres, iterum atque iterum transeamus usque Bethlehem, et diligentius intueamur hoc Verbum quod caro factum est per Deum immensum, qui parvulus factus est, ut in hoc visibili et breviato Verbo discamus sapientiam Dei, quae tota humilitas facta est. In hanc quippe virtutem illa omnimoda virtus se interim totam contulit. Nihil aliud illa summa Sapientia interim scire voluit, nisi illam scilicet humilitatem, cujus se postmodum magistrum voluit profiteri. Et iste quidem, quod nimirum in suggillationem mei ipsius loquor, iste, inquam, digne et juste magister docendae humilitatis effectus est; qui, licet eam sibi non ignoraret et originalem a Matre, et naturalem a Patre, nihilominus tamen ab ipso Matris utero didicit eam ex his quae passus est. In diversorio viatorum natus est, ut hospites et peregrinos super terram, ipsius exemplo edocti, nos esse confiteremur. Ibi quoque novissimum eligens locum, in praesepio positus est, ut et nos illud Davidicum: Elegi abjectus esse in domo Dei, magis quam habitare in tabernaculis peccatorum (Psal. LXXXIII, 11), opere disceremus. Pannis involutus est, ut habentes tantum quibus tegamur, his contenti simus; per omnia paupertate materna contentus, Matrique per omnia subditus, ut totius religionis forma jam in ejus nativitate nata videretur. Beata sane simplicium fides pastorum, quae, cum invenisset infantem pannis involutum, nequaquam in illis scandalizata est ad incredulitatem, ut minora de eo sentiret, sed aedificata potius ad pietatem, ut tantae dignationi gratior fieret! Quanto namque se pro ipsis profundius humiliatam ac funditus exinanitam illa exhibuit majestas, tanto facilius et plenius, si digne de eis sentire volumus, totos eorum affectus sibi illius rapuit et vindicavit charitas.

English translation

4. If we wish to become such as these, brothers, let us again and again go over to Bethlehem, and look more diligently upon this Word made flesh through the immense God, who became a little one, so that in this visible and shortened Word we may learn the wisdom of God, which has become wholly humility. For indeed that all-encompassing power has for the time being wholly given itself over to this one virtue. For the time being that highest Wisdom willed to know nothing else but that very humility, of which it afterward willed to profess itself the teacher. And he indeed—which I say to my own reproach—he, I say, became worthily and justly the teacher appointed to teach humility; who, although he was not ignorant of it, having it as original from his Mother and natural from his Father, nevertheless learned it even from his Mother's womb, from the things which he suffered. He was born in a wayfarers' inn, so that, taught by his example, we might confess ourselves to be guests and pilgrims upon the earth. There also, choosing the lowest place, he was laid in a manger, so that we too might learn by deed that word of David: 'I have chosen to be an outcast in the house of God, rather than to dwell in the tabernacles of sinners' (Psal. LXXXIII, 11). He was wrapped in swaddling clothes, so that we, having only enough to cover us, might be content with that; content in all things with his Mother's poverty, and subject to his Mother in all things, so that the whole pattern of religion might be seen to be already born in his very birth. Blessed indeed was the simple faith of the shepherds, which, when it found the infant wrapped in swaddling clothes, was by no means scandalized by them into unbelief, so as to think less of him, but was rather built up into piety, so as to become more pleasing to so great a condescension! For the more deeply that majesty showed itself humbled and utterly emptied out for their sake, the more easily and fully—if we wish to think worthily of them—did his love seize and claim for itself the whole of their affections.

5. Fratres, et vos invenietis hodie infantem pannis involutum, et positum in praesepio altaris; videte vilitas tegminis ne offendat aut turbet obtutum vestrae fidei, veritatem reverendi corporis sub aliis rerum formis intuentis. Sicut enim mater Maria quibusdam assumentis pannorum involvit infantem, sic mater gratia dispensatoriis speciebus rerum ejusdem sacri corporis nobis obtegit veritatem; sic etiam mater sapientia aenigmatibus et figuris arcanam divini Verbi contegit majestatem; quatenus et ibi simplicitas fidei, et hic exercitium studii, meritum sibi cumulet ad salutem. Nam ego, fratres, cum vobis veritatem, quae Christus est, verbis meis enuntio, quid aliud quam Christum pannis satis vilibus involvo? Beatus tamen, cui nec in pannis istis Christus est vilior, sicut nec merces pretiosae prudenti vilescunt propter veteres quibus amiciuntur sacculos. Christus plane est, quem vobis tradere cupio, qualibuscunque sermonibus nostris, ut, juxta sermonem Petri apostoli, sanctificetis eum in cordibus vestris (I Petr. III, 15.) Suscipite in mansuetudine insitum verbum, quod potest salvare animas vestras (Jacobi I, 21). Et verbum Christi abundanter habitet in vobis, amor scilicet et memoria Verbi incarnati, quatenus tam feliciter quam fideliter decantetis: Verbum caro factum est, et habitavit in nobis (Joan. I, 14). Tota igitur pietate cogitemus Christum in pannis, quibus Mater eum operuit, ut aeterna felicitate videamus gloriam et decorem quem ei Pater induit, gloriam quasi Unigeniti a Patre, cum quo et cum Spiritu sancto honor et gloria, per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

5. Brothers, you too will find today an infant wrapped in swaddling clothes, and laid in the manger of the altar; see that the cheapness of the covering does not offend or disturb the gaze of your faith, which beholds the truth of that venerable body under other forms of things. For just as Mother Mary wrapped the infant in some scraps of cloth, so Mother Grace covers for us the truth of that same sacred body with outward forms of things appointed for the dispensation; and so too Mother Wisdom covers the hidden majesty of the divine Word with enigmas and figures; so that in the one case the simplicity of faith, and in the other the exercise of study, may each heap up its own merit unto salvation. For I, brothers, when with my words I announce to you the truth which is Christ, what else am I doing but wrapping Christ in rather cheap swaddling-cloths? Yet blessed is he to whom Christ is not thereby made cheaper, even in such cloths — just as precious wares do not become worthless in the eyes of a prudent man because of the old sacks in which they are wrapped. It is truly Christ whom I desire to hand over to you, by whatever kind of words of ours, so that, according to the saying of the apostle Peter, you may sanctify him in your hearts (I Petr. III, 15.). Receive with meekness the implanted word, which is able to save your souls (Jacobi I, 21). And let the word of Christ dwell abundantly in you — that is, the love and remembrance of the incarnate Word — so that you may sing, as happily as faithfully: The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us (Joan. I, 14). Let us therefore, with all devotion, think upon Christ in the swaddling-clothes with which his Mother covered him, so that in eternal blessedness we may see the glory and beauty with which the Father has clothed him — the glory as it were of the Only-Begotten from the Father — with whom, and with the Holy Spirit, be honor and glory, through all ages of ages. Amen.
