Introduction & Scripture references

Editorial introduction

Guerric preaches Christ crucified in the days of the Passion. He presents the cross as hidden strength that overcame the devil, and Christ's ascent of the cross as free, triumphant and fruitful for the redeemed. He warns those who bear the sign of the cross falsely and urges the brothers to crucify their vices through the fear of God and to persevere on the cross until evening. His argument includes polemic against Jews. The sermon appears under the Palm Sunday heading in the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157; the individual sermon is undated.

English translation

On the knowledge and power of the cross.

¶cols. 129-130; cols. 131-132
Latin original

1. Paulus, doctor noster in fide et veritate, si hodie veniret ad nos, puto quia non judicaret se aliquid scire inter nos, nisi Jesum Christum, et hunc crucifixum (I Cor. II, 2). In his siquidem diebus in quibus anniversaria dominicae passionis et crucis memoria solemniter celebratur, nihil, ut arbitror, convenientius praedicatur, quam Jesus Christus, et hic crucifixus. Nam et aliis quibuslibet diebus quid unquam potest praedicari fidelius? quid audiri salubrius? quid cogitari fructuosius? Quid enim tam pium fidelium affectibus, tam medicinale moribus; quid sic interficit peccata, crucifigit vitia, virtutes nutrit et roborat, sicut crucifixi memoria? Loquatur ergo Paulus inter perfectos sapientiam in mysterio absconditam; mihi, cujus imperfectum vident etiam oculi hominum, loquatur Christum crucifixum, his quidem qui pereunt stultitiam, mihi autem et his qui salvi fiunt, plane Dei virtutem Deique sapientiam; mihi prorsus altissimam atque nobilissimam philosophiam, per quam infatuatam irrideo tam mundi quam carnis sapientiam. Quam perfectum me putarem, quam provectum in sapientia, si Crucifixi vel idoneus invenirer auditor, qui factus est nobis a Deo non solum sapientia, sed et justitia, et sanctificatio, et redemptio! (Id. I, 30.) Omnino si Christo confixus es cruci, sapiens es, justus es, sanctus es, liber es. An non sapiens, qui, cum Christo exaltatus a terra, sapit et quaerit quae sursum sunt? an non justus, in quo destructum est corpus peccati, ut ultra non serviat peccato? an non sanctus, qui semetipsum exhibuit hostiam vivam, sanctam, placentem Deo? an non vere liber, quem Filius liberavit, qui de libertate conscientiae illam liberam vocem Filii sibi assumere confidit: Venit princeps mundi hujus, et in me non habet quidquam? (Joan. XIV, 30.) Vere apud Crucifixum misericordia, et copiosa apud eum redemptio, qui sic redimit Israel ex omnibus iniquitatibus ejus, ut principis hujus mundi calumnias liber evadere mereatur.

English translation

1. Paul, our teacher in faith and truth, if he came to us today, I think he would judge that he knew nothing among us except Jesus Christ, and him crucified (I Cor. II, 2). Indeed, in these days in which the yearly memory of the Lord's passion and cross is solemnly celebrated, nothing, I think, is more fittingly preached than Jesus Christ, and him crucified. For on any other days as well, what can ever be preached more faithfully? What heard more wholesomely? What thought upon more fruitfully? For what is so pious for the affections of the faithful, so medicinal for morals; what so kills sins, crucifies vices, nourishes and strengthens virtues, as the memory of the Crucified One? Let Paul, then, speak wisdom hidden in mystery among the perfect; to me, whose imperfection even the eyes of men can see, let him speak of Christ crucified — to those indeed who perish, foolishness, but to me and to those who are being saved, plainly the power of God and the wisdom of God; to me indeed the loftiest and most noble philosophy, by which, having made foolish the wisdom of both world and flesh, I laugh it to scorn. How perfect I would think myself, how advanced in wisdom, if I were found a fit hearer of the Crucified, who was made for us by God not only wisdom, but also righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption! (Id. I, 30.) Altogether, if you have been nailed with Christ to the cross, you are wise, you are righteous, you are holy, you are free. Is he not wise who, lifted up from the earth with Christ, savors and seeks the things that are above? Is he not righteous in whom the body of sin has been destroyed, so that he no longer serves sin? Is he not holy who has presented himself a living sacrifice, holy, pleasing to God? Is he not truly free whom the Son has set free, who from the freedom of conscience dares to take up for himself that free utterance of the Son: The prince of this world is coming, and he has nothing in me? (Joan. XIV, 30.) Truly with the Crucified is mercy, and with him abundant redemption, who so redeems Israel from all his iniquities that he deserves to escape free from the slanders of the prince of this world.

2. Sciat tamen, sciat quicunque est ille beatus et verus Israel, non esse hoc suae meritum perfectionis, sed divinae gratiam redemptionis; non scilicet quia peccatum non fecerit, nec inventus sit dolus in ore ejus, sed quia ille cujus haec laus propria est, Christus scilicet, purgavit peccata illius, qui per sanguinem crucis suae purgationem peccatorum faciens, ibi maxime triumphavit principatus et potestates, ubi abscondita erat fortitudo ejus. Erat abscondita, sed non perdita; quia crucifixus ex infirmitate, vivebat ex virtute Dei. Erat occulta, sed non erat otiosa; quia crucifixus veterem hominem in omnibus electis crucifigebat. Crucifigebat mundum Paulo, et Paulum mundo (Galat. VI, 14). Crucifigebat denique tyrannum hujus mundi, omnesque suae antiquae ministros tyrannidis. Hamum plane sub esca recondebat, qui sub infirmitate fortitudinem abscondebat. Ac perinde ille homicida ab initio sitiens humanum sanguinem, dum irruit in infirmitatem, incidit in virtutem: morsus dum momordit, confixus dum Crucifixum appetivit.

English translation

2. Yet let him know, let whoever is that blessed and true Israel know, that this is not the merit of his own perfection, but the grace of divine redemption; not indeed because he has committed no sin, nor has guile been found in his mouth, but because he to whom this praise properly belongs, namely Christ, has purged his sins — he who, by making purgation of sins through the blood of his cross, triumphed over principalities and powers precisely there where his strength was hidden. It was hidden, but not lost; for, crucified out of weakness, he lived by the power of God. It was concealed, but not idle; for the Crucified One was crucifying the old man in all the elect. He was crucifying the world to Paul, and Paul to the world (Galat. VI, 14). He was crucifying, in short, the tyrant of this world, and all the ministers of his ancient tyranny. He was plainly hiding the hook beneath the bait, he who was hiding strength beneath weakness. And accordingly that murderer from the beginning, thirsting for human blood, while he rushed upon weakness, fell into power: biting, he was bitten; and while he sought to seize the Crucified, he was himself pierced.

3. Gratias cruci et clavis tuis, Domine Jesu: video perforatas fauces illius draconis, ut transeant liberati, qui etiam fuerant absorpti. Et qui fiduciam habebat quod influeret Jordanis in os ejus (Job. XL, 18), fluvium, quem absorbuerat, se non minima ex parte perdidisse frendens irascitur. De illis quippe faucibus nobis isti venerunt qui hodie nobiscum nobilem ac magnificum crucis decantant triumphum. Plane de ore leonis liberati sunt, imo de ventre inferi regressi sunt. Irascatur ergo, fremat et tabescat de cujus dentibus praeda erepta est: nam Christus gratulabitur quod non gratis crucifixus est. Lugeat infernus et mors; ille morsus, ista mortua: nam coeli laetantur, et exsultat Ecclesia, quod Christus infernum spoliat, mortemque triumphat. In conversione nempe istorum triumphum suae renovavit passionis, crucisque miracula suscitavit. In istis crux refloruit, hunc fructum pretiosum nunc etiam arbor vitae tulit. Quomodo namque maneret sterilis, quae non modo rigata, sed etiam vivifica facta est sanguine Salvatoris? jam non poenitebit eum quod in palmam ascendit, cum tantum tamque pretiosum de ea fructum collegerit. Hunc quippe fructum inter alios praevidebat, cum voluntarius ad crucem properabat. Dixi, inquit, ascendam in palmam, et apprehendam fructus ejus (Cantic. VII, 8). Paucis absolvit quod voluntate passus sit, quod passione exaltatus sit, quod non sine fructu nostro passus sit. In dicto siquidem proponentis, arbitrii libertas; in ascensu, triumphi sublimitas; in apprehensione fructuum, redemptionis monstratur utilitas. Vos, insensati Judaei, clamabatis quidem, Ascende, calve; ascende, calve (IV Reg. II, 23); sed ordinationi ejus liberrimae, qui sponte decreverat ascendere, non potuit furor vester nisi servire. Ascendit crucem voluntate, triumphavit in ea potestate, fructum ex ea collegit pietate. Uno igitur opere et Judaeum irrisit, et diabolum peremit, atque christianum redemit.

English translation

3. Thanks be to your cross and your nails, Lord Jesus: I see the jaws of that dragon pierced through, so that they who had even been swallowed up might pass through, now set free. And he who had confidence that the Jordan would flow into his mouth (Job. XL, 18) now, gnashing his teeth, is enraged that he has lost no small part of the river he had swallowed. For from those jaws came to us these who today with us sing the noble and magnificent triumph of the cross. Plainly they have been freed from the mouth of the lion, indeed they have returned from the belly of hell. Let him then be enraged, let him gnash and waste away, from whose teeth the prey has been snatched away: for Christ will rejoice that he was not crucified in vain. Let hell and death mourn; the one bitten, the other dead: for the heavens rejoice, and the Church exults, that Christ despoils hell and triumphs over death. In the conversion of these, indeed, he has renewed the triumph of his passion, and raised up again the miracles of the cross. In these the cross has flowered again; this precious fruit the tree of life has borne even now. For how could it remain barren, which has been not only watered, but even made life-giving by the blood of the Savior? Now he will not regret that he climbed up into the palm tree, since he has gathered from it so great and so precious a fruit. For indeed he foresaw this fruit among others when, of his own will, he hastened to the cross. I said, he says, I will climb up into the palm tree, and I will take hold of its fruits (Cantic. VII, 8). In a few words he sums up that he suffered willingly, that he was exalted by his passion, that he did not suffer without fruit for us. For in the saying of one proposing, the freedom of will is shown; in the climbing, the sublimity of triumph; in the taking hold of the fruits, the usefulness of redemption is displayed. You, senseless Jews, cried out indeed, Go up, you baldhead; go up, you baldhead (IV Reg. II, 23); but your rage could do nothing but serve the most free ordinance of him who of his own accord had decreed to go up. He climbed the cross by his will, he triumphed on it by his power, he gathered fruit from it by his loving-kindness. By one and the same work, then, he mocked the Jew, destroyed the devil, and redeemed the Christian.

4. Dicant igitur qui redempti sunt a Domino, quos redemit de manu inimici, et de regionibus congregavit eos (Psal. CVI, 2); dicant, inquam, voce et mente magistri sui: Mihi autem absit gloriari, nisi in cruce Domini nostri Jesu Cristi (Galat. VI, 14)! in qua sapientia Dei nequam infatuavit consilium, justitia destruxit eum qui habebat mortis imperium, misericordia liberavit captivum. Merito prorsus, o sapiens gloriator, gloriaberis in cruce Domini tui, cujus liberatus es triumpho, vivificatus mysterio, justificatus exemplo, munitus signo. Sic enim justae rationis consequentia postulare videtur, ut exemplum Crucifixi ad justificationem suam imprimant moribus, qui signum crucis ad munimen sui imprimunt frontibus, et illius vivant lege, cujus armantur fide. Alioquin fallaciter miles characterem regis gestat, cujus praescriptum non observat; nec recte se tuetur illius signo, cujus non paret imperio. Vide autem quanta perversitas et abusio sit, ut inimici crucis Christi tueri se velint signo crucis Christi: dum securi sunt, luxuriantes contra pietatem crucis; cum in periculo deprehensi sunt, volentes esse defensi virtute crucis. Plane inimici crucis Christi sunt, amici ventris sui; quorum deus venter est, quorum idolum nummus est. Sciat tamen, sciat quicunque nunc fallaciter sibi Crucifixi signum usurpat; nequaquam se illo tueri poterit in illa necessitate suprema, quando jam non arbitrio hominum, sed judicio et ministerio Angelorum signabitur Thau super frontes virorum gementium et dolentium, ut discernantur salvandi a multitudine perditorum (Ezech. IX, 4-7).

English translation

4. Let those therefore say who have been redeemed by the Lord, whom he redeemed from the hand of the enemy, and gathered out of the regions (Psal. CVI, 2); let them say, I say, with the voice and mind of their teacher: But far be it from me to glory, save in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ (Galat. VI, 14)! In which the wisdom of God made foolish the counsel of the wicked one, justice destroyed him who had the empire of death, mercy freed the captive. Most deservedly indeed, O wise boaster, will you glory in the cross of your Lord, by whose triumph you were set free, by whose mystery you were given life, by whose example you were justified, by whose sign you were fortified. For thus the sequence of right reason seems to require: that those who imprint the sign of the cross on their foreheads for their protection should also imprint the example of the Crucified on their character for their justification, and should live by the law of him by whose faith they are armed. Otherwise a soldier falsely bears the mark of a king whose decree he does not observe; nor does he rightly protect himself by the sign of him whose command he does not obey. But see what great perversity and abuse it is, that enemies of the cross of Christ should wish to protect themselves by the sign of the cross of Christ: while they are secure, living wantonly against the piety of the cross; when they are caught in danger, wishing to be defended by the power of the cross. Plainly they are enemies of the cross of Christ, friends of their own belly; whose god is the belly, whose idol is money. Let him know, however, let whoever now falsely usurps to himself the sign of the Crucified know this: he will by no means be able to protect himself by it in that supreme necessity, when no longer at the discretion of men, but by the judgment and ministry of Angels, the Thau will be marked upon the foreheads of men who groan and grieve, so that those to be saved may be distinguished from the multitude of the lost (Ezech. IX, 4-7).

5. Sed et Paulus, dux ille strenuus militiae christianae, fidelis signifer, qui stigmata Crucifixi in corpore suo portabat, nunc quoque in hac verorum ac falsorum militum confusione, signo utique notabili istos ab illis discern[e]bat1, cum dicebat: Qui autem sunt Christi, carnem crucifixerunt cum vitiis et concupiscentiis (Galat. V, 24). Cauta prorsus et circumspecta definitio, ac velut formam referens de ipsius veritatis exemplari impresso. Nimirum quod impressum retinet vita, signantius exprimit lingua. Qui ergo Christo confixus erat cruci, de propriae conscientiae exemplari formam istam protulit: Qui Christi sunt, carnem crucifixerunt cum vitiis et concupiscentiis. Noverat vir multae scientiae et experientiae, multos esse aut fore qui crucifigerent concupiscentias carnis, et sinerent regnare vitia cordis; multos e contrario qui, fiducia pacati cordis, negligerent mortificationem corporis. Sed sicut nonnunquam divina justitia spiritum sibi non subditum, contumeliis carnis licet afflictae flagellat, sic plerumque corpus incrassatum recalcitrat, et nova bella spiritui jam quieto resuscitat. Eapropter vult Apostolus ut, crucifixis tam vitiis interioribus quam concupiscentiis exterioribus, mundemus nos ab omni inquinamento carnis et spiritus, perficientes sanctimoniam in timore Dei (II Cor. VII, 1). Timor nempe Dei, velut quidam clavi in altum defixi, cruci nos configit, et velut affixus justitiae continere nos facit, ut non exhibeamus membra nostra arma iniquitatis, sed justitiae; et, licet sit, non tamen regnet peccatum in nostro mortali corpore. Sane quod timor Dei comparetur configentibus clavis, sententia est David, qui ait: Confige, quasi a clavis, timore tuo carnes meas; a judiciis enim tuis timui (Psal. CXVIII, 120). Si ergo nondum praevales ut vitia exstinguas, in hoc vult te esse sollicitum Apostolus, ut crucifigas. Non ait: Qui Christi sunt, exstinxerunt vitia, quae virtus paucorum est; sed, crucifixerunt, sine quo salus nulla est, sicut, praeter crucem Christi, nulla redemptio est. Ideo namque Redemptor ad operandam et informandam salutem nostram hunc passionis modum elegit, ut redemptionis sacramentum, justificationis fieret exemplum; ut quomodo videlicet ipse similitudinem carnis peccati crucifixit, de peccato damnans peccatum, sic nos, imo multo magis nos, carnem peccatricem cruciemus, crucifigentes, etsi nondum exstinguentes in ea peccatum.

English translation

5. But Paul too, that vigorous leader of the Christian army, that faithful standard-bearer, who bore the marks of the Crucified in his own body, now also, in this confusion of true and false soldiers, distinguished1 the one from the other by a quite notable sign, when he said: But those who are Christ's have crucified the flesh with its vices and desires (Galat. V, 24). A truly cautious and circumspect definition, and one that, as it were, reproduces the form impressed from the very model of truth itself. For indeed, what life retains as impressed, the tongue expresses the more pointedly. He, then, who had been fastened with Christ to the cross, brought forth this form from the model of his own conscience: Those who are Christ's have crucified the flesh with its vices and desires. This man of great knowledge and experience knew that there are, or will be, many who crucify the desires of the flesh and yet allow the vices of the heart to reign; and many others, on the contrary, who, trusting in a heart at peace, neglect the mortification of the body. But just as divine justice sometimes scourges a spirit not subject to itself with the reproaches of the flesh, even though the flesh be already afflicted, so too the body, once fattened, often grows restive, and stirs up new wars against a spirit already at rest. For this reason the Apostle wishes that, with both the inner vices and the outer desires crucified, we should cleanse ourselves from all defilement of flesh and spirit, perfecting holiness in the fear of God (II Cor. VII, 1). For indeed the fear of God, like certain nails driven deep, fastens us to the cross, as though fixed to righteousness, makes us hold ourselves in check, so that we do not present our members as weapons of iniquity, but of righteousness; and, though sin be present, it does not reign in our mortal body. Indeed, that the fear of God should be compared to nails that fasten us is the saying of David, who says: Pierce my flesh, as with nails, with your fear; for I have feared your judgments (Psal. CXVIII, 120). If, then, you are not yet able to extinguish your vices, the Apostle wishes you to be anxious about this at least: that you crucify them. He does not say: Those who are Christ's have extinguished their vices — which is the virtue of few — but, have crucified them, without which there is no salvation, just as, apart from the cross of Christ, there is no redemption. For this is why the Redeemer chose this manner of suffering for working out and shaping our salvation: that the sacrament of redemption might become the pattern of justification; so that, just as he himself crucified the likeness of sinful flesh, for sin condemning sin, so we — indeed, all the more we — should torment our sinful flesh, crucifying it, even if we do not yet extinguish sin within it.

6. Hic recordari potes quia Moyses ad placandam iram Domini crucifixit principes Israelitarum (Num. XXV, 4-9); Josue autem, qui et Jesus, quinque reges Amorrhaeorum (Josue X, 26). Si enim iram Domini, quam meruimus, placare volumus, necesse est ut per continentiam nos cruciemus. Jesus autem noster, qui nos in terram promissionis introducturus est, vitia quinque sensuum in nobis crucifiget, imo et exstinguet; si tamen, sicut est justum, pendeamus in patibulis usque ad vesperum. Hujus enim perseverantiae in cruce Salvator de se ipso tibi curavit exemplum praebere, nolens consummari nisi in cruce, aut deponi de cruce ante vesperum tam diei ipsius quam vitae suae. Balaam dicebat, Moriatur anima mea morte justorum (Num. XXIII, 10); tu vero dic, Moriatur anima mea morte Domini mei Jesu Christi, et fiant novissima mea illius similia; ut videlicet in voluntaria cruce poenitentiae pendere merear usque ad terminum vitae. Quanta fiducia de cruce Filii, in manus Patris spiritum commendabis! imo quanta clementia Pater suscipiet quem Filius commendabit (8)2! Filius namque qui causam animae tuae semel in cruce suscepit agendam, nunquam eam agere desinit, apud Patrem semper interpellans. Vade securus, vade gratulabundus, ubi judex tuus est advocatus tuus; tantummodo signum crucis, mortificationem Jesu, quam circumfers in corpore tuo, secum deferat spiritus tuus. Dominus autem gloriae, qui pro vobis, fratres, passus, in vobis est glorificatus, dignetur vos habere socios passionis et gloriae, et gloriantes in cruce glorificet in ea claritate quam habuit apud Patrem ante initium saeculorum, et habiturus est per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

6. Here you may recall that Moses, to appease the wrath of the Lord, crucified the princes of the Israelites (Num. XXV, 4-9); and Joshua—who is also called Jesus—crucified the five kings of the Amorites (Josue X, 26). For if we wish to appease the wrath of the Lord, which we have deserved, it is necessary that we crucify ourselves through self-restraint. But our Jesus, who is to lead us into the land of promise, will crucify in us the vices of the five senses—indeed, will even extinguish them—provided only that, as is right, we hang upon the gibbets until evening. For of this perseverance on the cross the Savior took care to offer you an example in his own person, unwilling to be consummated except on the cross, or to be taken down from the cross before the evening both of that very day and of his life. Balaam said, 'Let my soul die the death of the righteous' (Num. XXIII, 10); but you should say, 'Let my soul die the death of my Lord Jesus Christ, and let my last end be like his'—that is, that I may merit to hang upon the voluntary cross of penance even to the end of life. With what confidence, from the cross of the Son, will you commend your spirit into the hands of the Father! Indeed, with what tenderness will the Father receive him whom the Son commends! (8)2 For the Son, who once on the cross undertook to plead the cause of your soul, never ceases to plead it, interceding always before the Father. Go in security, go rejoicing, where your judge is your advocate; only let your spirit carry with it the sign of the cross, the mortification of Jesus, which you bear about in your body. And may the Lord of glory, who suffered for you, brothers, and was glorified in you, deign to count you companions of his passion and his glory, and, as you glory in the cross, glorify you in that splendor which he had with the Father before the beginning of the ages, and which he will have through all ages of ages. Amen.
