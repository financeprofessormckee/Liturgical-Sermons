Editorial introduction

Guerric hears the children's Hosanna as praise prompted by the Holy Spirit. The poverty of the king riding on an ass is the sign by which the humble recognize Christ, and he warns against a lukewarm response to grace. He contrasts the Church that received Christ with the Synagogue that rejected him, then invites the brothers to nest like doves in the clefts of the rock by meditating on Christ's wounds. His argument includes polemic against the Synagogue. The sermon appears under the Palm Sunday heading in the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157; the individual sermon is undated.

Qualiter Christus benedicendus.

English translation

How Christ Is to Be Blessed.

¶cols. 137-138
Latin original

1. Hosanna filio David (Matth. XXI, 9). Vox exsultationis et salutis, vox gaudii et pietatis, vox fidei et amoris, adgratulans adventui Redemptoris, atque prophetico protestans gaudio desideratae laetitiam redemptionis. Hosanna filio David, ait haec familia David; salus est illi qui factus est ex semine David, ut salvet eos qui sunt ex fide David. Laudate, laudate, pueri, Dominum, laudate nomen Domini (Psal. XII, 1, 21); dicite: Sit nomen Domini benedictum (Job. I, 21), sit benedictus, qui venit in nomine Domini. Ex ore istorum perfecisti, o Pater, laudem Filii tui, ut incorrupto simplicis innocentiae testimonio destruas inimicum et ultorem (Psal. VIII, 3), pharisaeum et pontificem; non ultorem divinae legis, ut mentita est iniquitas sibi, sed invidiae propriae et furoris. Sed convertetur dolor ejus in caput ejus, et in verticem ipsius iniquitas ejus descendet (Psal. VII, 17). Deus laudem meam ne tacueris, ait Filius Patri; quia os peccatoris et os dolosi super me apertum est (Psal. CVIII, 2). Nihil potest negare Pater Filio; non tacuit laudem Filii vox paterna saepius audita de coelo, non tacuerunt creaturae, in tot signis et prodigiis confitentes eum auctorem naturae. Angeli testati sunt, daemones confessi sunt, ordo propheticus voce consona sibi invicem respondentes cecinerunt. Sed ista demum perfecta laus est, quam aetas illa non tacet, quae nescit adulari, et quod Spiritus suggerit, dissimulare non potest. Quid enim manifestius quam quod illa aetas a semetipsa, tam nova, tam insolita nec dicebat nec faciebat; sed Spiritus sanctus more suo per ora simplicium loquens, testimonium Filio perhibebat?

English translation

1. Hosanna to the Son of David (Matth. XXI, 9). A voice of exultation and salvation, a voice of joy and devotion, a voice of faith and love, rejoicing at the coming of the Redeemer, and with prophetic gladness proclaiming the joy of the long-desired redemption. Hosanna to the Son of David, says this household of David; salvation belongs to him who was made of the seed of David, that he might save those who are of the faith of David. Praise, praise, O children, the Lord; praise the name of the Lord (Psal. XII, 1, 21); say: Blessed be the name of the Lord (Job. I, 21); blessed be he who comes in the name of the Lord. Out of the mouths of these, O Father, you have perfected the praise of your Son, that by the incorrupt testimony of simple innocence you might destroy the enemy and the avenger (Psal. VIII, 3) — the Pharisee and the high priest; not an avenger of the divine law, as iniquity falsely claims of itself, but an avenger of his own envy and fury. But his sorrow shall be turned back upon his own head, and his iniquity shall come down upon his own crown (Psal. VII, 17). O God, do not be silent concerning my praise, says the Son to the Father; for the mouth of the sinner and the mouth of the deceitful has been opened against me (Psal. CVIII, 2). The Father can deny nothing to the Son; the Father's voice, heard many times from heaven, did not keep silent the praise of the Son; the creatures did not keep silent, confessing him, in so many signs and wonders, to be the author of nature. The angels bore witness, the demons confessed, the order of the prophets sang in harmonious voice, answering one another. But this, after all, is the perfect praise, which that age does not keep silent — an age that does not know how to flatter, and cannot hide what the Spirit suggests. For what could be plainer than that that age, of itself, neither said nor did things so new, so unheard of; but the Holy Spirit, speaking in his own manner through the mouths of the simple, was bearing witness to the Son?

2. Plane Spiritus sanctus conscius operum Christi, quid ille scilicet Christi pareret adventus, quid gaudii, quid salutis humano generi passio parturiret illius, praesaga in cordibus innocentium gaudia suscitabat, atque ad prophetandam mundo redemptionis laetitiam, simplicium sibi ministeria sensuum assumebat. Istos nempe Spiritus alloquebatur, vocans ea quae non sunt, tanquam ea quae sunt, cum per prophetam suum dicebat quae hodie per ipsum facta sunt, mandabat tunc, et nunc creata sunt. Exsulta, inquit, satis, filia Sion; jubila, filia Jerusalem: ecce Rex tuus veniet tibi justus et Salvator, ipse pauper et ascendens super asinum (Zach. IX, 9 et Matth. XXI, 5). Jam, inquit, exsulta satis, quae hactenus in tristitia fuisti; jam te satia, si tamen satiari potes, gaudio ineffabili, quod sic desiderium satiat ut amplius et felicius esurire faciat. Repleatur gaudio os tuum, et lingua tua exsultatione; cui si nec os nec lingua possunt sufficere, supereffundat jubilus quod non capit affectus. Jubila, inquit, filia Jerusalem. Beatus enim populus qui scit jubilationem (Psal. LXXXVIII, 16). Plane beatus populus qui scit et intelligit hodie sibi ineffabiliter gaudendum, quando ei Salvator advenit promissus et exspectatus ab initio saeculorum. Beatus populus qui tota alacritate devotionis hodie ei occurrit, corde simul et voce acclamans ei: Benedictus qui venit in nomine Domini (Matth. XXI, 9). In hoc siquidem benedictus est a Patre Filius, ut qui benedixerit ei, benedictionibus repleatur, non una scilicet, sed pluribus; quia nimirum benedictio quam Domino quia feneratur, cum multiplici fenore redit in caput ejus. Vae genti peccatrici, semini nequam, filiis sceleratis! ad quos spectat illa Domini querela terribilis: Non feneravi, nec feneravit mihi quisquam; omnes maledicunt mihi, dicit Dominus (Jerem. XV, 10, 11). Judaicus populus hic erat, qui in ratione dati et accepti communicare contempsit Domino, nolens benedicere Benedictum Dei Patris, ut benediceretur ab eo; sed Samaritanum, daemoniacum vocans, insanis atque blasphemis Deum insectabatur maledictis (Joan. VIII, 48, 52, 53). Maledicent illi, inquit, et tu benedices (Psal. CVIII, 28); nam ab eis benedictio quam noluerunt, elongabitur ad gentes. Benedicite, gentes, Deum nostrum, quoniam qui prior feneravit vobis, praeveniens vos in benedictionibus dulcedinis, fenerantibus sibi multiplices restituet usuras in benedictionibus beatitudinis.

English translation

2. Clearly the Holy Spirit, aware of the works of Christ — of what the coming of Christ would bring forth, what joy, what salvation his passion would bring forth for the human race — was stirring up prophetic joys in the hearts of the innocent, and was taking to himself the service of simple senses in order to prophesy to the world the gladness of redemption. Indeed the Spirit was speaking to these, calling the things that are not as though they were, when through his prophet he spoke of what is today being accomplished through him; he commanded it then, and now it has been brought into being. Rejoice greatly, he says, O daughter of Sion; shout for joy, O daughter of Jerusalem: behold, your King shall come to you, just and a Savior, himself poor and riding upon an ass (Zach. IX, 9 et Matth. XXI, 5). Now, he says, rejoice greatly, you who until now have been in sorrow; now sate yourself — if indeed you can be sated — with ineffable joy, which so satisfies desire that it makes it hunger more abundantly and more happily. Let your mouth be filled with joy, and your tongue with exultation; and if neither mouth nor tongue can suffice for this, let the shout of joy overflow with what feeling cannot contain. Shout for joy, he says, O daughter of Jerusalem. For blessed is the people that knows jubilation (Psal. LXXXVIII, 16). Truly blessed is the people that knows and understands that today it must rejoice ineffably, since the Savior has come to it, promised and awaited from the beginning of the ages. Blessed is the people that today, with the whole eagerness of devotion, goes out to meet him, acclaiming him with heart and voice together: Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord (Matth. XXI, 9). For in this the Son is blessed by the Father, so that whoever blesses him may be filled with blessings — not one only, but many; because indeed the blessing that is lent to the Lord returns upon the lender's own head with manifold interest. Woe to the sinful nation, the wicked seed, the children of wickedness! It is to them that the Lord's terrible complaint applies: I have not lent, nor has anyone lent to me; all curse me, says the Lord (Jerem. XV, 10, 11). This was the Jewish people, who scorned to share with the Lord in the account of giving and receiving, refusing to bless the Blessed One of God the Father, so that they might be blessed by him; but instead, calling him a Samaritan and possessed by a demon, they pursued God with mad and blasphemous curses (Joan. VIII, 48, 52, 53). They will curse, he says, but you will bless (Psal. CVIII, 28); for from them the blessing which they refused will be carried far off to the nations. Bless, O nations, our God, for he who first lent to you, going before you in blessings of sweetness, will restore to those who lend to him manifold interest in blessings of beatitude.

3. Ego tamen vereor, fratres mei, ne forte haec tempora teporis et infidelitatis tangat illa querela Domini: Non feneravi, nec feneravit mihi quisquam (Jerem. XV, 10); quia videlicet gratia offertur, nec suscipitur; promittitur merces operi, et vix aliquis spe illius operatur. Dominus siquidem fenerat, cum talenta servis partiens, scientiam verbi vel gratiam cujuslibet muneris ad lucrum praerogat: Domino autem fenerat jucundus homo qui miseretur et commodat, dicente Scriptura: Feneratur Domino, qui misericordiam facit proximo (Prov. XIX, 17). Imo Domino feneratur quicunque spe divinae retributionis aliquid operatur, ut dicere possit: Scio cui credidi (II Tim. I, 12), scio qui dixit: Si quid erogaveris, cum rediero reddam tibi (Luc. X, 35). Nos autem aut nihil aut ita timide gelideque feneramus ei, quasi debitor infidelis sit, aut non habeat unde reddere possit. Magnae prorsus fidei illa plebs Hebraea, quae, cum eum pauperem sedentem super asellum, et ipsum non suum, cerneret, omni tamen securitate ac devotione fenerabat ei, non solum vestimenta sua in via sternens, sed et se totam, quantum poterat, honori ejus impendens. Sed nimirum intelligebat super egenum et pauperem; quippe cui propheta signum dederat intelligendi Salvatorem, ipsam qua superbis viluit, paupertatem. Ipse, inquit, pauper, et ascendens super asinum (Zach. IX, 9). Hoc, inquit, signo poteris venientem agnoscere Regem tuum, cujus regnum non est de hoc mundo, quia scilicet, ad debellandam superbiam quae regnat in mundo, paupertatem seu humilitatem evangelizabit sicut verbo sic etiam exemplo.

English translation

3. Yet I fear, my brothers, lest perhaps these times of lukewarmness and unbelief be touched by that complaint of the Lord: "I have not lent, nor has anyone lent to me" (Jerem. XV, 10); because, that is, grace is offered and not received, a reward is promised for the work, and scarcely anyone works in hope of it. For the Lord lends when, distributing talents to his servants, he grants beforehand knowledge of the word or the grace of any gift, for profit; but a man who is kind, who shows mercy and lends, lends to the Lord, as Scripture says: "He who shows mercy to his neighbor lends to the Lord" (Prov. XIX, 17). Indeed, whoever does anything in hope of divine repayment lends to the Lord, so that he can say: "I know whom I have believed" (II Tim. I, 12), "I know him who said: If you spend anything, when I return I will repay you" (Luc. X, 35). But we either lend nothing to him, or lend so timidly and coldly, as if he were an unfaithful debtor, or had nothing with which to repay. Truly great was the faith of that Hebrew people, who, although they saw him sitting poor upon a young ass, and not even his own, nevertheless lent to him with complete confidence and devotion, not only spreading their garments in the way, but spending their whole selves, as much as they could, in his honor. For they understood, no doubt, concerning the needy and the poor one; since the prophet had given them a sign by which to recognize the Savior—namely, that very poverty for which the proud held him cheap. "He himself," it says, "poor, and riding upon an ass" (Zach. IX, 9). By this sign, it says, you will be able to recognize your King as he comes, whose kingdom is not of this world, because, to overthrow the pride that reigns in the world, he would preach poverty or humility as much by example as by word.

4. Beata itaque filia Sion, quae humilitatem tanquam armaturam coelestem, tanquam insigne regium didicit venerari. Misera mater ejus infidelis illa Sion, quae cum humilem vidit, fastidivit, cum honoratum vidit, invidit. Beata, inquam, Ecclesia primitivorum, quae tam fideliter agnovit, tamque gratanter suscepit venientem in nomine Domini; infelix Synagoga perfidorum, quae, venturum in suo nomine parata suscipere, illius qui Patris gloriam quaerebat cruciabatur honore. Increpa, inquiunt, discipulos tuos; quasi scilicet aut illorum simplicitas posset adulari, aut ipsius puritas vanis posset laudibus delectari. Dico, inquit, vobis, quia si hi tacuerint, lapides clamabunt (Luc. XIX, 39, 40); quia Deus laudem meam non tacebit. Prorsus ita est: si tacuerint, lapides clamabunt; quia tempore passionis isti tacuerunt, sed lapides clamaverunt, dum in testimonium et laudem morientis, petrae scissae sunt, et monumenta aperta sunt (Matth. XXVII, 51, 52). Prorsus ita est, quia modo tacente Synagoga, juxta illud: Nocte tacere feci matrem tuam (Ose. IV, 5), clamat vivis ex lapidibus Gentium Ecclesia, clamant plane lapides, de quibus suscitavit filios Abrahae qui potens est. Laudate, inquit propheta, habitatores petrae, de vertice montium clamabunt (Isai. XLII, 11). Ecce hodie in foraminibus petrae, in cavernis maceriae vox columbae resonat, clamantis et dicentis: Hosanna filio David; benedictus qui venit in nomine Domini (Matth. XXI, 9).

English translation

4. Blessed, then, is the daughter of Sion, who has learned to venerate humility as a heavenly armor, as a royal insignia. Wretched is her unbelieving mother, that Sion, who, when she saw him humble, disdained him, and when she saw him honored, envied him. Blessed, I say, is the Church of the firstborn, which so faithfully recognized and so gratefully received him who came in the name of the Lord; unhappy is the Synagogue of the faithless, which, prepared to receive one coming in his own name, was tormented by the honor paid to him who sought the glory of the Father. "Rebuke," they say, "your disciples"—as though either their simplicity could flatter, or his purity could be delighted by empty praises. "I tell you," he says, "that if these are silent, the stones will cry out" (Luc. XIX, 39, 40); because God will not be silent about my praise. And indeed it is so: if they are silent, the stones will cry out; for at the time of the Passion these men were silent, but the stones cried out, when, in testimony and praise of the dying one, the rocks were split and the tombs were opened (Matth. XXVII, 51, 52). And indeed it is so, because now, while the Synagogue is silent—according to that word: "I have made your mother be silent at night" (Ose. IV, 5)—the Church of the Gentiles cries out from living stones; truly the stones cry out, from which he who is able raised up sons of Abraham. "Praise," says the prophet, "you inhabitants of the rock, they will cry out from the top of the mountains" (Isai. XLII, 11). Behold, today in the clefts of the rock, in the hollows of the wall, the voice of the dove resounds, crying out and saying: "Hosanna to the Son of David; blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord" (Matth. XXI, 9).

5. Benedictus qui, ut nidificare possem in foraminibus petrae, manus, pedes et latus perforari sibi tulit, et se mihi totum aperuit, ut ingrediar in locum tabernaculi admirabilis, et protegar in abscondito tabernaculi sui. Opportunum quidem petra refugium erinaciis, sed et habitatio grata columbis, cujus foramina tot vulneribus toto fere corpore patentia, et veniam offerunt reis, et gratiam conferunt justis. Imo vero tuta habitatio, fratres mei, turrisque fortitudinis a facie inimici, pia ac sedula meditatione Christi Domini vulneribus immorari, fideque et amore Crucifixi, animam ab aestu carnis, a turbine saeculi, ab impetu diaboli contutari. Super omnem gloriam mundi protectio hujus tabernaculi, videlicet in umbraculum diei ab aestu, in securitatem et absconsionem a turbine et a pluvia, ut per diem sol non urat te prosperitate, nec turbo moveat in tempestate. Ingredere igitur in petram, o homo, abscondere in fossa humo (Isai. II, 10), pone tibi latibulum in Crucifixo. Ipse petra, ipse humus, quia Deus et homo; ipse petra forata, humus fossa, quia foderunt manus meas, inquit, et pedes meos (Psal. XXI, 17). Abscondere, inquit, in fossa humo a facie timoris Domini (Isai. II, 10): hoc est, ab ipso fuge ad ipsum, a judice ad redemptorem, a tribunali ad crucem, a justo ad misericordem; ab eo qui percutiet terram virga oris sui, ad eum qui inebriat terram stillicidiis cruoris sui; ab eo qui spiritu labiorum suorum interficiet impium, ad eum qui sanguine vulnerum suorum vivificat exstinctum. Quinimo non ad ipsum tantum, sed in ipsum fuge, in foramina petrae ingredere, in fossa humo abscondere, in ipsis manibus foratis, in fosso latere te ipsum reconde. Vulnus enim in latere Christi, quid nisi ostium in latere Arcae salvandis a facie diluvii? Illud tamen figura, istud autem veritas, ubi non modo servatur vita mortalis, sed recuperatur immortalis. Ideo quippe latus suum pius et misericors aperuit, ut cruor te vulneris vivificet, calor corporis refocillet, spiritus cordis quasi patenti et libero meatu aspiret. Ibi tuto latebis, donec traneat iniquitas; ibi nequaquam algebis, eo quod in visceribus Christi non frigescat charitas; ibi deliciis afflues, ibi gaudiis superefflues; vel tunc demum cum a vita capitis tua et omnium membrorum corporis ejus absorpta fuerit mortalitas.

English translation

5. Blessed is he who, so that I might build a nest in the clefts of the rock, allowed his hands, feet, and side to be pierced, and opened himself wholly to me, that I might enter into the place of the wonderful tabernacle, and be sheltered in the hidden place of his tabernacle. The rock is indeed a fitting refuge for hedgehogs, but it is also a pleasing dwelling for doves, and its openings—laid open by so many wounds over almost the whole body—both offer pardon to the guilty and bestow grace upon the just. Indeed, my brothers, it is a safe dwelling, a tower of strength against the face of the enemy, to dwell with pious and diligent meditation upon the wounds of the Lord Christ, and by faith and love of the Crucified, to keep the soul safe from the heat of the flesh, from the whirlwind of the world, from the assault of the devil. Above all the glory of the world is the protection of this tabernacle, namely, a shelter by day from the heat, a security and hiding place from whirlwind and rain, so that the sun of prosperity may not burn you by day, nor the whirlwind disturb you in time of storm. Enter, therefore, into the rock, O man; hide yourself in the dug earth (Isai. II, 10); make for yourself a hiding place in the Crucified One. He himself is the rock, he himself the earth, because he is God and man; he himself is the pierced rock, the dug earth, for he says, "they have dug through my hands and my feet" (Psal. XXI, 17). "Hide yourself," he says, "in the dug earth from before the fear of the Lord" (Isai. II, 10): that is, flee from him to him, from the judge to the redeemer, from the tribunal to the cross, from the just one to the merciful one; from him who shall strike the earth with the rod of his mouth, to him who makes the earth drunk with the drippings of his blood; from him who shall slay the wicked with the breath of his lips, to him who gives life to the dead by the blood of his wounds. Nay, flee not only to him, but into him; enter into the clefts of the rock, hide yourself in the dug earth; conceal yourself in his very pierced hands, in his opened side. For what is the wound in the side of Christ but the door in the side of the Ark for those to be saved from the face of the flood? That, however, was a figure, but this is the truth, where not only is mortal life preserved, but immortal life is recovered. For this reason indeed the pious and merciful one opened his side, so that the blood of the wound might give you life, the warmth of the body might refresh you, and the breath of the heart might breathe upon you, as it were, through an open and free passage. There you will lie hidden in safety until iniquity passes away; there you will never grow cold, because love does not grow cold in the heart of Christ; there you will abound in delights, there you will overflow with joys—or rather, then at last, when your mortality, and that of all the members of his body, shall have been swallowed up by the life of the Head.

6. Merito itaque columba Christi, formosa Christi, cui ipsius vulnera tam tuta quam grata ad nidificandum paravere foramina, laudes ipsius utique hodie laetabunda decantat, ac de passionis memoria vel imitatione, de vulnerum meditatione, tanquam de foraminibus petrae vox suavis in auribus sponsi resonat. Caeterum vobis, fratres mei, qui tanto interius nidificastis in foraminibus petrae, quanto secretius vivitis in Christo, et vita vestra abscondita est cum Christo in Deo (Coloss. III, 3); vobis incumbit omnino ut quorum est conversatio quietior et tutior, sit etiam devotio dulcior; praesertim hodie, cum et recursus temporis et repraesentatio operis velut interesse nos facit illi gaudio solemni quo susceptus est in Jerusalem. Benedictus qui venit in nomine Domini; ipsi benedictio, regnum et imperium, qui est super omnia Deus benedictus in saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

6. Rightly, then, does the dove of Christ, the fair one of Christ—for whom his very wounds have prepared clefts as safe as they are pleasant for nesting—today joyfully sing forth his praises; and from the memory or imitation of the passion, from meditation on the wounds, as it were from the clefts of the rock, a sweet voice resounds in the ears of the Bridegroom. But as for you, my brothers, who have nested the more inwardly in the clefts of the rock the more secretly you live in Christ, and whose life is hidden with Christ in God (Coloss. III, 3): it falls upon you entirely that, since your way of life is quieter and safer, your devotion should also be sweeter—especially today, when both the return of the season and the re-enactment of the deed make us, as it were, present at that solemn joy with which he was received into Jerusalem. Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord; to him be blessing, kingdom, and dominion, who is over all things, God blessed for ever and ever. Amen.
