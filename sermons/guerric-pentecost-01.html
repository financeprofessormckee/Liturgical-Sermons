Introduction & Scripture references

Editorial introduction

Guerric of Igny presents the Father's gift of the Son for redemption and of the Holy Spirit for adoption as signs of divine generosity. He contrasts that generosity with human ingratitude, urging his hearers to hope for others' repentance while avoiding both despair about others and presumption about themselves. Fear made chaste by love guards spiritual joy. The Spirit's outpouring at Pentecost becomes an invitation to thirst for God's gifts; Guerric then warns against spiritual loss of appetite, slander and the passions that conflict with sharing Christ's table. The individual sermon is undated; its collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157 and is read here from the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The historical heading names the feast of Pentecost, and Guerric recalls the Spirit given to the Apostles on this day.

1. Ineffabilis Deus, ineffabilis misericordia ejus. Secundum nomen ejus, ita et opus ejus. Plane ineffabilis divinae circa nos dignatio charitatis. Parum erat Patri, tradidisse Filium, ut redimeret servum; nisi daret et Spiritum sanctum, quo servum adoptaret in Filium. Dedit filium in pretium redemptionis; dedit Spiritum in privilegium adoptationis: se denique totum servat haereditatem adoptatis. O Deum, si fas est dici, prodigum sui, prae desiderio hominis! An non prodigum, qui non solum sua, sed et se ipsum impendit, ut hominem recuperaret, non tam sibi, quam homini ipsi? an non prodigum, qui, sicut proprio Filio suo non pepercit, sed pro nobis omnibus tradidit illum (Rom. viii, 32), sic nec Spiritui sancto pepercit, ut ita loquar, sed nova et mira largitate super omnem carnem effudit illum? Multum quidem effusus sui prodigus ille filius, qui tam patrimonium, quam se ipsum donavit meretricibus; sed multo effusior pater in recuperationem perditi filii, quam ille in perditionem sui (Luc. v)2; si tamen ulla debet esse comparatio gratiae et pecuniae, spiritus et carnis, Dei et hominis: Vide enim quanta largitate toto orbe gratia Spiritus effusa sit, non solum justis confirmandis, sed et peccatoribus justificandis: quomodo ubique gentium, Spiritu creante novum genus hominum, renovata sit facies terrae: imo quanta quotidie mutatio fiat per dexteram Excelsi, ut repente perditissimi hominum publicani et meretrices, justos quam plurimos praecedant in regno Dei, et novissimi fiant primi. Prorsus non sicut delictum, ita et donum: quia ubi abundavit delictum, ibi superabundans gratia (Rom. v, 15, 20): non solum donat delicta, sed etiam virtutum accumulat merita; et sublimius restituit lapsos redemptio, quam constituerat prima creatio. Sane in his omnibus quanto mirabilior Dei gratia praedicatur, tanto damnabilior hominis duritia convincitur, si gratiam vel oblatam recuset, vel acceptam non servet. Cui enim non offertur? super quem non effulget lumen illius? quis se abscondit a calore ejus? Neque enim reliquit se Deus sine testimonio apud conscientias hominum, illustrans eos splendore veritatis, et fovens calore bonitatis; cum et lux vera illuminet omnem hominem venientem in hunc mundum (Joan. i, 9), et Deus faciat oriri solem suum super bonos et malos, et pluat super justos et injustos (Matth. v, 45). Sed vae illis qui rebelles sunt lumini, et resistunt Spiritui sancto, nec acquiescunt veritati quam intelligunt; et sicut lutum ex calore solis, ita ex bonitate et beneficiis Dei duriores fiunt, audacter etiam provocantes eum, cum ipse dederit omnia in manibus eorum!

English translation

1. God is beyond all telling; his mercy is beyond all telling. As is his name, so also is his work. Beyond all telling indeed is the condescension of divine charity towards us. It was too little for the Father to have handed over the Son to redeem the servant, unless he also gave the Holy Spirit, through whom he might adopt the servant as a son. He gave the Son as the price of redemption; he gave the Spirit as the privilege of adoption; finally, he keeps his whole self as the inheritance of those adopted. O God, prodigal of himself, if one may say so, in his longing for man! Is he not prodigal, who spends not only what is his but his very self, to recover man, not so much for himself as for man's own sake? Is he not prodigal, who, just as he did not spare his own Son but handed him over for us all (Rom. viii, 32), likewise did not spare the Holy Spirit, so to speak, but poured him out upon all flesh with a new and wondrous generosity? That prodigal son indeed poured himself out lavishly, giving both his patrimony and himself to harlots; but the father was far more lavish in recovering his lost son than the son had been in losing himself (Luc. v)2, if indeed grace and money, spirit and flesh, God and man ought to be compared at all. For see how generously the grace of the Spirit has been poured out throughout the world, not only to strengthen the just but also to justify sinners; how everywhere among the nations the face of the earth has been renewed, as the Spirit creates a new race of men; indeed, how great a change is wrought daily by the right hand of the Most High, so that suddenly the most abandoned of men, publicans and harlots, go before very many of the just into the kingdom of God, and the last become first. Truly, the gift is not like the trespass, for where the trespass abounded, grace is superabundant (Rom. v, 15, 20): it not only forgives trespasses but heaps up the merits of virtues; and redemption restores the fallen to a higher state than the first creation had established. In all this, surely, the more wondrous God's grace is proclaimed to be, the more deserving of condemnation man's hardness is proved to be, if he either refuses grace when offered or fails to keep it once received. For to whom is it not offered? Upon whom does its light not shine? Who hides himself from its warmth? For God has not left himself without witness in men's consciences, enlightening them with the splendour of truth and warming them with the heat of goodness, since the true light enlightens every man coming into this world (Joan. i, 9), and God makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust (Matth. v, 45). But woe to those who rebel against the light and resist the Holy Spirit, who will not assent to the truth they understand; and who, like clay under the sun's heat, grow harder through God's goodness and benefits, even boldly provoking him, although he himself has put all things into their hands!

2. Verum quid ad nos de his qui foris sunt? Ad vos potius nobis est sermo, qui spiritum adoptionis filiorum accepistis, habentes eumdem spiritum in signum adoptionis et pignus haereditatis; qui velut quidam character insignis, vasa misericordiae a vasis irae discernit. Quanquam de salute nostra, vel magis de spe salutis, ea nobis modestia gaudendum sit, ut quod in illis dolemus, adhuc de nobis pro nostra mutabilitate timeamus. Sicut enim de illis desperare impium, ita de nobis praesumere est temerarium, cum tam de nobis quam de illis, qualiscunque sit scientia praesentium, nulla possit esse praescientia futurorum; nec levis sit injuria summae potestati, penes quam est arbitrium vitae et mortis, si vel de illis tam crudeliter, vel de nobis tam audacter praejudicetur ejus judiciis. Denique de massa eorum sumus assumpti, et qui paulo ante eramus, sicut et illi, natura et vita filii irae, repente facti sumus filii gratiae. Nos ergo simus illis exemplum spei ad poenitentiam; ipsi nobis, timoris ad perseverantiam. In nobis se misericordia commendat, ut ametur; in illis se judicium exserit ut timeatur. Misericordiam et judicium cantabo tibi, Domine (Psal. c, 1): pro ista gratias agens, pro illo preces fundens, pro ista gratus et laetus, pro illo timoratus et cautus: de utroque eruditus exsultabo tibi cum tremore, quia tunc ingrediar in veritate tua, si ita laetetur cor meum ut timeat nomen tuum. Neque enim timor iste, quem amor castum facit, gaudium tollit, sed custodit; non destruit, sed instruit; non inamaricat, sed condit: ut tanto sit durabilius, quanto modestius; tanto verius, quanto severius; tanto dulcius, quanto sanctius. O castum et fidele gaudium! quam fidelis est illa de te sententia Sapientis: Non est census super salutem corporis, et non est oblectatio super gaudium cordis! (Eccli. xxx, 16) Hoc utique gaudio non est gaudere impiis, quorum fatuos risus et frivola gaudia Sapiens detestatur et increpat. Risum, inquit, reputavi errorem, et gaudio dixi: Quid frustra deciperis? (Eccle. ii, 2) Risus dolore miscebitur, et extrema gaudii luctus occupat (Prov. xiv, 13).

English translation

2. But what have we to do with those who are outside? Our discourse is addressed rather to you, who have received the spirit of adoption as sons, possessing that same spirit as the sign of adoption and pledge of the inheritance; like a distinctive seal, he distinguishes vessels of mercy from vessels of wrath. Yet we must rejoice in our salvation, or rather in the hope of salvation, with such restraint that, because of our own changeableness, we still fear for ourselves what we lament in them. For just as it is impious to despair of them, so it is rash to presume concerning ourselves, since, whatever knowledge there may be of present things, there can be no foreknowledge of things to come, either concerning ourselves or concerning them; nor is it a slight affront to the supreme power, in whose hands lies the determination of life and death, if his judgments are prejudged so cruelly concerning them or so boldly concerning ourselves. After all, we have been taken from their same mass, and we who a little while ago, like them, were children of wrath by nature and manner of life have suddenly become children of grace. Let us therefore be for them an example of hope leading to repentance; let them be for us an example of fear leading to perseverance. In us mercy commends itself, that it may be loved; in them judgment shows itself, that it may be feared. I will sing to you of mercy and judgment, O Lord (Psal. c, 1): giving thanks for the one, pouring out prayers concerning the other; grateful and glad for the one, fearful and watchful concerning the other. Instructed by both, I will exult before you with trembling, for I shall walk in your truth when my heart rejoices in such a way as to fear your name. For this fear, which love makes chaste, does not take joy away but guards it; does not destroy it but builds it up; does not embitter it but seasons it, so that it is the more lasting for being more restrained, the truer for being more serious, the sweeter for being holier. O chaste and faithful joy! How true is the Wise Man's saying about you: There is no wealth above bodily health, and no delight above joy of heart! (Eccli. xxx, 16) It is not for the impious to rejoice with this joy: the Wise Man detests and rebukes their foolish laughter and frivolous joys. Laughter, he says, I reckoned an error, and to joy I said: Why are you vainly deceived? (Eccle. ii, 2) Laughter will be mingled with sorrow, and mourning takes possession of the end of joy (Prov. xiv, 13).

English translation

3. O good Jesus, how different is that joy of yours, with which you meanwhile console those who renounce that false and deceitful joy! How much better is your mercy than lives! How much better is one day in your courts than thousands! How much happier you make your poor through your very poverty than the world could make them with any abundance of its own, where whatever flows in flows away, and makes whoever clings to it flow away with it! That poor household of Christ abounded in other delights, flooded by the rushing river that gladdens the city of God, when on this very day the Spirit, like a torrent, filled the whole house where the Apostles were sitting (Act. ii, 2.). For divine Truth was fulfilling what he had promised through the prophet: Behold, I turn towards you as a river of peace, and as an overflowing torrent of the glory of the nations (Isai. lxvi, 12.3 How abundantly he flowed upon them, he who flowed into them within in such abundance! And how much flowed forth from those out of whose innermost being flowed rivers of living water! Not only did the benevolence of charity flow from their hearts, but from their mouths also flowed the force of a torrent of speech, which none of their adversaries could resist or contradict, as it is said of Stephen that they could not resist the wisdom and the Spirit who spoke (Act. vi, 10).

Si quis, inquit, sitit, veniat et bibat (Joan. vii, 37). O affluens liberalitas Dei! o indeficiens largitas divinae bonitatis! Spiritum, cujus hodie primitias dedit Apostolis, offert universis. Ecce personas non accipit, conditiones non discernit, merita non requirit: tantum sitire quis noverit, venire velit. English translation

4. Meanwhile, brothers, your Comforter invites you to joys of this kind. With this torrent of his delight he longs to give drink to the thirsty minds of those who love him. If anyone thirsts, he says, let him come and drink (Joan. vii, 37). O overflowing generosity of God! O unfailing bounty of divine goodness! The Spirit whose firstfruits he gave to the Apostles today he offers to everyone. He opens his treasury, the fountain of living water, to men and beasts alike, as though he too were debtor to all, wise and unwise. All you who thirst, he says, come to the waters (Isai. lv, 1). See, he shows no partiality, distinguishes no ranks, requires no merits: let a man only know how to thirst and be willing to come. For grace does not admit those who have lost their appetite; but as it fills the hungry with good things, so it sends the rich away empty. O loss of appetite, moth of hearts, rust of minds, most grievous sickness of souls, which makes them loathe the good word of God, despise the heavenly gift, and tire of manna for the sake of the flesh pots! For what plague is so deadly, what sickness so lethal, as to make a man so forgetful of his salvation that he approaches the very gates of death laughing and falsely secure? But how, I ask, has this plague infected Christ's sheepfolds so widely and invaded his flocks, that to so many of the Lord's flock even the pasture where they have been placed seems a place of horror and vast solitude, and in the richest pastures, amid green grass, they miserably perish, sick with loss of appetite? Have they not, I ask, tasted the heavenly gift and become sharers in the Holy Spirit; have they not also tasted the good word of God and the powers of the age to come? (Hebr. vi, 5.)4 For if they have not tasted the good word of God, how did their hearts so often pour forth a good word, when their lips poured forth a hymn from the remembrance of his abundant sweetness? But now they attend the divine praises and doze; or turn over idle or even harmful thoughts in their minds. They sit at their books and yawn; they hear a word of exhortation and find even listening a burden; they move from pasture to pasture and tire of the one as much as the other; they are continually surrounded by dishes of life, and die of hunger. How then, after that happy experience and sweet taste of heavenly sweetness, have such forgetfulness, such neglect of the good and such sickness of mind crept in? All that remains to them is that cry of lament, if indeed they are willing to lament: I have been struck down like grass, and my heart has withered, because I have forgotten to eat my bread (Psal. ci, 5. They were indeed running well: who has bewitched them into turning back? They began in the Spirit: how are they now being brought to completion in the flesh? They used to feed in delight: how are they now perishing on the roads?

English translation

5. Let them themselves consider, I beg, whether perhaps the enemy, who is accustomed to sow his tares over the householder's good seed, has, after that sweet food of Christ, stained their throats with his gall, and thereby blotted out not only the desire but even the memory of their former taste. Indeed, the Apostle at once warns those who are like this: You cannot drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons. You cannot share in the table of the Lord and the table of demons (I Cor. x, 20, 21)4. Does he not seem to you to have drunk from the cup of demons and even to be drunk with it, who is swept away by the fury of lust, anger, impatience or similar passions? To speak of things which are now more often treated as customary than as sinful, I think that he too shares in the table of demons, feeding them and being fed by them, whose mouth abounds in malice, whose tongue contrives deceit, who sits and speaks against his brother and sets a stumbling block against his mother's son; who, even if he does not slander, gladly listens to a slanderer; who with the buffoonery of foolish talk makes his hearers dissolve in loud laughter. Let such a man consider, then, whether it is fitting that, after wallowing as though in sacrificial blood5 and the filth of demons, he should at once be admitted to the table of Christ and the Angels. But far be it from me, my brothers, in saying this to charge with wickedness your innocence, in which I take very great delight. I say these things so that you may be saved and, made more watchful by the example of others and washing your hands in the blood of the sinner, may take care with all vigilance to preserve the grace of the Holy Spirit which others lose through negligence, to the praise and glory of its giver himself, Jesus Christ our Lord, who lives and reigns for ever and ever. Amen.
