Introduction & Scripture references

Editorial introduction

Guerric reads Zechariah's vision of two olive trees beside the golden lampstand as Peter and Paul, sons of the oil of splendor who stand before the Lord. Christ is the lampstand, golden by substance, while believers are gilded by grace. Guerric reminds the brothers, who share one property and one house, to keep the fervor of their first profession, and reads the oil of splendor as the Holy Spirit, who anoints and enlightens. The sermon appears under the heading for the Apostles Peter and Paul in the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157; the individual sermon is undated.

¶cols. 177-178
Latin original

1. Et respondi, inquit Zacharias, et dixi ad angelum qui loquebatur in me: Quid sunt duae olivae istae ad dexteram candelabri et ad sinistram ejus? Isti sunt, inquit, duo filii olei splendoris, qui assistunt Dominatori universae terrae (Zach. IV, 4, 11, 14). De candelabro quod viderat totum aureum, et septem lucernas ejus super illud intellexerat Propheta, sed nec nobis dubitare jam licet, cum in lumine ejus videamus lumen. Hoc lumen cum alia demonstret, seipsum ignorari non patitur, sed testimonium perhibens de seipso loquitur: Ego sum lux mundi (Joan. VIII, 12). Lux ex divinitate, candelabrum ex humanitate. Et ipsum totum aureum, non auratum; quia sive divinitas, sive sapientia, sive charitas auro significetur, haec omnia Christus non per participationem, sed per substantiam. Nos cum simus natura plumbei, vel magis lutei, gratia et participatione ipsius, non aurum vel aurei, sed ut multum aurati. Septem lucernae super ipsum, septem spiritus, septem oculi Domini, qui discurrunt in universam terram. De his ergo Propheta certus, de duabus olivis quidnam sint percunctatur, auditque ab angelo qui in eo loquebatur: Isti sunt duo filii olei splendoris, qui assistunt Dominatori universae terrae (Zach. IV, 14). Interpretentur alii ut volunt aut possunt, unusquisque pro sensu suo: sive Enoch et Eliam, qui apud Deum in secreto vivunt; sive Moysen et Eliam, qui cum eo in monte visi sunt; sive duos ordines praedicatorum, veteris scilicet ac novi Testamenti: ut qui Veteris, ad sinistram sint propter promissa temporalia; qui Novi, ad dexteram, propter aeterna: ego quidquid a regula fidei non discrepat, aut a contextu lectionis non nimie dissonat, suscipio, veneror, amplector.

English translation

1. "And I answered," says Zechariah, "and I said to the angel who spoke in me: What are these two olive trees at the right hand of the lampstand, and at the left thereof? These," he says, "are the two sons of the oil of splendor, who stand before the Ruler of the whole earth" (Zach. IV, 4, 11, 14). The Prophet had understood the lampstand which he had seen, all of gold, and its seven lamps upon it; but we are no longer permitted to doubt, since in his light we see light. This light, though it shows other things, does not allow itself to go unknown, but bears witness of itself and says: "I am the light of the world" (Joan. VIII, 12). Light from his divinity, lampstand from his humanity. And it itself is wholly gold, not gilded; because whether divinity, or wisdom, or charity is signified by gold, Christ is all these, not by participation, but by substance. We, since by nature we are leaden, or rather of clay, by grace and participation in him are not gold or golden, but at most gilded. The seven lamps upon him are the seven spirits, the seven eyes of the Lord, which run through the whole earth. Concerning these, then, the Prophet, being certain, inquires what the two olive trees might be, and hears from the angel who was speaking in him: "These are the two sons of the oil of splendor, who stand before the Ruler of the whole earth" (Zach. IV, 14). Let others interpret as they will or can, each according to his own understanding: whether Enoch and Elijah, who live with God in secret; or Moses and Elijah, who were seen with him on the mountain; or the two orders of preachers, namely of the Old and the New Testament—so that those of the Old are on the left on account of temporal promises, and those of the New are on the right on account of eternal ones: I accept, venerate, and embrace whatever does not disagree with the rule of faith, nor conflict excessively with the context of the reading.

2. Puto tamen, sine praejudicio melioris intellectus, Petrum et Paulum non absurde posse intelligi: imo absurdum videri, qui hodie aliud interpretari velit, cum hodie nihil aliud cantet aut tractet Ecclesia ubique terrarum, quam praeconia Patrum doctorumque suorum. Nam etsi aliis possit aptari, puto quia istis specialius congruit, seu illud quod tanquam duae olivae circa candelabrum a Propheta videntur, seu quod duo filii esse olei splendoris ab angelo memorantur. Et alterum quidem altero exponitur, visio scilicet prophetica interpretatione angelica: ut inde intelligantur olivae, quia filii olei splendoris; inde circa candelabrum, quia Domino assistunt. Quia enim ex oleo Spiritus sancti sunt regenerati, hodieque florent et fructificant sicut oliva fructifera in domo Domini, etiam oleastro gentili, qui in ipsos insertus est, impertientes pinguedinis suae succum ac dulcedinis, gratias illis agat sterilis dudum et amara gentilitas; et si non ab ipsis, tamen per ipsos est, quod nunc tam copioso, tam pretioso fructu per orbem abundat.

English translation

2. I think, however, without prejudice to a better understanding, that Peter and Paul can not unreasonably be understood here; indeed it would seem unreasonable for anyone today to wish to interpret it otherwise, since today the Church throughout the whole world sings or treats of nothing else than the praises of her Fathers and teachers. For even though it could be fitted to others, I think that it applies more specially to these two, whether as regards what is seen by the Prophet as two olive trees around the lampstand, or what is called by the angel the two sons of the oil of splendor. And indeed the one is explained by the other—the prophetic vision, that is, by the angelic interpretation—so that from it we understand them to be olive trees because they are sons of the oil of splendor, and around the lampstand, because they stand before the Lord. For since they were regenerated from the oil of the Holy Spirit, and today they flourish and bear fruit like a fruitful olive tree in the house of the Lord, even the wild olive of the Gentiles, which has been grafted into them, receiving from them the sap of their richness and sweetness—let the Gentile world, once barren and bitter, give thanks to them; and even if it is not from them, yet it is through them that there now abounds throughout the world so abundant, so precious a fruit.

3. Vobis tamen, fratres, quibus sicut substantia et domus una, ita cor unum et anima una: vobis, inquam, specialius in ipsis censeo gloriandum, qui sicut novella olivarum de eorum radice traduxistis non solum fidei succum, sed et formam vitae atque ordinis exemplum. Quod si novella olivarum vos, utique et filii olei vos, filii uncti, qui est Christus Dei; diceturque et de vobis, cum hodie ad altare accedetis: Filii tui sicut novellae olivarum, in circuitu mensae tuae (Psal. CXXVII, 3). O novella olivarum nobilium! o generosum germen tam pinguium, tamque feracium arborum! semper, obsecro, recordare de qua pullulaveris stirpe, de qua succreveris radice, ne unquam sterilitas operum, aut amaritudo morum te degenerem arguat, aut, quod absit, inutilem ignibus addicat. Cave, quaeso, notam infelicium temporum, de quibus meministi prophetatum: Mentietur opus olivae, et arva non afferent cibum (Habac. III, 17). Duo tamen praecipue tibi existimo cavenda: ne aut per teporem fias sicut oliva projiciens florem suum, sicut legis in Propheta, ut videlicet fructum quem in initiis novitii fervoris promittebas non facias; aut cum feceris, ne per superbiam perdas, et dicat tibi Jeremias: Olivam uberem, pulchram, fructiferam, speciosam vocavit Dominus nomen tuum: sed heu! ad vocem loquelae grandis exarsit ignis in ea, et combusta sunt omnia fruteta ejus (Jerem. XI, 16). Confidimus autem de vobis meliora, dilectissimi, tametsi ita loquamur. Confidimus quia oleum splendoris, vel magis olei splendor, cujus estis filii, sicut et patres nostri Apostoli, unctione sua docebit vos de omnibus: nec solum deterget torporem, quia oleum est; sed et discutiet errorem, quia splendor est.

English translation

3. Yet to you, brothers, for whom, just as there is one property and one house, so there is one heart and one soul: to you, I say, I judge it belongs more especially to glory in them, you who, like young shoots of the olive, have derived from their root not only the sap of faith, but also the pattern of life and the example of your order. But if you are young shoots of the olive trees, then surely you too are sons of the oil, sons of the Anointed, who is the Christ of God; and it shall be said of you also, when today you approach the altar: "Your children are like young olive plants round about your table" (Psal. CXXVII, 3). O noble young shoots of olive trees! O generous offspring of trees so rich and so fruitful! Always, I beg you, remember from what stock you have sprung, from what root you have grown, lest ever the barrenness of works, or the bitterness of conduct, convict you of degeneracy, or—God forbid—condemn you as useless to the fires. Beware, I beg, of the mark of unhappy times, of which you remember it was prophesied: "The labor of the olive shall fail, and the fields shall yield no food" (Habac. III, 17). Yet I think there are two things especially you must guard against: either that through lukewarmness you become like an olive tree casting off its flower, as you read in the Prophet—that is, that you fail to produce the fruit which in the beginnings of your novice fervor you promised; or, having produced it, that through pride you lose it, and Jeremiah say to you: "The Lord called your name a fruitful olive tree, fair, fruitful, beautiful: but alas! at the voice of a great utterance fire has blazed up in it, and its branches are all burned" (Jerem. XI, 16). We trust, however, better things of you, most beloved, even though we speak thus. We trust that the oil of splendor—or rather, the splendor of the oil—whose sons you are, as were our fathers the Apostles, will by its anointing teach you all things: not only will it wipe away sluggishness, because it is oil, but it will also dispel error, because it is splendor.

4. Scio posse intelligi olei splendoris, id est, quod de splendore Filio procedit, vel quod splendorem illuminationis diffundit: sed puto quia non inconvenienter etiam per intransitionem accipi potest oleum, quod splendor est. Utrumque siquidem Spiritus est in natura sui, et unctio scilicet spiritualis, et splendor invisibilis: utrumque in nobis operatur munere sui. Ungit affectus ex eo quod oleum est: illuminat intellectus ex eo quod splendor est. Qui tamen non ex alio atque alio, sed ex uno ipso et oleum, et splendor est; quia nonnisi unum ipsum est. Ungit affectus, quia charitas est: illuminat intellectus, quia veritas est. Ungit affectus, cum devotionem donat: illuminat intellectus, cum mysteria revelat. Cum bonitatem docet, ut simus simplices sicut columbae, ungit affectus: cum scientiam docet, ut simus prudentes sicut serpentes, illuminat intellectus. Illuminat, ut habeamus in nobis salem: ungit, ut habeamus inter nos pacem. In hoc igitur te nobis probabis filium olei splendoris, si suavem et unctum per benevolentiam, discretum et maturum per sapientiam te nobis exhibueris; ut suavitas veritate sit condita, et aequitate charitas ordinata. Puto autem quod hanc germinam virtutem sui, vel affectum muneris sui, propter quam splendor voluit dici, etiam in illo igne Spiritus sanctus significaverit, in quo ipse invisibilis invisibiliter Apostolis apparuit. Qui enim splendor fuit in aspectu oculorum, oleum, nisi fallor, fuit in sensu corporum: ut veluti in quodam lavacro olei viderentur sibi baptizati, cum super eos effunderetur ignis iste coelestis. Verumtamen qualiscunque fuerit in eis corporaliter sensus illius ignis (parum enim interest qualis sentiatur, sed qualiter operetur), illud, ut opinor, et probabilius dicitur, et religiosius creditur, quod idem ignis qui inflammavit et illuminavit corda, refrigeravit corpora: ut per charitatem mentes arderent, et per castitatem membra frigerent, et utrumque congrueret singulis, sive illud Jeremiae, De excelso misit ignem in ossibus meis (Thren. I, 13); sive illud Psalmistae, Caro mea immutata est propter oleum (Psal. CVIII, 24). Nisi forte aliquid de lege membrorum contra legem mentis relinquere voluit Spiritus, ut virtus in infirmitate perficeretur.

English translation

4. I know that 'oil of splendor' can be understood as meaning that which proceeds from Splendor, the Son, or that which pours forth the splendor of illumination. But I think that, not unfittingly, it can also be taken appositionally, as 'oil, which is splendor.' For indeed the Spirit is both of these in his very nature—spiritual anointing, and invisible splendor—and he works both of these in us by the gift of himself. He anoints the affections insofar as he is oil; he illuminates the intellect insofar as he is splendor. Yet he is not oil from one source and splendor from another, but from one and the same self he is both oil and splendor, because he is nothing other than one and the same. He anoints the affections, because he is charity; he illuminates the intellect, because he is truth. He anoints the affections when he grants devotion; he illuminates the intellect when he reveals mysteries. When he teaches goodness, that we may be simple as doves, he anoints the affections; when he teaches knowledge, that we may be prudent as serpents, he illuminates the intellect. He illuminates, that we may have salt within us; he anoints, that we may have peace among ourselves. In this, then, you will prove yourself to us a son of the oil of splendor, if you show yourself to us sweet and anointed through benevolence, and discreet and mature through wisdom; so that sweetness may be seasoned with truth, and charity ordered by equity. I think, moreover, that the Holy Spirit signified this twofold power of his—or this affective working of his gift—on account of which he wished to be called splendor, even in that fire in which he, invisible, appeared invisibly to the Apostles. For what was splendor to the sight of their eyes was, unless I am mistaken, oil to the sense of their bodies: so that they seemed to themselves baptized as in a kind of bath of oil, when that heavenly fire was poured out upon them. Yet whatever the bodily sensation of that fire may have been in them (for it matters little how it was felt, but how it worked), this, I think, is both the more probable and the more devout belief: that the same fire which kindled and illumined their hearts also cooled their bodies, so that through charity their minds burned, and through chastity their members grew cool; and each saying fit the case—whether that of Jeremiah, 'He sent fire from on high into my bones' (Thren. I, 13), or that of the Psalmist, 'My flesh is changed because of oil' (Psal. CVIII, 24). Unless perhaps the Spirit wished to leave something of the law of the members at war with the law of the mind, so that power might be perfected in weakness.

5. Sed prosequamur quod proposuimus. Isti sunt, inquit, duo filii olei splendoris, qui assistunt Dominatori universae terrae (Zach. IV, 14). Ecce habes bonitatem in oleo, scientiam in splendore: accipe disciplinam in eo quod ait, qui assistunt Dominatori universae terrae; ut agnoscas eos vere discipulos illius fuisse cui cantamus, Bonitatem et disciplinam et scientiam doce me (Psal. CXVIII, 66). Haec est namque perfectio disciplinae, tam in mente, quam in corpore; ut proponentes Dominum in conspectu nostro semper, semper et ubique majestati aeternae, quae nos inspicit et judicat sine intermissione, cum metu et reverentia assistamus, pervigili fide ac jugi devotione. Sicut oculi, inquit, servorum in manibus dominorum suorum, ita oculi nostri ad Dominum Deum nostrum (Psal. CXXII, 2), intentissime et indeclinabiliter observantes nutus ejus, quid velit, quid jubeat, ut quam celerrime devotio nostra obediat. Sic Moyses invisibilem tanquam videns sustinebat, nec unquam imminentis metum judicis dissimulare poterat. Sic Elias et alii Prophetae qui dicebant: Vivit Dominus, in cujus conspectu sto (IV Reg. V, 16). Inde nimirum et gratiam, et nomen assecuti sunt Videntium; quoniam quae semper intuebantur per fidem non fictam, merebantur saepius intueri per speciem, licet factam. Haec fides vigil, non ficta, non dissimulatrix, ipsa est, ut arbitror, quae sola vel maxime facili compendio ad perfectionem perducit; quae gravitatem disciplinae, sobrietatemque modestiae, tam interiori quam exteriori nostro tribuit, et velut in disciplina famulantium Domino semper assistere facit. Hac virtute meruerunt Apostoli dici filii olei splendoris: ut sicut Domino astiterunt in terris contemplando per fidem, ita nunc ei assistant in coelis contemplando per speciem; Regemque in decore suo videant oculi eorum, cujus timorem hic meditabatur cor eorum. Hac via, fratres, sequimini Patres fidei vestrae, et auctores Ordinis, quatenus eorum provocati exemplis et adjuti suffragiis, quo ipsi hodie praecesserunt, pervenire valeatis, adjuvante Domino nostro Jesu Christo, qui vivit et regnat per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

5. But let us continue what we have proposed. 'These,' he says, 'are the two sons of the oil of splendor, who stand before the Lord of all the earth' (Zach. IV, 14). Behold, you have goodness in the oil, knowledge in the splendor: receive discipline in what he says, 'who stand before the Lord of all the earth'; that you may recognize that they were truly disciples of him to whom we sing, 'Teach me goodness, and discipline, and knowledge' (Psal. CXVIII, 66). For this is the perfection of discipline, both in mind and in body: that, setting the Lord ever before our sight, we should always and everywhere stand before the eternal majesty—which beholds and judges us without ceasing—with fear and reverence, with ever-watchful faith and unfailing devotion. 'As the eyes of servants are on the hands of their masters, so are our eyes toward the Lord our God' (Psal. CXXII, 2), watching most intently and unswervingly for his least sign—what he wills, what he commands—so that our devotion may obey as swiftly as possible. Thus Moses endured, as one who saw him who is invisible, and could never put out of mind the fear of the Judge who was at hand. Thus Elijah and the other Prophets, who said: 'The Lord lives, in whose sight I stand' (IV Reg. V, 16). From this, surely, they obtained both the grace and the name of 'Seers'; because that which they always beheld through unfeigned faith, they often merited to behold through a vision—though a created one. This watchful faith, not feigned, not dissembling, is itself, I think, that which alone, or at least most readily and by the easiest path, leads to perfection; which bestows the gravity of discipline and the sobriety of modesty on both our inward and our outward self, and causes us, as it were in the discipline of servants, to stand always before the Lord. By this virtue the Apostles deserved to be called sons of the oil of splendor: so that just as they stood before the Lord on earth, contemplating through faith, so now they stand before him in heaven, contemplating through vision; and their eyes behold the King in his beauty, whose fear their heart meditated here below. By this way, brothers, follow the Fathers of your faith and the founders of your Order, so that, roused by their examples and helped by their intercessions, you may be able to arrive where they themselves have gone before this day, with the help of our Lord Jesus Christ, who lives and reigns for ever and ever. Amen.
