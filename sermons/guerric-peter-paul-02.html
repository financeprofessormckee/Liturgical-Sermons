Introduction & Scripture references

Editorial introduction

Guerric reads the bride's two breasts in the Song of Songs as Peter and Paul, who nourish the Church with the milk of doctrine. The heavenly Church grieved that its little sister had no breasts until the Spirit filled the apostles at Pentecost. Paul fed others with his words and was fed by their works; Peter received the revelation of the Son and the keys of heaven. The sermon appears under the heading for the Apostles Peter and Paul in the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157; the individual sermon is undated.

Quomodo fidelium animos alant apostoli.

English translation

How the apostles nourish the souls of the faithful.

¶cols. 179-180
Latin original

1. Duo ubera tua sicut duo hinnuli capreae gemelli, qui pascuntur in liliis, donec aspiret dies, et inclinentur umbrae (Cant. IV, 5, 6). Videte, fratres, ne degeneretis. Ut taceam de nobilitate patris vestri, nobilis est mater quae vos genuit, generosa sunt ubera quibus lactati estis. Mater vestra est sponsa cui haec dicuntur, cujus ubera voce sponsi laudantur. Quae nimirum duo ubera Ecclesiae Petrum et Paulum esse, non solum interpretatur nobis dies ista, in qua potamur ab uberibus consolationis eorum satis jucunde et abunde; sed etiam evidentia operis ipsorum approbant argumenta ac monumenta doctrinae. Quibus enim aliis uberibus nutriti sunt filii Ecclesiae, sive ex praeputio, sive ex circumcisione? Istos utique ego reor esse, de quibus olim parvulae et pauperculae consolatio divina promittebat Ecclesiae: Reges nutritii tui erunt (Isai. XLIX, 23), et mammilla regum lactaberis (Isa. LX, 16). Nisi enim tam generoso lacte nutrita esset, nunquam ad hoc virtutis ac gloriae culmen excrevisset. Ad ubera parvulos Petrus invitabat, quibus dicebat: Sicut modo geniti infantes, lac concupiscite (I Petr. II, 2). Ubera Paulus praebuerat quibus dicebat: Lac vobis potum dedi (I Cor. III, 2); et illud; Factus sum in medio vestri sicut nutrix sedens et nutriens filios suos (I Thess. II, 7). Quam plenus autem lacte redundaverit spirituali, pulchre significatum est unda illius lactis visibilis, quod de corpore ipsius fertur exisse loco sanguinis, cum decollatus hodierna die pro illis, quibus dabat mammillam, dedit et animam. Plane nihil in eo sanguineum, sed totum lacteum; qui nihil carnale, nihil suum cogitabat, sed tantum quod aliis utile erat. Nec tam habebat ubera, quam ipse totus uber erat; qui ita misericordia uberi affluebat, ut non modo spiritum suum desideraret totum filiis immulgere, sed et corpus impendere.

English translation

1. Your two breasts are like two twin fawns of a roe, which feed among the lilies, until the day break and the shadows retire (Cant. IV, 5, 6). Take care, brothers, that you do not degenerate. To say nothing of the nobility of your father, noble is the mother who bore you, and generous are the breasts at which you were nursed. Your mother is the bride to whom these words are spoken, whose breasts are praised by the voice of the bridegroom. That these two breasts of the Church are, without doubt, Peter and Paul, is interpreted for us not only by this present day, on which we drink from the breasts of their consolation most pleasantly and abundantly, but also the clear evidence of their work and the monuments of their teaching confirm it. For by what other breasts have the children of the Church been nourished, whether from the uncircumcised or from the circumcision? These, surely, I judge to be they of whom long ago the divine consolation promised to the little and poor Church: Kings shall be your nursing fathers (Isai. XLIX, 23), and you shall be suckled with the breast of kings (Isa. LX, 16). For unless she had been nourished with milk so generous, she would never have grown to this summit of virtue and glory. Peter invited little children to the breasts, to whom he said: As newborn babes, desire the milk (I Petr. II, 2). Paul had offered breasts to those to whom he said: I gave you milk to drink (I Cor. III, 2); and also this: I became in the midst of you as a nurse sitting and nourishing her children (I Thess. II, 7). How full he overflowed with spiritual milk is beautifully signified by that wave of visible milk which is reported to have flowed from his body in place of blood, when, beheaded on this very day for those to whom he gave the breast, he gave also his soul. Clearly nothing bloody was in him, but all was milky; for he thought nothing carnal, nothing of his own, but only what was useful to others. Nor did he so much have breasts, as he himself was wholly a breast; for he so abounded in overflowing mercy that he desired not only to pour out his whole spirit for his children, but also to spend his very body.

2. Antequam haec duo ubera, Petrum et Paulum, accepisset primitiva Ecclesia sanctorum in terris, conquerebatur et dicebat Ecclesia beatorum spirituum in coelis: Soror nostra parva est, et ubera non habet (Cantic. VIII, 8). Cum enim Christus rediens in coelum reliquisset pusillum gregem discipulorum, ac nondum misisset Spiritum suum, quo erant fetanda viscera, et implenda ubera sanctorum: erat, ut opinor, illa Ecclesia in coelis sollicita pro filiolis sponsi, sive jam natis, sive nascituris, cui videlicet committerentur alendi; eo quod Ecclesiam istam viderent parvam numero, virtute et auctoritate, et sine uberibus doctrinae. Nam et ipse Sponsus in diebus carnis suae genuerat aliquos verbo veritatis, et quandiu fuerat cum eis, lactaverat eos uberibus aedificationis et consolationis. Et ipse siquidem Sponsus habet ubera meliora vino (Cant. I, 1), id est, doctrina legali, vel gaudio saeculari. Habet, inquam, Sponsus ubera, ne ullum de omnibus officiis et nominibus pietatis eum praetereat: ut qui pater est creatione naturae, vel regeneratione gratiae, seu etiam auctoritate disciplinae, sit etiam mater affectu clementiae, sit et nutrix sedulitate officii curae. Erant igitur parvuli, quos alebat initium aliquod creaturae ejus, sed nonnisi initium: multumque supererat curae et laboris, donec adducerentur ad perfectum, et formaretur Christus in eis. Cum autem et ipse reliquisset eos; superni spiritus, etsi laeti de reditu Unigeniti, de nova tamen adoptionis progenie solliciti, quodammodo affectu suo videbantur apud eum conqueri: Quis illos nutriet? Lactasti eos, sed ante tempus ablactasti. Non enutristi juvenes, nec ad incrementum perduxisti virgines: quis illos nutriet? Soror nostra parva est, et ubera non habet. Dixisti Petro: Pasce agnos meos (Joan. XXI, 15, 16): sed nec ipse adhuc sufficientiam lactis in uberibus habet. Cito pietas ejus aresceret, qui plus adhuc cuti suae quam animabus parvulorum timet. Facile relinqueret agnos in tentatione, qui Pastorem tam suum quam illorum negavit in interrogatione. Ecce autem repente Spiritu misso de coelo, tanquam lacte de propriis Christi uberibus effuso, Petrus impletus est lacte plurimo: nec multo post de Saulo fit Paulus, praedicator de persecutore, mater de tortore, nutrix de carnifice; ut plane intelligeres totum ejus sanguinem mutatum in lactis dulcedinem, saevitiam in pietatem.

English translation

2. Before the primitive Church of the saints on earth had received these two breasts, Peter and Paul, the Church of the blessed spirits in heaven was complaining and saying: Our sister is little, and has no breasts (Cantic. VIII, 8). For when Christ, returning to heaven, had left behind a tiny flock of disciples, and had not yet sent his Spirit, by which the wombs of the saints were to conceive and their breasts to be filled, that Church in heaven was, I suppose, anxious for the little children of the bridegroom, whether already born or yet to be born, to whom they were to be entrusted for nourishing—because they saw this Church to be small in number, in power and in authority, and without the breasts of doctrine. For the Bridegroom himself, in the days of his flesh, had begotten some by the word of truth, and as long as he was with them had suckled them with the breasts of edification and consolation. For indeed the Bridegroom himself has breasts better than wine (Cant. I, 1), that is, better than the teaching of the law, or than worldly joy. The Bridegroom has breasts, I say, lest any of all the offices and titles of piety should pass him by: so that he who is father by the creation of nature, or by the regeneration of grace, or even by the authority of discipline, might also be mother by the affection of his clemency, and nurse by the diligence of his caring office. There were, then, little children, whom he was nourishing, a kind of beginning of his creation, but only a beginning: and much of care and labor still remained, until they should be brought to perfection, and Christ be formed in them. But when he too had left them, the spirits above, though rejoicing at the return of the Only-begotten, yet anxious about the new offspring of adoption, seemed in a way, by their own affection, to complain before him: Who will nourish them? You have nursed them, but you have weaned them before their time. You have not nourished the young men, nor brought virgins to full growth: who will nourish them? Our sister is little, and has no breasts. You said to Peter: Feed my lambs (Joan. XXI, 15, 16); but even he does not yet have sufficiency of milk in his breasts. Quickly would his devotion wither, who still fears more for his own skin than for the souls of the little ones. Easily would he abandon the lambs in temptation, who denied the Shepherd, both his own and theirs, under questioning. But behold, suddenly, the Spirit having been sent from heaven, as if milk had been poured out from Christ's own breasts, Peter was filled with abundant milk: and not long after, Saul became Paul—a preacher from a persecutor, a mother from a torturer, a nurse from an executioner; so that you might clearly understand that all his blood had been changed into the sweetness of milk, and his savagery into tenderness.

3. His igitur uberibus geminis pectori suo affixis gloriatur Ecclesia, non modo matrem se esse fecundam, sed et civitatem munitam. Ego, inquit, murus, et ubera mea turris (Cantic. VIII, 10). Mille clypei pendent jam de turribus istis, omnis armatura fortium. Ipse quoque sponsus de eisdem uberibus in laudem sponsae in carmine amoris, mystico sermone sic ait: Duo ubera tua sicut duo hinnuli capreae gemelli (Cant. IV, 5). Capream hoc loco accipimus Ecclesiam; acutam visu ad penetranda mysteria Christi, agilem saltu ad transilienda spinosa hujus saeculi, potentem ad exsuperanda venena serpentis antiqui. Cujus duo gemelli non inconvenienter, ut arbitror, intelliguntur hi duo Apostoli; germani fide, consimiles devotione, pares merito et virtute, unanimes charitate, conjuncti in ipsa etiam passione et morte. Quomodo enim in vita sua dilexerunt se, ita et in morte non sunt separati. Quia vero desideria summorum pascuntur in profectibus minorum, donec scilicet aspirante gratia tollant se ad contemplandum sublimia, bene de his hinnulis subditur: Qui pascuntur in liliis, donec aspiret dies, et inclinentur umbrae (Cant. II, 16, 17). Animalia cum declinant aestum diei, captant umbrosa pascua convallium, ubi et liliorum solet esse proventus uberior, donec spirante tepescentis aura vesperi, egrediantur ad aperta camporum, vel ardua montium. Sic et hinnuli nostri quandiu non aspirat eis ad contemplandum clementior aura diei aeterni, pascuntur in liliis convallium; id est, delectantur in virtutibus humilium, aut etiam occupantur in actionibus eorum. Cum autem aspirat dies ille mentibus eorum, egrediuntur et saliunt ad uberiora et feliciora pascua montium aeternorum. Per me, inquit Pastor et ostium, si quis introierit salvabitur, et ingredietur Ecclesiam, et egredietur saepius contemplando, semel migrando ad coelestem patriam; et pascua inveniet, et hic, et ibi. Hic, inter lilia agri; ibi, inter ligna paradisi: hic, in floribus; ibi, in fructibus: hic, in virtutibus sanctorum; ibi, in gaudiis Angelorum.

English translation

3. With these two breasts, then, fastened to her bosom, the Church glories not only that she is a fruitful mother, but also a fortified city. 'I,' she says, 'am a wall, and my breasts are a tower' (Cantic. VIII, 10). A thousand shields now hang from these towers, the whole armory of the mighty. The Bridegroom himself, too, speaking of these same breasts in praise of the Bride, in the song of love, says in mystical language: 'Your two breasts are like two twin fawns of a roe' (Cant. IV, 5). By 'roe' here we understand the Church: sharp of sight to penetrate the mysteries of Christ, nimble in leaping to spring over the thorns of this world, strong to overcome the poisons of the ancient serpent. Her two twin fawns, I think, are not unfittingly understood to be these two Apostles: brothers in faith, alike in devotion, equal in merit and virtue, of one mind in charity, joined even in their very passion and death. For as they loved one another in life, so in death they were not separated. Now because the desires of the highest are fed on the progress of the lesser, until, that is, with grace breathing upon them they lift themselves up to contemplate things sublime, it is rightly added of these fawns: 'Who feed among the lilies till the day break, and the shadows retire' (Cant. II, 16, 17). Animals, when they avoid the heat of the day, seek out the shaded pastures of the valleys, where too the growth of lilies is usually more abundant, until, as the cooling breeze of evening breathes, they go out to the open fields or the steep mountains. So too our fawns, so long as the gentler breeze of the eternal day does not breathe upon them for contemplation, feed among the lilies of the valleys; that is, they delight in the virtues of the humble, or are even occupied in their actions. But when that day breathes upon their minds, they go out and leap to the richer and happier pastures of the eternal mountains. 'By me,' says the Shepherd and the door, 'if any man enter in, he shall be saved'; and he shall enter the Church, and shall go out—often by contemplating, once by passing over to the heavenly homeland—and he shall find pastures, both here and there. Here, among the lilies of the field; there, among the trees of paradise: here, in flowers; there, in fruits: here, in the virtues of the saints; there, in the joys of the Angels.

4. Prorsus in loco pascuae ibi me Dominus collocavit, cum ecclesiae sanctorum associavit, cujus venter sicut acervus tritici vallatus liliis (Cant. VII, 2), ut pascat simul et tritici gustu, et liliorum aspectu. Quid enim acervus tritici, nisi copia divini sermonis in tot libris undique congestis? Quid lilia, nisi justi qui germinant sicut lilium, et florent immarcessibiliter ante Dominum; quorum tantus candor in sanctimonia corporis et cordis puritate, tanta fragrantia in opinione, tanta medicinalis virtus in opere et sermone? Non mediocre pabulum istud fideli animae, videre circa se tot lilia tanta venustate et gratia florentia, ubi omnium virtutum ex eis, licet diversarum ex diversis, capere possit exempla. Iste fundatior humilitate, ille amplior charitate. Alius robustior ad patientiam, alius velocior ad obedientiam. Iste frugalitate parcior, ille labore utilior: iste devotior in oratione, ille in lectione studiosior: iste in administratione prudentior, ille in quiete sanctior. Sed licet aliquam insignius florentem in unoquoque gratiam mireris, non una tamen, sed multae virtutes in singulis, sicut flores in liliis. Quot enim justi, tot lilia: quot virtutes eorum, tot flores liliorum. Qui igitur haec videns gaudet in illis, aut etiam proficit ex illis, quid, nisi pascitur in liliis?

English translation

4. Indeed, it was there, in that place of pasture, that the Lord set me, when he joined me to the church of the saints, whose belly is like a heap of wheat set about with lilies (Cant. VII, 2), so that it may feed at once by the taste of the wheat and the sight of the lilies. For what is the heap of wheat but the abundance of the divine word, gathered together from so many books on every side? And what are the lilies but the just, who spring up like the lily and flower unfadingly before the Lord; whose whiteness is so great in the holiness of body and purity of heart, whose fragrance so great in reputation, whose healing power so great in deed and word? It is no small pasture for a faithful soul to see around it so many lilies flowering with such beauty and grace, where it may gather examples of all the virtues from them, though these be diverse virtues from diverse persons. This one is more firmly grounded in humility, that one more abundant in charity. One is stronger for patience, another swifter for obedience. This one is more sparing in frugality, that one more useful in labor; this one more devout in prayer, that one more studious in reading; this one more prudent in administration, that one more holy in quiet. But although in each you may admire some one grace flowering more conspicuously, yet there is not one virtue alone, but many, in each, as there are many flowers in lilies. For as many as the just, so many the lilies; as many as their virtues, so many the flowers of the lilies. He, then, who, seeing these things, rejoices in them, or even profits by them—what is he doing, but feeding among the lilies?

5. Pascebat Paulus: sed nihilominus in illis quos pascebat. Pascebat eos sermonibus, pascebatur eorum operibus. Ventrem quidem suum, imo et alienum pascebat manibus propriis (Act. XX, 34; I Cor. IV, 12; I Thess. II, 9, et II Thess. III, 8): mentem vero non tam suis pascebat bonis quam alienis, qui non quaerebat quod sibi utile, sed quod aliis. Nunc vivimus, inquit, si vos statis in Domino (I Thess. III, 8). Quae enim gloria mea, aut gaudium, aut corona? Nonne vos ante Deum? (I Thess. II, 19; Philipp. IV, 1.) Ideo saepius ei spirabat dies aeterni luminis, atque afflabat eum lenior aura Spiritus sancti, cujus vi rapiebatur ad interna pascua, aliquando paradisi, aliquando tertii coeli, sive in corpore, sive extra corpus, nescio, inquit, Deus scit (II Cor. XII, 3). Dignum quippe erat, ut qui fideliter pascebat, fideliter pasceretur: et qui foris gaudebat in bonis dominicae familiae, in gaudium Domini sui juberetur intrare, atque repleretur in bonis domus Dei, non solum istius visibilis, pro qua sudabat, sed etiam illius invisibilis, ad quam spirabat, unde et dies ei aspirabat.

English translation

5. Paul fed others, but nonetheless was himself fed in those whom he fed. He fed them with his words, and was fed by their works. His own belly indeed—nay, even that of others—he fed with his own hands (Act. XX, 34; I Cor. IV, 12; I Thess. II, 9, et II Thess. III, 8); but his mind he fed not so much with his own goods as with those of others, for he sought not what was profitable to himself, but what was profitable to others. 'Now we live,' he says, 'if you stand fast in the Lord' (I Thess. III, 8). 'For what is my glory, or my joy, or my crown? Are you not it, before God?' (I Thess. II, 19; Philipp. IV, 1.). Therefore the day of eternal light breathed upon him the more often, and the gentler breeze of the Holy Spirit blew upon him, by whose power he was caught up to the inward pastures, now of paradise, now of the third heaven—'whether in the body, or out of the body, I know not,' he says, 'God knows' (II Cor. XII, 3). It was indeed fitting that he who fed others faithfully should himself be faithfully fed, and that he who outwardly rejoiced in the goods of the Lord's household should be bidden to enter into the joy of his Lord, and be filled with the good things of the house of God—not only of that visible house, for which he toiled, but also of that invisible one, toward which he breathed, whence also the day breathed upon him.

6. Nec minora de beato Petro sentienda puto, cum maxima sit gloria Pauli, illius coaequari meritis, quem judicium Veritatis potestate et primatu praetulit universis. Cum enim Pater qui est in coelis veritatem Unigeniti Petro revelabat (Matth. XVI, 2-61), quid aliud quam dies ei aeternus aspirabat, et dies diei verbum eructabat? Cui autem magis pervium fuisse coelum credendum est, quam ipsi Janitori, cujus lingua clavis coeli facta est? Qui potestatem habebat claudere coelum nubibus, et aperire portas ejus, quomodo ipse non saepius intrasse credetur? Esuriit aliquando Petrus, et coelum ei apertum est, cum adhuc in terra consisteret; tantaque ei inde ciborum copia missa est, ut adhuc multa supersint, licet plurima mactaverit, et manducaverit, immunda et virulenta animalia in sancta et florulenta convertens lilia (Act. X, 10-16): ut videlicet deliciose pasceretur in liliis, donec aspirante die, gloriose in ipsis pasceretur coelis. Sed ecce dum commendamus vobis duo haec ubera sponsae, hora jam praeteriit, qua de ipsis uberibus lac vobis cupiebamus immulgere: quanquam et aliis diebus hoc ipsum facere possimus et soleamus. De uberibus siquidem Apostolorum lac vobis trahimus, quoties de verbis eorum ad aedificationem vestram tractamus. Nunc igitur pro tempore satis sit admonere, si tamen moneri indigetis, ut sicut ubera ista diligitis, ita lac eorum semper concupiscatis; ut in eo crescatis in salutem, donec formatum in vobis exhibeatis Salvatorem Jesum Christum Dominum nostrum, qui vivit et regnat per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

6. I think we should judge no less highly of blessed Peter, since it is Paul's greatest glory to be made equal in merits to him whom the judgment of Truth preferred above all others in power and primacy. For when the Father who is in heaven revealed to Peter the truth concerning the Only-Begotten (Matth. XVI, 2-61), what else was this but that the eternal day breathed upon him, and day uttered speech unto day? And to whom must heaven be believed to have been more open than to that very Doorkeeper, whose tongue was made the key of heaven? He who had the power to shut heaven with clouds, and to open its gates — how shall he not be believed to have entered it the more often himself? Once Peter hungered, and heaven was opened to him while he still stood on earth; and so great an abundance of food was sent to him from there, that much still remained over, although he had already slaughtered and eaten many unclean and venomous creatures, turning them into holy and flowering lilies (Act. X, 10-16): so that, to be sure, he might be fed delightfully among lilies, until, when the day breaks,, he might be fed gloriously in the very heavens themselves. But behold, while we commend to you these two breasts of the Bride, the hour has already passed in which we desired to draw out for you the milk from those very breasts — although on other days we are able, and are accustomed, to do this very thing. For indeed we draw milk for you from the breasts of the Apostles, as often as we treat of their words for your edification. For now, then, let it suffice for the time to admonish you, if indeed you need admonishing, that just as you love these breasts, so you may always long for their milk; that in it you may grow unto salvation, until you show forth, formed in you, the Saviour Jesus Christ our Lord, who lives and reigns through all ages of ages. Amen.
