Introduction & Scripture references

Editorial introduction

Guerric of Igny reads the Purification's ceremony of lights through Simeon receiving Christ in the temple. Mary's obedience prompts a call to humility, bodily chastity and purity of heart; carrying a candle should express devotion and good deeds. Simeon cherishes the Word in flesh like light in wax, and Guerric invites his hearers to become lamps themselves. He develops the image through faith, charitable works, speech that builds up others, prayer and a sincere intention before God. The sermon closes by contrasting divine examination with the enduring light promised beyond death. The individual sermon is undated; its collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157 and is read here from the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The source names the Purification of the Blessed Virgin Mary, traditionally known as Candlemas.

¶cols. 63-64
Latin original

1. Sint lumbi vestri praecincti, et lucernae ardentes in manibus vestris (Luc. xxii, 35)2. Sint lumbi nostri praecincti, ut Purificationem Mariae aemulemur; sint lucernae ardentes in manibus, ut gaudium Simeonis, lumen in manibus portantis, etiam visibili signo in nobis repraesentemus. Simus videlicet casti corpore, et mundi corde; et Purificationem Mariae expressimus: simus ardentes devotione, et lucentes opere; et cum Simeone Christum portamus in manibus. Maria tamen non tam purificata fuit, quam mysterium Purificationis commendavit, implendo legalem, significando spiritualem. Quid enim in ea purificandum esse potuit, quae virgo concepit, virgo peperit, virgoque permansit, imo quae ipso conceptu plane purificata fuit, si quid forte minus ante puritatis habuisset [alias habuit)3? Quid, inquam, mundandum habuit conceptus ille, qui solus potest facere mundum de immundo conceptum semine (Job. xiv, 4), qui fontem quo immundus purificetur edidit, fontem domus David, qui hodieque patet indefesseque scatet in ablutionem peccatoris, et menstruatae? (Zach.. xiii, 1.) Suscepit tamen Mater totius puritatis, purgationis legitimae speciem, ut simul et obedientissimae humilitatis virtutem, et evangelicae purificationis insinuaret veritatem. Ubi nunc ille, qui tam fallaciter quam contumaciter praesumens de sanctitate, detrectat purgatoria poenitentium remedia subire? Esto quod sanctus sit; nunquid sicut sanctarum Sanctissima, quae Sanctum sanctorum peperit, Maria? Utinam, fratres mei, utinam nos illam haberemus humilitatem in peccatis nostris quam vere habuerunt sancti in virtutibus suis. Sed omittimus nunc istud, ut quod coepimus prosequamur. Ac primo quidem de illa spirituali Purificatione, quam Maria, offerens pro se et pro Filio par turturum aut duos pullos columbarum, nobis commendavit, prosequeremur, nisi quod patres nostri, sicut vobis notissimum est, omnia ad liquidum prosecuti sunt; et quomodo turtur ad castitatem carnis, columba ad simplicitatem mentis pertineat, diligenter discurrerunt; nec illud quidem praetereuntes, quid sit sacrificium de uno turture vel pullo columbino, quid holocaustum de altero. Hoc unum tamen adjicimus, quod quanto notiora nobis sunt mysteria, tanto districtius et justius eorum a nobis exiguntur opera; quia scienti bonum et non facienti, peccatum est illi (Jac. iv, 17); et servus sciens voluntatem domini sui, et non faciens digna, plagis vapulabit multis (Luc. xii, 47).

English translation

1. Let your loins be girded, and lamps burning in your hands (Luc. xxii, 35)2. Let our loins be girded, so that we may imitate Mary's Purification; let lamps be burning in our hands, so that by a visible sign we too may represent the joy of Simeon carrying the light in his hands. Let us be chaste in body and clean in heart, and we have expressed Mary's Purification; let us burn with devotion and shine in our deeds, and with Simeon we carry Christ in our hands. Yet Mary did not so much undergo purification as commend the mystery of Purification, by fulfilling the legal purification and signifying the spiritual one. For what could there have been in her needing purification, when she conceived as a virgin, gave birth as a virgin, and remained a virgin; indeed, when in the very act of conceiving she was fully purified, if perhaps she had previously possessed any lesser degree of purity [variant: possessed)3? What, I ask, did that conceived child have that needed cleansing, he who alone can make clean one conceived of unclean seed (Job. xiv, 4), who brought forth the fountain by which the unclean may be purified, the fountain of the house of David, which even today stands open and flows unceasingly for the washing of the sinner and the menstruating woman? (Zach.. xiii, 1.) Nevertheless, the Mother of all purity accepted the outward form of purification prescribed by the law, to convey both the virtue of most obedient humility and the reality of evangelical purification. Where now is the man who, presuming on his holiness as deceitfully as obstinately, refuses to undergo the cleansing remedies of penitents? Granted that he is holy: is he as holy as Mary, the Holiest of holy women, who bore the Holy of holies? Would that, my brothers, would that we had in our sins the humility that the saints truly had in their virtues! But we leave this aside now, to continue what we began. First, indeed, we would pursue that spiritual Purification which Mary commended to us by offering for herself and her Son a pair of turtledoves or two young pigeons, were it not that our fathers, as you well know, have treated everything with complete clarity. They have carefully explained how the turtledove pertains to chastity of the flesh and the dove to simplicity of mind, without passing over what is meant by the sacrifice of one turtledove or young pigeon and the whole burnt offering of the other. Yet we add this one point: the better known the mysteries are to us, the more strictly and justly are the deeds they require demanded of us; for to him who knows the good and does not do it, it is sin (Jac. iv, 17); and the servant who knows his master's will and does not act worthily will be beaten with many blows (Luc. xii, 47).

2. Relinquentes igitur quod tractatum est sufficienter, illud potius tractemus, si placet, quid haec pulcherrima Ecclesiae consuetudo luminarium hodie gestandorum, aut factum repraesentet, aut faciendum demonstret. Quanquam ne id puto quidem vobis incognitum, etiamsi nunquam sit auditum. Quis enim hodie, cereum ardentem gestans in manibus, non statim illius senis recordetur, qui, hodie Jesum accipiens in ulnas suas, Verbum in carne tanquam lumen in cera, ipsum esse lumen ad illuminationem gentium perhibebat? Erat profecto et ipse lucerna ardens et lucens, testimonium perhibens de lumine, qui ad hoc ipsum in Spiritu quo plenus erat, venit in templum, ut, suscipiens misericordiam tuam, Deus, in medio templi tui praedicaret ipsam esse misericordiam, et lumen populi tui. Vere tu, senex serene, lumen portabas non modo manibus, sed et sensibus; qui sic eras illuminatus, ut, cum tanto ante futuram illuminationem gentium tam clare videres, etiam nunc inter tenebras Judaicae infidelitatis, hodierna claritas fidei nostrae tibi coruscaret. Laetare jam, senior juste, vide jam quod praevidebas: discussae sunt mundi tenebrae, ambulant gentes in lumine tuo; plena est omnis terra gloria luminis illius quod occultum in sinu fovebas, imo quo sensus tuos refovebas. O fomes amoris, ipse amor suavis et dulcis ad refocillandam animam! Prorsus suavis et dulcis, etiam cum crucias et affligis; cruciatus feliciter delectans, sicut ignis salubriter refrigerans. Ignis iste, fratres, si in sinu absconditus fuerit, vestimenta non comburit; imo illum ignem exstinguit, qui, in sinu celatus, non modo vestimentum adurit, sed et quod vestitum est, depascit, id est, corpus et animam pariter, consumit. Deus, inquit, noster ignis consumens est (Deut. iv, 24), sed ignem consumentem4; nam animam refovet, demulcet mentem, spiritum recreat, reparatque pereuntem. Hujus ignis vim Simeon non ignorabat, qui in sinu suo eum portare gaudebat, pectusque senile fotu illius calefaciebat. Quanto salubrius et suavius ignis iste senem nostrum calefecit, quam regem David Abisag Sunamitis? (III Reg. i, 17)2 nisi forte Abisag hic ignis idem fuit, sapientia scilicet, cujus amplexu non solum recalescunt frigidi, sed etiam reviviscunt mortui.

English translation

2. Leaving aside, then, what has been sufficiently treated, let us consider instead, if you please, what past event this most beautiful custom of the Church, carrying lights today, represents, or what action it shows us we should perform. Yet I do not think even this is unknown to you, though you may never have heard it explained. For who today, carrying a burning candle in his hands, would not immediately remember that old man who today took Jesus in his arms, the Word in flesh like light in wax, and testified that he was the light to enlighten the nations? He himself, indeed, was also a burning and shining lamp, bearing witness to the light. In the Spirit who filled him he came into the temple for this very purpose: receiving your mercy, O God, in the midst of your temple, to proclaim it to be the mercy and light of your people. Truly, serene old man, you carried the light in your understanding as well as in your hands. You were so enlightened that, while seeing so clearly so far in advance the future enlightenment of the nations, even then, amid the darkness of Jewish unbelief, the present brightness of our faith flashed before you. Rejoice now, righteous elder; see now what you foresaw: the world's darkness has been scattered, the nations walk in your light; the whole earth is full of the glory of that light which you cherished hidden in your bosom, or rather, by which you warmed your understanding. O kindling of love, love itself, pleasant and sweet for reviving the soul! Truly pleasant and sweet, even when you torment and afflict: a torment that brings happy delight, like a fire that gives wholesome coolness. This fire, brothers, if it is hidden in the bosom, does not burn the garments; rather, it extinguishes that fire which, concealed in the bosom, not only scorches the garment but devours what is clothed, that is, consumes body and soul together. Our God, it says, is a consuming fire (Deut. iv, 24), but one that consumes the consuming fire4; for it warms the soul, soothes the mind, refreshes the spirit, and restores the one who is perishing. Simeon knew the power of this fire: he rejoiced to carry it in his bosom and warmed his aged breast with its heat. How much more wholesomely and pleasantly this fire warmed our old man than Abishag the Shunammite warmed King David! (III Reg. i, 17)2 Unless perhaps Abishag was this very same fire, namely wisdom, in whose embrace not only do the cold grow warm again, but even the dead return to life.

4. Amplectere igitur, o beate senex, sapientiam Dei, et recalescant atque recanescant4 quasi de novo sensus tui; astringe uberibus tuis misericordiam Dei, et erit senectus tua in misericordia uberi. Inter ubera, inquit, mea commorabitur (Cantic. i, 12), etiam cum reddam illum Matri suae, manebit apud me. Et cum inter materna ubera continebitur, nihilominus inter ubera mea commorabitur, et ubera quoque mea inebriabit misericordia uberi; etsi non quantum ubera Matris. Illa quippe sicut singulariter mater est summae misericordiae, sic excellenter habet ubera uberis misericordiae. Gratulor et gratificor tibi, gratia plena, quae misericordiam genuisti quam suscepi; cereum praeparasti, quem accepi. Tu ceram ministrasti suscepto lumini, Virgo virginum Virgini; dum incorrupta mater incorruptibile Verbum, incorrupta carne vestisti. Eia, fratres mei, ecce ardet cereus in manibus Simeonis, accendite et vos cereos vestros de illius mutuatione luminis, lucernas dico quas Dominus jubet esse in manibus vestris (Luc. xii, 35). Accedite ad eum, et illuminamini, ut non tam lucernas feratis quam ipsi lucernae sitis, lucentes intus et foris, vobis et proximis. Sit ergo lucerna in corde, sit in manu, sit in ore. Lucerna in corde luceat vobis: lucerna in manu vel ore luceat proximis Lucerna in corde, est pietas fidei; lucerna in manu, exemplum operis; lucerna in ore, sermo aedificationis. Non solum autem coram hominibus lucere nos necesse est per operationem et sermonem, sed et coram angelis, per orationem, coram Deo per intentionem. Lucerna nostra coram angelis est pura devotio, cum in conspectu angelorum psallimus sapienter vel oramus ardenter; lucerna coram Deo, simplex intentio, ut ei soli placeamus, cui nos probavimus.

English translation

4. Embrace, then, blessed old man, the wisdom of God, and let your faculties grow warm and gray again4, as though anew; clasp God's mercy to your breasts, and your old age will abound in mercy. Between my breasts, he says, he will dwell (Cantic. i, 12); even when I give him back to his Mother, he will remain with me. And when he is held between his Mother's breasts, he will nevertheless dwell between my breasts, and will also fill my breasts to overflowing with abundant mercy, though not as much as his Mother's breasts. For just as she is uniquely the mother of supreme mercy, so her breasts possess abundant mercy in an outstanding way. I rejoice with you and give you thanks, full of grace, who bore the mercy I have received; you prepared the candle I have taken. You supplied wax for the light that was received, O Virgin of virgins, for the Virgin, when as an uncorrupted mother you clothed the incorruptible Word in uncorrupted flesh. Come, my brothers: see, the candle burns in Simeon's hands. Light your own candles too by borrowing from his light, I mean the lamps the Lord commands to be in your hands (Luc. xii, 35). Come to him and be enlightened, so that you may not so much carry lamps as be lamps yourselves, shining within and without, for yourselves and your neighbors. Let there be a lamp, then, in the heart, in the hand, and in the mouth. Let the lamp in the heart shine for you; let the lamp in the hand or mouth shine for your neighbors. The lamp in the heart is the devotion of faith; the lamp in the hand, the example of deeds; the lamp in the mouth, speech that builds up. We must shine not only before men through deeds and speech, but also before angels through prayer, and before God through intention. Our lamp before angels is pure devotion, when in the sight of angels we sing psalms with understanding or pray fervently; the lamp before God is a single intention, to please him alone, to whom we have approved ourselves.

Latin original

4. Et ne putemur, in his quae dicimus, sensus nostri somnia secuti fuisse; probemus nos potius in omnibus adhaesisse testimoniis Scripturae. Quod enim fides lucerna sit, testatur Salomon loquens de muliere forti, Non exstinguetur in nocte lucerna ejus (Prov. xxxi, 18); quod utique intelligimus, Non deficiet in tentatione fides ejus. Nam et de charitate, quae nihil aliud est quam fides operans per dilectionem, apud eumdem legimus Salomonem: Lampades ejus, lampades ignis atque flammarum. Aquae multae non poterunt exstinguere charitatem, nec flumina obruent illam (Cantic. viii, 6, 7). Quod opus bonum lucerna sit, lux ipsa veritatis indicat, cum ait, Sint lumbi vestri praecincti, et lucernae ardentes in manibus vestris (Luc. xii, 35): et, Luceat lux vestra coram hominibus, ut videant opera vestra bona (Matth. v, 16). Quod sermo aedificationis lucerna sit, David dicit: Lucerna pedibus meis verbum tuum, et lumen semitis meis (Psal. cxviii, 105). Et Petrus apostolus de sermone prophetico, cui benefacitis, inquit, intendentes, tanquam lucernae lucenti in obscuro loco (II Petr. i, 19). Declaratio quoque sermonum Dei illuminat, et intellectum dat parvulis (Psal. cxviii, 130), sive cum sermones evangelici declarant umbram legis, sive cum ipsi sermones tam Evangelii quam legis declarantur expositionibus dilucidis, qui non intelligentibus parvulis videbantur obscuri; et in lumine dicimus quod dictum erat nobis in tenebris. Ideo Philippus in figura cujuslibet ecclesiastici doctoris dictus est os lampadis4, eo quod de ore ipsius ad illuminationem audientium coruscaret ignitum eloquium Domini. Sermonem autem Dei intelligo, fratres mei, quidquid spiritus ejus dignatione sua loquitur in vobis; omnem utique sermonem qui bonus est ad aedificationem fidei, in provocationem charitatis. Si quis ergo vestrum loquitur, quasi sermones Dei (I Petr. iv, 11); ut etiam in privatis colloquiis omnis sermo malus non procedat de ore vestro, sed si quis est ad aedificationem fidei, ut gratiam det audienti; ut qui audierit, dicat gratias agens tibi, Benedictus sermo oris tui, quia lucerna pedibus meis verbum tuum, et lumen semitis meis (Psal. cxviii, 105). De oratione autem quod et ipsa lucerna sit, maxime si divinitus sit illuminata, ex eo colligimus quod Salomon ait: Lucerna Domini spiraculum hominis, quae investigat omnia secreta ventris (Prov xx, 27). Lumen namque quod nobis orantibus vel psallentibus desuper aperitur, spiraculum vitae est, in quod suaviter respiramus. Hujus lucernae videtur Job recordari, cum in moerore tentationis recolens cum suspirio laetitiam praeteritae consolationis, ait: Quis mihi tribuat ut sim juxta menses pristinos, secundum dies quibus Deus custodiebat me, quando splendebat lucerna ejus super caput meum, et ad lumen ejus ambulabam in tenebris? (Job, xxix, 2, 3.) Caeterum quod lucerna haec dicitur investigare omnia secreta ventris, id est mentis; non illud esse putandum est, quod Dominus comminatur se scrutaturum in lucernis (Sophon. i, 12): cum istud gratiae sit illuminantis, illud judicii exquirentis. Nam et aliter investigat secreta ventris potio medici; aliter gladius carnificis. Jam vero de intentione, quod et ipsa dicatur lucerna, nemo est qui ambigat, si tamen evangelicum illud intelligat: Lucerna corporis tui est oculus tuus (Matth. vi, 22, et Luc. xi, 34).2

English translation

4. And lest we be thought, in what we say, to have followed the dreams of our own understanding, let us show instead that in everything we have kept to the testimonies of Scripture. For Solomon testifies that faith is a lamp when he says of the valiant woman, Her lamp will not be extinguished in the night (Prov. xxxi, 18); which we understand to mean, Her faith will not fail in temptation. Concerning charity too, which is nothing other than faith working through love, we read in the same Solomon: Its lamps are lamps of fire and flame. Many waters will not be able to extinguish charity, nor will rivers overwhelm it (Cantic. viii, 6, 7). That a good deed is a lamp is shown by the light of truth itself when it says, Let your loins be girded, and lamps burning in your hands (Luc. xii, 35); and, Let your light shine before men, that they may see your good deeds (Matth. v, 16). That speech which builds up is a lamp, David declares: Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my paths (Psal. cxviii, 105). And the Apostle Peter says of the prophetic word, to which you do well to attend as to a lamp shining in a dark place (II Petr. i, 19). The unfolding of God's words also gives light and understanding to little ones (Psal. cxviii, 130), whether the words of the Gospel make plain the shadow of the law, or the words of both Gospel and law themselves are made plain by clear explanations, having seemed obscure to little ones who did not understand; and we speak in the light what was said to us in darkness. Thus Philip, as a figure of every teacher in the Church, was called the mouth of a lamp4, because the Lord's fiery utterance flashed from his mouth to enlighten his hearers. By God's word, my brothers, I mean whatever his Spirit graciously speaks in you: every word, indeed, that is good for building up faith and stirring up charity. If any of you speaks, let him speak as the words of God (I Petr. iv, 11), so that even in private conversations no evil word may proceed from your mouth, but only such as serves to build up faith and give grace to the hearer; so that whoever hears may thank you and say, Blessed is the word of your mouth, for your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my paths (Psal. cxviii, 105). That prayer too is a lamp, especially when divinely illuminated, we gather from Solomon's saying: The breath of man is the Lord's lamp, which searches all the hidden places of the belly (Prov xx, 27). For the light opened to us from above as we pray or sing psalms is the breath of life, into which we breathe with delight. Job seems to remember this lamp when, in the sorrow of trial, recalling with a sigh the joy of past consolation, he says: Who will grant me to be as in former months, in the days when God guarded me, when his lamp shone above my head and by its light I walked in darkness? (Job, xxix, 2, 3.) Yet when this lamp is said to search all the hidden places of the belly, that is, of the mind, we must not suppose it to be the same as the Lord's threat to search with lamps (Sophon. i, 12): this belongs to illuminating grace, that to searching judgment. For a physician's medicine searches the hidden places of the belly in one way, an executioner's sword in another. As for intention, no one doubts that it too is called a lamp, provided he understands the Gospel saying: The lamp of your body is your eye (Matth. vi, 22, et Luc. xi, 34).2

Latin original

5. Ut igitur tot lucernas vobis, fratres, illuminetis, accedite ad fontem luminis, et illuminamini, Jesum dico, qui lucet in manibus Simeonis, ut scilicet fidem illuminet, opus illustret, sermonem homini subministret, orationem inflammet, intentionem purget; ut sive opere, sive sermone vel oratione quaeratis placere coram eo in lumine viventium, qui scrutari habet Jerusalem in lucernis suis (Sophon. i, 12), et examinare etiam lumen nostrum. His omnibus accensis, o fili lucis, jam non ambulabis in tenebris, nec timenda tibi erit sententia maledicti, Qui maledixerit patri, vel matri, exstinguetur lucerna ejus in tenebris (Prov. xx, 20), id est, deficiet consolatio hujus lucis, irruentibus undique hinc exterioribus, inde interioribus tenebris. Tibi enim, cui tot interius ardent lucernae, cum exstincta fuerit lucerna hujus vitae, orietur lux inexstinguibilis vitae, et quasi fulgor meridianus consurget tibi ad vesperum: et cum te consumptum putaveris, orieris ut lucifer, et tenebrae tuae erunt sicut meridies (Job. xi, 17, et Isai. lviii, 10)2. Non tamen erit tibi sol amplius ad lucendum per diem, aut splendor lunae illuminabit te; sed Dominus erit tibi in lucem sempiternam; quia Agnus lucerna est novae Jerusalem cui benedictio et claritas in saecula saeculorum. Amen.4

English translation

5. Therefore, brothers, to light so many lamps for yourselves, come to the fountain of light and be enlightened: I mean Jesus, who shines in Simeon's hands, so that he may enlighten faith, make deeds radiant, supply man with speech, inflame prayer, and purify intention. Thus, whether by deed, speech, or prayer, you may seek to please him in the light of the living, before him who is to search Jerusalem with his lamps (Sophon. i, 12) and examine even our light. With all these kindled, O child of light, you will no longer walk in darkness, nor will you have to fear the sentence of the curse: Whoever curses father or mother, his lamp will be extinguished in darkness (Prov. xx, 20); that is, the consolation of this light will fail, as darkness rushes in from every side, outward darkness on one side and inward darkness on the other. For you, within whom so many lamps burn, when the lamp of this life is extinguished, the inextinguishable light of life will arise, and at evening a brightness like noonday will rise for you. When you think yourself consumed, you will rise like the morning star, and your darkness will be as midday (Job. xi, 17, et Isai. lviii, 10)2. Yet the sun will no longer serve to give you light by day, nor will the brightness of the moon illuminate you; rather, the Lord will be your everlasting light, for the Lamb is the lamp of the new Jerusalem, to whom be blessing and glory for ever and ever. Amen.4
