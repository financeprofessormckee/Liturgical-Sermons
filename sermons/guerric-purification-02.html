Introduction & Scripture references

Editorial introduction

Simeon and Anna receive Christ with longing, joy and praise. Guerric describes the encounter as an anointing of the heart, then reads the four figures in the temple as examples of prayer, devotion, humility and charity. The sermon appears in the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157; the individual sermon is undated.

English translation

With What Love We Should Go Out to Meet the Coming Jesus

¶cols. 67-68
Latin original

1. Sicut ad diem nativitatis, id est adventus Dominici, pertinuit illa gratiarum actio et vox laudis, Benedictus qui venit in nomine Domini; et ad diem apparitionis, id est, nostrae illuminationis, illud quod sequitur: Deus Dominus et illuxit nobis, sic etiam quod subsequitur, Constituite diem solemnem in confrequentationibus [Vulg. in condensis] usque ad cornu altaris; non absurde, ut arbitror, pertinere dicetur ad hunc diem, nobis utique celebrem, frequentia solemni, diem scilicet Dominicae Praesentationis, quando a Matre solemniter ad altare praesentatus est Patri. Nam et in eo quod adjungitur, Deus meus es tu, et confitebor tibi; Deus meus es tu, et exaltabo te; confitebor tibi quoniam exaudisti me, et factus es mihi in salutem (Psal. CXVII, 26-28), quid tam convenienter accipimus quam fidem et confessionem Simeonis et Annae, quae et ipsa hora superveniens confitebatur Domino, et loquebatur de illo omnibus qui exspectabant consolationem Israel. (Luc. II, 38)? Bene ergo citharista noster monebat laetari Jerusalem et conventum facere in die tam solemni: Constituite, inquiens, diem solemnem in confrequentationibus usque ad cornu altaris (Psal. CXVII, 27). Non mediocris aut parum solemnis illa frequentia fuit, cum inde venit Jesus et Maria cum turba parentum inducentium puerum, ut facerent secundum consuetudinem legis pro eo (Luc. II, 27); inde occurrit Simeon et Anna cum turba exspectantium consolationem Israel, quibus testificati sunt de eo.

English translation

1. Just as that thanksgiving and voice of praise, "Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord," belonged to the day of the Nativity, that is, of the Lord's Advent; and just as what follows, "The Lord is God, and he has shone upon us," belonged to the day of the Appearing, that is, of our illumination; so too, I think, it may not unfittingly be said that what comes next, "Appoint a solemn day in the gatherings [Vulg. amid the thick boughs] even to the horn of the altar," belongs to this day — a day certainly made festive for us by a solemn gathering — namely, the day of the Lord's Presentation, when he was solemnly presented by his Mother to the Father at the altar. For in what follows, "You are my God, and I will give thanks to you; you are my God, and I will exalt you; I will give thanks to you, for you have heard me, and you have become my salvation" (Psal. CXVII, 26-28), what do we more fittingly understand than the faith and confession of Simeon and Anna, who, coming in at that very hour, gave thanks to the Lord and spoke of him to all who were awaiting the consolation of Israel (Luc. II, 38)? Well, then, did our harpist urge Jerusalem to rejoice and to hold assembly on so solemn a day, saying: "Appoint a solemn day in the gatherings, even to the horn of the altar" (Psal. CXVII, 27). That gathering was no mean or unimpressive one, when from one side came Jesus and Mary with the crowd of relatives bringing in the child, that they might do for him according to the custom of the law (Luc. II, 27); and from the other side Simeon and Anna came to meet them with the crowd of those awaiting the consolation of Israel, to whom they bore witness concerning him.

Latin original

2. Non crediderim ego de hac solemnitate quae tam pium, tam laetum conventum habuit, illam fuisse prophetae querelam lacrymabilem, Viae Sion lugent, quia non est qui veniat ad solemnitatem (Thren. I, 4): nisi forte arguat paucos venisse de tanto populo qui erat in omni Judaea et Jerusalem. Ad comparationem siquidem tantae multitudinis pauci venerunt, et de ipsis paucis paucissimi, ut arbitror, eum receperunt, cum praesentem viderent diu exspectatum, sicut voce Patris par Malachiam1 fuerat repromissum: Ecce, inquit, veniet ad templum suum sanctum Dominator quem vos quaeritis, et Angelus testamenti quem vos vultis (Malach. III, 1). O Judaei, adest, ecce, Dominator quem vos quaeritis? cur offerentem se vobis non recipitis? Quaeritis et non quaeritis. Sed si quaeritis; quaerite: convertimini et venite (Isai. XXI, 12). Quaeritis qui redimat de manu hominum, non quaeritis qui redimat de manu daemonum. Quaeritis qui eruat de servitute Romanorum, non quaeritis qui eruat de captivitate vitiorum. Si vere utique liberatorem quaeritis, quaerite liberatorem animarum vestrarum, qui salvum faciat populum suum a peccatis eorum. Si quaeritis sublimem, quaerite prius humilem, quoniam, ut ait Daniel: Humillimum hominem constituet Deus super omne regnum (Dan. IV, 14). Si quaeritis dominatorem potentiae, quaerite prius doctorem justitiae, quia justitia firmabitur thronus ejus (Prov. XXV, 5), et justitia et judicium praeparatio sedis ejus (Psal. LXXXVIII, 15). Quia enim perfecte non quaeritis, ideo nec praesentem invenitis, sicut de vobis ipse praedixit: Quaerent me mali, et non invenient me, quia oderunt me (Joan. VII, 34-36). Quaerent absentem, nec invenient etiam praesentem, quia oderunt lucem mala eorum opera arguentem, cum sapientia non nisi per dilectionem possit quaeri, nedum inveniri. Simeon namque, quia pio et fideli quaerebat desiderio, quaesitum invenit, et inventum agnovit sine alterius indicio, sine humano scilicet testimonio. Nam Spiritus est qui testificatur quoniam Christus est veritas (I Joan. V, 6); virtus utique quae de ipso procedit, unctio docens de omnibus, quae de illo, singulariter secundum nomen suum uncto, multipliciter profluit.

English translation

2. I would not believe that of this solemnity, which had so devout and so joyful a gathering, there held true that tearful complaint of the prophet: "The ways of Sion mourn, because there is none that comes to the solemnity" (Thren. I, 4) — unless perhaps it be charging that few came, out of so great a people as was then in all Judea and Jerusalem. For in comparison with so great a multitude, few came; and of those few, I think, very few indeed received him, though they saw present before them him who had long been awaited, just as had been promised by the voice of the Father through Malachi1: "Behold," he says, "the Ruler whom you seek shall come to his holy temple, and the Angel of the covenant whom you desire" (Malach. III, 1). O Jews, behold — is the Ruler whom you seek present here? Why do you not receive him who offers himself to you? You seek, and you do not seek. But if you seek, then seek: turn and come (Isai. XXI, 12). You seek one who may redeem you from the hand of men; you do not seek one who may redeem you from the hand of demons. You seek one who may rescue you from servitude to the Romans; you do not seek one who may rescue you from captivity to vices. If indeed you truly seek a liberator, seek the liberator of your souls, who will save his people from their sins. If you seek one who is exalted, seek first one who is humble, since, as Daniel says: "God will set the most humble of men over every kingdom" (Dan. IV, 14). If you seek a ruler of power, seek first a teacher of justice, for "his throne shall be established by justice" (Prov. XXV, 5), and "justice and judgment are the preparation of his seat" (Psal. LXXXVIII, 15). For because you do not seek perfectly, therefore you do not find him even when he is present, just as he himself foretold concerning you: "The wicked will seek me, and will not find me, because they have hated me" (Joan. VII, 34-36). They will seek him absent, and will not find him even when present, because they hated the light that exposes their evil works, since wisdom cannot be sought — much less found — except through love. For Simeon, because he sought with pious and faithful desire, found what he sought, and recognized what he had found without any other indication, that is, without human testimony. For it is the Spirit who testifies that Christ is the truth (I Joan. V, 6) — namely, the power that proceeds from him, the anointing that teaches concerning all things, which flows forth in many ways from him who, uniquely according to his own name, is the Anointed One.

¶cols. 67-68; cols. 69-70
Latin original

3. Quid autem putamus, fratres mei, unctus iste, qui ungit etiam quos non tangit, quomodo candidum et sanctum pectus senis nostri perunxerit, cum suscipiens eum in ulnas suas, astringeret eum uberibus suis, et gestiret includere, si posset, in intimis cordis sui? Quid, inquam, putamus ille suavis et mitis, qualem insinuabat seipsum castissimo sinui pii senis, qualis illabebatur medullis, quam delectabilem et salubrem, imo quam omnino ineffabilem se implicabat ossibus, inspirabat sensibus? Prorsus liquescebat unctus in unctionem, quasi ad ignem resolutus ad amplectentis amorem; ut nimirum doceret unctio quod dicit lectio, sed non discit nisi dilectio. Quomodo enim legis? Unguentum, inquit, exinanitum [Vulg. effusum] nomen tuum (Cantic. I, 2). Hoc siquidem unguentum exinanitur, cum a quadam duritia sui resolvitur, non ut pereffluat substantia illius, sed ut fluant aromata illius, ut quasi nihil habens solidi sentiri possit, teneri non possit. Aut certe magis anima senis liquescebat in amplexu hujus uncti, vel unguenti, ut diceret: Anima mea liquefacta est, ut dilectus meus infusus est mihi. Infusio itaque unctionis, testimonium est uncti. Haec seni testabatur de puero, Simeoni de Christo, tanquam spiritus veritatis et charitatis, veritate docens, charitate ungens et accendens, et omnia interiora gelidi senis ponens, quasi cum unguenta bulliunt. Motus bullientium unguentorum, aestus desideriorum: sonus eorum, jubilus affectuum pariter dicentium (si tamen aliquid exprimere valentium), Deus meus es tu, et confitebor tibi; Deus meus es tu, et exaltabo te (Isa. XXV, 1). Lumen ad revelationem gentium, et gloriam plebis tuae Israel (Luc. II, 32), annuntians te. Confitebor, inquam, tibi, quoniam exaudisti me, quotidie cum lacrymis dicentem, Quando consolaberis me? (Psal. CXVIII, 82.) Exaudisti me, et nunc demum Simeonem, id est, exauditum fecisti me; quia factus es mihi in salutem (Psal. CXVII, 28). Jesus mihi factus ex semine David secundum carnem, qui David et omnia fecisti secundum deitatem, omnibus quidem generaliter factus es in Salvatorem; sed mihi specialiter factus es in salutem, pectus morbidum perungens, vitalemque resuscitans calorem, animam refocillans, et oculos illuminans. Defecerat enim anima mea in salutare tuum, o Domine, Pater Domini mei Jesu Christi, defecerunt oculi mei in eloquium tuum, dicentes: Quando consolaberis me? (Psal. CXVIII, 82.) Factus eram sicut uter in pruina, frigidus corpore, aridus corde, spiritu marcidus, et totus desiderio languidus. Sed quia in verbum tuae promissionis supersperavi, non visurum scilicet me mortem, nisi viderem Christum tuum, quod speravi jam video, quod desideravi teneo, quod concupivi complector. Video Deum Salvatorem meum in carne mea, et salva facta est anima mea. Viderunt oculi mei salutare Dei, et illuminati sunt interiores oculi mei, qui languebant prae inopia. Omnino ad tactum hujus pueri, hujus hominis novi, renovata est ut aquilae juventus mea, sicut paulo ante mihi loquens pollicebar. Introibo ad altare Dei, ubi Maria Jesum offert Patri, ad Deum qui laetificat senectutem meam, imo renovabit juventutem meam (Psal. XLII, 4).

English translation

3. But what do we think, my brothers, as to how this Anointed One—who anoints even those he does not touch—anointed the white and holy breast of our old man, when, taking him up into his arms, he pressed him to his breast and longed, if he could, to enclose him in the innermost depths of his heart? What, I say, do we think—how sweet and gentle he was, in the way he insinuated himself into the most chaste bosom of the devout old man, how he glided into his inmost marrow, how delightful and health-giving—nay, how utterly ineffable—was the way he entwined himself in his bones and breathed himself into his senses? Truly the Anointed was melting into anointing, as though dissolved by fire into the love of the one embracing him; so that the anointing itself might teach what the reading tells, but which only love can learn. For how do you read? 'Your name,' it says, 'is an ointment emptied out [Vulg. poured out]' (Cantic. I, 2). This ointment indeed is emptied out when it is dissolved from a certain hardness of itself, not so that its substance flows entirely away, but so that its fragrant spices may flow forth, so that, having as it were nothing solid, it may be perceptible but not graspable. Or rather, the old man's soul was melting in the embrace of this Anointed One, or of this ointment, so that he might say: 'My soul has melted, as my beloved has been poured into me.' The pouring-in of the anointing, then, is the testimony of the Anointed One. This it testified to the old man concerning the child, to Simeon concerning Christ, as the Spirit of truth and of love, teaching by truth, anointing and kindling by love, and setting all the inward parts of the chilled old man aglow, as it were, when ointments come to a boil. The stirring of boiling ointments is the ardor of desires; their sound is the jubilation of affections speaking together (if indeed anything can be expressed), 'You are my God, and I will give thanks to you; you are my God, and I will exalt you' (Isa. XXV, 1). 'A light for the revelation of the Gentiles, and the glory of your people Israel' (Luc. II, 32), announcing you. I will give thanks to you, I say, because you have heard me, day after day saying with tears, 'When will you console me?' (Psal. CXVIII, 82.). You have heard me, and now at last you have made Simeon—that is, you have made me—one who is heard; because you have become my salvation (Psal. CXVII, 28). Jesus, made for me of the seed of David according to the flesh, you who made David and all things according to your Godhead, have become Savior of all generally; but to me specially you have become salvation, anointing my sick breast, reviving my vital warmth, refreshing my soul, and enlightening my eyes. For my soul had fainted for your salvation, O Lord, Father of my Lord Jesus Christ, my eyes had failed for your word, saying, 'When will you console me?' (Psal. CXVIII, 82.). I had become like a wineskin in the frost, cold in body, dry in heart, withered in spirit, and altogether languishing with desire. But because I had hoped beyond hope in the word of your promise, namely that I should not see death unless I had seen your Christ, what I hoped for I now see, what I desired I now hold, what I longed for I now embrace. I see God my Savior in my flesh, and my soul has been saved. My eyes have seen the salvation of God, and my inward eyes, which were failing for want, have been enlightened. Altogether, at the touch of this child, this new man, my youth has been renewed like the eagle's, just as a little while ago I was promising myself, speaking to myself. 'I will go in to the altar of God, where Mary offers Jesus to the Father, to God who gives joy to my old age—nay, who will renew my youth' (Psal. XLII, 4).

Latin original

4. Nec minore fortassis gaudio exsultabat cor Annae in Domino; quae, ut arbitror tanto majoris fuit meriti, quanto altioris propositi et sanctioris desiderii, quam illa Anna mater Samuelis; cum illa cogitaret quae mundi sunt, quomodo placeret viro; haec quomodo Deo. illa de thoro viri quaereret sobolem; haec de sinu Patris peteret Salvatorem. Haec igitur, quodam presagio gratiae excellentis quae in vita ejus effloruit, qua etiam gratiam Redemptoris agnoscere et praedicare meruit, gratiae nomen accepit; exemplar verae viduitatis, speculum sanctitatis, et vivum in emortuo corpore simulacrum totius virtutis. Haec finem Legis et initium Evangelii sentiens propinquare, ritus Judaicos christiana jam coeperat pietate mutare, jejuniis potius et orationibus serviens nocte ac die, quam cibis et potibus et variis baptismatibus, justitiis carnis, non animae. Inde erat quod benedictione filiorum, quae in Lege magni habebatur, contempta, nomen melius a filiis et filiabus elegerat, et jam per annos octoginta quatuor, lignum esse aridum lignoque insidere arido, tanquam turtur amissa semel conjuge, gaudebat.

English translation

4. Nor was the heart of Anna perhaps exulting in the Lord with any less joy; who, I think, was of so much the greater merit as she was of a higher purpose and a holier desire than that other Anna, the mother of Samuel; since that Anna thought on the things of the world, how she might please her husband, but this one thought how she might please God; that Anna sought offspring from her husband's bed, but this one sought the Savior from the bosom of the Father. This woman, therefore, by a kind of presaging of the excellent grace which flowered in her life—by which she also deserved to recognize and proclaim the grace of the Redeemer—received the name of grace; an example of true widowhood, a mirror of holiness, and a living image, in a body already as good as dead, of every virtue. Sensing that the end of the Law and the beginning of the Gospel were drawing near, she had already, with Christian devotion, begun to exchange the Jewish rites, serving night and day rather in fastings and prayers than in foods and drinks and various washings—righteousnesses of the flesh, not of the soul. Hence it was that, despising the blessing of children, which was held in great esteem under the Law, she had chosen a better name than sons and daughters, and now, for eighty-four years, rejoiced to be a dry stick and to sit upon a dry stick, like a turtledove that has lost its mate once for all.

5. Ecce habes par turturum, senem justum, et viduam anum, utrumque castum, utrumque desiderio Redemptoris gemebundum; qui utique semetipsos hostiam vivam, sanctam, Deo placentem, Domino et pro Domino obtulerunt. Par turturum sane dico, non conjugum, sed conjunctorum, non copula matrimonii, sed sacratioris mysterii; pares nimirum fide, aequales castitate, similes devotione, consortes praedicatione gratiae, ambos provectae aetatis, ambos perfectae sanctitatis, qui etiam ante Evangelium, evangelicae puritatis ac pietatis in utroque sexu primitias dedicaverunt. Hi duo vario decore virtutem adornaverunt thalamum tuum, Sion, ad suscipiendum Christum Regem; non parietes templi tui, sed penetralia cordis sui secreta cubiculi, ubi adorare Patrem in spiritu et veritate jubemur (Joan. IV, 23), ubi et suscipitur qui in manufactis se nequaquam habitare testatur (Act. VII, 48). In istis itaque suscepit Christum fidelis Sion, in istis gaudens et laetabunda occurrit Deo suo. Alias vix habet ubi reclinet caput suum, et secundum Isaiam, Aporiatus est, et non est qui occurrat (Isa. LXIX, 16). Sufficit tamen istorum occursus ad omnem solemnitatem et laetitiam, eo quod consummatio abbreviata inundet justitiam, et consummata atque abundans justitia paucorum facile compenset infidelitatem multorum.

English translation

5. Behold, you have a pair of turtledoves: the righteous old man, and the aged widow, both chaste, both groaning with desire for the Redeemer; who indeed offered themselves as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to God, to the Lord and for the Lord. I call them, indeed, a pair of turtledoves — not of spouses, but of those joined together; not by the bond of matrimony, but by a more sacred mystery; equal, surely, in faith, equal in chastity, alike in devotion, companions in the preaching of grace, both of advanced age, both of perfect holiness, who, even before the Gospel, dedicated in both sexes the firstfruits of evangelical purity and piety. These two, with varied beauty, adorned your bridal chamber, O Zion, with virtue, for the receiving of Christ the King — not the walls of your temple, but the inner recesses of their hearts, the secret chamber, where we are bidden to worship the Father in spirit and in truth (Joan. IV, 23), where also he is received who testifies that he dwells in no way in things made by hands (Act. VII, 48). In these, then, faithful Zion received Christ; in these, rejoicing and glad, she went out to meet her God. Apart from these, he scarcely has anywhere to lay his head, and, according to Isaiah, 'He was dismayed, and there was none to meet him' (Isa. LXIX, 16). Yet the meeting of these two suffices for all solemnity and gladness, because a shortened consummation overflows with righteousness, and the consummated and abundant righteousness of a few easily compensates for the unfaithfulness of many.

7. Mihi omnino2 celebris est iste conventus, mihi solemnis ista processio, omnique festiva gaudio, ubi hinc advenit puer et puerpera, Jesus et Maria; inde occurrit senex et vidua, Simeon et Anna; hinc Dominus et Domina, inde servus et ancilla; hinc Mediator et Mediatrix, inde tam fideles tamque devoti ipsorum testes et ministri. In hoc denique conventu misericordia et veritas obviaverunt sibi, misericordia scilicet redemptionis in Jesu, veritas confessionis in senibus. In hoc occursu, justitia et pax osculatae sunt; dum justitia piorum senum, et pax reconciliantis mundum invicem in osculo affectuum et gaudio Spiritus consociatae sunt. Recte ergo quod hac nocte cantavimus, ad laetitiam hujus diei pertinere credimus, salvo duntaxat alio profundiore intellectu: Laetare, Jerusalem; et diem festum agite, omnes qui diligitis eam (Isai. LXVI, 10). Ac si diceretur: Laetata est Nazareth de annuntiatione, Bethlehem de nativitate: laetare et tu, Jerusalem, de purificatione, quoniam qui in Nazareth conceptus, et in Bethlehem natus est, in Jerusalem est susceptus et praedicatus. Et diem, inquit, festum agite, sive conventum facite, omnes qui diligitis eam, scilicet Jerusalem, id est, Festiva devotione convenite in templum, ad suscipiendum Redemptorem, omnes qui exspectatis redemptionem Israel.

English translation

7. To me this gathering2 is altogether festive, to me this procession is solemn and full of festive joy, where on this side comes the child and the woman who bore him, Jesus and Mary; on that side comes to meet them the old man and the widow, Simeon and Anna; here the Lord and the Lady, there the servant and the handmaid; here the Mediator and the Mediatrix, there such faithful and devoted witnesses and ministers of them. In this gathering, finally, mercy and truth have met one another — namely, the mercy of redemption in Jesus, the truth of confession in the elders. In this meeting, righteousness and peace have kissed, while the righteousness of the devout elders and the peace of him who reconciles the world have been joined together in the kiss of affections and the joy of the Spirit. Rightly, then, do we believe that what we sang this night pertains to the joy of this day — without prejudice to another, deeper interpretation: 'Rejoice, Jerusalem, and keep festival, all you who love her' (Isai. LXVI, 10). As if it were said: Nazareth rejoiced at the annunciation, Bethlehem at the nativity; rejoice also, you, Jerusalem, at the purification, since he who was conceived in Nazareth and born in Bethlehem is received and proclaimed in Jerusalem. 'And keep festival,' it says, or 'make assembly, all you who love her' — namely, Jerusalem — that is: Gather with festive devotion into the temple, to receive the Redeemer, all you who await the redemption of Israel.

Latin original

7. Jam vero, fratres, si moraliter, sicut mos vester est, aedificari vestra quaerit Sanctitas, quatuor illas illustres in hac processione consideret personas; quarum vita, non solum ecclesias illustrat, sed et coelos adornat: Jesum loquor et Mariam, Simeonem et Annam. Et, ut ab inferioribus ad superiora progrediatur sermo, in Anna, quae jejuniis et obsecrationibus serviebat nocte ac die, jejunium nobis commendatur et oratio; in Simeone, qui tanto Jesum amplectitur gaudio, pietas et devotio; in Maria, quae cum nihil deberet legi, in hac scilicet parte purificationis legem nihilominus implevit, humilitas et obedientia; in Domino autem Jesu, qui factus ex muliere, factus est etiam sub lege, ut eos qui sub lege erant redimeret, charitas et misericordia. Nam castitatis sanctimonia, cujus laus, ut comperi, vestram semper puritatem delectat, in omnibus effulsit communiter, licet satis dissimiliter; cum in senibus fuerit labore continentiae; in puella, munere gratiae; in puero, jure et proprietate naturae, non pueritiae recens natae, sed puritatis innatae. In ipso non solum hujus virtutis, sed etiam omnium origo et perfectio; ab ipso singularum distributio; per ipsum earum conservatio; apud ipsum remuneratio. Apud ipsum igitur ambiamus, tam virginis Mariae, quam Simeonis et Annae meritis suffragantibus, ut quod minus habemus virtutis largiatur, quod largitus fuerit tueatur; ut depositum ipsius, per tutelam ipsius integrum ei reconsignantes, remunerationem consequamur ipsius, qui vivit et regnat per omnia saecula seculorum. Amen.

English translation

7. Now indeed, brothers, if your Holiness seeks to be built up morally, as is your custom, let it consider those four illustrious persons in this procession, whose life not only illumines churches but also adorns the heavens: I mean Jesus and Mary, Simeon and Anna. And, that the sermon may proceed from lower things to higher, in Anna, who served night and day in fastings and prayers, fasting and prayer are commended to us; in Simeon, who embraces Jesus with such great joy, piety and devotion; in Mary, who, though she owed nothing to the Law, nevertheless in this matter of the purification fulfilled the Law all the same, humility and obedience; and in the Lord Jesus, who, made of a woman, was also made under the Law, that he might redeem those who were under the Law, charity and mercy. For the holiness of chastity — the praise of which, as I have learned, always delights your own purity — shone forth in all of them alike, though in quite dissimilar ways: in the elders it was through the labor of continence; in the maiden, through the gift of grace; in the child, by the right and property of nature — not of a childhood recently born, but of purity that is innate. In him is the origin and perfection not only of this virtue but of all virtues; from him comes the distribution of each; through him their preservation; with him their reward. Let us therefore petition him, with the merits of the Virgin Mary, and of Simeon and Anna, interceding for us, that he may grant what virtue we lack, and may guard what he has granted; so that, handing back to him intact, under his own protection, his deposit entrusted to us, we may obtain his reward — who lives and reigns through all the ages of ages. Amen.
