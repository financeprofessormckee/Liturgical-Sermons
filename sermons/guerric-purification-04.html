Introduction & Scripture references

Editorial introduction

Mary's purification introduces an account of contrition, bodily penance, works of mercy and faith, and patience in tribulation. Guerric closes by asking how faithfulness and mercy govern the use of goods shared with the poor. The sermon appears in the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157; the individual sermon is undated.

De quatuor purificationis nostrae generibus.

English translation

On the Four Kinds of Our Purification

¶cols. 75-76
Latin original

1. Postquam impleti sunt dies purgationis Mariae (Luc. II, 21). Scriptura redemptionis nostrae narrans mysteria, sic refert quae pro nobis historialiter sunt gesta, ut significet quae moraliter a nobis sunt gerenda. Cum enim hodie beatae Purificatio Mariae recensetur, manifeste de nostra ipsorum purificatione admonemur. Quem vero tanti non moveat auctoritas exempli, cum videamus scilicet, quia illa sanctarum sanctissima, quae nihil purgandum habuit, purgationis tamen legalis implere mandatum non renuit? O mater immaculata, mater intacta, nonne tuae tibi conscia es puritatis; quia scilicet integritatem tuam nec conceptus, nec partus violavit, sed sacravit? Quare ergo, tanquam muliebre aliquid in concipiendo vel pariendo sis passa, sic mundationis quae muliebri provisa est infirmitati, quaeris remedia? Sic, inquit, decet nos implere omnem justitiam (Matth. III, 15), ut quae summae mater electa sum justitiae, speculum quoque sim totius et exemplar justitiae. Novi ego superbiam filiorum Evae, quae promptior est ad excusandum, quam ad expurgandum commissa. Necessarium arbitror, ut vitiis originis antiquae statim in initiis omnia novae generationis occurrant exempla. Mater praevaricationis peccavit et excusavit procaciter: mater redemptionis non peccet, et satisfaciat humiliter; ut filii hominum qui de matre vetustatis traducunt necessitatem peccandi, de matre saltem novitatis trahant humilitatem purgandi.

English translation

1. "After the days of Mary's purification were fulfilled" (Luc. II, 21). Scripture, in narrating the mysteries of our redemption, relates the things that were historically done for us in such a way as to signify what must morally be done by us. For when today the Purification of the Blessed Mary is commemorated, we are plainly admonished concerning our own purification. For who would not be moved by the authority of so great an example, seeing that she, the holiest of holy women, who had nothing needing to be purged, nevertheless did not refuse to fulfill the mandate of the legal purification? O immaculate Mother, O inviolate Mother, are you not conscious of your own purity—since neither conception nor childbirth violated your integrity, but rather consecrated it? Why then do you seek the remedies of that cleansing which was provided for feminine infirmity, as though you had suffered something womanly in conceiving or giving birth? "Thus," she says, "it becomes us to fulfill all justice" (Matth. III, 15), so that I, who have been chosen as the mother of the highest Justice, may also be the mirror and exemplar of all justice. I know the pride of the children of Eve, which is readier to make excuses than to purge away what has been committed. I judge it necessary that, right from the beginning, all the examples of the new generation should at once confront the vices of the ancient origin. The mother of transgression sinned and insolently made excuses: let the mother of redemption not sin, and let her humbly make satisfaction; so that the sons of men, who inherit from the mother of the old order the necessity of sinning, may at least draw from the mother of the new order the humility of being purged.

2. O filii hominum, tempus purgationis advenit, quando Mater summae puritatis, de cujus purgatione dies festus est nobis, pariter et fontem edidit, et exemplum dedit nobis quod debeamus purgari. Satius est, o fratres, et suavius fonte purgari quam igne. Prorsus qui fonte non fuerint modo purgati, igne habent purgari, si tamen purgari meruerint: quando scilicet Judex ipse, quasi ignis conflans, sedebit conflans et emundans argentum, et purgabit filios Levi (Malach. III, 4, 5). Nunc Christus aqua diluens; tunc ignis consumens. Modo fons patens in ablutionem peccatoris et menstruatae; tunc flamma saeviens et ignis vorans usque ad medullas animae. De ardore siquidem irati fontis ille purgatorius ignis exardescet in conspectu ejus, et inflammabit in circuitu inimicos ejus. Ascendit, inquit, fumus in ira ejus, et ignis a facie ejus exarsit (Psal. XVII, 19). Inde cadent super eos carbones poenaliter purgatorii, quibus modo non adhibentur carbones salubriter desolatorii: qualem illum fuisse reor carbonem sublatum de altari, qui prophetae labia purgavit (Isai. VI, 5-7). An vero et illi carbones hujusmodi queant intelligi, de quibus cuidam dicitur: Ecce dati sunt tibi carbones, et sedebis super eos; hi erunt tibi in adjutorium: vobis relinquo dijudicandum. Illud autem non dubius assero, quia ignis ille, quem Dominus Jesus misit in terram (Luc. XII, 49), si vehementer (sicut est voluntas mittentis) in nobis arserit, ignis ille purgatorius qui in judicio purgabit filios Levi, non ligna, non fenum, non stipulam consumenda reperiet in nobis. Uterque quidem ignis purgatorius, sed satis differenti modo. Iste siquidem purificat ungendo, ille urendo. Hic refrigerium roris, ibi spiritus judicii et spiritus ardoris, quo abluet Dominus sordes filiarum Sion, et sanguinem Jerusalem lavabit de medio ejus.

English translation

2. O sons of men, the time of purification has come, when the Mother of the highest purity—whose purification is for us a feast day—has both brought forth the fountain and given us the example that we ought to be purified. It is better, brothers, and sweeter, to be purified by the fountain than by fire. Indeed, those who are not purified now by the fountain will have to be purified by fire, if indeed they are found worthy to be purified at all: namely when the Judge himself, like a refining fire, shall sit refining and cleansing silver, and shall purge the sons of Levi (Malach. III, 4, 5). Now Christ washes with water; then he will consume with fire. Now the fountain stands open for the washing of the sinner and the menstruous woman; then the raging flame and the devouring fire will reach to the very marrow of the soul. For indeed, from the heat of his wrathful fountain that purgatorial fire will blaze forth in his sight, and will inflame his enemies round about. "Smoke went up," he says, "in his wrath, and fire was kindled from his face" (Psal. XVII, 19). From it the coals of punitive purgation shall fall upon those to whom the healthfully desolating coals are not now applied—such a coal, I think, as that one taken from the altar, which purged the lips of the prophet (Isai. VI, 5-7). Or indeed, might those coals also be understood to be of this kind, of which it is said to a certain one: "Behold, coals have been given to you, and you shall sit upon them; these shall be for your help"—I leave it to you to judge. But this I assert without doubt: that the fire which the Lord Jesus sent upon the earth (Luc. XII, 49), if it burns fiercely in us (as is the will of the one who sends it), that purgatorial fire which in judgment will purge the sons of Levi will find in us no wood, no hay, no stubble to be consumed. Both fires are indeed purgatorial, but in a quite different manner. For the one purifies by anointing, the other by burning. In the one there is the refreshment of dew; in the other, the spirit of judgment and the spirit of burning, by which the Lord will wash away the filth of the daughters of Sion, and shall wash the blood of Jerusalem from the midst of her.

Latin original

3. Est quidem et in hoc ipso multa Domini clementia erga filias Sion: sed longe clementius et mitius purificata est illa, quam beatam praedicant filiae Sion, cui scilicet dictum est: Spiritus sanctus superveniet in te, et virtus Altissimi obumbrabit tibi. Haec nempe supernae virtutis obumbratio, vera fuit purificatio Mariae; non ista, quae quadam mystica dispensatione tantum specie tenus hodie celebrata est. Haec plane fuit tota ac vera sanctificatio Matris et Filii, sicut et angelus exposuit ei. Ideo, inquit, et quod nascetur ex te Sanctum, vocabitur Filius Dei (Luc. I, 35). Antea namque purificanda erat natura mortalis, ut Deum conciperet, non posteaquam concepisset: cum concepisse Sanctum sanctorum summa sanctificatio sit, et ea quae Mater ipsius sanctitatis effecta sit, nihil sanctius esse possit. Cum ergo filiae Sion quae mollius et dissolutius vivunt, et ardorem forsitan experiuntur carnis, merito purificandae sint spiritu judicii et spiritu ardoris; Virgo perpetua ac singularis, quae sola Spiritus obumbratione Deum concepit, solo purificata est spiritu gratiae et refrigerio roris. Omnium sane purgationum, quibus conditio reparatur mortalis, haec beatae purificatio Virginis mitissima atque felicissima fuit: nam illa filiarum Sion quae purgatorio fiet igne judicii, sicut novissima, sic et severissima omnium erit. Nec ista itaque potest esse nobis nisi admirationi, nec illa nisi formidini: quippe qui nec ad istam possimus aspirare, et in illam, si sapimus, semper formidamus incidere. Omnino si sapimus, semper clamabimus, plus emendatione morum, quam strepitu verborum: Domine, ne in furore tuo arguas me, neque in ira tua corripias me (Psal. VI, 2).

English translation

3. There is indeed in this very thing much clemency of the Lord toward the daughters of Sion: but far more clement and gentle was the purification of her whom the daughters of Sion proclaim blessed, to whom it was said: "The Holy Spirit shall come upon you, and the power of the Most High shall overshadow you." This overshadowing of supernal power was truly the purification of Mary—not that one which, by a certain mystical dispensation, was today celebrated only in outward appearance. That overshadowing was plainly the whole and true sanctification of both Mother and Son, as the angel also explained to her. "Therefore," he says, "that which shall be born of you, holy, shall be called the Son of God" (Luc. I, 35). For mortal nature had to be purified beforehand, so that it might conceive God—not afterward, once it had conceived; since to have conceived the Holy of Holies is the highest sanctification, and nothing can be holier than she who was made the Mother of that very holiness. Therefore, since the daughters of Sion, who live more softly and dissolutely, and perhaps experience the burning of the flesh, are rightly to be purified by the spirit of judgment and the spirit of burning; the perpetual and singular Virgin, who alone conceived God by the overshadowing of the Spirit, was purified by the spirit of grace alone and by the refreshment of dew. Of all the purifications by which mortal condition is restored, this purification of the Blessed Virgin was the gentlest and most blessed; for that purification of the daughters of Sion, which will be accomplished by the purgatorial fire of judgment, will be, as the last of all, so also the severest of all. Neither, therefore, can the one be anything to us but a matter for admiration, nor the other anything but a matter for dread: since we can neither aspire to that former one, and, if we are wise, we must always fear falling into that latter one. Altogether, if we are wise, we shall always cry out—more by the amendment of our conduct than by the noise of words—"Lord, rebuke me not in your fury, nor chastise me in your wrath" (Psal. VI, 2).

4. Necessarie igitur nobis et misericorditer inter utrumque genus purgationis, illud summum ad quod non attingimus, et illud ultimum quod metuimus, alia purgationum genera pietas providit divina, sicut tempus et locum poenitentiae, sic plurima salutis indulgens remedia. Quae licet multa ac varia sint, sub quatuor tamen generibus videntur omnia fere posse comprehendi; ne scilicet indigesta multitudine rerum memoriam confundamus auditoris. Haec igitur quatuor esse arbitror quibus opportune et commode his diebus purgationis nostrae purgari possumus: scilicet contritionem cordis, afflictionem corporis, opera pietatis et fidei, patientiam tribulationis. Ad hunc siquidem modum quatuor invenies, quibus mundum generaliter purificat Christus, purgationem peccatorum faciens: aquam scilicet et sanguinem, spiritum et ignem. Hic est, inquit Joannes, qui venit per aquam et sanguinem. Hic est qui baptizat in Spiritu sancto et igni (I Joan. V, 6, 8). Aqua baptizavit, pedesque discipulorum lavit, dedicans baptismum quoque lacrymarum, quae de contritione profluunt cordis. Sanguinem fudit; ut et nos mortificatione corporis participantes ejus passioni, lavemus stolas nostras in sanguine Agni. Spiritum dedit; ut charitas Dei ac proximi diffusa in cordibus nostris per Spiritum, multitudinem in nobis operiat peccatorum. Quae, si minus perfecta est, ut tot et tanta non sufficiat operire, admovet ignem conflator ille, qui purgat filios Levi; et quidquid residuum est rubiginis, ignis excoquit, seu praesentis, seu futurae tribulationis, ut tandem decantare possint: Transivimus per ignem et aquam, et eduxisti nos in refrigerium (Psal. LXV, 12). Sic nempe et mundus iste prius baptizatus aqua diluvii, postea purgatus igne judicii, in novum transibit statum incorruptionis.

English translation

4. Necessarily, then, and mercifully, between these two kinds of purgation—the highest, which we do not attain, and the last, which we fear—divine kindness has provided for us other kinds of purgation, granting, as it grants a time and place for penance, many remedies of salvation as well. Though these are many and varied, they can nevertheless almost all be comprehended under four kinds, lest we confuse the hearer's memory with an undigested multitude of things. These, then, I judge to be the four by which we can be fittingly and conveniently purified in these days of our purification: namely, contrition of heart, affliction of the body, works of piety and faith, and patience in tribulation. In this manner you will find four things by which Christ generally purifies the world, effecting a purgation of sins: namely, water and blood, spirit and fire. 'This is he,' says John, 'who came by water and blood. This is he who baptizes in the Holy Spirit and in fire' (I Joan. V, 6, 8). He baptized with water, and washed the feet of the disciples, consecrating also the baptism of tears, which flow from contrition of heart. He shed blood, that we too, sharing in his passion through mortification of the body, might wash our robes in the blood of the Lamb. He gave the Spirit, that the love of God and of neighbor, poured into our hearts through the Spirit, might cover a multitude of sins in us. If this love is too imperfect to suffice to cover so many and so great sins, that refiner applies the fire, who purges the sons of Levi; and whatever rust remains, the fire—whether of present or future tribulation—smelts away, so that at last they may be able to sing: 'We have passed through fire and water, and you have brought us out into a place of refreshment' (Psal. LXV, 12). Thus, indeed, this world too, first baptized with the water of the flood, and afterward purged by the fire of judgment, will pass into a new state of incorruption.

5. Denique, ut teneris atque delicatis geramus aliquatenus morem, si lacrymas non habent, si labores horrent, si nesciunt ferre tribulationem: nunquid et illud excusabunt, quod Salomon commendat: Per misericordiam, inquiens, et fidem purgantur peccata? (Prov. XV, 27; XVI, 6.) Quid misericordia suavius? quid fide jucundius? Haec oleum est membris, illa lumen oculis. Haec ungit affectus, illa sensus illuminat, dirigitque gressus: ut ad lumen ejus ambules in tenebris, ut invisibilia contempleris, et futuram tuam beatitudinem jam animo praesumptam mediteris. Bene autem dicitur, per misericordiam et fidem purgari peccata: quia misericordia de proprio impendens, redimit debita; fides autem etiam gratis sine operibus indulgentiam impetrat. De misericordia nempe dicit propheta: Iniquitates tuas redime misericordiis pauperum (Dan. IV, 24); et Dominus prophetarum: Date eleemosynam, et omnia munda sunt vobis (Luc. XI, 41). De fide autem ait Apostolus: Fide mundans corda eorum (Act. XV, 9); et Dominus apostolorum: Vade, inquit, fides tua te salvum fecit (Marc. X, 52). Quanquam in hac Salomonis sententia, possit intelligi non fides qua credimus, sed qua credibiles nos exhibemus, dum fidem Deo et hominibus servamus; ut quod hic dicitur: Per misericordiam et fidem purgantur peccata (Prov. XV, 27), et paulo post: Misericordia et veritate redimitur iniquitas (Prov. XVI, 6), eadem sit in diversitate verborum repetita sententia. Recte igitur misericordia et veritas seu fides sociata sunt: cum in omnibus viis nostris nisi misericordia et veritas obvient sibi, plus augeri quam purgari peccata sit verendum. Ut enim de aliis actionibus interim taceam, et de misericordiis pauperum loquar, quas abundantius nunc fieri temporis hujus postulat necessitas: quid in ipsis etiam, quibus peccata purganda sunt, peccati committitur, si misericordia fide, vel fides misericordia destituatur?

English translation

5. Finally, that we may to some extent make allowance for the tender and delicate—if they have no tears, if they shrink from labors, if they do not know how to bear tribulation—will they also excuse themselves from what Solomon commends, saying: 'By mercy and faith sins are purged'? (Prov. XV, 27; XVI, 6.). What is sweeter than mercy? What more delightful than faith? The one is oil for the limbs, the other light for the eyes. The one anoints the affections, the other illumines the senses and directs the steps, so that by its light you may walk in darkness, that you may contemplate invisible things, and meditate upon your future blessedness already anticipated in the soul. And it is well said that sins are purged by mercy and faith: because mercy, spending from one's own goods, redeems debts, while faith, even freely, without works, obtains pardon. Of mercy indeed the prophet says: 'Redeem your iniquities by mercies shown to the poor' (Dan. IV, 24); and the Lord of the prophets: 'Give alms, and all things are clean to you' (Luc. XI, 41). Of faith the Apostle says: 'cleansing their hearts by faith' (Act. XV, 9); and the Lord of the apostles says: 'Go, your faith has made you whole' (Marc. X, 52). Although in this saying of Solomon one might understand not the faith by which we believe, but that by which we show ourselves trustworthy, in that we keep faith with God and with men; so that what is said here, 'By mercy and faith sins are purged' (Prov. XV, 27), and a little afterward, 'By mercy and truth iniquity is redeemed' (Prov. XVI, 6), is the same statement repeated in different words. Rightly, then, are mercy and truth, or faith, joined together: since unless mercy and truth meet one another in all our ways, it is to be feared that sins are increased rather than purged. For, to say nothing for now of other actions, and to speak of mercies shown to the poor, which the necessity of this present time demands be done more abundantly: what sin is committed even in those very acts by which sins are to be purged, if mercy is deprived of faith, or faith of mercy?

Latin original

6. Audiant ergo quibus dispensatio credita est; audiant quibus credita non est: isti scilicet, ne sic velint esse fideles, ut misericordiam deserant; illi, ne sic velint esse misericordes, ut fidem laedant. Sunt enim aliqui qui plus volunt esse fideles quam oportet; et sunt aliqui qui plus volunt esse misericordes quam eis liceat. Qui ergo contra fidem indebitam usurpant misericordiam, audiant Salomonem dicentem: Multi homines misericordes vocantur; fidelem autem virum quis inveniet? (Proverb. XX, 6.) Qui autem velut propter fidem negligunt debitam misericordiam, audiant plus quam Salomonem dicentem: Quod superest date eleemosynam (Luc. XI, 41); ut saltem abundantia vestra aliorum suppleat inopiam. Multum prorsus pepercit infirmae et pusillae nostrae fidei, qui bona communia non ex aequo fratribus partiri, sed ex eo quod superest propriae necessitati, jussit eleemosynam eis impertiri. Ut enim taceamus de jure naturae, quae quidquid parens omnium terra generat, omnibus commune terrigenis adjudicat: illis saltem quibus indifferens est germanitas adoptionis, et communis sors haereditatis quomodo communis non erit usus panis quem Pater administrat coelestis? Esto tamen, sit istud jus ordinatae charitatis, quod moderatio Evangelii suas cuique necessitates primum excepit: quis ille est qui recte metiatur necessitates proprias qui inter necessarium et superfluum aequo judicio discernat? Attendamus nobis, fratres, ne de mortibus pauperum fratrum nostrorum incipiamus judicari, si superflue retinemus aut insumimus nobis unde vita eorum valeat sustentari. Et quoniam hodierna purificatio purissimae ac pauperrimae Virginis de nostra nos purificatione loqui commonuit, in hoc maxime nostram constare puritatem noverimus, si quidquid superfluum est nobis amputemus: ut non solum sanctitate castitatis, sed etiam simplicitate paupertatis aliquatenus aemulemur pauperem matrem pauperis Christi, cui regnum et imperium, et nunc et per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

6. Let them hear, then, to whom the stewardship has been entrusted; and let them hear to whom it has not been entrusted: those without the stewardship, lest they wish to be so faithful that they abandon mercy; those entrusted with it, lest they wish to be so merciful that they injure faith. For there are some who wish to be more faithful than they ought, and there are some who wish to be more merciful than is lawful for them. Those, therefore, who contrary to faith, arrogate to themselves an undue mercy, let them hear Solomon saying: "Many men are called merciful; but who shall find a faithful man?" (Proverb. XX, 6.) But those who, as it were for the sake of faith, neglect due mercy, let them hear one greater than Solomon saying: "That which remains over, give as alms" (Luc. XI, 41), that at least your abundance may supply the want of others. He has indeed greatly spared our weak and feeble faith, who did not command that common goods be divided equally among the brethren, but that alms be given to them out of what remains over and above one's own necessity. For to say nothing of the law of nature, which assigns whatever the earth, the parent of all, brings forth, as common to all her children—for those at least to whom the brotherhood of adoption is undivided, and the lot of inheritance common, how shall the use of the bread which the heavenly Father dispenses not also be common? Yet granted that this is the right of ordered charity, that the moderation of the Gospel has first reserved to each his own necessities: who is he that rightly measures his own necessities, who with fair judgment distinguishes between the necessary and the superfluous? Let us take heed, brothers, lest we begin to be judged for the deaths of our poor brothers, if we superfluously keep back or spend upon ourselves that by which their life might be sustained. And since today's purification of the most pure and most poor Virgin has reminded us to speak of our own purification, let us know that our purity consists chiefly in this: that we cut away whatever is superfluous to us; so that not only in holiness of chastity, but also in simplicity of poverty, we may in some measure imitate the poor mother of the poor Christ, to whom belong kingdom and dominion, both now and for ever and ever. Amen.
