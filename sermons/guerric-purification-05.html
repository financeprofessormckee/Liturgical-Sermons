Introduction & Scripture references

Editorial introduction

Cleansing the bridal chamber of the soul begins with expelling vices and cultivating virtues. The sermon then turns to Mary and Simeon in the temple and to reverence for the Eucharist. The 1879 edition includes it under Guerric, but its footnote records Horstius's doubt about that attribution and its earlier printing among Bernard's works. The individual sermon is undated.

English translation

On Cleansing and Adorning the Soul for Christ

¶cols. 79-80
Latin original

1. « Adorna thalamum tuum, Sion, et suscipe regem Christum, quem virgo concepit, virgo peperit, virgo post partum quem genuit adoravit (3)3. » Ad nos, fratres, ad nos loquitur Spiritus sanctus, qui sumus Sion, qui sumus speculantes, qui die ac nocte levamus oculos nostros ad Dominum Deum nostrum, donec misereatur nostri. Adornemus igitur thalamum nostrum, et suscipiamus regem Christum. Ecce, fratres, Christus in propria veniens, et a suis repulsus, non habet ubi caput reclinet. Prae foribus nostris inter materna brachia vagiens, per Spiritum sanctum quaerit hospitium, dicens: « Adorna thalamum tuum, Sion. » Emundemus igitur thalamum nostrum, prius expellendo quae oculos Christi intrando offendere possunt; et sic demum adornemus ea inferendo quibus delectatur et gaudet. Quae sunt, ait forsitan aliquis vestrum, quae oculos Christi offendere possunt? Cui dicimus: Nihil ita oculos ejus offendit, sicut superbia, quae angelum de coelo, et hominem expulit de paradiso: quae est initium omnis peccati: quam hortatur nos Spiritus sanctus vitare, dicens: Quid superbis, terra et cinis? (Eccli. X, 9); et alibi: Omnis caro fenum; exsiccatum est fenum, et flos ejus decidit (Isai. XL, 6, 7; Eccli. XIV, 18). Videamus nunc quid superbia fecit. Angelum de coelo, et hominem expulit de paradiso. Merito haec oculos Christi offendit quae pulcherrimum opus ejus in coelo et in paradiso foedavit; quae fecit de angelo diabolum, et de homine nuper creato justo perfidum. Quae sicut est peccatorum primum et maximum, sic est omnium regina et mater vitiorum.

English translation

1. "Adorn your bridal chamber, Sion, and welcome Christ the king, whom a virgin conceived, a virgin bore, and after bearing him a virgin adored him (3)3." It is to us, brothers, to us that the Holy Spirit speaks, we who are Sion, we who are watchmen, who day and night lift up our eyes to the Lord our God until he have mercy on us. Let us, then, adorn our bridal chamber and welcome Christ the king. Behold, brothers, Christ coming into his own, and rejected by his own, has nowhere to lay his head. Crying before our doors amid his mother's arms, he seeks lodging through the Holy Spirit, saying: "Adorn your bridal chamber, Sion." Let us therefore cleanse our bridal chamber, first by expelling whatever might offend the eyes of Christ upon his entering; and only then let us adorn it by bringing in those things in which he delights and rejoices. What things, someone among you may perhaps ask, can offend the eyes of Christ? To him we say: Nothing offends his eyes so much as pride, which cast an angel out of heaven and man out of paradise; which is the beginning of all sin; which the Holy Spirit urges us to avoid, saying: Why are you proud, earth and ashes? (Eccli. X, 9); and elsewhere: All flesh is grass; the grass withers and its flower falls (Isai. XL, 6, 7; Eccli. XIV, 18). Let us now see what pride has done. It cast an angel out of heaven and man out of paradise. Rightly does this offend the eyes of Christ, which defiled his most beautiful work in heaven and in paradise; which made of an angel a devil, and of man, newly created righteous, a faithless man. As it is the first and greatest of sins, so it is the queen and mother of all vices.

Latin original

2. Videndum est igitur et sollicite considerandum, ut eam mens nostra cum tota prole sua excludat, ut thalamus noster emundatus et ornatus Christum regem qui foris [al. fores] pulsat, suscipiat. Cum tota, inquam, prole sua, septem scilicet principalibus vitiis. Quae sunt haec? Inanis gloria, invidia, ira, tristitia, avaritia, ventris ingluvies, luxuria. Hae sunt filiae superbiae, quae matrem suam sequuntur: quae hospitium quodcunque intraverint, fetidum reddunt. Sed quae nobis cum istis communio? Quis consensus templo Dei cum idolis? Nulla nobis (Deo agimus gratias) cum eis societas, quas, exuentes veterem hominem cum actibus suis, gladio verae confessionis et poenitentiae enecavimus. Nihil igitur in nobis ultra possit superbia, quae de archangelo diabolum, et de primo homine fecit impium et Creatori suo rebellem. Nihil habeat in nobis vana gloria, qui a die conversionis nostrae, in cruce Domini nostri Jesu Christi coepimus gloriari cum Apostolo dicentes: Mihi autem absit gloriari, nisi in cruce Domini nostri Jesu Christi, per quem mihi mundus crucifixus est, et ego mundo! (Galat. VI, 14). Gloria itaque nostra sit crux Christi quam portamus, et testimonium conscientiae per crucem Christi purgatae, de qua Apostolus ait: Gloria nostra haec est, testimonium conscientiae nostrae (II Cor. I, 12). In qua conscientia gloriari, de donis Dei est Deo gratias agere. Ad quod nos hortatur Apostolus, dicens: Qui gloriatur, in Domino glorietur (I Cor. I, 31; Jer. IX, 24; II Cor. X, 17): id est, in Domino de puritate conscientiae suae glorietur. Non enim qui se ipsum commendat, etc. Caeterum qui in se gloriatur, et qui sibi placet, stulto homini placet. Qui in alio homine gloriatur, aut laudem in mente ab ore alterius exspectat, mercedem operum recipit, ac oleo peccatoris ungit caput suum, auditque prophetam sibi dicentem: Maledictus homo qui ponit spem suam in homine (Jerem. XVII, 6). Et in die judicii cum dixerint: Domine, Domine, aperi nobis, ait: Amen dico vobis, nescio vos (Matth. XXV, 11, 12). Haec vana gloria filia est superbiae; quae in quocunque fuerit, inobedientem facit.

English translation

2. We must therefore watch and carefully consider that our mind exclude pride together with its whole offspring, so that our chamber, cleansed and adorned, may welcome Christ the king who knocks outside [or: at the doors]. With its whole offspring, I say, namely the seven principal vices. What are these? Vainglory, envy, anger, sadness, avarice, gluttony of the belly, lust. These are the daughters of pride, who follow their mother: whatever lodging they enter, they make foul. But what fellowship have we with these? What agreement has the temple of God with idols? We have no partnership with them (thanks be to God), for, stripping off the old man with his deeds, we have slain them with the sword of true confession and penance. Let pride, then, have no further power in us — pride which made of an archangel a devil, and of the first man a wicked man rebellious against his Creator. Let vainglory have nothing in us, we who from the day of our conversion have begun to glory in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, saying with the Apostle: But God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom the world is crucified to me, and I to the world! (Galat. VI, 14). Let our glory, then, be the cross of Christ which we bear, and the testimony of a conscience purged through the cross of Christ, of which the Apostle says: Our glory is this, the testimony of our conscience (II Cor. I, 12). To glory in such a conscience is to give thanks to God for God's gifts. To this the Apostle exhorts us, saying: He who glories, let him glory in the Lord (I Cor. I, 31; Jer. IX, 24; II Cor. X, 17): that is, let him glory in the Lord concerning the purity of his conscience. For it is not he who commends himself, etc. But he who glories in himself, and who pleases himself, pleases a foolish man. He who glories in another man, or expects praise in his mind from another's mouth, receives the reward of his works, and anoints his own head with the oil of the sinner, and hears the prophet saying to him: Cursed is the man who puts his hope in man (Jerem. XVII, 6). And on the day of judgment, when they shall say: Lord, Lord, open to us, he says: Amen I say to you, I know you not (Matth. XXV, 11, 12). This vainglory is a daughter of pride; whoever harbors it, it makes disobedient.

Latin original

3. Rursum, post conversionem, nihil ultra in nobis habeat invidia, per quam mors intravit in orbem terrarum. Diabolus enim invidit homini ascensum unde corruerat, et propter hoc tentavit, et in anima occidit. Haec est illa, quae non magnos, sed parvos semper torquet (Sap. II, 24, 25), dicente sancto Job: Parvulum occidit invidia (Job V, 2). In eo enim quod aliquis alicui invidet, minorem se ostendit ei cui invidet. Sic diabolus invidit primo homini, sic Esau Jacob, et Cain Abel. Haec invidia parvulum occidit; quia nisi inferior esset, de bono alterius non doleret. Hac peste nullus moritur, nisi qui terrena haec appetit, quae tota non veniunt pluribus. Unde si qua desiderat, illi invidet qui penitus ea accepit, vel illi partem habendo quantitatem ei restringit. Hunc parvulum occidit invidia, quia dum non potest adipisci quod alii invidet, in odium, invidiae filiam, cadit.

English translation

3. Again, after conversion, let envy have no further place in us, through which death entered into the world. For the devil envied man the ascent from which he himself had fallen, and for this reason tempted him, and slew him in the soul. This is that vice which torments not the great, but always the small (Sap. II, 24, 25), as holy Job says: Envy slays the little one (Job V, 2). For in that someone envies another, he shows himself lesser than the one he envies. Thus the devil envied the first man, thus Esau envied Jacob, and Cain envied Abel. This envy slays the little one; because unless he were inferior, he would not grieve over another's good. No one dies of this plague except one who craves these earthly things, which do not come whole to many. Hence if anyone desires such things, he envies the one who has received them entirely, or the one who, by having a part, restricts their quantity for him. Envy slays this little one, because, since he cannot obtain what he envies in another, he falls into hatred, the daughter of envy.

¶cols. 81-82
Latin original

4. Rursum, fratres, post conversionem nostram ira non habeat in nobis locum, quia, ut ait Dominus in Evangelio, gradatim ducit ad gehennae puteum, dicens: Qui irascitur fratri suo, reus erit judicio. Qui dixerit, Racha, hoc est, vocem indignationis per iram emiserit, reus erit concilio, in quo tractatur qua poena sit mulctandus. Qui dixerit, Fatue, hoc est, tale quid fecerit aut verbo aut verbere, ut inter fratres despiciatur, reus erit gehennae ignis (Matth. v, 22), nisi resipuerit. Haec est ira, quae stultorum oculos exstinguit, sapientium oculos nonnunquam turbat, dicente Psalmista: Turbatus est prae ira oculus meus (Psal. vi, 8). Et de ira stulti Job ait: Virum stultum interficit iracundia (Job. v, 2). Ecce duas iras videmus; quarum altera nonnunquam turbat oculum, altera semper exstinguit. Altera venit ex impatientia; altera, ex zelo rectitudinis. Altera venit ex vitio; altera, ex virtute. De altera quae venit ex virtute Psalmista ait: Irascimini, et nolite peccare (Psal. iv, 5; Ephes. iv, 26). Quasi dicat: Crudeles estote in vitium, mites in fratrem, considerantes vosmetipsos, ne et vos tentemini. De altera, quae venit ex vitio, ait Apostolus: Sol non occidat super iracundiam vestram (Ephes. iv, 26): id est, Christus, qui est sol justitiae, mentem vestram irascentem non deserat, qui cum ira nunquam habitat. Ira, quae venit ex zelo rectitudinis, debemus irasci prius nostris erratibus; et post, erratibus proximorum: quae melior est risu, quia per tristitiam vultus corrigitur proximus (Eccle. vii, 4). Quae debet tamen subesse rationi, ne sit immoderata, sed in ultione peccati tempus et modum consideret. Haec sapientes interdum turbat: ira per vitium stultos occidit, dum in rixas et in contumeliam linguam relaxat.

English translation

4. Again, brothers, after our conversion let anger have no place in us, because, as the Lord says in the Gospel, it leads step by step down to the pit of hell, saying: Whoever is angry with his brother shall be liable to judgment. Whoever says, Raca—that is, utters a cry of indignation through anger—shall be liable to the council, in which it is determined with what penalty he is to be punished. Whoever says, Fool—that is, does something, either by word or by blow, such that his brother is held in contempt among the brothers—shall be liable to the fire of hell (Matth. v, 22), unless he comes to his senses. This is the anger that puts out the eyes of fools, and sometimes troubles the eyes of the wise, as the Psalmist says: My eye is troubled because of anger (Psal. vi, 8). And of the fool's anger Job says: Anger kills the foolish man (Job. v, 2). Behold, we see two angers; of which the one sometimes troubles the eye, the other always puts it out. The one comes from impatience; the other, from zeal for righteousness. The one comes from vice; the other, from virtue. Of the one that comes from virtue the Psalmist says: Be angry, and do not sin (Psal. iv, 5; Ephes. iv, 26). As if he were saying: Be fierce against vice, gentle toward your brother, considering yourselves, lest you too be tempted. Of the other, which comes from vice, the Apostle says: Let not the sun go down upon your anger (Ephes. iv, 26): that is, let Christ, who is the sun of righteousness, not abandon your angry mind, for he never dwells together with anger. As for the anger that comes from zeal for righteousness, we ought to be angry first at our own faults, and afterward at the faults of our neighbors; and this is better than laughter, because through the sadness of the countenance the neighbor is corrected (Eccle. vii, 4). Yet it must remain subject to reason, lest it be immoderate, but should consider the time and measure in punishing sin. This anger sometimes troubles the wise; but anger through vice kills fools, as it lets the tongue loose into quarrels and insult.

Latin original

5. Rursum, fratres, post conversionem nostram muniendus est thalamus cordis nostri, ne tristitia rediens introeat: illam dico quae mortem operatur, quae de amissione rerum temporalium venit. Haec est illa quae pravas cogitationes generat, murmurosum monachum reddit, quae fastidium parit, et tenentem aratrum Dei cum uxore Loth retro respicere compellit. Sine dolore namque non potest amittere, quod cum inordinato amore possedit. Hanc itaque tristitiam quae mortem operatur excludamus: et intromittamus eam quae venit de memoria peccatorum, et de peregrinatione nostra, dicamusque cum Psalmista: Heu mihi, quia incolatus meus prolongatus est! habitavi cum habitantibus Cedar, multum incola fuit anima mea (Psal. cxix, 5, 6). Haec est tristitia, quae vitam operatur, de qua ait Apostolus: Quasi tristes, semper autem gaudentes (II Cor. vi, 10). Haec est illa de qua Dominus ad discipulos dicit: Tristitia vestra vertetur in gaudium (Joan. xvi, 20). Altera parit mortem, parit animi rancorem.

English translation

5. Again, brothers, after our conversion the bedchamber of our heart must be fortified, lest sadness return and enter in: I mean that sadness which works death, which comes from the loss of temporal things. This is the sadness that breeds wicked thoughts, makes the monk a grumbler, gives rise to disgust, and compels the one holding the plow of God to look back, like the wife of Lot. For one cannot lose without sorrow what one possessed with disordered love. Let us therefore shut out this sadness which works death, and let us admit that sadness which comes from the memory of our sins and from our condition as pilgrims, and let us say with the Psalmist: Woe is me, for my sojourning is prolonged! I have dwelt with the inhabitants of Kedar, my soul has been long a sojourner (Psal. cxix, 5, 6). This is the sadness that works life, of which the Apostle says: As sorrowful, yet always rejoicing (II Cor. vi, 10). This is that of which the Lord says to his disciples: Your sorrow shall be turned into joy (Joan. xvi, 20). The other sadness brings forth death, brings forth rancor of soul.

Latin original

6. Rursum, fratres, post conversionem muniamus thalamum nostrum, ne avaritia, quae est radix omnium malorum, rediens introeat, nosque ab unius Dei cultura abstrahat, idolorum cultores faciat. De qua inter caetera sic ait Apostolus ad discipulos suos: Mortificate membra vestra quae sunt super terram: fornicationem, immunditiam, libidinem, concupiscentiam malam, et avaritiam, quae est idolorum servitus (Coloss. iii, 5). Ecce avaritiam idololatriae comparat, et merito: quia sicut uni Deo idololatra nititur auferre gloriam, ne solus habeat nomen Deitatis; ita avarus in Dei res se extendit, ut solus usurpet quae Deus omnipotens in usum plurimorum fecit. Utraque Deo inimica, quia negat Deo quae sunt ejus. Est et avaritia non solum amor congregandae pecuniae aut conservandae, sed ambitio praelationis, cum nititur homo homini praeesse. Haec, ut verum dicam, etiam pauperes (qui semel pro Christo reliquerunt sua) in tantum vexat, ut contra jus et fas, et propositum suum, non tantum velint, sed etiam studeant fratribus et conservis praeesse. Quod si abbas aut prior eorum voto non consenserit, irascuntur, obloquuntur, eosque aut invidos, aut sibi malevolos (qui se non provehunt) clamant. Haec est avaritia quae memoriam mandatorum Dei turbat, dicente Psalmista Domino: Inclina cor meum in testimonia tua, et non in avaritiam (Psal. cxviii, 36): quae proditionis est mater, quae Judam Dei discipulum primum ad mandatorum Dei oblivionem, de oblivione ad proditionem, de proditione traxit ad laqueum.

English translation

6. Again, brothers, after our conversion let us fortify our bedchamber, lest avarice, which is the root of all evils, return and enter in, and draw us away from the worship of the one God, making us worshippers of idols. Concerning this, among other things, the Apostle says thus to his disciples: Put to death your members which are upon the earth: fornication, uncleanness, lust, evil desire, and avarice, which is the service of idols (Coloss. iii, 5). See how he compares avarice to idolatry, and rightly so: for just as the idolater strives to take glory away from the one God, so that he alone may not bear the name of Deity, so the avaricious man extends himself into the things of God, so that he alone may seize for himself what almighty God made for the use of many. Both are enemies of God, because each denies to God what belongs to him. And avarice is not only the love of gathering or keeping money, but also the ambition for precedence, when a man strives to set himself over another man. This, to speak truly, torments even the poor (who have once left their own goods for Christ's sake) to such a degree that, against right and justice and against their own purpose, they not only desire but even strive to be set over their brothers and fellow-servants. And if the abbot or prior does not consent to their wish, they grow angry, they murmur against him, and cry out that those who do not advance them are either envious or ill-disposed toward them. This is the avarice that disturbs the memory of God's commandments, as the Psalmist says to the Lord: Incline my heart to your testimonies, and not to avarice (Psal. cxviii, 36): which is the mother of betrayal, which drew Judas, God's disciple, first to forgetfulness of God's commandments, then from forgetfulness into betrayal, and from betrayal to the noose.

7. Rursum, fratres, post conversionem nostram muniamus thalamum cordis, ne gula rediens introeat, quae et Adam innocentiae veste, et Esau privavit primogeniti dignitate. Haec est illa pestis quae patres nostros in deserto ingratos beneficiis Dei fecit, quae compulit eos murmurare contra Deum, et dicere: Utinam mortui essemus in Aegypto, quando sedebamus super ollas carnium, et comedebamus allia, cepe, et pepones (Exod. xvi, 3; Num. xi, 5, 6; xiv, 3.) Haec est illa funesta pestis quae eos ad blasphemiam duxit, quasi non esset alia vita post istam, cum dicebant: Comedamus et bibamus; cras enim moriemur (Isa. xxii, 13; II Cor. xv, 32), id est, in futuro. Quasi dicerent: Hoc solum habemus quod comedimus; nihil in futuro post hanc vitam nos habituros speramus. Hanc pestem cum homine perituram pronuntiat Apostolus, dicens: Esca ventri, et venter escis. Deus autem et hunc et hanc destruet (I Cor. vi, 13). Haec pestis tripartita est: quia aut ante horam canonicam, aut aliud quam licet, aut plus quam necesse est facit comedere, et carnis curam in concupiscentiis nititur facere. De qua nascitur inepta laetitia: sicut de populo Israel dicitur: Sedit populus manducare et bibere, et surrexerunt ludere (Exod. XXXII, 6; I Cor. X, 7).

English translation

7. Again, brothers, after our conversion let us fortify the chamber of our heart, lest gluttony, returning, enter in — the vice which deprived Adam of the garment of innocence, and Esau of the dignity of the firstborn. This is that plague which made our fathers in the desert ungrateful for the benefits of God, which drove them to murmur against God and say: "Would that we had died in Egypt, when we sat by the pots of flesh and ate garlic, onions, and melons" (Exod. xvi, 3; Num. xi, 5, 6; xiv, 3.). This is that deadly plague which led them to blasphemy, as though there were no other life after this one, when they said: "Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we shall die" (Isa. xxii, 13; II Cor. xv, 32) — that is, in the life to come. (4) That the priest alone is the proper and sufficient minister for consecrating the Eucharist is a matter of faith, as is clear from canon 4 of the Council of Nicaea, where the Fathers, in rebuking the deacons because in certain places they were administering the Eucharist to the presbyters, and forbidding that this be done henceforth, give this reason for the decree: "Neither rule nor custom," they say, "has handed down that those who have no power of offering the sacrifice should give the Body of Christ to those who offer it."
