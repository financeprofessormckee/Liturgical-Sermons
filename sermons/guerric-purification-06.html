Editorial introduction

Simeon's completed purification prompts an examination of negligence and the use of time. Guerric considers purification through prayer, watchful faith and the final vision of God. The sermon appears in the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157; the individual sermon is undated.

SERMON VI.

De perficienda nostri purificatione, ut Domino sisti possimus.

English translation

On Perfecting Our Purification So That We May Be Presented to the Lord

¶cols. 89-90
Latin original

1. Postquam impleti sunt dies purgationis, tulerunt Jesum in Jerusalem. O quam felix est, de quo dici potest: Impleti sunt dies purgationis ejus: ut jam nihil aliud ei supersit, nisi ut ferant eum in Jerusalem supernam, et sistant eum Domino (Luc. II, 22). Talis nimirum erat Simeon ille noster senex, tam desiderabilis quam plenus desideriis, cujus cum jam olim, ut arbitror, essent impleti dies purgationis, hodie impleti sunt etiam dies exspectationis: ut jam, secundum verbum Domini, nihil aliud ei restaret, postquam vidisset Christum Domini, Christum pacem Dei et hominum, nisi ut dimitteretur in pace, et dormiret in pace in idipsum; hoc est, ut ferrent eum in Jerusalem pacis aeternae, et sisterent eum ad contemplandam pacem quae exsuperat omnem sensum. O Simeon vir desideriorum, impletum est in bonis desiderium tuum. O beate senex, renovata est ut aquilae juventus tua. Introisti jam ad altare Dei illud supernum, aeternum, totum aureum, ad Deum qui laetificat juventutem tuam aeterna visione sui, cujus laetificavit senectutem hodierna visione Christi sui. Ad altare illud invisibile jam ipse praesentatus es Patri; cui ad altare hoc visibile Filium hodie praesentasti. Ibi Filium ipsum, quem hodie tuo sinu gestasti, aeterno jam et indissolubili tibi conseris amplexu.

English translation

1. "After the days of purification were fulfilled, they carried Jesus up to Jerusalem." O how happy is he of whom it can be said: "The days of his purgation were fulfilled," so that nothing else remains for him but that they carry him up to the heavenly Jerusalem and present him to the Lord (Luc. II, 22). Such, surely, was our Simeon of old, that elder as desirable as he was full of desires, whose days of purgation had, I think, long since been fulfilled; and today his days of expectation also were fulfilled, so that, according to the word of the Lord, nothing else remained for him, once he had seen the Christ of the Lord, the Christ who is the peace of God and of men, but to be dismissed in peace and to sleep in peace in that very peace — that is, that they should carry him into the Jerusalem of eternal peace, and present him to behold the peace which surpasses all understanding. O Simeon, man of desires, your desire has been fulfilled with good things! O blessed elder, your youth has been renewed like the eagle's! You have already come to that heavenly altar, eternal, wholly golden, to God who gladdens your youth with the eternal vision of himself, who gladdened your old age today with the vision of his own Christ. At that invisible altar you yourself have already been presented to the Father, to whom at this visible altar you presented the Son today. There you now embrace in an eternal and indissoluble embrace that very Son whom today you bore in your own arms.

2. Repletum est igitur in bonis beati senis desiderium, cujus tota erat exspectatio et desiderium, Exspectatio gentium et Desideratus earum. Renovata est ut aquilae juventus ejus, quoniam in senectute bona impleti sunt dies purgationis ejus. Alioquin nequaquam illius capax novitatis esse posset, nisi sedulo vetustatem his diebus expurgasset. Fratres, expurgate vetus fermentum, dum tempus habetis ad expurgandum; ut cum impleti fuerint dies vestrae purgationis, inveniamini et vos capaces illius gaudii, quo plena est jam anima Simeonis. Et quidem dies qui ad purgandum nobis dati sunt, velimus, nolimus, implemus: sed vae nobis si dies implentur, et purgatio minime impletur; ut postea necesse sit illo nos igne repurgari, quo nihil poenalius, nihil acrius, aut vehementius in hac vita excogitari potest. Sed quis ille tam perfectus, tam sanctus, qui cum hinc exierit, nihil illi debeat igni: qui sic ad purum omnem in se scoriam excoxerit peccati, ut gloriari possit castum se habere cor, ut dicere possit: Mundum est cor meum, purus sum a peccato? (Prov. XX, 9.) Pauci quidem sunt electi; sed inter illos paucos paucissimi sunt, ut arbitror, ita perfecti, ut purgationem illam impleverint, de qua Sapiens dicit: De negligentia purga te cum paucis (Eccli. VII, 34). Omnino, si de negligentia nos purgaremus, de paucis essemus. Nunc autem quoniam non modo minima, sed et maxima negligimus, longe a paucis de terra divisi sumus, et de nobis dicit ille, qui paucitatem sanctorum multitudini praefert negligentium: Inter multos erant mecum (Psal. LIV, 19).

English translation

2. The desire of the blessed old man, therefore, was fulfilled with good things: his whole expectation and desire was the Expectation of the nations and their Desired One. His youth was renewed like the eagle's, because in a good old age the days of his purgation were fulfilled. Otherwise he could by no means have been capable of that newness, had he not diligently purged away his oldness in those days. Brethren, purge out the old leaven while you have time to purge it, so that when the days of your purgation have been fulfilled, you too may be found capable of that joy with which the soul of Simeon is now filled. And indeed the days given us for purging, whether we will or not, we do fulfill; but woe to us if the days are fulfilled and the purgation is by no means fulfilled, so that afterward we must needs be purged again by that fire than which nothing more penal, nothing more bitter or more violent can be conceived in this life. But who is so perfect, so holy, that when he departs from here he owes nothing to that fire — who has so thoroughly burned out every dross of sin in himself that he can boast of having a chaste heart, that he can say: "My heart is clean, I am pure from sin"? (Prov. XX, 9.) Few indeed are the elect; but among those few, fewest of all, I think, are so perfect as to have fulfilled that purgation of which the Wise Man says: "Purge yourself from negligence with the few" (Eccli. VII, 34). Certainly, if we purged ourselves of negligence, we would be among the few. But now, since we neglect not only the smallest things but even the greatest, we are far removed from the few on earth, and of us he says, who prefers the fewness of the saints to the multitude of the negligent: "They were with me among the many" (Psal. LIV, 19).

¶cols. 89-90
Latin original

3. Et quid hodie tam proprium simul et commune his quos religionis fovet umbra, sicut negligentia? et, ut de meipso loquar, quoties derident hostes Sabbata mea? quoties dormitat anima mea prae taedio, diemque fere totum, ac si tempus revocabile esset, inertia consumo? Et utinam sic dormitaret anima mea, ut apertis et vigilantibus oculis, ipse mihi misero non fingerem somnia vana et illusoria! At nunc in perversum mutata sententia, ego vigilo, et cor meum dormit, somnoque tam gravi, ut vix aliquando valeat expergisci ad tonitruum supernae illius increpationis: Usquequo, piger, dormis? quando consurges e somno tuo? Paululum dormies, paululum dormitabis, paululum conseres manus ut dormias; et veniet tibi quasi viator egestas, et pauperies quasi vir armatus (Prov. VI, 8-11; XXIV, 33, 34). Illud sane miserrimum est, quod hodie non putatur jactura, sed lucrum, si dies qui nobis ad purgationem dati sunt, elabantur nobis dormiendo, praetereant et pereant negligendo. Gentilis, aiunt, cum sederet ad mensam, venissetque in mentem nihil strenuum, aut memorabile se egisse die illo; cum gemitu gravi et dolore mensam repulit, exclamans: « O amici, diem perdidi (5)1 » Nos hodie dicere videmur, diem lucrifeci, si sine opere totus per inertiam possit elabi. Sic nullus fere est qui tempori pretium ponat, qui cogitet apud se quantum valeat ad comparandam aeternitatem dies una. Putasne est aliquis in nobis, qui quotidianam dierum suorum a semetipso rationem exigat; qui cujusque diei dispendia vel emolumenta diligenter sibi describat: ut si deprehenderit se hodie negligenter egisse, vindicet in seipsum, ac de caetero cautius agat? Beatus ille servus, quem, cum venerit dies extremus, invenerit ita facientem.

English translation

3. And what today is at once so proper and so common to those whom the shadow of religion shelters, as negligence? And, to speak of myself, how often do enemies mock my Sabbaths? How often does my soul drowse from weariness, and I consume almost the whole day in idleness, as though time could be recalled! And would that my soul drowsed in such a way that, with eyes open and watchful, I did not fashion for myself, wretched man, vain and illusory dreams! But now, in a perverse reversal, I am awake, and my heart sleeps — and sleeps so heavily that it can scarcely ever be roused by the thunder of that heavenly rebuke: "How long will you sleep, O sluggard? When will you rise from your sleep? You will sleep a little, you will slumber a little, you will fold your hands a little to sleep; and your want shall come upon you like a traveler, and your poverty like an armed man" (Prov. VI, 8-11; XXIV, 33, 34). This indeed is most wretched, that today it is not thought a loss but a gain, if the days given us for purgation slip away from us in sleep, and pass by and perish through negligence. The pagan, they say, sitting at table, when it came to his mind that he had done nothing vigorous or memorable that day, pushed back the table with heavy groaning and grief, exclaiming: "O friends, I have lost a day (5)1!" We today seem to say, "I have gained a day," if it can slip away entirely through idleness, without any work. Thus there is almost no one who sets a price on time, who considers within himself how much a single day is worth toward securing eternity. Do you think there is anyone among us who demands of himself a daily account of his days, who diligently sets down for himself the losses or gains of each day, so that if he finds he has acted negligently today, he punishes himself for it, and acts more cautiously thereafter? Blessed is that servant whom, when the last day comes, he shall find so doing.

Latin original

4. Hoc nempe esset de negligentia cum paucis purgari, si recte et utiliter implerentur dies purgationis, ut aliquando posset accessus ad sancta salubriter obtineri. Alioquin, quandiu manserit quis in sanguine purificationis suae, sancivit aeterna justitia (sicut scriptum est in lege Moysi) ut omne sanctum non tangat, nec ingrediatur sanctuarium, donec impleantur dies purgationis ejus (Levit. XII, 4). Viderint ergo qua fronte ingrediantur sanctuarium, qua conscientia non solum tangant sancta, sed etiam tractent et sumant Sanctum sanctorum; qui scilicet nondum liberati de sanguinibus, nullum tamen suae purificationis studium gerunt. Annon audiunt protestantem et dicentem: Cum extenderitis manus vestras, avertam oculos meos; manus enim vestrae sanguine plenae sunt? (Isai. I, 15.) Quid enim, si mundus sum a sanguine alieno, et conculcari me Dominus videt in sanguine proprio (Ezech. XVI, 6), sicut de Jerusalem loquitur per Ezechielem? Quid, inquam, mihi proderit, si veloces non sunt pedes mei ad effundendum sanguinem proximorum; et veloces sunt affectus mei ad acquiescendum carni et sanguini, de quibus dicit Apostolus: Quia caro et sanguis regnum Dei non possidebunt? (I Cor. XV, 50.) Maledictus, inquit Jeremias, qui prohibet gladium suum a sanguine (Jerem. XLVIII, 10): id est, qui vitia carnis et sanguinis sibi parcit abscindere. Libera me de sanguinibus, Deus, Deus salutis meae (Psal. L, 16), ne cum sanguine purificationis meae sanctuarium ingrediens, aut sancta contingens, reus inveniar corporis et sanguinis tui, indigne manducans et bibens. Verumtamen gratias tibi, Deus, Deus salutis meae, qui in hoc ipsum pretiosum tuum fudisti sanguinem, ut immunditiam nostri purificares sanguinis, tactumque peccatricis sanguinem fluentis, sed salutem desiderantis non abhorreres: sciens nimirum quod nullus potest facere mundum de immundo conceptum semine, nisi tu, qui solus es; nec sanis, sed male habentibus, melius tamen habere cupientibus, medicus opus est. Tangere itaque Sanctum sanctorum, sicut illis quos delectat in sanguine suo versari, judicium est mortis: sic illis qui desiderant purificari, remedium est salutis, juxta prophetiam Simeonis, Quia hic positus est in ruinam et in resurrectionem multorum (Luc II, 34).

English translation

4. This indeed would be to be purified from negligence along with the few, if the days of purification were rightly and profitably fulfilled, so that access to holy things might at some point be beneficially obtained. Otherwise, as long as anyone remains in the blood of his purification, eternal justice has decreed (as it is written in the law of Moses) that he shall not touch anything holy, nor enter the sanctuary, until the days of his purification are fulfilled (Levit. XII, 4). Let them consider, then, with what effrontery they enter the sanctuary, with what conscience they not only touch holy things but even handle and consume the Holy of Holies — those, namely, who are not yet freed from blood, and yet make no effort at all toward their own purification. Do they not hear him protesting and saying: 'When you stretch out your hands, I will turn away my eyes; for your hands are full of blood'? (Isai. I, 15.). For what does it profit me if I am clean of another's blood, while the Lord sees me trampled underfoot in my own blood (Ezech. XVI, 6), as he speaks of Jerusalem through Ezekiel? What, I say, will it profit me if my feet are not swift to shed the blood of my neighbors, while my affections are swift to acquiesce to flesh and blood, of which the Apostle says: 'For flesh and blood shall not possess the kingdom of God' (I Cor. XV, 50.)? 'Cursed,' says Jeremiah, 'is he who withholds his sword from blood' (Jerem. XLVIII, 10): that is, he who spares himself from cutting away the vices of flesh and blood. Deliver me from blood, O God, God of my salvation (Psal. L, 16), lest, entering the sanctuary with the blood of my purification, or touching holy things, I be found guilty of your body and blood, eating and drinking unworthily. Yet thanks be to you, God, God of my salvation, who for this very purpose poured out your precious blood, that you might purify the uncleanness of our blood, and did not abhor the touch of a sinful woman flowing with blood, yet desiring salvation: knowing well that no one can make clean what was conceived of unclean seed, except you, who alone can; nor is the physician needed for the healthy, but for those who are ill, yet desire to be better. Thus to touch the Holy of Holies is, for those who delight in wallowing in their own blood, a sentence of death; but for those who desire to be purified, it is a remedy of salvation, according to the prophecy of Simeon: 'For this one is set for the fall and for the resurrection of many' (Luc II, 34).

5. Fratres, et vos quidem in sanguine fuistis, sed abluti estis, sed purificati estis: nisi quia, sicut justus confitetur: Si loti fueritis velut aquis nivis, sordibus vos statim intingit (Job IX, 30, 31), non solum conditio corrupta, sed etiam voluntas infirma; ut semper novis sordibus opus sit purificatione nova. Illa vero mirabilis et miserabilis est in hominibus sui negligentia, ut, cum nemo sit qui se purificatione non indigere praesumat, vix tamen est aliquis qui tempus ad hoc indultum, quasi purgatissimus sit, non negligat. Fratres, breves dies hominis sunt (ibid., 25), sed tamen valde pretiosi sunt; imo quo breviores, eo pretiosiores; quia dies sunt purificationis, cum statim postmodum futuri sint dies retributionis. Beati qui in diebus istis lavant stolas suas, et candidas eas faciunt in sanguine Agni; ne postea vestimentum eorum, si mistum fuerit sanguine, sit in combustionem et cibus ignis (Isai. IX, 5). Nec solum timor urget supplicii, sed etiam spes invitat praemii, ut omni cura et instantia nostrae modo studeamus purificationi; ut quod hodie figurare voluit in seipso Filius Dei quadam dispensatoria specie, completum quandoque gaudeamus in nobis ipsa rerum veritate. Quid illud? Postquam, inquit, impleti sunt dies purgationis, tulerunt Jesum in Jerusalem, ut sisterent eum Domino (Luc. II, 23). Et nos utique, si tempora purgationis legitime fuerint a nobis impleta, tollent in supernam Jerusalem ministeria angelorum, ut illic assistentes vultui Dei, oblatio ejus simus accepta et immaculata. Ibi demum purificabimur ex toto; sicut a peccato, sic etiam a poena peccati. Ibi nostrae purificationis ipsa erit consummatio, quae et remuneratio; cum scilicet divinus ignis totos nos absumet in holocaustum Domino.

English translation

5. Brothers, you too were indeed in blood, but you have been washed, you have been purified — except that, as the just man confesses: 'If you have been washed as it were with waters of snow, it immediately plunges you into filth' (Job IX, 30, 31), it is not only our corrupted condition but also our weak will that does this, so that there is always need of new purification for new filth. But this is a marvelous and wretched thing in men — this negligence of themselves — that, though there is no one who presumes himself not to need purification, there is scarcely anyone who does not neglect the time granted for this, as though he were already most thoroughly purified. Brothers, the days of man are few (ibid., 25), yet they are very precious; indeed, the shorter they are, the more precious — because they are days of purification, since immediately afterward will come the days of retribution. Blessed are those who in these days wash their robes, and make them white in the blood of the Lamb; lest afterward their garment, if it be mingled with blood, be for burning and fuel for fire (Isai. IX, 5). Not only does the fear of punishment press us, but the hope of reward also invites us, so that we should now strive toward our purification with all care and urgency; so that what the Son of God today willed to prefigure in himself by a certain outward dispensation, we may one day rejoice to see fulfilled in us in the very truth of the reality. What is that? 'After,' it says, 'the days of purification were fulfilled, they brought Jesus to Jerusalem, to present him to the Lord' (Luc. II, 23). And we too, surely, if the times of our purification have been rightly fulfilled by us, the ministries of angels will carry us up into the heavenly Jerusalem, so that, standing there before the face of God, we may be his acceptable and unblemished offering. There at last we shall be purified wholly — as from sin, so also from the punishment of sin. There the very consummation of our purification will take place, which will also be our reward; when, that is, the divine fire shall consume us wholly as a holocaust to the Lord.

6. Porro hunc ipsum beatissimae purificationis inexplicabilem modum nec modo quidem cessat aemulari devotio sanctorum, quantum tamen corruptela corporum, et sollicitudo terrenae inhabitationis permittunt: dum scilicet mente excedentes in illam Jerusalem, verum orationis locum, illic in conspectu Domini quasi turturem et columbam pro seipsis ac de seipsis offerunt; corde videlicet et carne exsultantes in Deum vivum, quia scilicet columba invenit sibi domum, et turtur nidum, altaria tua, Domine virtutum (Psal. LXXXIII, 4). Ego tamen arbitror quod ad meritum non sit inferius, et fortassis ad purgationem efficacius, si illud quod per speculum et in aenigmate raro et raris experiri datur, id est, ut in Jerusalem sistantur Domino, nos per fidem jugiter actitemus: proponentes scilicet Dominum in conspectu nostro semper, et oculos ejus et judicia super nos pervigili fide, irremissoque semper timore cogitantes. Fides ista, fratres, sit in vobis, et eritis de paucis: timor iste sit in vobis, et de negligentia purgabitis vos cum paucis; quia timor iste nihil facile negligit. Denique ab initio hujus pertransibitis, sicut sponsus sponsae pollicetur, euntes de virtute in virtutem, a claritate in claritatem, tanquam a Domini Spiritu (II Cor. III, 18): proficientes de visione quae est per fidem, ad illam quae est per speculum et imaginem, ac postremo, de illa, quae est in imagine speciei, ad eam quae erit in ipsa veritate faciei, seu facie veritatis. Si enim jugiter praesentiam Domini, licet velatam, intendatis per fidem; aliquando etiam revelata facie gloriam ejus speculabimini, licet per speculum et imaginem. Cum autem impletis diebus purgationis venerit quod perfectum est, assistetis Domino in Jerusalem, habitantes cum vultu ejus, ac sine fine contuentes eum facie ad faciem, cui benedictio, et claritas in saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

6. Moreover, the devotion of the saints does not even now cease to imitate this inexpressible manner of most blessed purification, so far as the corruption of our bodies and the anxious care of our earthly dwelling permit: that is, when, rising above in mind into that Jerusalem which is the true place of prayer, they there offer in the sight of the Lord, as it were, a turtledove and a dove for themselves and of themselves; rejoicing, namely, in heart and flesh unto the living God, because indeed the dove has found herself a house, and the turtledove a nest, even your altars, O Lord of hosts (Psal. LXXXIII, 4). Yet I reckon that it is no less meritorious, and perhaps more effective for purgation, if that which is granted to be experienced rarely and by the few through a mirror and in a riddle — namely, to be presented to the Lord in Jerusalem — we continually practice through faith: setting the Lord always before our sight, and pondering, with ever-watchful faith and unrelaxing fear, his eyes and his judgments upon us. Let this faith, brothers, be in you, and you will be among the few; let this fear be in you, and you will purify yourselves from negligence along with the few, since this fear neglects nothing easily. Finally, from this beginning you will pass on, as the Bridegroom promises to his bride, going from strength to strength, from glory to glory, as from the Spirit of the Lord (II Cor. III, 18): advancing from the vision that comes through faith, to that which comes through a mirror and image, and at last, from that which is in the image of a likeness, to that which will be in the very truth of the face, or the face of truth. For if you continually fix your attention, through faith, upon the presence of the Lord, veiled though it be, you will one day also behold his glory with face unveiled, though still as through a mirror and image. But when, the days of purification being fulfilled, that which is perfect shall come, you will stand before the Lord in Jerusalem, dwelling with his countenance, and gazing upon him face to face without end — to whom be blessing and glory for ages of ages. Amen.
