Introduction & Scripture references

Editorial introduction

Guerric of Igny reads the news that Joseph is alive as a figure of the announcement that Jesus has risen. Jacob’s grief and renewed spirit become a pattern for the apostles’ passage from doubt to joy, and for the hearers’ growth in love. The Holy Spirit’s witness, love that seeks Christ’s life rather than its own advantage, and Christ’s flesh as provision for the journey shape the concluding exhortation. The individual sermon is not dated; the collection is associated approximately with Guerric’s abbacy in 1138–1157 and is read here from the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. Its historical heading names the Resurrection of the Lord, and the sermon explicitly celebrates Easter.

English translation

Jacob’s Words about Joseph Are Aptly Applied to Christ’s Resurrection

¶col. 141
Latin original

1. Nuntiaverunt Jacob, dicentes: Joseph vivit. Quo audito, revixit spiritus ejus, et ait: Sufficit mihi, si Joseph filius meus vivit. Vadam et videbo eum antequam moriar. (Gen. xlv, 26, 28). Dicetis forsitan, mihi, Bene; sed quid ad rem? quid de Joseph ad gaudium hujus diei, ad gloriam resurrectionis Christi? Pascha est, et tu iterum apponis quadragesimalia? Agnum paschalem esurit anima nostra, cui se per tam longa praeparavit jejunia. Ardens est cor nostrum in nobis de Jesu, Jesum desideramus; sed nondum meremur ipsum videre vel de ipso audire. Jesum, non Joseph esurimus; Salvatorem, non somniatorem; dominatorem coeli, non Aegypti; non qui pavit ventres, sed qui pascit mentes, sed esurientes. In hoc saltem nos adjuvet sermo tuus, ut, quem esurimus, amplius esuriamus. Legimus enim: Beati qui esuriunt, quoniam ipsi saturabuntur. (Matth. v, 6). Cum audimus, magis esurimus. Nam et qui epulas commendat, famem irritat. Si de Jesu audierimus, auditui nostro dabitur gaudium et laetitia, et exsultabunt ossa humiliata. Humiliata sunt ossa nostra quadragesimali afflictione et luctu, magis autem dolore passionis ejus; sed exsultabunt nuntio resurrectionis ejus. Cur ergo tuum Joseph nobis ingeris, cum praeter Jesum, quidquid loquaris non sapiat nobis: praesertim hodie, cum paschalis agnus comeditur, cum Pascha nostrum immolatus est Christus?

English translation

1. They brought news to Jacob, saying: Joseph is alive. On hearing this, his spirit revived, and he said: It is enough for me if Joseph my son is alive. I will go and see him before I die. (Gen. xlv, 26, 28). Perhaps you will say to me: Very well; but what has this to do with the matter? What has Joseph to do with the joy of this day, with the glory of Christ’s resurrection? It is Easter, and you are serving Lenten fare again? Our soul hungers for the Paschal Lamb, for whom it has prepared itself through such long fasts. Our heart burns within us for Jesus; we long for Jesus, but we do not yet deserve to see him or hear about him. We hunger for Jesus, not Joseph; for the Saviour, not the dreamer; for the ruler of heaven, not Egypt; for him who feeds minds, and hungry minds at that, rather than him who fed bellies. At least let your sermon help us in this: to hunger still more for him for whom we hunger. For we read: Blessed are those who hunger, for they shall be filled. (Matth. v, 6). As we hear, we hunger more. For one who praises a feast also whets the appetite. If we hear about Jesus, joy and gladness will be granted to our hearing, and the bones brought low will exult. Our bones have been brought low by Lenten affliction and mourning, and still more by grief for his Passion; but they will exult at the news of his resurrection. Why, then, do you thrust your Joseph upon us, when whatever you say apart from Jesus has no savour for us, especially today, when the Paschal Lamb is eaten, when Christ our Passover has been sacrificed?

2. Ovum sive nucem apposui vobis, fratres; frangite testam, et invenietis escam. Joseph discutiatur, et Jesus invenietur agnus paschalis, quem esuritis; qui tanto dulcius comeditur, quanto latens abstrusius, et studiosius quaeritur, et difficilius invenitur Dicitis mihi: Quid Joseph ad Christum? quid historia quam proposui ad diem istum? Multum per omnem modum. Historiam recordamini, et ultro sese vobis revelabit pietas mysterii, si modo Jesum interpretem habeatis, qui, hodieque resurgens, de occidente littera loquitur suis in via, et aperit Scripturas. Quis enim in omnibus Patriarchis et Prophetis manifestius aut signantius quam Joseph figuram exprimit Salvatoris? Et, ut breviter summam perstringam, secundum illud: Da occasionem sapienti, et addetur ei sapientia (Prov. ix, 9), si cum fide ac pietate cogitemus interpretationem nominis ejus, deinde quod prae caeteris fratribus pulchra facie et decorus aspectu (Gen. xxxix, 6), quod innocens actu, prudens intellectu, quod venditus a suis, suos a morte redemit, quod prius humiliatus usque ad ergastulum, et sic exaltatus usque ad solium, quod denique pro merito operis sui novo nomine appellatus est inter gentes Salvator mundi (Gen. xli, 45); si haec omnia, inquam, pie ac fideliter cogitemus, nonne incunctanter agnoscimus quam vere sit a Domino dictum: In manibus Prophetarum assimilatus sum? (Ose. xii, 10).

English translation

2. I have served you an egg or a nut, brothers; break the shell, and you will find food. Let Joseph be examined, and Jesus will be found, the Paschal Lamb for whom you hunger. He is eaten all the more sweetly when, hidden more deeply, he is sought more earnestly and found with greater difficulty. You say to me: What has Joseph to do with Christ? What has the story I have set before you to do with this day? Much in every way. Recall the story, and the loving mercy of the mystery will reveal itself to you of its own accord, provided you have Jesus as your interpreter, who, rising even today from the letter that kills, speaks to his own on the road and opens the Scriptures. For who among all the patriarchs and prophets expresses the figure of the Saviour more clearly or distinctly than Joseph? And, to touch briefly on the main points, according to the saying, Give the wise man an opportunity, and wisdom will be added to him (Prov. ix, 9): if we consider with faith and devotion the meaning of his name; then how, beyond his other brothers, he was beautiful in face and comely in appearance (Gen. xxxix, 6); how he was innocent in deed and prudent in understanding; how, sold by his own, he redeemed his own from death; how he was first humbled as far as the prison and then raised as high as the throne; and finally how, in recognition of his work, he was given a new name among the nations, Saviour of the world (Gen. xli, 45)—if, I say, we consider all these things devoutly and faithfully, do we not acknowledge without hesitation how truly the Lord said: Through the hands of the prophets I have been represented in likeness? (Ose. xii, 10).

3. Jam vero si veniamus ad verba illa quae de historia proposui, puto quia non tam expositione indigebunt, quam admirationem et gaudium movebunt; quod videlicet tam evidenter resurrectio Christi testificata sit a Lege et Prophetis, et historia vetus tam proprie nova sacramenta loquatur, ut, cum prophetia legitur, pene Evangelium mutatis duntaxat nominibus audiri videatur. Nuntiaverunt, inquit, Jacob, dicentes: Joseph vivit. Quid aliud hic intelligere possum, nisi, Nuntiaverunt Apostolis dicentes: Jesus vivit? Jacob namque nihil aliud quam Apostolorum chorum intelligo; nec absurde, ut arbitror: non solum quia de Jacob orti sunt, non solum quia de Jacob Israel facti sunt, cum de lucta activae ad visionem et requiem contemplativae transierunt; sed etiam quia Patres sunt multitudinis credentium, id est verorum Israelitarum, sicut ille carnalium. Sicut ille, sic isti, suum Joseph cum putarent periisse, inconsolabiliter doluerunt; cum audissent vivere, difficulter et tarde crediderunt; cum cognovissent, ineffabiliter gavisi sunt. Nuntiaverunt, inquit, Jacob, dicentes: Joseph vivit. Quo audito Jacob, quasi de gravi somno evigilans, tamen non credebat eis (Gen. xlv, 26). Quasi aliis verbis mihi videtur dictum, quod in Evangelio legitur scriptum: Illa vadens, haud dubium quin Maria Magdalene, nuntiavit his qui cum ea fuerant2 lugentibus et flentibus; et illi audientes quia viveret et visus esset ab ea, non crediderunt. Post haec ostensus est duobus in via, et illi exeuntes nuntiaverunt caeteris, nec illis crediderunt (Marc. xvi, 10-13). Item apud Lucam. Et regressae a monumento, nuntiaverunt haec omnia illis undecim, et caeteris omnibus; et visa sunt ante illos sicut deliramentum verba ista, et non credebant illis (Luc. xxiv, 9, 11); nimirum quia evigilabant de gravi somno taedii et desperationis. Cum autem, inquit, vidisset Jacob universa quae Joseph miserat ei, revixit spiritus ejus, et ait: Sufficit mihi si Joseph filius meus vivit. Vadam et videbo eum, antequam moriar. (Gen. xlv, 27, 28). Sic utique in Apostolis parum profecerunt verba, donec acceperunt munera. Nam et ipse Jesus cum se eis praesentem exhibuit, non tam persuasit ostensione corporis, quam aspiratione muneris.

English translation

3. Now, if we turn to those words from the story which I set before you, I think they will need explanation less than they will stir wonder and joy. For Christ’s resurrection is so plainly attested by the Law and the Prophets, and the ancient story speaks so aptly of the new mysteries, that when the prophecy is read, it seems almost as though we were hearing the Gospel with only the names changed. They brought news to Jacob, it says, saying: Joseph is alive. What else can I understand here but: They brought news to the apostles, saying: Jesus is alive? By Jacob I understand none other than the company of the apostles, and not unreasonably, I think. This is not only because they descended from Jacob, nor only because from Jacob they became Israel when they passed from the struggle of the active life to the vision and rest of the contemplative life, but also because they are fathers of the multitude of believers, that is, of the true Israelites, just as he was of the Israelites according to the flesh. Like him, when they thought their Joseph had perished, they grieved inconsolably; when they heard he was alive, they believed with difficulty and slowly; when they knew it, they rejoiced beyond words. They brought news to Jacob, it says, saying: Joseph is alive. On hearing this, Jacob, as though waking from a deep sleep, nevertheless did not believe them (Gen. xlv, 26). It seems to me to say in other words what we read written in the Gospel: She went—undoubtedly Mary Magdalene—and brought the news to those who had been with her2, mourning and weeping; and when they heard that he was alive and had been seen by her, they did not believe. After this he appeared to two on the road, and they went out and brought the news to the others, but they did not believe them either (Marc. xvi, 10-13). Again, in Luke: Returning from the tomb, they told all these things to the eleven and to all the others; and these words seemed to them like nonsense, and they did not believe them (Luc. xxiv, 9, 11). For they were indeed waking from the deep sleep of weariness and despair. But when Jacob saw all that Joseph had sent him, it says, his spirit revived, and he said: It is enough for me if Joseph my son is alive. I will go and see him before I die. (Gen. xlv, 27, 28). So too, words made little headway with the apostles until they received gifts. For when Jesus himself showed himself present to them, he persuaded them less by displaying his body than by breathing his gift upon them.

4. Scitis quia cum venit ad eos januis clausis, et stetit in medio eorum, illi conturbati et conterriti existimabant se spiritum videre (Luc. xxiv, 37). Cum autem insufflavit in eos, dicens: Accipite Spiritum sanctum (Joan. xx, 22), vel cum coelitus misit eumdem Spiritum, sed aliud donum, haec utique dona, resurrectionis et vitae indubitata testimonia fuerunt et argumenta. Spiritus enim est qui testificatur apud sanctorum corda et per eorum ora, quoniam Christus est veritas, vera resurrectio et vita. Ideo et Apostoli, qui prius dubitabant etiam post intuitum corporis viventis, post gustum spiritus vivificantis, virtute magna reddebant testimonium resurrectionis. Adeo plus est corde Jesum concipere, quam oculis videre, vel auribus de ipso audire; tantoque potentior est operatio Spiritus apud sensus hominis interioris, quam corporalium apud sensus exterioris. Quis enim dubietati relinquatur locus, ubi qui testificatur, et cui testificatur, unus fuerit Spiritus? Si unus Spiritus et unus sensus, parque consensus. Tunc ergo vere, sicut de Jacob legitur, revixit eorum spiritus, qui jam propemodum luctu erat exstinctus, imo desperatione sepultus. Tunc, nisi fallor, quisque eorum dicebat sibi: Sufficit mihi quod Joseph meus vivit; quia vivere mihi Christus est, et mori lucrum. Vadam igitur in Galilaeam, in montem ubi constituit nobis Jesus, et videbo eum, et adorabo antequam moriar, ut postea nunquam moriar; quia omnis qui videt Filium et credit in eum, habet vitam aeternam, ut, etiamsi mortuus fuerit, vivat (Joan. vi, 40, et xi, 26)3.1

English translation

4. You know that when he came to them with the doors closed and stood in their midst, they were troubled and terrified and thought they were seeing a spirit (Luc. xxiv, 37). But when he breathed upon them, saying: Receive the Holy Spirit (Joan. xx, 22), or when he sent from heaven the same Spirit but a different gift, these gifts were indeed unquestionable testimonies and proofs of resurrection and life. For it is the Spirit who bears witness in the hearts of the saints and through their mouths that Christ is the truth, the true resurrection and life. Thus the apostles, who had doubted even after looking upon his living body, after tasting the life-giving Spirit bore witness to the resurrection with great power. So much greater is it to receive Jesus in the heart than to see him with the eyes or hear about him with the ears; and so much more powerful is the Spirit’s action upon the senses of the inward man than that of bodily things upon the senses of the outward man. For what room can remain for doubt when the one who bears witness and the one to whom witness is borne are one Spirit? If there is one Spirit, there is also one perception and equal agreement. Then truly, as we read of Jacob, their spirit revived, which had already been almost extinguished by grief, indeed buried by despair. Then, unless I am mistaken, each of them said to himself: It is enough for me that my Joseph is alive, for to me to live is Christ and to die is gain. I will therefore go to Galilee, to the mountain where Jesus has appointed to meet us, and I will see him and worship before I die, so that thereafter I may never die; for everyone who sees the Son and believes in him has eternal life, so that even if he has died, he may live (Joan. vi, 40, et xi, 26)3.1

5. Nunc ergo, fratres mei, quid vobis testatur gaudium cordis vestri de amore Christi? Puto equidem (an recte, vos videritis) quoniam si unquam amastis Jesum, seu vivum, seu mortuum, sive redivivum, hodie, cum tam crebro in Ecclesia personant et consonant nuntii Resurrectionis, cor vestrum in vobis gloriatur sibi, et dicit: Nuntiaverunt mihi, dicentes: Jesus Deus meus vivit. Quo audito revixit spiritus meus, qui dormitabat prae taedio, vel languebat tepore, aut nimia deficiebat pusillanimitate. Nam et de morte suscitat criminosos jucunda vox hujus felicis nuntii. Alioquin certe desperandus est et oblivione sepeliendus, quem Jesus, regrediens ab inferis, in intimo reliquit inferni. In hoc sane noveris quod spiritus tuus plene in Christo revixerit. si quod sequitur ex sententia dixerit: Sufficit mihi, si Jesus vivit. O vocem fidelem et plane dignam amicis Jesu! o castissimum affectum, qui sic loquitur: Sufficit mihi si Jesus vivit! Si vivit, vivo, cum de ipso pendeat anima mea, imo ipse sit vita mea, ipse sufficientia mea. Quid enim mihi deesse poterit, si Jesus vivit? Imo desint omnia alia, nihil interest mea, dummodo Jesus vivat. Ipse ergo, si placet ei, desit mihi; sufficit mihi, dummodo vivat ipse vel sibi. Cum sic amor Christi totum absorbuerit affectum hominis, ut, negligens et immemor sui nonnisi Jesum Christum et ea quae sunt Jesu Christi sentiat, tunc demum, ut arbitror, perfecta est in eo charitas. Huic utique qui sic affectus est, non est onerosa paupertas: iste non sentit injurias, ridet opprobria, contemnit damna, mortem lucrum deputat; imo nec mori se putat, cum magis de morte ad vitam transire se sciat, et fidenter dicat; Vadam et videbo eum, antequam moriar.

English translation

5. Now then, my brothers, what does the joy of your heart testify to you about love for Christ? I think indeed—you must judge whether rightly—that if you have ever loved Jesus, whether living, dead, or restored to life, today, when the messengers of the Resurrection sound out so often and in harmony in the Church, your heart within you takes its delight and says: They brought me news, saying: Jesus my God is alive. On hearing this, my spirit revived, which had been dozing from weariness, languishing in lukewarmness, or failing through excessive faintheartedness. For the joyful voice of this happy news raises even sinners from death. Otherwise, surely, that man must be given up for lost and buried in oblivion whom Jesus, returning from the underworld, has left in its deepest depths. By this you may know for certain that your spirit has fully come to life again in Christ: if it sincerely says what follows, It is enough for me if Jesus is alive. O faithful utterance, entirely worthy of Jesus’ friends! O most chaste love, which speaks in this way: It is enough for me if Jesus is alive! If he lives, I live, since my soul depends upon him; indeed, he himself is my life, he himself all that suffices me. For what could I lack if Jesus is alive? Indeed, let everything else be lacking: it matters nothing to me, provided Jesus lives. Let even he himself, then, be absent from me, if that pleases him; it is enough for me, provided he himself lives, even for himself alone. When love for Christ has so absorbed all a person’s affection that, neglectful and forgetful of self, that person is mindful only of Jesus Christ and the things of Jesus Christ, then at last, I think, charity is perfected in that person. For one so disposed, poverty is no burden; he does not feel injuries, laughs at reproaches, despises losses, and counts death as gain. Indeed, he does not even think he is dying, knowing rather that he is passing from death to life, and confidently saying: I will go and see him before I die.

6. Nos tamen, fratres mei, licet non simus tantae nobis puritatis conscii, eamus nihilominus, eamus videre Jesum in montem Galilaeae coelestis, ubi constituit nobis. Eundo crescet affectus, et saltem perveniendo perficietur. Eundo, via prius arcta et difficilis dilatatur, et virtus infirmis augetur. Ut enim nec Jacob, nec aliquis de domo Jacob se excusaret a via praeter alia munera, missa sunt pauperi seni sumptus et vehicula, ne quis videlicet aut paupertatem causaretur, aut infirmitatem. Caro Christi est viaticum, spiritus vehiculum. Ipse est cibus, ipse currus Israel et auriga ejus. Cum perveneris, tua sunt optima quaeque, non Aegypti, sed coeli: in optimo loco regni tuus Joseph tibi requiem providit. Qui prius misit Angelos, mulieres, et Apostolos resurrectionis suae testes et nuntios, nunc ipse clamat e coelo: Ecce ego quem mortuum lugebatis hoc triduo, mortuus quidem fui propter vos, sed ecce vivo, et data est mihi omnis potestas in coelo et in terra (Matth. xxviii, 18). Venite ad me, qui laboratis fame4: et ego reficiam vos. (Id. xi, 28), Venite, benedicti Patris mei, percipite regnum quod vobis paravi (Id, xxv, 34). Qui vos eo vocat, ipse perducat, ubi cum Patre et Spiritu sancto vivit et regnat per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

6. Yet, my brothers, although we are not conscious of such purity in ourselves, let us go nonetheless; let us go to see Jesus on the mountain of heavenly Galilee, where he has appointed to meet us. As we go, love will grow, and at least on arriving it will be perfected. As we go, the road, at first narrow and difficult, grows wider, and strength increases for the weak. For, so that neither Jacob nor anyone of Jacob’s household might excuse himself from the journey, besides the other gifts, provisions and vehicles were sent to the poor old man, so that no one might plead either poverty or weakness. Christ’s flesh is provision for the journey; the spirit is the conveyance. He himself is the food, he himself the chariot of Israel and its driver. When you arrive, all the best things are yours, of heaven rather than Egypt: in the best place in the kingdom your Joseph has provided rest for you. He who first sent angels, women, and apostles as witnesses and messengers of his resurrection now himself cries from heaven: Behold, I whom you mourned as dead these three days was indeed dead for your sake, but behold, I live, and all power in heaven and on earth has been given to me (Matth. xxviii, 18). Come to me, you who labour with hunger4, and I will refresh you. (Id. xi, 28), Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which I have prepared for you (Id, xxv, 34). May he who calls you there himself lead you to where, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, he lives and reigns for ever and ever. Amen.
