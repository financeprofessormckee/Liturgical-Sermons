Editorial introduction

Guerric reads the blessing on those who share in the first resurrection. Christ is both resurrections: by the sacrament of his rising he works the first, of souls, and by its example he will work the second, of bodies. Death reigned from Adam until Christ, free of sin and of the debt of death, died and rose for us. Guerric calls the poor to buy and eat the paschal bread, and warns those who squander in idle talk the sweetness received in devotion. The sermon appears under the Resurrection heading in the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157; the individual sermon is undated.

SERMON II.

De duplici nostra resurrectione.

English translation

On our twofold resurrection.

¶cols. 143-144; cols. 145-146
Latin original

1. Beatus et sanctus qui habet partem in resurrectione prima (Apoc. XX, 51). Ego sum, inquit Jesus, resurrectio et vita (Joan. XI, 25). Ipse utique resurrectio prima, ipse et resurrectio secunda. Resurgens quippe a mortuis primitiæ dormientium Christus, et sacramento suæ resurrectionis operatus est nobis resurrectionem primam, et exemplo ejusdem suæ resurrectionis operabitur nobis secundam. Prima enim est animarum, cum eas sibi conresuscitat in novitatem vitae: secunda erit corporum cum reformabit corpus humilitatis nostrae, configuratum corpori claritatis suae (Philipp. iii, 21). Recte igitur resurrectionem et vitam Christus se profitetur, quandoquidem per ipsum et in ipsum resurgimus, ut secundum ipsum et apud ipsum vivamus: nunc quidem secundum ipsum, in sanctitate et justitia; postmodum apud ipsum, in beatitudine et gloria. Porro sicut resurrectio prima capitis nostri Domini Jesu Christi causa est et argumentum secundae resurrectionis, quae erit totius ipsius corporis, sic unicuique nostrum prima resurrectio animae, qua reviviscit a morte peccati, argumentum est et causa secundae suae resurrectionis, qua corpus liberabitur non modo a corruptione mortis, sed etiam ab omni corruptibilitate mortalitatis. Quod enim haec illius argumentum et causa sit, Apostolus evidenter ostendit, ubi ait: Si spiritus Christi habitat in vobis, qui suscitavit Christum a mortuis vivificabit et mortalia corpora vestra, propter inhabitantem spiritum ejus in vobis (Rom. viii, 11).

English translation

1. Blessed and holy is he who has a part in the first resurrection (Apoc. XX, 51). I am, says Jesus, the resurrection and the life (Joan. XI, 25). He himself is indeed the first resurrection, and he himself the second resurrection. For Christ, rising from the dead, the firstfruits of those who sleep, has by the sacrament of his own resurrection wrought for us the first resurrection, and by the example of that same resurrection of his will accomplish for us the second. For the first is of souls, when he raises them together with himself into newness of life; the second will be of bodies, when he will refashion the body of our lowliness, conformed to the body of his glory (Philipp. iii, 21). Rightly, therefore, does Christ declare himself to be resurrection and life, since it is through him and in him that we rise, that according to him and with him we may live: now indeed according to him, in holiness and justice; afterward with him, in blessedness and glory. Moreover, just as the first resurrection of our Head, the Lord Jesus Christ, is the cause and proof of the second resurrection, which will be that of his whole body, so for each one of us the first resurrection of the soul, by which it revives from the death of sin, is the proof and cause of his second resurrection, by which the body will be freed not only from the corruption of death but also from all corruptibility of mortality. That the one is indeed the proof and cause of the other, the Apostle clearly shows where he says: If the Spirit of Christ dwells in you, he who raised up Christ from the dead will also give life to your mortal bodies, because of his Spirit dwelling in you (Rom. viii, 11).

3 Bene2 igitur dicitur. Beatus et sanctus qui habet partem in prima resurrectione (Apoc. xx, 6). Sanctus videlicet propter primam, quam jam adeptus est per innovationem animae; beatus propter secundam, quam feliciter exspectat in corporis restitutione. Denique unde beatus sit, eadem aperit Scriptura, cum subdit: In his scilicet qui partem habent in prima resurrectione, mors secunda non habet potestatem, etiamsi prima mors visa est ad horam in eis exercuisse dominationem. Regnavit quippe mors ab Adam usque ad Moysen, etiam in eos qui peccaverunt in similitudinem praevaricationis Adae (Rom. v, 14). Sed sicut Christus, sic et christianus resurgens a mortuis, jam non moritur, mors illi ultra non dominabitur (Id. vi, 9). Itaque in beatis illic nec mors secunda potestatem habebit, nec prima potestatem quam ad tempus habuit, retinebit; quoniam mors una Christi utramque nostram triumphavit, ab ista liberans jam captos, ab illa, capiendos; ne videlicet in illam incidamus, nec in ista remaneamus. Quam vera, quam pia simul et magnifica illa morientis comminatio: Ero mors tua, o mors (Osee. xiii, 14)! Quam pulchre et mirifice triumphavit, qui, mortem pro omnibus gustans, tam suam quam omnium omnimodam mortem absorbuit! Prorsus absorpta est mors in victoria. Securus insultet ei quicunque beatus ille est, qui partem habet in resurrectione prima: Ubi est, mors, victoria tua? ubi est, mors, stimulus tuus? Victa es, quae omnia vincebas; sed et arma perdidisti, in quibus confidebas. Ubi enim est stimulus tuus? Stimulus mortis peccatum (I Cor. xv, 55, 56), quod semel pungens humani radicem generis, in totam propaginem immedicabile mortis venenum diffudit, dicente Apostolo quia per unum hominem peccatum, et per peccatum mors, et ita in omnes homines pertransivit (Rom. v, 12). Victrix ergo regnabat mors ab Adam primo etiam usque ad secundum; quoniam sicut originali lege tenebatur universum genus humanum vinculo peccati, sic etiam debito moriendi.

English translation

3. Rightly2, then, is it said: Blessed and holy is he who has a part in the first resurrection (Apoc. xx, 6). He is holy, namely, on account of the first resurrection, which he has already attained through the renewal of the soul; he is blessed on account of the second, which he happily awaits in the restoration of the body. Finally, Scripture itself reveals the reason for this blessedness when it adds: namely, that in those who have a part in the first resurrection, the second death has no power, even though the first death seemed for a time to have exercised dominion over them. For death reigned from Adam to Moses, even over those who sinned after the likeness of Adam's transgression (Rom. v, 14). But just as Christ, so too the Christian, rising from the dead, no longer dies; death will no longer have dominion over him (Id. vi, 9). And so among the blessed there, neither will the second death have power, nor will the first retain the power it held for a time; since the one death of Christ triumphed over both our deaths, freeing from the one those already captive, and from the other those who were to be captured—namely, that we might not fall into that second death, nor remain in this first one. How true, how pious and at the same time how magnificent is that threat of the dying Christ: I will be your death, O death! (Osee. xiii, 14). How beautifully and wonderfully he triumphed, who, tasting death for all, swallowed up death utterly, both his own and that of all! Death is indeed swallowed up in victory. Let whoever is that blessed one who has a part in the first resurrection boldly taunt death: Where, O death, is your victory? Where, O death, is your sting? You who conquered all things are conquered; and you have lost also the weapons in which you trusted. For where is your sting? The sting of death is sin (I Cor. xv, 55, 56), which, once piercing the root of the human race, spread an incurable poison of death through the whole lineage, as the Apostle says, that through one man sin, and through sin death, entered, and so passed into all men (Rom. v, 12). Death, then, reigned as victor from the first Adam even to the second, since, just as the whole human race was held bound by the original law in the bond of sin, so also it was held under the debt of dying.

3. Deo autem gratias, qui dedit nobis victoriam tam peccati quam mortis, utique per Dominum Jesum Christum, qui prorsus a peccato immunis, ac per hoc liber a debito mortis, hanc tamen ultro pro nobis exsolvit moriens, et peccato nos absolvit resurgens. Christus enim, ait Apostolus, mortuus est propter delicta nostra, et resurrexit propter justificationem nostram (Rom. iv, 25) Moriens namque delictorum nostrorum pendit poenam, et resurgens justificationis perpetuae nobis condidit formam et causam, ut, quomodo videlicet Christus resurgens a mortuis jam non moritur, mors illi ultra non dominabitur (Rom. vi, 9), sic christianus conresurgens Christo jam non peccet ad mortem, nec ei peccatum ultra dominetur. Hic est ille beatus et sanctus, qui partem habet in resurrectione prima, in quem nec potestatem habebit mors secunda, sed absorbebitur in victoriam resurrectionis Christi etiam mors prima. Hic est qui non solum agnovit, sed etiam apprehendit virtutem resurrectionis Christi, et societatem passionis illius; configuratus morti ejus, ut occurrat ad resurrectionem ex mortuis. Non errabat Apostolus, qui, ob lucrum illud, omnia quae ei lucra fuerant non solum detrimenta reputabat, sed etiam velut stercora arbitrabatur, tantum ut Christo inveniretur, configuratus sicut morti, sic et resurrectioni ipsius (Philipp. iii, 7-11). Lucrosum omnino commercium, contemnere ea quae te deterunt et polluunt, ut lucrifacias Christum; imo, si opus sit, non solum tua, sed et te ipsum superimpendere, ut cum tanto fenore immortalitatis et gloriae te ipsum merearis recipere! Quis enim ambigat lucrosum esse commercium, seminare corpus mortale, animale, ignobile, ut surgat immortale, spirituale, gloriosum; mori mundo, ut possis dicere, Mihi vivere Christus est, et mori lucrum? (Id. i, 21.) O cupidi, quid immoramini studio lucrandi? cur non discitis artem lucrandi? cur non vilia, imo detrimenta et stercora contemnitis, ut Christum lucrifaciatis? Quare appenditis argentum non in panibus, et laborem vestrum non in saturitate (Isai. lv, 2)? Ut video, vilior est vobis argento vestro panis ille qui de coelo descendit, et dat vitam mundo. Sed non potest scire quanti [ alias quantus. ] sit, qui non vult gustare qualis sit. Utinam avarus vel ipse sibi pretiosior esset pecunia sua, nec amore illius venalem exponeret animam suam, projiceretque in vita sua intima sua (Eccli. x, 10). Prudens ille negotiator dignusque rerum aestimator, Paulum nimirum loquor, qui nec animam suam, id est animalem sensualemque vitam, pretiosiorem fecit se ipso (Act. xx, 24), id est spiritu suo, quo videlicet connexus inhaerebat Christo, paratus animam perdere, ut in vitam aeternam posset eam custodire.

English translation

3. Thanks be to God, who gave us victory over both sin and death, namely through our Lord Jesus Christ, who, being entirely free from sin, and therefore free from the debt of death, nevertheless willingly paid that death for us by dying, and absolved us from sin by rising again. For Christ, says the Apostle, died for our sins, and rose again for our justification (Rom. iv, 25). For by dying he paid the penalty of our sins, and by rising he established for us the pattern and cause of perpetual justification, so that just as Christ, rising from the dead, dies no more, and death shall no longer have dominion over him (Rom. vi, 9), so the Christian, rising together with Christ, may no longer sin unto death, nor may sin have dominion over him any longer. This is that blessed and holy one who has a part in the first resurrection, over whom the second death shall have no power, but even the first death shall be swallowed up in the victory of Christ's resurrection. This is he who has not only recognized but also laid hold of the power of Christ's resurrection, and the fellowship of his passion; being conformed to his death, so that he may attain to the resurrection from the dead. The Apostle was not mistaken, who, for the sake of that gain, counted all the things that had been gains to him not only as losses, but even reckoned them as dung, provided only that he might be found in Christ, conformed both to his death and to his resurrection (Philipp. iii, 7-11). It is altogether a profitable exchange to despise the things that wear you down and defile you, so that you may gain Christ; indeed, if need be, to spend not only your goods but even yourself, so that with such great interest of immortality and glory you may deserve to receive yourself back! For who could doubt that it is a profitable exchange, to sow a mortal, natural, ignoble body, that it may rise immortal, spiritual, glorious; to die to the world, so that you may say, For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain? (Id. i, 21.) O you who are greedy, why do you linger in your zeal for gaining? Why do you not learn the art of gaining? Why do you not despise cheap things — indeed losses and dung — so that you may gain Christ? Why do you weigh out your silver for that which is not bread, and your labor for that which does not satisfy (Isai. lv, 2)? As I see it, that bread which came down from heaven and gives life to the world is of less worth to you than your silver. But one who refuses to taste what it is like cannot know how great it is. Would that the miser were at least more precious to himself than his money, and would not, for love of it, offer his own soul up for sale, and cast away his inmost self while yet alive (Eccli. x, 10). That prudent merchant and worthy appraiser of things — I mean, of course, Paul — who did not reckon his own soul, that is, his natural and sensual life, more precious than himself (Act. xx, 24), that is, than his spirit, by which, closely joined, he clung to Christ, ready to lose his soul so that he might keep it unto eternal life.

4. Quia igitur qui divitias habent, difficile intrant in regnum coelorum; et qui thesaurizant argentum, citius appendunt illud in inanibus, quam in panibus, in azymis dico sinceritatis et veritatis, cum quibus hodie comedendus est Agnus paschalis; vos beati pauperes, filii pauperis Crucifixi, vos inquam, qui non habetis argentum, properate, emite et comedite. Promptius omnino et facilius emunt bonum illud nihil habentes, quam multa possidentes. Cum deest facultas, sufficit ad emendum bona voluntas, qua plerumque sunt ditiores, qui rebus sunt pauperiores. Hos nimirum recte invitat Scriptura: Venite, emite absque argento, et absque ulla commutatione, vinum et lac (Isai. LV, 1). Vides, o felix pauper, solam a te voluntatem bonam exigi, in illa sola rationem tanti constare commercii? Ne recuses ingratus quod tam gratis offertur, imo ne perdas ingrata voluntate, quod jam merueras beata paupertate. Agnosce quanti lucri sit, non habere partem in ruina mundi, ut partem habeas in resurrectione Christi. Intellige quantae felicitatis sit, non inebriari luxu et furore saeculi, ut cum Christo novum bibas vinum in regno Patris sui. Invitat ipse Agnus paschalis amicos ad delicias corporis et sanguinis sui: Comedite, inquiens, amici, bibite, et inebriamini, charissimi (Cantic. V, 1). Cibus iste et potus, vitae est mysterium, immortalitatis medicamentum, causa resurrectionis primae, pignusque secundae, quia divinae plane in nobis initium substantiae. Participes, inquit Apostolus, Christi effecti sumus, si tamen initium substantiae ejus usque ad finem firmum retineamus (Hebr. III, 14). Qui enim post acceptam gratiam ad vomitum suum revertitur, Divitias quas devoravit evomet, et de ventre illius extrahet eas Deus. Vel certe panis ejus in utero illius vertetur in fel aspidum intrinsecus: quia nimirum gratia percepta in poenam conscientiae vertitur, cum sanguis Testamenti, quo sanctificatus quis fuerat, pollutus ducitur, et Spiritui gratiae contumelia irrogatur. Hoc contemptu et nausea divitiae devoratae evomuntur, ita ut de eo qui ejusmodi est, dicatur: Non remansit quidquam de cibo ejus, et propterea nihil permanebit de bonis illius.

English translation

4. Therefore, since those who have riches enter the kingdom of heaven with difficulty, and those who hoard up silver sooner weigh it out for empty things than for bread — I mean the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth, with which the paschal Lamb is to be eaten today — you blessed poor, children of the poor Crucified One, you, I say, who have no silver, hasten, buy and eat. Those who have nothing buy that good thing far more readily and easily than those who possess much. When means are lacking, good will suffices for the purchase — and those who are poorer in goods are often the richer in that very will. It is indeed such as these whom Scripture rightly invites: Come, buy wine and milk without silver, and without any price (Isai. LV, 1). Do you see, O happy poor man, that only good will is demanded of you, and that the whole worth of so great a transaction consists in that alone? Do not ungratefully refuse what is offered so freely; rather, do not lose, by an ungrateful will, what you had already merited by blessed poverty. Recognize how great a gain it is to have no part in the ruin of the world, so that you may have a part in the resurrection of Christ. Understand how great a happiness it is not to be made drunk with the luxury and fury of this age, so that you may drink new wine with Christ in the kingdom of his Father. The paschal Lamb himself invites his friends to the delights of his body and blood: Eat, he says, O friends, drink, and be inebriated, O dearly beloved (Cantic. V, 1). This food and drink is the mystery of life, the medicine of immortality, the cause of the first resurrection, and the pledge of the second, because it is plainly in us the beginning of divine substance. We have been made partakers, says the Apostle, of Christ, if indeed we hold the beginning of his substance firm unto the end (Hebr. III, 14). For he who, after receiving grace, returns to his own vomit, shall vomit up the riches he had devoured, and God shall draw them out of his belly. Or certainly, within him, his bread shall be turned to the gall of asps in his belly — because indeed the grace received is turned into punishment of conscience, when the blood of the Testament, by which one had been sanctified, is accounted polluted, and insult is done to the Spirit of grace. By this contempt and loathing, the riches that had been devoured are vomited up, so that of such a one it is said: Nothing remained of his food, and therefore nothing of his goods shall remain.

5. Videat autem ne forte et ipsum terribilis ista perstringat sententia, qui, cum per gratiam devotionis repletus fuerit in bonis domus Dei, nihil prorsus ex illis in memoria retinet, ut memoriam abundantiae suavitatis divinae nobis eructet; non saporem in sermonibus, tanquam gustum in faucibus; non virtutem in moribus, tanquam succum in visceribus; sed dum statim totum per inania vel scurrilia revomit, gratiam sibi in iram convertit. Prorsus in iram, et iram terribilem, si in ipsum cadit quod Scriptura contexuit dicens: Cum satiatus fuerit, arctabitur, et irruet in eum omnis dolor. Utinam impleatur venter ejus, ut immittat in eum iram furoris sui, et pluat super eum bellum suum (Job XX, 15, 21, 23). Justum quippe sentiebat atque consentiebat Propheta ut pluat super peccatores bellum, qui ad pluviam voluntariam, fructum pacis non retulerunt; sitque ignis, sulphur et spiritus procellarum pars calicis eorum, qui calicem Domini indigne biberunt. Terra enim saepe venientem bibens imbrem, et generans herbam opportunam, accipit benedictionem; proferens autem spinas et tribulos, reproba est et maledictio proxima; cujus consummatio in combustionem (Hebr. VI, 7, 8). Confidimus autem de vobis, fratres, meliora et viciniora saluti. Tantum gratiae Dei grati sitis, et sicut in novam creaturam translati estis paschalibus mysteriis, sic in novitate vitae semper ambuletis. Qui ergo participes Christi effecti estis societate fidei, participatione sacramenti, communione Spiritus sancti, initium substantiae ejus non solum usque ad finem firmum retinere, sed etiam sedulo augere nitimini: ut, qui per tot munerum praerogativas partem habere coepistis in resurrectione prima, tanti fiducia pignoris in die agnitionis consignati, jus vobis perpetuum vindicetis in resurrectione secunda, praestante Domino nostro Jesu Christo, resurrectione et vita nostra, qui pro nobis mortuus per triduum, jam vivit et regnat per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

5. But let him take care lest this terrible sentence strike him as well who, when he has been filled through the grace of devotion with the good things of the house of God, retains nothing at all of them in memory from which to belch forth—no savor in his speech, as a taste lingering in the mouth; no virtue in his conduct, as sap in the inward parts—but who, straightway vomiting back the whole through empty or scurrilous things, turns grace into wrath for himself. Into wrath indeed, and terrible wrath, if upon him falls what Scripture has woven together, saying: 'When he shall be filled, he shall be straitened, and every sorrow shall rush upon him. Would that his belly might be filled, that God may send upon him the wrath of His fury, and rain down His war upon him' (Job XX, 15, 21, 23). For the Prophet rightly judged, and rightly consented, that war should rain down upon sinners who, in return for the willing rain, did not bring forth the fruit of peace; and that fire, brimstone, and a spirit of storms should be the portion of the cup of those who drank the cup of the Lord unworthily. For the earth, which often drinks in the rain that comes upon it, and brings forth herbage fit for use, receives a blessing; but that which brings forth thorns and briers is worthless and near to a curse, whose end is to be burned (Hebr. VI, 7, 8). But we are confident of better things concerning you, brothers, and things nearer to salvation. Only be grateful for the grace of God, and just as you have been transformed into a new creature by the paschal mysteries, so may you always walk in newness of life. You, then, who have been made partakers of Christ through fellowship of faith, participation in the sacrament, and communion of the Holy Spirit, strive not only to hold firm the beginning of his substance even to the end, but also diligently to increase it: so that you, who through so many prerogatives of gifts have begun to have part in the first resurrection, sealed by the assurance of so great a pledge on the day of recognition, may claim for yourselves a perpetual right in the second resurrection, through the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, our resurrection and our life, having been dead for us for three days, now lives and reigns through all ages of ages. Amen.
