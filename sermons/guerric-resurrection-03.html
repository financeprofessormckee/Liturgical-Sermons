Editorial introduction

Guerric calls the brothers to watchfulness, so that they may share in the resurrection of Christ, the morning of a day without evening. Like Abraham and the women at the tomb, those who keep vigil at the doors of Wisdom see the risen Christ, who meets those who pray and those who work. Elisha's raising of the Shunammite's son marks the stages of the soul's return to life: a warmed heart, sighs that enlarge desire, and eyes opened to contemplation. The sermon appears under the Resurrection heading in the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157; the individual sermon is undated.

SERMON III.

¶cols. 147-148
Latin original

De vigilantia necessaria ut partem habeamus in resurrectione Christi.

English translation

On the vigilance necessary that we may have a part in the resurrection of Christ.

¶cols. 147-148; cols. 149-150
Latin original

1. Beatus et sanctus qui habet partem in resurrectione prima (Apoc. XX, 6). Primitiae dormientium Christus, primogenitus ex mortuis, quia sua resurrectione, quae prima est omnium, et primam nobis resurrectionem animarum, et secundam corporum dedicavit; dum in corpore suo, quod suscitavit a mortuis, et animabus sacramentum, et corporibus exemplum resurgendi nuntiavit. Sed et animabus ipsis duplicem gratiam resurrectionis simplex Christi resurrectio praeparavit, dum et quotidie reviviscunt a morte peccati per operationem mysterii, et hodie maxime resurgunt a somno torporis per devotionem gaudii. Quis enim ille tam piger aut tepidus, qui hodie audiens vocem illam omni gaudio plenam, Resurrexit Dominus, non totus in exsultationem suscitetur, non totus reviviscat et recalescat spiritu? Imo et cor meum et caro mea exsultaverunt, inquit, in Deum vivum (Psal. LXXXIII, 3), qui totus concideram in moerorem ac desperationem, Jesum aspiciens mortuum. Non mediocri lucro fidei, nec parvo fenore gaudii, Jesus mihi de sepulchro redit, dum et Deus vivus agnoscitur, qui paulo ante ut homo mortuus lugebatur, et quem occisum cor meum dolebat, jam in eum vivum non solum cor meum, sed et caro mea exsultat, de sua scilicet per eum resurrectione et immortalitate secura. O anima mea, Ego dormivi et exsurrexi (Psal. III, 6), ait Christus; surge et tu, quae dormis, et exsurge a mortuis, et illuminabit te Christus (Ephes. V, 14). An non, fratres, mortuo similis, qui adhuc stetit orto jam sole, qui adhuc negligentia et socordia premitur, quasi desperato sepultus torpore, jam ubique gratia resurrectionis rutilante? Ferit oculos Sol novus emergens ab inferis, his qui de mane vigilant ad eum, diem reserans aeternitatis. Dies iste vesperam nescit, quia non occidet ultra sol ejus, qui semel occidens, semel ascendit super occasum, mortem sibi subjiciens.

English translation

1. Blessed and holy is he who has a part in the first resurrection (Apoc. XX, 6). Christ is the firstfruits of those who sleep, the firstborn from the dead, because by his resurrection, which is first of all, he consecrated for us both the first resurrection, that of souls, and the second, that of bodies; since in his body, which he raised from the dead, he announced to souls a sacrament, and to bodies an example, of rising again. But Christ's single resurrection also prepared a twofold grace of resurrection for souls themselves, since they daily come back to life from the death of sin through the working of the mystery, and today especially they rise from the sleep of sluggishness through the devotion of joy. For who is so sluggish or lukewarm that, hearing today that word so full of all joy, 'The Lord has risen,' is not wholly roused to exultation, does not wholly come back to life and grow warm again in spirit? Indeed, 'my heart and my flesh have exulted,' he says, 'in the living God' (Psal. LXXXIII, 3) — I who had wholly collapsed into grief and despair, beholding Jesus dead. With no small gain of faith, nor with slight interest of joy, Jesus returns to me from the tomb, since he who a little before, as man, was mourned as dead, is now recognized as the living God; and he whom, slain, my heart grieved over, now — living — not only my heart but my flesh as well exults in him, being made secure, through him, of its own resurrection and immortality. O my soul, 'I have slept and risen up' (Psal. III, 6), says Christ; rise up, you too who sleep, and arise from the dead, and Christ will enlighten you (Ephes. V, 14). Is he not, brothers, like one dead, who still stands motionless when the sun has already risen, who is still weighed down by negligence and sloth, as if buried in hopeless torpor, now that the grace of the resurrection glows red everywhere? The new Sun, emerging from the world below, strikes the eyes of those who watch for him early in the morning, unlocking for them the day of eternity. This day knows no evening, because its sun will set no more — he who, setting once, rose once above his setting, subjecting death to himself.

2. O fratres, haec dies quam fecit Dominus, exsultemus et laetemur in ea (Psal. CXVII, 24). Exsultemus in spe ejus, ut videamus et gaudeamus in luce ejus. Exsultavit Abraham ut videret diem Christi, et hoc merito vidit, et gavisus est (Joan. VIII, 56). Nam et tu quoque si vigiles quotidie ad fores Sapientiae, et observes ad postes ejus, pervigilque excubes cum illa Magdalena ad ostium monumenti ejus (Matth. XXVII. 61), probabis, ni fallor, et ipse cum eadem Maria (Luc. XXIV, 1-8), quam verum sit quod de ipsa Sapientia, quae Christus est, legitur: Facile videtur ab his qui diligunt eam, et invenitur ab his qui quaerunt illam. Praeoccupat qui se concupiscunt, ut illis se prior ostendat. Qui de luce vigilaverit ad illam, non laborabit assidentem enim illam foribus suis inveniet (Sap. VI, 13-15). Sic enim et ipse promisit dicens: Ego diligentes me diligo, et qui mane vigilaverint ad me, invenient me (Prov. VIII, 17). Invenit quidem Maria corporaliter Jesum ad quem vigilabat, ad cujus monumentum, cum adhuc essent tenebrae, excubare venerat. Tu vero qui jam non debes nosse Jesum secundum carnem, sed secundum spiritum, spiritualiter nimirum eum invenire poteris, si simili eum desiderio quaesieris, si te similiter pervigilem in oratione adverterit. Dic ergo Domino Jesu desiderio et affectu Mariae: Anima mea desideravit te in nocte, sed et spiritus meus in praecordiis meis; de mane vigilabo ad te (Isai. XXVI, 9). Dic voce et mente Psalmistae: Deus, Deus meus, ad te de luce vigilo: sitivit in te anima mea (Psal. LXII, 2). Et vide si non cum eis tibi cantare congruerit: Repleti sumus mane misericordia tua et exsultavimus, et delectati sumus (Psal. LXXXIX, 14).

English translation

2. O brothers, 'this is the day which the Lord made; let us exult and rejoice in it' (Psal. CXVII, 24). Let us exult in hope of it, that we may see and rejoice in its light. Abraham exulted that he might see the day of Christ, and this he deservedly saw, and rejoiced (Joan. VIII, 56). For you too, if you watch daily at the doors of Wisdom, and keep watch at her posts, and keep vigil, like that Magdalene, at the door of his tomb (Matth. XXVII. 61), will prove, unless I am mistaken, together with that same Mary (Luc. XXIV, 1-8), how true is what is read of Wisdom herself, who is Christ: 'She is easily seen by those who love her, and is found by those who seek her. She anticipates those who desire her, that she may show herself to them first. He who watches for her from early light shall not labor, for he shall find her sitting at his doors' (Sap. VI, 13-15). For he himself promised thus, saying: 'I love those who love me, and those who watch for me early shall find me' (Prov. VIII, 17). Mary indeed found, bodily, the Jesus for whom she watched, having come to keep vigil at his tomb while it was still dark. But you, who ought now to know Jesus no longer according to the flesh but according to the spirit, will surely be able to find him spiritually, if you seek him with a like desire, if he finds you likewise keeping vigil in prayer. Say therefore to the Lord Jesus, with the desire and affection of Mary: 'My soul has desired you in the night, and my spirit also within me; from the morning I will watch for you' (Isai. XXVI, 9). Say with the voice and mind of the Psalmist: 'O God, my God, from early light I watch for you; my soul has thirsted for you' (Psal. LXII, 2). And see whether it will not be fitting for you to sing with them: 'We have been filled in the morning with your mercy, and we have exulted and been delighted' (Psal. LXXXIX, 14).

3. Vigilate ergo, fratres, in orationibus intenti, vigilate in actionibus circumspecti; praesertim quia jam mane illius diei indeclinabilis inclaruit, postquam videlicet lux aeterna serenior nobis et gratior ab inferis rediit, solemque novum susceptio matutina reparavit. Prorsus hora est jam nos de somno surgere, postquam nox praecessit, dies autem appropinquavit (Rom. XIII, 11. 12). Vigilate, inquam, ut oriatur vobis matutina lux, Christus scilicet, cujus quasi diluculum praeparatus est egressus, paratus utique vigilantibus ad se, mysterium suae matutinae resurrectionis saepius innovare. Tunc utique cantabis in jubilo cordis, Deus Dominus illuxit nobis. Haec dies quam fecit Dominus, exsultemus et laetemur in ea (Psal. CXVII, 27. 24), cum videlicet lucem quam in manibus abscondit interlucere tibi permiserit, annuntians de ea amico quod possessio ejus sit, et ad eam possit ascendere. Usquequo, piger, dormis? usquequo dormitas? Paululum dormies, paululum dormitabis, paululum conseres manus ut dormias (Prov. VI, 9, 10, et XXIV, 33); et cum te dormiente, te nesciente Christus de sepulcro surrexerit, et gloria ejus transeunte, nec posteriora ejus videre merueris; tunc sera poenitentia planges, et dices cum impiis, Ergo erravimus a via veritatis, et justitiae lumen non luxit nobis, et sol intelligentiae non est ortus nobis (Sap. V, 6). Vobis autem, inquit, timentibus nomen meum, orietur sol justitiae (Malach. IV, 2); et, qui ambulat in justitiis, Regem in decore suo videbunt oculi ejus (Isai. XXXIII, 15, 17). Et id quidem beatitudo est vitae sequentis, sed juxta quemdam modum indulgetur etiam ad consolationem praesentis, sicut evidenter probat resurrectio Christi. In multis siquidem argumentis per dies quadraginta Sapientia nobis probavit quoniam dignos se ipsa circuit quaerens, et in viis suis ostendit se illis hilariter, et in omni prudentia occurrit illis (Sap. VI, 17.) Ut enim Jesus se illam esse sapientiam, de qua haec scripta sunt, ostenderet; et, quod quotidie spiritualiter actitare non desinit, id est, in viis justitiae se ostendere hilariter, hodie corporalis etiam operis specie figuraret, in via hodie occurrit mulieribus a monumento redeuntibus, in via se ostendit duobus euntibus in Emmaus (Luc. XXIV, 3, 4, 13-27).

English translation

3. Watch, then, brothers, intent on your prayers; watch, circumspect in your actions—especially since now the morning of that unending day has shone forth, since indeed the eternal light, more serene and more welcome to us, has returned from the underworld, and the morning's rising has restored to us a new sun. Truly it is now the hour for us to rise from sleep, since the night is past and the day is at hand (Rom. XIII, 11. 12). Watch, I say, that the morning light may rise for you—namely Christ, whose going forth is prepared like the dawn, ready indeed, for those who watch for him, to renew again and again the mystery of his morning resurrection. Then truly you will sing with the jubilation of your heart: 'The Lord God has shone upon us. This is the day which the Lord has made; let us exult and be glad in it' (Psal. CXVII, 27. 24)—when, that is, he allows the light which he hides in his hands to shine through to you, declaring of it to his friend that it is his possession, and that he can ascend to it. How long, O sluggard, will you sleep? How long will you slumber? A little you will sleep, a little you will slumber, a little you will fold your hands to sleep (Prov. VI, 9, 10, et XXIV, 33); and when, while you are sleeping, while you know nothing of it, Christ has risen from the tomb, and his glory has passed by, and you have not merited even to see his back as he goes; then with late repentance you will weep, and say with the wicked: 'So we have strayed from the way of truth, and the light of righteousness has not shined on us, and the sun of understanding has not risen for us' (Sap. V, 6). But to you, he says, who fear my name, the sun of righteousness shall rise (Malach. IV, 2); and he who walks in righteousness—his eyes shall see the King in his beauty (Isai. XXXIII, 15, 17). And this indeed is the blessedness of the life to come, but in a certain measure it is granted even for the consolation of the present life, as the resurrection of Christ plainly proves. For by many proofs, over forty days, Wisdom showed us that she herself goes about seeking out those worthy of her, and shows herself to them cheerfully in their ways, and meets them with all prudence (Sap. VI, 17.). For in order that Jesus might show himself to be that very Wisdom of whom these things are written, and might today figure, even under the appearance of a bodily deed, what he never ceases to do daily in a spiritual way—that is, to show himself cheerfully in the ways of righteousness—today he met the women returning from the tomb on the road, and on the road he showed himself to the two going to Emmaus (Luc. XXIV, 3, 4, 13-27).

4. Audiant et gaudeant qui in viis justitiae ambulant; audiant, inquam, quia non solum inhaerentes studio contemplationis, sed etiam ambulantes juste ac pie vias actionis, Jesus dignatur et occursu et manifestatione sui. Agnoscit, ni fallor, experientia quorumdam vestrum, quia saepe Jesus quem quaesierunt velut ad monumentum, ad memorias altarium, nec invenerunt, insperatus occurrit eis in viis laborum. Tunc nimirum accesserunt, et tenuerunt pedes ejus, quorum scilicet pedes non tenuerat pigritia, prae desiderio ejus. Noli ergo tu, frater, nimis parcere pedibus tuis a viis obedientiae et discursibus operum, quandoquidem Jesus pedibus suis non perpercit propter te etiam a dolore clavorum; et adhuc eorumdem amplexu et osculo pedum non gravatur laborem remunerare aut relevare pedum tuorum. Nam et illud quantae consolationis erit, si se tibi viae comitem adjunxerit, miraque delectatione suae sermocinationis, etiam sensum tibi laboris ademerit, aperiens tibi sensum, ut intelligas Scripturas, quas fortasse domi sedens legebas et non intelligebas? Obsecro vos, fratres mei, quibus hoc aliquando dedit experiri dignatio divina, nonne cor vestrum ardens erat in vobis de Jesu, cum loqueretur vobis in via, et aperiret Scripturas? Recordentur igitur illud experti, et cantent in viis Domini, quoniam magna est gloria Domini; credant, et experiri satagant inexperti, ut aliquando et ipsis cantabiles fiant justificationes Dei, in loco peregrinationis suae et afflictionis.

English translation

4. Let those who walk in the ways of righteousness hear and rejoice; let them hear, I say, that Jesus deigns to grant both his meeting and the manifestation of himself not only to those who cling to the pursuit of contemplation, but also to those who walk justly and devoutly in the ways of action. The experience of some of you recognizes, unless I am mistaken, that often Jesus, whom they sought as it were at the tomb, at the memorials of the altars, and did not find, meets them unexpectedly in the ways of their labors. Then indeed they approached and held his feet—those, namely, whose feet laziness had not held back, because of their desire for him. Do not, then, brother, spare your feet too much from the ways of obedience and the busy comings and goings of works, since Jesus did not spare his own feet for your sake, even from the pain of the nails; and even now he is not burdened, by the embrace and kiss of those same feet, to reward or relieve the labor of your feet. For how great a consolation it will be, if he joins himself to you as a companion on the road, and by the wondrous delight of his conversation even takes from you the very feeling of your labor, opening your understanding, that you may understand the Scriptures which perhaps, sitting at home, you were reading and not understanding? I beseech you, my brothers, to whom the divine condescension has at some time granted to experience this—was not your heart burning within you concerning Jesus, when he spoke to you on the road and opened the Scriptures? Let those, then, who have experienced this remember it, and sing in the ways of the Lord, for great is the glory of the Lord; and let those who have not experienced it believe, and strive earnestly to experience it, that at some time the justifications of God may become songs even for them, in the place of their pilgrimage and affliction.

5. Resurgat itaque ac reviviscat spiritus omnium nostrum, sive ad vigilantiam orandi, sive ad instantiam operandi, ut quadam rediviva ac vivida alacritate probet se de novo portionem accepisse in resurrectione Christi. Et primum quidem vitae in hominem redeuntis indicium est, si strenuus et impiger sit ad actionem; nam perfecta resurrectio ejus est in hoc duntaxat moribundo corpore, si oculos aperiat ad contemplationem. Quod tamen non ante promeretur intellectus, quam crebris suspiriis et vehementibus desideriis dilatetur affectus, ut tantae nimirum capax majestatis reddatur. Quod videlicet velut per quosdam gradus incrementum resurgendi signanter figuratum arbitror in mortuo quem Elisaeus suscitavit, qui cum ad vitam redire inciperet, primo dicitur quia calefacta est caro pueri; deinde, quia septies oscitavit, et sic demum oculos aperuit (IV Reg. IV, 34, 35). Caro pueri, cor carneum est in Christo parvuli, cui prima spes vitae est, si dicere possit: Concaluit cor meum intra me, et in meditatione mea exardescet ignis (Psal. XXXVIII, 4). Quanquam et vestimenta ejus calida sint, cum terra ejus perflata austro, Spiritu utique sancto, quem verus Elisaeus praeveniendo aspirat redivivo. Amplius autem et evidentius ad resurrectionem proficit, cum desiderio et esurie quadam justitiae oscitare frequentius incipit, illo scilicet modo quo oscitabat qui dicebat: Os meum aperui et attraxi spiritum, quia mandata tua desiderabam (Psal. CXVIII, 131). Oscitatio quippe ista, distensio est affectus, ut capacior fiat spiritus vitae, quatenus post alia charismata septiformis gratiae, infusus etiam spiritus intellectus et sapientiae, oculos tandem ad contemplandum Deum faciat aperire. Primus ergo calor vitae redeuntis est, cum bonus exercetur actus: secundus resurrectionis profectus, cum per orationem dilatatur affectus: perfectio autem, cum ad contemplandum illuminatur intellectus. His gradibus virtutum, his sanctioris vitae incrementis, fratres mei, resurgere magis ac magis enitimini, si quo modo occurratis, ut Apostolus ait, ad resurrectionem Christi ex mortuis (Philipp. III, 11), qui vivit et regnat per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

5. Let the spirit of us all, therefore, rise again and come back to life, whether for watchfulness in prayer or for eagerness in working, so that by a certain revived and lively readiness it may prove that it has newly received a share in the resurrection of Christ. And the first sign indeed of life returning into a man is if he be vigorous and unwearied for action; for his resurrection is perfect in this mortal body only to this extent, that he open his eyes to contemplation. Yet the intellect does not merit this before the affection is first enlarged by frequent sighs and ardent desires, so that it may be made capable of so great a majesty. This increase of rising again I think is signified, as it were by certain steps, in the dead man whom Elisha raised to life; who, when he began to return to life, is said first to have had the flesh of the boy grow warm; then, that he yawned seven times, and only then at last opened his eyes (IV Reg. IV, 34, 35). The flesh of the boy is the heart of flesh in a little one in Christ, for whom the first hope of life is if he can say: My heart grew warm within me, and in my meditation a fire shall flame out (Psal. XXXVIII, 4). And indeed his garments too are warm, when his earth is fanned by the south wind — that is, by the Holy Spirit, whom the true Elisha, going before, breathes into the one being revived. But he progresses further and more plainly toward resurrection when he begins to yawn more frequently with a certain desire and hunger for righteousness, in that manner in which he yawned who said: I opened my mouth and drew in breath, because I longed for your commandments (Psal. CXVIII, 131). For that yawning is a stretching of the affection, so that the affection may become more capable of receiving the spirit of life, until, after the other gifts of the sevenfold grace, the spirit of understanding and wisdom also being poured in, it at last makes the eyes open to the contemplation of God. The first warmth, then, of returning life is when a good act is exercised; the second advance of resurrection, when the affection is enlarged through prayer; but the perfection, when the understanding is illumined for contemplation. By these steps of virtues, by these increases of a holier life, my brothers, strive to rise more and more, that you may somehow attain, as the Apostle says, to the resurrection of Christ from the dead (Philipp. III, 11), who lives and reigns through all ages of ages. Amen.
