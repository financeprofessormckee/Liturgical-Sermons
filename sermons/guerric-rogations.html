Editorial introduction

Guerric takes the friend who asks for three loaves at midnight as the figure of one who must feed his brothers with the word. He confesses that he presides by office without bread of his own, asks God to make him humble and useful, and pleads the merit of those to be fed. He leaves the loaves of speculation on the Trinity to the angels, since God is to be believed rather than discussed, and sets out other threefold loaves from Scripture. The sermon appears under the Rogation Days heading in the 1879 edition of Patrologia Latina. The collection is associated approximately with Guerric's abbacy in 1138–1157; the individual sermon is undated.

English translation

On asking for and setting forth the bread of the word

¶cols. 151-152
Latin original

1. Commoda mihi tres panes Luc. XI, 5)1. Amici venerunt ad nos de via, sed non habeo quod ponam ante illos. Non sum medicus, et in domo mea non est panis: ideo dicebam ab initio, Nolite me constituere principem (Isai. III, 7). Non enim debet praeesse, qui non potest prodesse. Quomodo autem prodesse potest, qui nec medicus est, nec panem in domo habet, id est, nec artem novit qua sciat curare, nec doctrinam habet qua sufficiat pascere? Haec quidem dicebam; sed heu! non audistis me: principem enim constituistis me. Supererat igitur ut, quia non potui effugere periculum, confugerem ad remedium, et audirem super hoc illud Sapientis consilium: Principem te constituerunt; esto inter illos quasi unus ex ipsis (Eccli. XXXII, 1). Sed vae mihi! neque hoc ipsum relictum est mihi. Sicut enim imperitia prohibet esse super alios, sic imbecillitas non patitur inter alios esse; et sicut spiritu non sufficio ad ministrandum verbum, sic corpore deficio ad praebendum exemplum. Qui igitur idoneus non sum nec praeesse, nec coesse: ubi potero esse, nisi ultimum et tutissimum eligam locum, scilicet omnibus subesse? Et hoc quidem valeo, humilia scilicet, imo vera de me sentiendo; nihilque me prohibet, imo plurimum veritas ipsa monet, subesse cunctis animo, licet praeesse cogar officio.

English translation

1. "Lend me three loaves" Luc. XI, 5)1. Friends have come to us from the road, but I have nothing to set before them. I am not a physician, and in my house there is no bread: therefore I said from the beginning, "Do not make me a ruler" (Isai. III, 7). For he who cannot be of use ought not to be set over others. But how can he be of use who is neither a physician nor has bread in his house—that is, who neither knows the skill by which he might know how to heal, nor has the teaching by which he might suffice to feed? This indeed I said; but alas! you did not listen to me: for you made me a ruler. It remained, then, since I could not escape the danger, that I should take refuge in the remedy, and hear on this matter that counsel of the Wise Man: "They have made you a ruler; be among them as one of themselves" (Eccli. XXXII, 1). But woe is me! not even this was left to me. For just as inexperience forbids being over others, so weakness does not allow one to be among others; and just as in spirit I am not sufficient to minister the word, so in body I fail to provide example. Since, then, I am fit neither to preside nor to stand as a companion among others, where can I be, unless I choose the last and safest place—namely, to be beneath all? And this indeed I am able to do, that is, by thinking humbly—or rather truly—of myself; nor does anything prevent me, indeed the truth itself most strongly urges me, to be beneath all in spirit, though I am compelled by office to preside.

2. Tu, Domine Deus, ipse es qui et subesse mones, et nihilominus praeesse jubes; a te expeto, a te exspecto ut humilem simul et utilem in injuncto me facias ministerio: humilem, vera de me sentiendo; utilem, recta de te loquendo. Illud inspira cordi, hoc ministra ori. Da sermonem rectum et bene sonantem in apertione oris mei, qui dixisti, Aperi os tuum et implebo illud (Psal. LXXX, 11), ut omnis familia tua benedictionibus impleatur. Ecce venerunt amici, mei quidem amici, sed amplius tui. Non habeo quod ponam ante illos, nisi commodatum fuerit ab alio. Et quis alius aeque dives aut liberalis ad praestandum, sicut Dominus omnium, dives in omnes qui invocant illum (Rom. X, 12); qui aperit manum suam, et implet omne animal benedictione (Psal. CXLIV, 16), qui dat omnibus affluenter, et non improperat (Jacobi I, 5), nisi forte illi qui vel pigre petit, vel ingrate detinet gratiam quam accepit? Quanti mercenarii in domo Patrisfamilias istius abundant panibus, qui, quoniam Christum annuntiant, licet non sincere, propter alios tamen eis doctrinae gratia non negatur? Et ubi abundant mercenarii, nunquid egebunt filii? Tu ergo, Domine (amicum enim dicere vereor, sed Dominum confiteor), commoda mihi tres panes, unde reficiantur amici, ne, si dimisero eos jejunos, deficiant in via, et tunc vocer in causam pro eis, et dicatur mihi: Parvuli petierunt panem, et non fuit qui frangeret eis (Thren. IV, 5). Commoda Domine, quod in lucrum tuum proficiat, recepturus utique cum tibi placuerit, quod tuum est, cum usura. Commoda, inquam, tres panes, si placet; aut certe quodcunque placet, quantulumque fuerit, etiamsi buccella panis sufficiet in quantalibet millia, si tantum benedicas. Scio equidem quia vis nos esse tibi importunos, ut, licet dissimules, licet excuses, eo quod jam in coelum receptus sis, et Apostoli tui tecum sint in cubili, nos tamen nihilominus perseveremus in petendo, quaerendo, pulsando, sciente quia doctrinam non sola vitae meretur innocentia, qua amici efficimur, sine assiduitate studii et instantia precis, qua importuni videamur. Mihi tamen neutrum suffragatur: solum eorum qui pascendi sunt meritum obtendo; illi merentur quod ego non mereor.

English translation

2. You, Lord God, are the very one who both counsel me to be beneath, and nonetheless command me to preside; from you I ask, from you I expect, that you may make me at once humble and useful in the ministry laid upon me: humble, by thinking truly of myself; useful, by speaking rightly of you. Breathe the one into my heart; supply the other to my mouth. Give a right word, well-sounding, at the opening of my mouth, you who said, "Open your mouth, and I will fill it" (Psal. LXXX, 11), that all your household may be filled with blessings. Behold, friends have come—my friends indeed, but yours even more. I have nothing to set before them unless it be lent me by another. And who else is equally rich or generous in granting, as the Lord of all, rich toward all who call upon him (Rom. X, 12); who opens his hand and fills every living thing with blessing (Psal. CXLIV, 16); who gives to all abundantly and does not reproach (Jacobi I, 5)—unless perhaps to him who either asks sluggishly, or ungratefully withholds the grace he has received? How many hired men abound with loaves in the house of this Father of the family, who, because they proclaim Christ—though not sincerely—yet for the sake of others the grace of teaching is not denied them? And where hired men abound, shall the sons be in want? You, therefore, Lord—for I fear to call you friend, yet I confess you Lord—lend me three loaves, from which my friends may be refreshed, lest, if I send them away hungry, they faint along the way, and I then be called to account for them, and it be said to me: "The little ones asked for bread, and there was none to break it for them" (Thren. IV, 5). Lend, Lord, what will turn to your profit, since you will certainly receive back, when it pleases you, what is yours, with interest. Lend, I say, three loaves, if it please you; or indeed whatever pleases you, however small it be—even if a single morsel of bread will suffice for however many thousands, if only you bless it. I know well that you wish us to be importunate with you, so that, although you pretend otherwise, although you make excuse on the ground that you have already been received into heaven, and your Apostles are with you in your chamber, we should nevertheless persevere in asking, seeking, knocking—knowing that teaching is not merited by innocence of life alone, by which we become friends, without diligence of study and the insistence of prayer, by which we may seem importunate. Yet neither of these avails for me: I plead only the merit of those who are to be fed; they deserve what I do not deserve.

3. Vos autem, fratres mei, usibus quorum, et pro meritis quorum peto panes istos, putatis, sufficimus, sive ego ad frangendum, sive vos ad edendum? Timeo ne dicatur mihi: Altiora te ne quaesieris, et fortiora te ne scrutatus fueris (Eccli. III, 21). Timeo ne dicatur et vobis: Facti estis quibus opus est lacte, non pane (Hebr. V, 12). Scio revera panes esse apud Patrem familias, quos si nostra praesumat infantia, citius frangentur dentes, quam impleantur ventres, id est aedificentur mentes. Quis enim capiat, quis explicare aut cogitare digne sufficiat illud Trinitatis mysterium ineffabile: quomodo Pater a se, Filius a Patre, Spiritus sanctus ab utroque, et tres personae in unitate substantiae? Mulier insipiens, audax haereticorum vanitas, prurientes auribus ad talia discutienda sollicitat, cum Deus credendus sit, non discutiendus. Panes, inquit, occultos libenter attingite. Quasi vero tu insipiens attigeris, quod sublimius est Angelis. Et ad quid mihi panes occultos attingere, quos non licet sine periculo frangere vel edere? Sufficit mihi scire quia sunt, quia tres panes sunt; non dico Trinitatem personarum, sed trinitatem sermonum, vel magis intellectuum, qui de personis habendi sunt: tres, inquam, panes unius magnitudinis et ponderis, unius formae et saporis. Quanta enim et qualia de Patre, tanta et talia de Filio seu de Spiritu sancto sentienda sunt, excepto duntaxat, quod proprietatem discretio numerum facit personarum, sicut in Trinitate, sic intelligentiarum in earum distinctione.

English translation

3. But you, my brothers, for whose use, and for whose merits, I ask these loaves — do you think we are sufficient, either I for the breaking, or you for the eating? I fear lest it be said to me: Seek not the things that are too high for thee, and search not into things above thy ability (Eccli. III, 21). I fear lest it also be said to you: You are become such as have need of milk, not of bread (Hebr. V, 12). I know indeed that there are loaves with the Father of the household which, if our infancy should presume upon them, our teeth would be broken sooner than our stomachs filled — that is, our minds built up. For who could grasp, who could suffice to explain or worthily consider that ineffable mystery of the Trinity: how the Father is from himself, the Son from the Father, the Holy Spirit from both, and yet three persons in unity of substance? A foolish woman, the bold vanity of heretics, stirs up those whose ears itch to discuss such things, though God is to be believed, not discussed. 'Gladly touch hidden loaves,' she says. As though you, foolish one, had touched what is loftier than the Angels! And to what purpose should I touch hidden loaves, which it is not lawful to break or eat without danger? It is enough for me to know that they exist, that there are three loaves — I do not say a Trinity of persons, but a trinity of words, or rather of understandings, which are to be held concerning the persons: three loaves, I say, of one magnitude and weight, of one form and savor. For whatever and however great things are to be thought of the Father, the same and equally great things are to be thought of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, except only that the distinction of properties makes a number of persons, just as in the Trinity, so in the distinction of our understandings concerning them.

4. Hos ergo panes relinquamus frangendos angelicae sublimitati, donec ad eorum crescentes aequalitatem, mensae eorum simus idonei. Multifariae siquidem aliorum trinitates panum de Scriptura nobis possunt proponi, qui nostrae magis congruant infirmitati, verbi gratia, ut nondum longius ab illa summa Trinitate recedamus, quod ex ipso, et per ipsum, et in ipso sunt omnia; quod a Patre creati, per Filium redempti, in Spiritu sancto sumus sanctificati. In hunc modum tam multa dici possunt, ut quamlibet famelicus venerit amicus de via, si vel dimidium ei apponas, non minus fortasse incipiat periclitari fastidio, quam prius inedia: ut copia obruat, quem inopia prius angebat. Non solum enim ipsum qui fecit nos, et ea quae facta sunt propter nos, sed etiam quae scripta sunt propter nos, describere tripliciter poteris, ut copiosa refectio sit in tribus panibus, historiae, allegoriae, et moralitatis. Universa quoque Scripturae continentia in tres partes divisa, velut in tres panes digesta est, dum aut de naturali justitia disserit, aut de litterali, aut de spirituali; id est, ante legem, sub lege, et post legem, quod est sub gratia. Natura rectum dedit intellectum; lex, etiam actum; gratia autem, affectum. Sed et Doctor et pastor Gentium in fide et veritate, pascendam docet Ecclesiam quadam panum trinitate; ut qui Ecclesiam aedificat, loquatur ad aedificationem, ad exhortationem, ad consolationem (I Cor. XIV, 3). Ad aedificationem, ut quid agere debeas, noris; ad exhortationem, ut quod noris, velis; ad consolationem, ut etiam in adversis possis quod noveris et volueris. Denique non solum in materia rerum, sensibus et partibus Scripturarum, generibus et modis locutionum, sed etiam in omni fine earum, quamdam panum invenies trinitatem, et ipsam quidem sapidam satis et salubrem: fidem scilicet, spem, et charitatem. Eo quippe omnis Scripturarum, vel sermonum intentio spectare videtur, ut credamus, speremus, diligamus. Nam et charitas sola finis praecepti definita est, quae et ipsa quodammodo triplex est, dum ex toto corde, tota anima, tota virtute habenda est. Sed quoniam vitio ducitur longior et varia epulatio, nos finem dabimus lectioni: vestrum erit fragmenta quae superaverunt, id est subtiliora, quae manus nostras effugerunt, colligere; simul autem et vestrum et nostrum, ei qui pascit nos cantare, Benedictus Deus in donis suis, qui vivit et regnat per omnia saecula saeculorum. Amen.

English translation

4. Let us therefore leave these loaves to be broken by the sublimity of angels, until, growing to equality with them, we become fit for their table. For indeed many other trinities of loaves can be set before us from Scripture, which are better suited to our weakness — for example, that we may not yet withdraw too far from that highest Trinity: that of him, and through him, and in him are all things; that we were created by the Father, redeemed through the Son, sanctified in the Holy Spirit. In this way so many things can be said that, however hungry a friend may come from the road, if you set before him even half of it, by surfeit — so that abundance may overwhelm him whom want formerly distressed. For not only him who made us, and the things that were made for our sake, but also the things that were written for our sake, you may describe in a threefold way, so that there be an abundant refreshment in three loaves: of history, of allegory, and of moral teaching. The whole content of Scripture too, divided into three parts, is as it were arranged into three loaves, in that it treats either of natural justice, or of the literal, or of the spiritual — that is, before the law, under the law, and after the law, which is under grace. Nature gave a right understanding; the law gave also right action; but grace gave right affection. Moreover the Teacher and Pastor of the Gentiles in faith and truth teaches that the Church is to be fed with a certain trinity of loaves: that he who builds up the Church should speak unto edification, unto exhortation, unto consolation (I Cor. XIV, 3). Unto edification, that you may know what you ought to do; unto exhortation, that you may will what you know; unto consolation, that even in adversities you may be able to do what you have known and willed. Finally, not only in the matter of things, in the senses and parts of the Scriptures, in the kinds and modes of speech, but also in their whole end and purpose, you will find a certain trinity of loaves, and one indeed quite savory and wholesome: namely, faith, hope, and charity. For to this end the whole intention of the Scriptures, or of discourses, seems to look: that we may believe, hope, and love. For charity alone has been defined as the end of the commandment, and it too is in a certain way threefold, since it must be held with the whole heart, the whole soul, and the whole strength. But since a feast too long and too varied is accounted a fault, we will put an end to our reading: it will be yours to gather up the fragments that remain — that is, the finer points that have escaped our hands — and it will be both yours and ours together to sing to him who feeds us: Blessed be God in his gifts, who lives and reigns through all the ages of ages. Amen.
