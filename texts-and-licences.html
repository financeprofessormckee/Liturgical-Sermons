Texts & Licences

Historical editions and the sources of these reading texts.

The historical editions listed below are public domain in the United States.

How these reading editions were made

Original texts were checked against source pages. Historical spelling and Scripture wording follow the edition; corrections and uncertain readings are explained in notes. Introductions and references marked “added by editors” are separate from the sermons. Numbered note markers have been added for navigation.

AI translations were generated by AI and reviewed separately by AI for errors. Historical human translations are identified by translator and edition; AI-assisted transcriptions are checked against the scans and compared with the original. Their omissions and differences are explained separately, without filling them from AI text. No human language review has occurred. Newman’s sermons are English originals.

Bourdaloue sometimes gives a saying in French and then quotes it in Latin. The AI version retains these repetitions. Where the opening Bible passage is printed in both languages, its Reading view shows one English version and Scholar shows both. Historical translations follow their own edition’s paragraphing and quotation practice.

Historical occasions are preserved; calendar browsing follows the traditional calendar.

Rights and reuse

The digitized copies of those editions are held by the libraries and archives named with each entry. This library reproduces its own transcriptions, not the page images; any use of the images is governed by the institution that holds them.

The English translations, editorial introductions and notes prepared for this library are licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International licence (CC BY 4.0).
